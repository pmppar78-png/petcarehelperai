Understanding ?

Get Pet Care

Seven to thirteen pounds, and a coat that mats if you skip a day

Havanese on this site stand about 8.5 to 11.5 inches and weigh 7 to 13 pounds, with lives often listed at 14 to 16 years. The profile calls Cuba's national dog a companion built to be underfoot. A workday alone in a crate shows up as barking, as a mess, or as a greeting that looks like panic. That temperament is part of the size story. A tiny dog who cannot be left is a scheduling problem for a decade and a half, and a larger crate does not fix it. A walker or a second person does.

Cataracts are the eye disease the profile says to ask about first. The screening it names is the CAER eye exam recorded by the Orthopedic Foundation for Animals. Cherry eye, progressive retinal atrophy, patellar luxation, and in some lines a deafness that needs a BAER test sit on the same list. VCA's pages on cataracts are the owner reading the profile points to. A cloudy eye or a squint is a clinic visit. A puppy who does not wake to a squeak needs the hearing test. Calling that independence misses the diagnosis. The limp to know is a kneecap. Have the veterinarian show you a normal patella while the dog is still a puppy and the exam is easy.

The coat is the daily tax. Silk mats when the comb skips. A puppy cut is how a lot of healthy Havanese actually live, and tear staining on the face is housekeeping. A swollen eye is a different problem. Dry the beard. Keep the hair short around the eyes if that is the trim you chose, and still look at the eye itself. Exercise is moderate: walks, tricks, and a play session. These dogs do not need a mile of jogging to stay fit, and they do get fat on a 7 to 13 pound frame when the treats are never counted.

Height is finished early in a toy dog. What misleads people is the furnishings. After a groom the same dog looks two pounds lighter. The scale usually disagrees. Use a scale that reads in ounces. Pick an adult target inside 7 to 13 pounds with the clinic, and write it on the bin. Growth food stops when the veterinarian says the dog has leveled off. From there the cup is small, and a single biscuit can be a large fraction of the day.

Buy the comb before the puppy. Book the first groom before the coat is felted. Ask the breeder for the eye paperwork and, if the line has had deaf puppies, the hearing result. Fourteen to sixteen years is a long time to comb a dog, and it is also a long time to manage a cataract or a kneecap you never asked about.
