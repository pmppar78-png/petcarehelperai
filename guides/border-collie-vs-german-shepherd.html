Border Collie vs German Shepherd: Side-by-Side

Border Collie and German Shepherd look superficially similar to new owners but differ in ways that matter for daily care. German Shepherd is larger at 50-90 lbs, while Border Collie typically runs 30-55 lbs. That size gap shows up in feeding volume, crate size, vehicle space, and how much joint-stress management each dog needs over their lifetime.

Both breeds share a high energy level, so the differentiator here is temperament, not exercise volume. Watch how each individual dog responds to training pressure, novelty, and time alone — that tells you more than the AKC group label.

Lifespan: Border Collie typically lives 12-15 yrs; German Shepherd 9-13 yrs. Border Collie generally has the longer-term care window, which affects insurance math and the point at which senior diagnostics become the dominant cost line.

Health watchlists differ. Both breeds share concerns around hip dysplasia. Border Collie carries additional risk for epilepsy, collie eye anomaly. German Shepherd is more notably predisposed to degenerative myelopathy, bloat. These aren’t guaranteed diagnoses — they’re the conditions responsible vets screen for, and they shape insurance underwriting more than most owners realize.
