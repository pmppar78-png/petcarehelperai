Lhasa Apso Pet Insurance Overview

Pet insurance for Lhasa Apsos is particularly important given their predisposition to kidney disease, cherry eye, luxating patella.

Lhasa Apso premiums are dog premiums. Renal dysplasia, patellar luxation, cherry eye, and corneal disease are the claims. Renal dysplasia has no corrective surgery.

Health Awareness: Lhasa Apsos carry genetic predispositions to kidney disease, cherry eye, luxating patella. Prevalence varies by individual, so the practical approach is a screening cadence that matches your vet's read of the breed's real-world risks. For most of these conditions, earlier identification translates directly into better management.

Best Time to Insure Your Lhasa Apso

Enroll your Lhasa Apso as early as possible — ideally as a puppy or kitten. Pre-existing conditions are never covered, so insuring before health issues develop is critical. Lhasa Apsos are prone to kidney disease, which can develop at any point in their life.

How much does Lhasa Apso pet insurance cost?

NAPHIA's 2025 State of the Industry report, compiled by Willis Towers Watson from year-end 2024 premiums, puts US dog accident-and-illness coverage at $62.44 a month ($749.29 a year) and accident-only coverage at $16.10 a month. Enrollment age, ZIP code, deductible, reimbursement percentage, and annual limit set the quote. For a Lhasa Apso, the claims that move the quote are renal dysplasia, cherry eye, and patellar luxation.

Is pet insurance worth it for a Lhasa Apso?

Renal dysplasia in a Lhasa Apso is malformed kidneys managed as chronic kidney disease. There is no reconstructive operation. Cherry eye can be pocketed surgically, and a luxating patella can be repaired. Corneal ulcers are medical unless they perforate.

What pre-existing conditions affect Lhasa Apso insurance?

Any condition diagnosed before enrollment is excluded. For Lhasa Apsos, common pre-existing concerns include kidney disease and cherry eye. Early enrollment is key.
