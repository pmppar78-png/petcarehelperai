How to Train a Bichon Frise

This page is educational reference material for preparing questions. A licensed veterinarian examines the animal, makes the diagnosis, and sets the treatment.

Bichon Frise training. Tips for their moderate energy non-sporting breed temperament.

Training Approach

Bichon Frises are moderate-energy non-sporting dogs that benefit from regular but moderate training routines. Their intelligence and temperament make them responsive to positive reinforcement training methods.

Weighing around 12-18 lbs and lifespan of 14-15 yrs, the Bichon Frise has specific care needs shaped by its genetics and build. At 12-18 lbs with a life expectancy of 14-15 yrs, the Bichon Frise represents a significant commitment that rewards prepared owners with years of devoted companionship.

Health Awareness: Bichon Frises carry known breed-associated risks including allergies, bladder stones, luxating patella. A screening schedule tuned to those specific risks — which your vet can outline — is one of the highest-leverage moves you make as an owner, because most of these conditions are easier to treat earlier than later.

Bichon Frise Training Challenges

What are the most important considerations for how to train a bichon frise?

A Bichon Frise trains best in a few minutes with food, then a break before the coat work. Teach a stand for grooming early, because this dog will be on a table for the rest of its life. Barking at the door needs a cue to go to a mat, practiced when the house is quiet.
