Essential Oils Toxic

Why most essential oils are dangerous for cats. Covers which oils to avoid, diffuser safety, symptoms of toxicity, and cat-safe alternatives.

Cats process many oils poorly

Cats are missing efficient glucuronidation pathways in the liver that help other species break down phenols and related compounds. Essential oils are full of those compounds. A concentration a dog might tolerate as a brief smell can make a cat drool, wobble, and tremor. Tea tree oil is a leading example. So are wintergreen, peppermint, citrus, pine, clove, cinnamon, eucalyptus, and ylang ylang. The route is usually grooming. A diffuser coats the fur with a fine film. The cat licks the film off and swallows the oil. A drop applied to the skin "for fleas" does the same job faster. Undiluted oil on a paw is an emergency. Putting it on fur as a grooming step is a common way cats get poisoned.

Phenols in some oils injure the liver as well as the nervous system. Liquid potpourri, which often contains cationic detergents plus fragrance, can burn the mouth and cause liver injury and fever. That product is a separate and severe exposure. If the bottle says liquid potpourri or oil warmer, say those words on the phone. Ordinary dried flowers are a different object. The liquid is the danger.

What toxicity looks like in a cat

Drooling and a wet chin come first when the mouth is burned or the taste is strong. Then you may see a wobbly walk, depression, tremors, rapid breathing, or a seizure. Some cats vomit. Some hide and feel cold. Signs can start within a couple of hours of grooming and can worsen as more oil is licked off the coat. A cat who smells strongly of mint, citrus, or tea tree and is unsteady needs a clinic the same hour. Breathing effort, blue or gray gums, or a seizure means you travel while someone calls ahead. Keep the cat in a carrier, warm, and in fresh air. Do not put the cat back into the room where the diffuser is still running.

Wintergreen adds a salicylate problem on top of the oil problem. Citrus and pine oils are common in "pet-safe" cleaners that were never tested on cats. Read the label. A product marketed for dogs can be catastrophic on a cat. Permethrin flea spot-ons are a pesticide, and they cause severe tremors. If both an oil and a permethrin product might be involved, tell the clinic both names. The bath instructions differ.

First aid that does not make the burn worse

Call your veterinarian, an emergency clinic, or a poison hotline before you start a home bath. Have the bottle. . Cats are difficult to make vomit safely, oils aspirate easily, and a tremoring cat can choke. Do not give milk, butter, or another oil. If the clinic approves skin decontamination, they will usually describe a gentle wash with mild dish soap and lukewarm water, drying the cat fully so hypothermia does not follow. A struggling cat may need the wash done at the hospital under supervision. Wipe visible oil from the face carefully so none runs into the eyes. If the eyes were splashed, ask about a saline or water flush and have the clinic examine the cornea.

Hospital care can include tremor control, fluids, temperature support, and bloodwork if the oil is one that threatens the liver. There is no pantry antidote. Liquid potpourri burns may need pain control and follow-up for the mouth and esophagus. Ask what the next twelve hours should look like if you are sent home, and ask whether the other cats who groomed the same blanket should be examined. Oil transfers from cat to cat during allogrooming. Separate them until the coat is clean.

A house that does not rely on fragrance

The most reliable plan is to keep undiluted oils out of a cat home. If a person in the household uses oils on their own skin, they wash their hands and change a shirt before picking up the cat. Diffusers, wax melts, and reed sticks move to a building the cat does not enter, or they are retired. Cleaners labeled with essential oils get replaced with products your veterinarian is comfortable with, used while the cat is shut in a distant room, then rinsed and aired. Flea control comes from the clinic, in a product labeled for cats, in the weight band of this cat. Dog flea products stay in a different cabinet.

Store any remaining bottles where a cat cannot knock them from a shelf. Cats bat objects onto the floor and then walk through the puddle. A latch and a high closed cupboard beat a pretty tray. Tell houseguests that scented gifts should stay in their luggage. When a spill happens, confine the cat away from the room, blot the oil with gloves, wash the floor with dish soap, air the space, and call if the cat may already have stepped in it. The phrase on the label that should change your behavior is simple: if it is an essential oil and you have a cat, keep it bottled and out of the air.

Grooming, blankets, and the other cat

Oil on one cat becomes oil on a second cat when they groom each other. If a spill touched a shared bed, wash the bedding and separate the cats until coats are clean and the clinic has said they can reunite. Ask whether both need an exam. Tremors can start after a later grooming bout, well after the spill itself. Keep the bottle for the label. Liquid potpourri and dog permethrin products should be named separately if they might be involved, because the burns and the tremors are handled as their own emergencies.

Replace diffusers and wax melts with fresh air. People who wear oils should wash hands and change shirts before they pick up a cat. Store remaining bottles in a latched cupboard. A cat will bat a pretty bottle to the floor and then walk through it. The house rule is short enough to tell a guest: concentrated oils stay capped, and the cat does not attend the spa day.

Questions about oils and cats

Why are essential oils riskier for cats than for many other pets?

Cats lack robust glucuronidation, a liver pathway that helps break down phenols found in many oils. Grooming then turns a scented room into an oral ingestion. Tea tree, wintergreen, citrus, pine, and peppermint are frequent problems. A small spill can be enough to cause drooling and tremors.

What should I do if my cat walked through spilled tea tree oil?

Call a veterinarian or a poison hotline immediately and keep the bottle nearby. . Ask whether a gentle dish-soap bath is appropriate, and dry the cat so they do not get cold. Tremors, hiding, or labored breathing mean you should travel to a clinic now.

Can I run a diffuser if the cat seems to ignore it?

Oil lands on fur and is swallowed during grooming, even when the cat looks relaxed. The safer choice in a cat home is to skip diffusers, wax melts, and reed oils. If exposure already happened, air the room, separate the cat, and call. Liver and neurologic effects can be underway before anything looks dramatic.
