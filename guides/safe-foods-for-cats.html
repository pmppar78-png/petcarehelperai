Safe Human Foods for Cats

Which human foods cats can safely eat, proper portions, foods to always avoid, and healthy treat alternatives for cats.

The meal is a complete cat food

Cats are obligate carnivores. They need nutrients from animal tissue, including taurine, preformed vitamin A, and arachidonic acid, in amounts a dog food or a vegetarian plate will miss. The practical safe meal is a commercial food labeled complete and balanced for cats, in the life stage you are feeding: growth for kittens, adult maintenance, or a veterinary diet if one has been prescribed. Wet food adds water, which helps cats who rarely drink enough. Dry food can be part of the plan if the cat stays hydrated and the calories are measured. The label's feeding guide is a start. Body condition and the clinic scale are the adjustment.

Change foods over several days by mixing a little more of the new food each day. A sudden swap causes vomiting and diarrhea that look like illness. If the cat refuses the new food entirely for a day, call the clinic. Cats develop hepatic lipidosis when they stop eating, especially if they are overweight. Do not fast a cat to force a brand change. Ask for a transition plan. A hunger strike is a dangerous tool in a cat.

People-food extras that can fit

Plain cooked meat, unseasoned, can be a rare topper: a bit of boiled chicken, turkey, or lean beef, with no onion, garlic, salt, or sauce. A fully cooked egg, plain, is an occasional protein extra for many cats if the clinic agrees. These are toppers. They become a problem when they replace the balanced food and the cat holds out for them. Count them as calories so the waistline stays stable. Cats with kidney disease, urinary disease, or food allergies need the veterinarian to name the extra, because a "healthy" chicken topper can wreck a prescription plan.

A lick of tuna in water, drained, is a flavor some clinicians allow rarely. Daily human tuna brings mercury, salt, and an incomplete pattern, including thiamine concerns when fish crowds out everything else. That topic has its own guide. Here the rule is shorter: the complete food stays in the bowl, and fish from your sandwich is a special case you clear first. Some cats are offered a small taste of plain canned pumpkin for fiber when a veterinarian suggests it for a specific stool issue. Pie filling is seasoned and sweetened. Use the plain puree only when they have told you to, and stop if the cat vomits.

Foods and habits to leave out of the cat's bowl

Skip onions, garlic, chives, leeks, alcohol, caffeine, chocolate, raw yeast dough, cooked bones, and xylitol-sweetened foods. Grapes and raisins should be discussed with a clinician if they are eaten, because the dog risk is established and the cat picture should be handled by a professional. Dog food is the wrong staple. Milk gives many adult cats diarrhea because lactose is hard for them to digest. Bones splinter. Raw eggs carry bacteria, and raw fish-only feeding risks thiamine deficiency. Lilies are a plant, and they still belong in this conversation because a cat who chews a petal can go into kidney failure. Keep bouquets out of the house.

Seasonings are where safe meat turns unsafe. A rotisserie chicken is often coated in garlic and salt. Broth from a soup may list onion powder. Baby food meat must be read line by line. If the ingredient panel is long, choose a different topper. When a cat steals a plate, identify the sauce before you decide it was "just chicken." Call a veterinarian or a poison hotline if onion, xylitol, alcohol, dough, or chocolate was involved. ASPCA Animal Poison Control is 888-426-4435. Skip home vomiting unless the stolen-plate consult directs it.

Water, measuring, and the picky cat

Place water away from the food if your cat prefers that, and consider a second bowl or a fountain. Wash bowls daily. Measure meals so you notice a shrinking appetite early. A cat who leaves food for a day, or who eats less for two days, needs a call. Weight loss in an older cat with a good appetite can be thyroid disease or another illness. Weight loss with a poor appetite can be dental pain, kidney disease, or many other problems. The food is safe only if the cat is actually eating it. A beautiful diet in a bowl the cat walks past is a medical situation.

Kittens eat more often than adults. Follow the growth food's directions and the clinic's weight checks. Adult cats usually do well on set meals so you can see intake, though some veterinarians allow measured dry food for grazing when the cat's weight is already in a healthy range. Treats, including the cooked meat, should stay a small fraction of calories. Puzzle feeders and a short play session before dinner help a bored cat approach the bowl. If you need ideas because the cat is painful or nauseated, the clinic should drive that plan. Safe food is the food that matches the species, the life stage, and the individual chart on the fridge.

  • Feed a complete and balanced cat food as the meal.
  • Use plain cooked meat or a plain cooked egg only as a rare topper the clinic accepts.
  • Keep human tuna rare because of mercury and incomplete nutrition.
  • Leave out alliums, alcohol, caffeine, chocolate, dough, bones, and xylitol.
  • Read rotisserie coatings and baby-food labels for onion and garlic.
  • Call if a toxic ingredient was eaten, and skip home vomiting unless directed.
  • Measure meals and call when a cat skips a day of eating.
  • Keep lilies out of the house even though they are a plant, because kidney failure follows small exposures.

Toppers, prescriptions, and skipped meals

A prescription kidney or urinary diet fails when a tasty topper becomes the real meal. If your cat is on one of those foods, ask the prescriber before you add chicken, egg, or fish. Salt and phosphorus hide in leftovers. Plain water and the prescribed food are the default. A cat who refuses the diet for a day needs a call, because fasting is dangerous and because the refusal may be nausea from the disease itself. The clinic can suggest a transition or an appetite plan that keeps the medical goal intact.

Measure what you put down and what you pick up. A shrinking portion over two days is data. Weight loss with a strong appetite in an older cat should be reported. It can be thyroid disease or another illness that food tricks will not fix. Keep human tuna rare, keep onions out of the kitchen scraps, and keep lilies out of the building. The safe everyday food is the complete cat food. Extras are small, named, and agreed.

Questions about safe foods for cats

What should a cat eat every day?

A commercial diet labeled complete and balanced for cats, matched to age or to a veterinary prescription. Wet food helps with water intake. Measure the amount and transition new brands slowly. Do not fast a cat to force a switch, because cats can develop hepatic lipidosis when they stop eating.

Which human foods can be occasional extras?

Small amounts of plain cooked chicken, turkey, or beef, or a fully cooked plain egg, if your veterinarian agrees. No onion, garlic, salt, or sauce. Tuna from a human can should stay rare. Anything with xylitol, chocolate, alcohol, or dough is a reason to call a clinic.

Why is milk a bad everyday drink?

Many adult cats digest lactose poorly and get diarrhea. A complete cat food and fresh water cover hydration and calcium. If you want a topper, ask the clinic about options that fit this cat's health. A saucer of milk gives many adult cats diarrhea.
