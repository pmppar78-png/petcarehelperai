Corn Snake Care Guide

Beginner-friendly corn snake care guide covering enclosure, temperature, feeding, handling, shedding, and why corn snakes are ideal first snakes.

Housing and Enclosure

Corn snakes need an escape-proof enclosure — a 40-gallon terrarium minimum for adults. Maintain a temperature gradient with a warm side of 85°F and cool side of 75°F using under-tank heating or ceramic heat emitters. Provide multiple hides (warm and cool side), climbing branches, and a water bowl large enough for soaking. Maintain 40-50% humidity.

Diet and Nutrition

Feed appropriately sized frozen-thawed mice — hatchlings eat pinky mice every 5-7 days, adults eat adult mice every 10-14 days. The prey item should be roughly 1.5 times the widest part of the snake. Always use frozen-thawed prey rather than live to prevent injury to your snake.

Health and Veterinary Care

Common corn snake health issues include respiratory infections (from improper humidity or temperature), mites, scale rot, and retained shed. Ensure proper husbandry parameters to prevent most issues. Signs of illness include wheezing, mouth breathing, stuck shed, lethargy, and refusal to eat for extended periods.

Common Health Issues

  • Metabolic bone disease: Often caused by inadequate UVB lighting or calcium supplementation
  • Respiratory infections: Usually linked to incorrect temperature or humidity
  • Parasites: Both internal and external parasites can affect exotic pets
  • Nutritional deficiencies: Result from improper or monotonous diets
  • Stress-related illness: Caused by improper housing, handling, or environmental factors

Handling and Socialization

  • Allow new pets to acclimate to their enclosure before handling
  • Learn proper handling techniques specific to your pet's species
  • Start with short handling sessions and gradually increase duration
  • Wash hands before and after handling to prevent disease transmission
  • Respect your pet's signals — not all exotic pets enjoy being handled

Are corn snakes good for beginner reptile owners?

Some exotic pets are excellent for beginners (leopard geckos, corn snakes, budgies, betta fish) while others require advanced experience (chameleons, macaws, saltwater aquariums). Research thoroughly before committing.

How much do exotic pets cost to care for?

Initial setup costs (enclosure, lighting, heating) are usually the largest expense, ranging from $100-$1,000+. Ongoing costs for food, substrate, and veterinary care typically run $30-$100/month.

Because a feeding plan lives or dies on small personal details, loop in a veterinarian who has actually examined the pet.
