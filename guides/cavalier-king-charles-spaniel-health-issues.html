Cavalier King Charles Spaniel Health Issues

Common health problems in Cavalier King Charles Spaniels including mitral valve disease, syringomyelia, hip dysplasia. Prevention, symptoms to watch for, and treatment options.

Cavalier King Charles Spaniels are predisposed to several health conditions including mitral valve disease, syringomyelia, hip dysplasia. Understanding these risks allows you to screen early, prevent where possible, and catch problems before they become emergencies.

Hearts come first, before kisses and coat color. Mitral valve disease is the adult heart problem the parent club treats as expected screening for these toy spaniels. At home the early picture is a new cough, faster breathing on the couch, tiring on a route that used to be easy, or a pal who waits to be lifted. In the exam room the picture is a murmur. I need the parents screened and the note written down, and I need this dog's chest heard while the muzzle is still dark. A spaniel at roughly 13 pounds and a spaniel at 18 lb can both grow that murmur. Pounds fail to sort the valve.

Syringomyelia with Chiari-like malformation is the neck and brain problem. The sign families finally name is phantom scratching: a hind foot paddling at the neck or shoulder while the skin stays untouched, sometimes with a yelp when someone lifts the dog or the collar shifts. Neck pain can look like a refusal of the harness or sleep with the chin propped up. Episodic falling is a separate event. The dog stiffens, collapses, or moves in a strange stiff gait, then stands again. I request the DNA test for episodic falling on both parents so the folder separates that disease from a clumsy puppy.

Dry eye and curly coat arrive together in the unlucky pup: a harsh curly jacket plus eyes that are painful and short on tears. An eye check plus a coat that feels wrong for silk are the clues. A slipping kneecap announces itself as a hitch in the hind step. Hip trouble looks like a sway, or like a long pause before hopping onto the sofa. Both belong on the orthopedic list beside the heart notes. Height here is 12 to 13 inches, easy to forget once the dog lives in a lap.

I have heard murmurs in a Cavalier still playing at 9 and in another who tired on one flight of stairs at 14. Those are individual dogs, and they are why a gray muzzle is the wrong time to start listening. Book the listen during the 3-year mark and keep it scheduled during year 5, then at visits after that. People hope for a band of 12 to 15 years, and heart disease shortens many of those years. Merck's veterinary manual on mitral valve disease is the clinical frame I hand over for the cough and the murmur, without turning one spaniel into a made-up rate.

Film the air scratch and film a falling spell, because both are brief and an appointment can land between them. Bring the clips with the parents' heart notes. A gentle toy spaniel will keep greeting you while a valve leaks, which is why the stethoscope has to notice first. Phantom scratching gets waved off as itch until someone watches the foot miss the neck. Falling gets waved off as a trip on a rug. The phone clip is how those two stay different in the record.

Mental stimulation is as important as physical exercise for Cavalier King Charles Spaniel. Boredom is the root cause of most destructive behavior — not disobedience. Puzzle feeders, scent work, and novel experiences challenge your Cavalier King Charles Spaniel's mind in ways that a standard walk cannot. Change up the routine regularly: the same toys and the same routes lose their enrichment value quickly.
