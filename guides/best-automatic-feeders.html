Automatic Feeder Buying Guide

Types of Automatic Feeders

  • Gravity Feeders: Simple, no power needed. Food drops as bowl empties. Not for portion control.
  • Timed/Programmable: Dispense set portions at scheduled times. Best for weight management.
  • Smart/WiFi Connected: App control, remote feeding, notifications. Most flexible.
  • Compartment/Carousel: Pre-filled sections rotate to reveal meals. Good for wet food.
  • Microchip-Activated: Opens only for specific pets. Perfect for multi-pet households.

Key Features to Consider

  • Capacity: Match to pet size and how often you want to refill
  • Portion Control: Essential for weight management
  • Power Source: Plug-in with battery backup is most reliable
  • Food Type: Most work only with dry kibble; wet food needs special feeders
  • Connectivity: WiFi/app features add convenience but require setup
  • Cleaning: Removable, dishwasher-safe parts make maintenance easier

Important Considerations

Automatic feeders should supplement, not replace, human interaction. Monitor your pet's eating habits through app logs or visual checks. Clean feeders weekly to prevent bacteria growth. Always have a battery backup for timed feeders to avoid missed meals during power outages.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can automatic feeders work with wet food?

Most automatic feeders only work with dry kibble. For wet food, use a compartment-style feeder like the Cat Mate C500 with ice packs to keep food fresh. Never leave wet food out more than a few hours unrefrigerated.

Will my cat figure out how to get extra food?

Some clever cats try to reach into dispensers. Look for feeders with secure hoppers and anti-jam mechanisms. Microchip feeders with sealed lids are most secure against food thieves.

How long can I leave my pet with an automatic feeder?

Automatic feeders are great for work days or weekend trips. For longer absences, always have someone check on your pet at least every 24-48 hours. Feeders can jam, batteries can die, and pets need more than just food.
