Blue Tongue Skink Care Guide

Comprehensive blue tongue skink care covering species, enclosure setup, diet, handling, health issues, and why they make great pet reptiles.

Housing and Enclosure

Blue-tongued skinks are heavy, ground-dwelling lizards in the genus Tiliqua, and the species on the label changes the humidity you owe them. A northern blue tongue from Australia wants a drier cage with a moist hide. An Indonesian or Halmahera animal wants humid air and a substrate that stays slightly damp. Mixing those two care sheets is how keepers end up with respiratory infections or stuck sheds. Adults need floor space. A 4-foot by 2-foot by 2-foot enclosure is the practical home. A 40-gallon tank is a short-term juvenile cage and pinches an adult that cannot turn around on the basking slate.

Give a basking surface around 95 to 105 F, read with an infrared thermometer on the slate, and let the animal tell you where in that band it settles. Many northerns use the hotter end. Many Indonesian skinks bask a little softer and still need a warm zone. The warm side of the air can sit in the mid to high 80s F, the cool side in the mid to upper 70s, and the night can fall into the low 70s when the house allows. A thermostat runs the heat. Without one, a ceramic emitter in a closed cage will overshoot on a summer afternoon. UVB is part of this setup, a linear tube over the basking half, strong enough for a diurnal lizard and replaced on schedule. A forest-gecko lamp is the wrong tool on this cage. A tiny coil bulb in a dome at one corner leaves the body in shade while the keeper thinks "there is a light."

Substrate should be deep enough to dig. A mix of topsoil and coconut fiber, or a similar burrowing blend, lets a skink make a retreat. Keep a moist pocket even in a northern cage so sheds come off in one piece. Water is a heavy bowl that will not tip when a 500-gram lizard walks through it. Hides on both ends, a flat basking rock, and a secure lid complete the cage. Skinks are strong and will shove a loose screen. House them alone unless you have a specific, watched pair and a plan for the injuries that follow most "friendships."

Diet and Nutrition

Blue tongues are omnivores with a strong interest in protein and greens. A sensible week mixes animal foods and plants: gut-loaded insects, snails, a cooked egg, or a plain lean meat portion on some days, and a salad of squash, collards, and berries on others. Many healthy adults eat every two or three days. Juveniles eat more often. Calcium dust goes on the insect and meat meals several times a week, with a multivitamin less often, and the UVB tube does the vitamin D work a dark cupboard cannot. A formulated skink diet can be part of the rotation if the ingredient list is something your reptile veterinarian recognizes.

Dog or cat food shows up in older care sheets. A grain-inclusive wet dog food is sometimes used as one protein meal, and it is easy to overfeed. Fatty minced meat every day produces an obese skink with fatty liver. If you use a dog food at all, keep it occasional, low fat, and free of onion, garlic, and heavy seasoning, and ask the veterinarian who examines your skink whether it belongs in the bowl. Vegetables should be more than a garnish. Fruit is a small portion because these lizards gain weight fast in a 4-foot cage with no real exercise.

Feed in a dish. A skink that lunges through loose substrate swallows enough dirt to slow the gut. Pull leftovers the same day, especially meat, because a warm humid Indonesian cage will spoil a meatball by evening. Fresh water daily, even if you rarely see the animal drink. They often soak when you are out of the room.

Health and Veterinary Care

Respiratory infections are the humidity mismatch disease: bubbles, wheezing, and a skink that stays in the open with its mouth cracked. Indonesian animals kept desert-dry, and northerns kept in a swamp, both show up this way. Parasites are common enough that a fecal exam belongs with the first wellness visit. Metabolic bone disease follows missing UVB and undusted meals and looks like a soft jaw and a reluctance to lift the body. Obesity is the quiet problem. A skink should have a thick tail and a body you can still describe as muscular. A perfectly round animal that cannot lift its belly is overfed, and the fix is fewer fatty meals plus a larger cage, discussed with a veterinarian if the weight is already extreme.

Sheds should come off in large pieces, including the eye caps and the toe rings. A retained ring on a toe cuts circulation. Raise the humidity in the moist hide and see a reptile veterinarian if the toe darkens. Mouth rot and scale infections follow a cage that stays wet and dirty. Spot-clean daily. These lizards produce a lot of waste. Quarantine any new skink. Mites and respiratory disease travel with animals from crowded racks.

Handling is a strength of the species once the skink is tame. Support the body and the tail base. A startled skink hisses and may puff and bite, and the bite is a pinch more than a medical event for most adults, still worth avoiding. Wash hands after. Short, regular sessions beat a monthly wrestling match. Children should sit and let the skink walk across a lap, with an adult controlling the shoulders.

Species Check Before You Buy

  • Ask whether the animal is a northern, an eastern, an Indonesian, a Halmahera, or a Merauke, and write the answer on the cage.
  • Set humidity to that locality. Northerns stay drier with a moist hide. Indonesian species need humid air.
  • Measure the basking slate and both air zones after the lights have been on for two hours.
  • Offer a mixed plate and calcium, and keep fatty foods rare.
  • Book a reptile veterinarian for a fecal check while the skink is still in quarantine.

A blue tongue that patrols the front glass at feeding time, flicks a broad blue tongue, and digs a trench under the hide is doing the job the cage was built for. One that wheezes, sits with eyes shut under the lamp all day, or sheds in tiny flakes needs the humidity and the temperature rechecked the same afternoon.

Write the Locality on the Cage

Northern, eastern, Indonesian, Halmahera, and Merauke blue tongues do not share one humidity number. Put the locality where you will see it during every misting decision. Northerns stay drier and still need a moist hide for sheds. Indonesian animals need humid air and a substrate that does not dry to dust. The basking slate can sit around 95 to 105 F, read with an infrared thermometer, with air on the warm side in the 80s and a cool side in the 70s. A linear UVB tube over that warm half, replaced on schedule, belongs in the setup. A coil bulb in a dome at one corner leaves the body in shade.

Feed an omnivore plate every two or three days for a healthy adult: insects or a plain protein portion, squash and greens, and calcium on the animal foods several times a week. Fatty meat every day builds a skink that cannot lift its belly. A formulated diet can join the rotation if your reptile veterinarian agrees with the ingredients. Watch the next shed. It should peel in large pieces, toes and eye caps included. Wheezing or bubbles mean the humidity and the temperatures need a same-day review and usually a clinic. These are strong, handleable lizards when the species on the label matches the cage you built.

Quick Answers

Are blue tongue skinks good for beginner reptile owners?

They can be a solid first large lizard if you match humidity to the exact species, provide a 4-foot cage, UVB, and a basking slate near 95 to 105 F, and feed an omnivore diet with calcium.

How much does blue tongue skink care cost?

The enclosure, thermostat, and deep substrate are the costly opening, commonly several hundred dollars. Linear UVB joins them. Zoo Med's sheet says to write the purchase date on the lamp and replace it a year later (zoomed.com). Retailers listed a 22-inch ReptiSun 5.0 T5 HO near $22 to $26 in October 2026. A reptile wellness visit, including the fecal these skinks often need, starts from the 2026 exotic exam band of about $95 to $185 before the test itself (findanexoticvet.com).
