Under seven pounds, with three diseases to learn by name

The Maltese profile quotes the American Kennel Club picture of the breed as a toy companion at 7 to 9 inches, weight held under 7 pounds, with a white coat of hair that grows continuously. This size page's own maturity line says 4 to 7 pounds. Lifespan on the card is 12 to 15 years. A dog that light has no spare room for a missed meal or a missed diagnosis. The adult outline is a haircut. The body under it is only a few pounds, and a puppy cut still leaves a beard and a sanitary area that have to be dried.

Three conditions are named on the profile as the ones to know: portosystemic shunts, patellar luxation, and tracheal collapse. A puppy who seems unsteady or dull after eating needs a veterinarian, and the profile points at the Merck Veterinary Manual for the shunt. Dental extractions are described as what happens when the mouth is ignored until middle age, so a mouth check by about two years beats waiting. A honking cough on a walk is the trachea announcing itself. A sweater is reasonable in a cold apartment. It does not open an airway. Use a harness on a neck that small, and keep the collar off the trachea.

Mats form against the skin when the comb stops. You cannot score the ribs through a felted coat, and you can miss a lump the size of the whole dog's breakfast. Comb to the skin on a schedule you can keep for 12 to 15 years, or keep the trim short and still dry the face every day. Staining is often cosmetic. A swollen eye or a thick discharge is a corneal or tear-duct problem, which is the distinction the profile draws.

Exercise on the card is low to moderate, about 30 minutes. Indoor play plus a short walk is a full day. Other dogs are fine when the other dog understands how small this one is. A rough playmate is a fracture risk. Socialization is a calm dog who already understands a toy breed. Food is measured in tiny cups. Treats count. Pick the adult weight with the clinic, inside the 4 to 7 pounds this page already uses, and protect it. There is no "almost in the breed" once the scale says 9 pounds. That is a different body condition on a toy frame.

At the first visits, ask for the kneecaps, the throat, and a conversation about liver shunts if the puppy is small even for a Maltese or seems off after meals. Bring the coat question too: who will comb, and what the sanitary trim should look like. The size is the easy part to memorize. The care that keeps a 5 pound dog alive for the whole 12 to 15 years is the comb, the harness, and the clinic who has already looked in the mouth.
