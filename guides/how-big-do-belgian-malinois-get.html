Questions Owners Ask

Belgian Malinois adults commonly fall between 40 and 80 pounds and stay lean when the work matches the food. Height is mostly set by a year, and thigh muscle comes later. A Malinois that looks finished at eight months often adds chest and neck through 18 months. Feed to a waist you can see, and lengthen the training when the cup goes up.

What 40 to 80 pounds looks like on a Malinois

The card for this breed puts a grown Belgian Malinois at 22 to 26 inches and 40 to 80 pounds, and it lists 14 to 16 years when the dog stays sound. That pound range is wide on purpose. A lighter female and a male who has been in hard condition can both sit inside it. Weigh the dog you have. A label that only says large will not choose the cup or the crate.

On a herding build like this, most of the height is present around the first birthday. Chest, neck, and thigh keep changing afterward. People who order the adult harness at eight months usually order it again. Measure the girth behind the elbows every couple of months until the second birthday. Keep the collar on the neck with room for two fingers, because these dogs pull into whatever they wear and a tight collar on a growing throat is a bad habit to set.

The profile names hip dysplasia and elbow dysplasia first, then epilepsy, progressive retinal atrophy, cataracts, and MDR1 drug sensitivity. Hemangiosarcoma is on that same list. The profile points owners to VCA's plain-language pages on hip dysplasia and on seizures, which is the reading to do before the first limp that lasts a day or the first strange episode of staring and fly-biting. Ask the breeder which hip and elbow evaluations the parents hold, whether an ophthalmologist has seen the eyes, and whether any close relative has seized. Put an MDR1 result in the folder before anyone books anesthesia. A collapse in a middle-aged dog goes to a clinic the same day.

Food on the profile is often $600 to $1,200 for the year. The yearly ownership band printed beside it is about $1,350 to $4,000, and a trainer who has actually worked this breed costs more than a Saturday puppy class. While the dog is still growing, the costly mistake is a soft waist. Extra fat on a puppy that will later hit a tug or a sleeve is weight those hips did not need. Use a large-breed growth diet until the veterinarian is happy with the way the dog is maturing, then switch to the adult food and keep the waist visible from above.

The exercise line on the card is very high. A long jog with no training still leaves the brain unfinished. Obedience, scent, herding, or a real sport is the other half of the day, and it waits until the sessions are short enough for open growth plates. Slippery floors and endless ball throws on pavement can wait with them. Moderate shedding means a weekly brush keeps the coat decent. It does nothing for a dog whose adult body arrived and whose job did not. Size the fence, the crate a dog at the top of that 40 to 80 pound band can stand in, and the after-hours number before the puppy is twelve weeks old. Bring the parents' hip and elbow papers to the one-year visit and ask the clinic to watch the dog trot.
