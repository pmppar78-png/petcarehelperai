Nutritional Needs of Cane Corsos

As a large working breed with moderate energy levels, the Cane Corso has specific nutritional requirements that differ from other dogs. Understanding these needs is key to keeping your Cane Corso healthy throughout their 9-12 yrs lifespan.

Cane Corsos typically weigh 88-120 lbs and need approximately 1,400–2,200 calories per day, depending on age, activity level, and metabolism. With moderate energy levels, most Cane Corsos do well on standard feeding guidelines for their size.

Health Awareness: Key conditions flagged in Cane Corsos populations: hip dysplasia, bloat, cherry eye. These are probabilities, not destinies — but the probabilities are high enough that a structured screening plan with your vet pays off, especially given how much earlier detection improves outcomes.

Foods to Avoid

Never feed your Cane Corso these dangerous foods.

  • Macadamia nuts and alcohol
  • Cooked bones (splintering risk)

Supplements Worth Considering

Based on Cane Corso-specific health concerns, these supplements may benefit your dog.

  • Glucosamine & Chondroitin: Essential for joint health, especially important for Cane Corsos prone to hip dysplasia

Wet Food vs Dry Food for Cane Corsos

Both wet and dry food have advantages for Cane Corsos.

  • Mixed feeding: Many Cane Corso owners find success combining both for balanced nutrition and variety

Feeding Mistakes to Avoid

Common feeding errors that Cane Corso owners make include.

  • Overuse of treats: Treats should make up no more than 10% of your Cane Corso's daily caloric intake. Many training treats are high in calories, so adjust meal portions accordingly.
  • Ignoring body condition: Use the body condition score (BCS) system to assess your Cane Corso's weight rather than relying solely on scale numbers. You should be able to feel the ribs without pressing hard.

Age-Specific Nutrition Considerations

Adult stage (1-7 years): Maintain a consistent feeding routine with measured portions. Monitor weight monthly and adjust food amounts based on activity level, seasonal changes, and body condition. Adult Cane Corsos benefit from a protein content of 22-30%.

Senior stage (7+ years): Older Cane Corsos may need fewer calories but higher-quality protein to maintain muscle mass. How much should I feed my Cane Corso?

Adult Cane Corsos typically need 3–5 cups of high-quality food per day, split into two meals. Adjust based on your dog's activity level, age, and body condition score.

What is the best food brand for Cane Corsos?

Look for foods that list real meat as the first ingredient, meet AAFCO standards, and address Cane Corso-specific health needs like hip dysplasia. Brands offering large breed-specific formulas are often a good choice.
