Best Food for Devon Rex Cats: Nutrition Guide

Top rated cat food for Devon Rex cats. Diet recommendations for their short coat, high energy needs, and common concerns like HCM.

Nutritional Needs of Devon Rexs

As a small cat breed with high energy levels, the Devon Rex has specific nutritional requirements that differ from other cats. Understanding these needs is key to keeping your Devon Rex healthy throughout their 9-15 yrs lifespan.

Devon Rexs typically weigh 5-10 lbs and need approximately 400–800 calories per day, depending on age, activity level, and metabolism. Given their high energy levels, active Devon Rexs may need 20-30% more calories than the average cat of their size.

Genetic Health Considerations: The Devon Rex breed has documented susceptibility to HCM, luxating patella, myopathy. Awareness of these predispositions is valuable for two reasons: it guides preventive screening decisions, and it helps you recognize early symptoms that might otherwise be overlooked.

Never feed your Devon Rex these dangerous foods.

  • Cooked bones (splintering risk)

Supplements Worth Considering

Based on Devon Rex-specific health concerns, these supplements may benefit your cat.

Wet Food vs Dry Food for Devon Rexs

Both wet and dry food have advantages for Devon Rexs.

  • Mixed feeding: Many Devon Rex owners find success combining both for balanced nutrition and variety

Feeding Mistakes to Avoid

Common feeding errors that Devon Rex owners make include:

  • Overuse of treats: Treats should make up no more than 10% of your Devon Rex's daily caloric intake. Many training treats are high in calories, so adjust meal portions accordingly.
  • Ignoring body condition: Use the body condition score (BCS) system to assess your Devon Rex's weight rather than relying solely on scale numbers. You should be able to feel the ribs without pressing hard.

Age-Specific Nutrition Considerations

Your Devon Rex's nutritional needs change significantly throughout their life.

Adult stage (1-7 years): Maintain a consistent feeding routine with measured portions. Monitor weight monthly and adjust food amounts based on activity level, seasonal changes, and body condition. Adult Devon Rexs benefit from a protein content of 22-30%.

Senior stage (7+ years): Older Devon Rexs may need fewer calories but higher-quality protein to maintain muscle mass. Senior formulas often include joint-supporting nutrients like glucosamine and chondroitin, plus antioxidants for cognitive health. Watch for changes in appetite.

Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy Screening

Published reports include hypertrophic cardiomyopathy in the Devon Rex. Heart screening is an echocardiogram for hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. The breed also has hereditary myopathy from a COLQ mutation, which shows up as weak neck muscles and fatigue, and no food replaces that gene. Devon Rex cats have a high metabolic rate, so feed enough complete cat food to hold weight, measured to body condition. A murmur or a cat who tires after little play should be seen before the next routine visit.

How much should I feed my Devon Rex?

Adult Devon Rexs typically need 0.5–1.5 cups of high-quality food per day, split into two meals. Adjust based on your cat's activity level, age, and body condition score. Active Devon Rexs may need up to 30% more.

What is the best food brand for Devon Rexs?

Look for foods that list real meat as the first ingredient and meet AAFCO standards. Brands offering small breed-specific formulas are often a good choice.

Should I feed my Devon Rex grain-free food?

Devon Rex myopathy is a genetic muscle disease caused by a COLQ mutation, and grain-free food does not treat the muscle. The breed also has a high metabolic rate, so feed enough complete cat food to hold weight, measured to body condition. A grain allergy is uncommon in Devon Rex cats and is a clinic diagnosis.
