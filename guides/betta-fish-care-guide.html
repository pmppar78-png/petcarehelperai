Betta Fish Complete Care Guide

This page is educational reference material for preparing questions. A licensed veterinarian examines the animal, makes the diagnosis, and sets the treatment.

Everything about betta fish care including tank setup, water parameters, feeding, common diseases, tank mates, and debunking betta myths.

Housing and Enclosure

Betta fish require a minimum 5-gallon heated, filtered aquarium. Maintain water temperature between 76-82°F. Use a gentle filter with low flow, as bettas prefer calm water. Add live or silk plants for hiding spots and resting places near the surface.

Diet and Nutrition

Feed your betta high-quality betta-specific pellets as a staple diet, supplemented with frozen or freeze-dried bloodworms, brine shrimp, and daphnia 2-3 times per week. Feed 2-3 pellets twice daily — only what can be eaten in 2 minutes to prevent overfeeding and water quality issues.

Health and Veterinary Care

Common betta health issues include fin rot, ich (white spot disease), velvet, swim bladder disorder, and dropsy. Maintain clean water with weekly 25% water changes and test parameters regularly. Watch for lethargy, loss of color, clamped fins, or white spots as early warning signs.

Common Health Issues

  • Ammonia and nitrite poisoning: The most common aquarium problem. It follows an uncycled tank, overfeeding, a dead fish left in the tank, or a filter that stopped. Both should read 0 ppm.
  • Temperature stress: Fish cannot regulate their own body temperature, so every body process follows the water. Use a thermometer, and a thermostat heater for tropical species.
  • Ich and other parasites: Usually brought in on new fish or plants. Quarantine new arrivals in a separate tank before they join the display.
  • Bacterial infections such as fin rot: Usually follow poor water quality or an injury.
  • Medication harm: Copper treatments are toxic to shrimp, snails, and plants and can set back the filter bacteria, so ammonia and nitrite need watching for weeks afterward.

Source: Merck Veterinary Manual: Management of Aquarium Fish.

Stocking and Stress Reduction

  • Float a new fish's bag to match temperature, then mix in tank water slowly before release.
  • Add a few fish at a time so the filter bacteria can catch up with the extra waste.
  • Check adult size, temperament, and water needs before buying a tank mate.
  • Keep hands out of the tank when you can, and wash them before and after maintenance.
  • Use a net only when you must; chasing fish around the tank is a stressor in itself.

Quick Answers

Are betta fish good pets for beginners?

Yes, if they get a heated, filtered tank of at least 5 gallons kept at 76 to 82°F. A bowl without a heater or filter is the most common reason a beginner loses a betta.

How often should I change a betta's water?

Change about 25 percent of the water weekly and test the parameters regularly. Feed only what the fish eats in about two minutes so leftover food does not foul the water.
