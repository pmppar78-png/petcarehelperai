Cat Scratching Post Buying Guide

Material Types

  • Sisal Rope/Fabric: Most durable and popular. Satisfying texture cats love. Lasts for years.
  • Cardboard: Affordable, satisfying shredding sensation. Needs regular replacement.
  • Carpet: Soft texture some cats prefer. May encourage scratching other carpet. Less durable.
  • Wood: Natural texture. Very durable but less common. Some cats love bark texture.

Choosing the Right Style

  • Vertical Posts: For cats who scratch furniture legs, door frames, or stretch upward
  • Horizontal Pads: For cats who scratch carpets or prefer low positions
  • Inclined/Angled: Good compromise; many cats enjoy the angle
  • Wall-Mounted: Space-saving; can create climbing paths
  • Combo/Cat Trees: Multiple surfaces and heights in one unit

Placement Tips

Place scratching posts near areas your cat already tries to scratch (furniture, door frames). Cats often scratch after waking, so near sleeping spots is ideal. Use catnip or treats to encourage use. Have multiple scratching surfaces throughout your home - one per cat plus one extra.

Frequently Asked Questions

My cat ignores the scratching post - what can I do?

Try different materials and orientations - your cat may prefer cardboard over sisal, or horizontal over vertical. Rub catnip on the post. Play with wand toys near it so claws catch the surface. Place it where your cat already likes to scratch. Never force paws onto it.

How often should I replace a scratching post?

Quality sisal posts can last 2-5+ years. Replace when sisal is badly frayed or the post becomes unstable. Cardboard scratchers typically last 2-6 months depending on use. Don't replace too quickly - cats often prefer well-worn scratchers that carry their scent.

Will a scratching post stop my cat from scratching furniture?

Usually, yes - if you provide appropriate alternatives. Match the post to your cat's preferences (material, orientation, location). Use deterrents like double-sided tape on furniture temporarily. Trim nails regularly. Be patient - it takes time to redirect scratching behavior.
