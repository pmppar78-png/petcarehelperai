Nutritional Needs of Balineses

Balineses typically weigh 5-10 lbs and need approximately 200–350 calories per day, depending on age, activity level, and metabolism. Given their high energy levels, active Balineses may need 20-30% more calories than the average cat of their size.

Known Health Risks: Genetic screening data shows Balineses have elevated rates of amyloidosis, asthma, dental disease.

Wet Food vs Dry Food for Balineses

Both wet and dry food have advantages for Balineses.

Feeding Mistakes to

  • Overuse of treats: Treats should make up no more than 10% of your Balinese's daily caloric intake.
  • Ignoring body condition: Use the body condition score (BCS) system to assess your Balinese's weight rather than relying solely on scale numbers.

Age-Specific Nutrition Considerations

Your Balinese's nutritional needs change significantly throughout their life.

Adult Balineses benefit from a protein content of 22-30%.

Senior stage (7+ years): Older Balineses may need fewer calories but higher-quality protein to maintain muscle mass.

What is the best food brand for Balineses?

Hepatic amyloidosis occurs in the Balinese, as in the Siamese, and grain-free food does not protect the liver. If a Balinese shows liver changes on bloodwork, the veterinarian sets the diet from those results. Until the liver is involved, measure a Balinese's complete food to a slim waist. A grain allergy is uncommon in Balinese cats, and the clinic is where that diagnosis is made.
