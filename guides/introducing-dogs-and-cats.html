Common Questions

The American Veterinary Society of Animal Behavior and the ASPCA both publish step-by-step introduction plans: separate rooms, a closed door for scent, then a baby gate, then short supervised meetings. Read one of those plans before the animals share a room.

Have both animals current on vaccines and parasite control before they meet, and ask the clinic to check the cat for hidden illness if the cat stops eating during the introduction. A cat that skips meals for more than a day can develop hepatic lipidosis.

The expensive failure is a bite wound or a dead cat. A gate, a second litter box in a dog-proof room, and a week of separation cost less than an abscess repair. Do the introduction before someone leaves a door open.
