Dysplasia affecting the hips, and that same disease label on the elbows, appears as trouble rising, a choppy front step, or a youngster who quits play after lying down. Ask for hip images and elbow images on the parents, and image a current dog that is limping too. Big bodies hide a mild limp until the muscle wastes.

Exocrine pancreatic insufficiency is the German Shepherd who eats like a wolf and still loses kilograms of condition, with pale, greasy, cow-pie stool. People think it is a food fad. It is a pancreas that fails to supply enzymes. The fix, once diagnosed, is enzyme on the food and a recheck of body condition. Send stool photos when the appetite and the weight disagree.

Bloat is the night emergency. Restlessness after a meal, unproductive retching, and an abdomen that feels like a drum send someone to the emergency door without a wait-and-see lap. Perianal fistulas are slower and miserable: draining tracts beside the anus, constant licking, pain on sitting, a smell the bath does not clear. Look at that skin on every wellness visit for this breed. Schedule 1 focused hind-end and stool review, and repeat it within 2 months if either the gait or the droppings changed. Hind toenails worn flat across the top are the early degenerative myelopathy clue in a German Shepherd. The disease is tied to SOD1. At home it looks like a scrape when the dog turns, a sway in the rear, and a reluctance to back up. The companion may show little pain at the start, which is the reason families wait. Ask whether SOD1 status is known, and book a neurologic exam when the nails and the sway show up together. Waiting until the dog falls is waiting too long to name it.
