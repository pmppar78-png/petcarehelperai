Questions Owners Ask

Tervurens are usually 45 to 75 pounds, and the long coat hides a thickening waist. Part the fur over the ribs once a month. Guard hair is fully in around the second birthday, later than the height. A slicker brush and a measured meal matter more once that coat arrives than a bigger bowl does.

The long coat and the 45 to 75 pound dog under it

Belgian Tervurens on this site's card run 22 to 26 inches and 45 to 75 pounds, with a lifespan listed at 12 to 14 years. The silhouette people photograph is mostly guard hair. Part the coat over the last rib if you want to know whether the dog under it has finished growing or has only finished looking fluffy. Height settles before the coat does. Guard hair is still filling in around the second birthday, which is later than the leg length.

The breed page cites an Orthopedic Foundation for Animals figure of about 5.2 percent hip dysplasia among Tervurens who were actually evaluated, in a medium-large dog whose typical weight sits in that 45 to 75 pound span. Put elbows and eyes on the breeder sheet beside those hips, and the page names progressive retinal atrophy as the eye disease to ask about. Epilepsy shows up often enough in Belgian shepherds that a Tervuren who has a seizure needs a written log and a veterinarian, and a seizure that does not stop is an emergency. A puzzle toy will not replace the work these dogs were bred to do.

Grooming on the card is high because the double coat sheds all year and blows harder in spring and fall. Budget a slicker, a rake, and time on the ground, or pay someone during the blow. Food is listed at $600 to $1,200, routine veterinary care at $300 to $600, and insurance at $400 to $800. Those are yearly bands on the profile. During the first eighteen months the food cup should follow the ribs you can feel. The outline the coat draws is a poor guide. A thick ruff hides a gain that the scale will catch.

Energy on the card is high, and the profile describes a courageous, alert, devoted herder who will try to organize children and who stares at lights when the day is empty. Mileage for a puppy is shorter than mileage for the adult who has closed those plates. Teach a settle in the house while the body is still light. The adult who reaches 75 pounds and has only been walked around the block will spend the difference on the furniture. Compare that life with a Malinois only if you have read both cards: the Tervuren's coat and the 12 to 14 year line are the practical split, and the hip paperwork is the shared one.

Weigh monthly on the same scale through the second year. Write the number next to a note about whether you could feel a rib without digging. If the waist disappears under the coat, cut the cup before you change brands. The veterinarian who already knows the breed can tell you when growth food should stop. Bring the parents' hip, elbow, and eye papers to that visit so the conversation starts from the dog's own folder.
