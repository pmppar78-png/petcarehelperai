Golden Retriever Health Issues

Common health problems in Golden Retrievers including cancer, hip dysplasia, heart disease. Prevention, symptoms to watch for, and treatment options.

Golden Retrievers are predisposed to several health conditions including cancer, hip dysplasia, heart disease. Understanding these risks allows you to screen early, prevent where possible, and catch problems before they become emergencies.

Knowledge of breed-level risks helps you prioritize, but individual monitoring drives the most effective care decisions.. Golden Retrievers sit in the large-size category, shed at a heavy level, and carry documented risk for cancer and hip dysplasia — those three factors drive most of the daily-care decisions.

Pale gums and a sudden collapse in a middle-aged Golden Retriever put hemangiosarcoma first on the list I hand over. The cancer pattern in this breed includes hemangiosarcoma, lymphoma, and mast cell tumors. Hemangiosarcoma can hide until a bleed, so the signs I tell people to treat as urgent are weakness, a bloated quiet abdomen, white gums, or a patient who was fine at breakfast and cannot stand at noon. Lymphoma often announces itself as lymph nodes under the jaw or behind the knee that feel like firm grapes. Mast cell tumors are skin lumps that swell, shrink, or itch, and I plainly want every new lump noted while it is still small enough to point at. The Morris Animal Foundation runs the Golden Retriever Lifetime Study to follow this pattern across whole lives. I do not pretend the study has handed me a personal prediction. I use it as the reason a lump gets an appointment this week.

Hearts fail here too, and subaortic stenosis leads the named list along with other heart disease. What I hear in the clinic is a murmur. What people see at home is a patient who tires early on a retrieve, coughs, or faints. Puppies with a murmur get a cardiac workup, and adult dogs who fade on easy ground get one as well. I ask breeders about heart clearances on the parents in one conversation, next to the orthopedic films. A friendly coat hides a heart problem completely. The dog still wants the bird.

Hips and elbows carry dysplasia in this breed the way they do in other retrievers, with a different daily picture in my house. Morning stiffness, a reluctance to hop into the car, or a front-end limp after a long swim are the clues. I request hip radiographs and elbow radiographs, and I keep the dog lean enough that I can feel ribs under the feathering. Females are often in the 55 pound area and males often top out near 75, so leanness is fingertips on the rib edges, tied to those bands. Extra fat is quiet and expensive.

Ichthyosis shows up on the skin as flakes and scales, sometimes along the belly and trunk, a dull coat that no conditioner fixes. I have brushed goldens whose white scale dusted the table. It is a skin disease to name, and a diagnosis changes the bath product more than it changes the personality. Ears are the other skin-adjacent job. This is a swimmer. I quietly dry the ear openings after every session in the water, because a wet canal is how the head-shaking starts.

The published span is 10 to 12 yr, which means the cancer watch and the heart watch run for a long time. I book 1 hands-on lump and node check at home each month, and I add a 2nd look in the week after a swim vacation when I am already drying ears and parting feathers. Golden Retriever Lifetime Study at the Morris Animal Foundation is the project I name when someone asks why this breed's lumps get more urgency than a casual "watch it."
