Shetland Sheepdog exercise & Fitness Guide

How much exercise does a Shetland Sheepdog need? Activity recommendations for this small high-energy herding breed.

Daily exercise daily. This is a high-energy breed that thrives with vigorous activities like running, hiking, fetch, and swimming.

Weighing around 15-25 lbs and lifespan of 12-14 yrs, the Shetland Sheepdog does best with an owner who understands its breed-specific quirks. Breed descriptions provide averages, not guarantees. Your Shetland Sheepdog may differ significantly from the typical profile in energy, sociability, or health.

Health Awareness: Shetland Sheepdogs carry genetic predispositions to collie eye anomaly, hip dysplasia, thyroid disease. Not every individual will be affected, but knowing these risks lets you work with your vet to establish an appropriate screening schedule. Early detection changes outcomes significantly for most of these conditions.

Hip and Joint Health Management

Hip dysplasia — a polygenic condition where the femoral head fails to fit properly within the acetabulum — is a documented concern in the Shetland Sheepdog. The Orthopedic Foundation for Animals (OFA) maintains a breed-specific database showing dysplasia prevalence rates, and the PennHIP evaluation method provides a distraction index that can predict hip laxity as early as 16 weeks of age. Even in smaller-framed Shetland Sheepdogs, the biomechanical stress of daily activity accumulates over the breed's 12-14 yrs lifespan. Joint supplements containing glucosamine hydrochloride, chondroitin sulfate, and omega-3 fatty acids (EPA/DHA) have demonstrated clinical benefit in peer-reviewed veterinary orthopedic literature when started before symptomatic onset.
