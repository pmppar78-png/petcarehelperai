How to Train a Collie

Collie training. Tips for their moderate energy herding breed temperament.

Training Approach

Collies are moderate-energy herding dogs that benefit from regular but moderate training routines. As a herding breed, they have strong working instincts and excel at agility, obedience trials, and trick training.

Weighing around 50-75 lbs and lifespan of 12-14 yrs, the Collie requires attention to its unique health and temperament characteristics. The Collie's reputation in the herding group reflects generations of purposeful breeding, resulting in a large dog with predictable but nuanced care requirements.

Genetic Health Considerations: The Collie breed has documented susceptibility to collie eye anomaly, hip dysplasia, bloat. Awareness of these predispositions is valuable for two reasons: it guides preventive screening decisions, and it helps you recognize early symptoms that might otherwise be overlooked.

Socialization

Care decisions tuned to breed-level detail tend to stick, because they match the animal's actual behavior. Collies have particular requirements based on their large size, heavy shedding level, and genetic predispositions to collie eye anomaly and hip dysplasia.

Routine veterinary screenings catch many breed-related conditions at stages where intervention is most effective. Given the breed's health tendencies, proactive screening is important for this breed.

Obedience Commands

  • Provide 30–60 minutes of daily exercise appropriate to their energy level
  • Feed a high-quality diet formulated for large breed dogs (1,400–2,200 calories/day)
  • Maintain a daily brushing grooming routine
  • Schedule breed-appropriate health screenings for collie eye anomaly
  • Consider pet insurance while your pet is young and healthy — premiums are lower and pre-existing conditions aren't an issue

Collies should receive breed-specific screening for collie eye anomaly starting at 1-2 years of age, as large breeds develop structural issues early. Proactive testing tends to pay for itself in avoided complications.

Real-World Owner Insight

Owners of How To Train A Collie frequently describe a pattern that is rarely captured in generic breed summaries. Trust takes longer to form than most timelines suggest, and trying to rush it usually extends the process instead of shortening it. Small environmental triggers — a different rug, a new scent, a rearranged room — can unsettle routines in ways first-time owners do not anticipate. A remote worker shared that the single most useful change was not a product or a technique but simply a consistent 10:30 a.m. break in the day. The most useful practical tip: keep a short notebook for the first 60 days and jot down what worked, what did not, and what surprised you. Patterns emerge faster than memory would suggest.
