Miniature Schnauzer vs Scottish Terrier: Side-by-Side

Miniature Schnauzer and Scottish Terrier look superficially similar to new owners but differ in ways that matter for daily care. Scottish Terrier is larger at 18-22 lbs, while Miniature Schnauzer typically runs 11-20 lbs. That size gap shows up in feeding volume, crate size, vehicle space, and how much joint-stress management each dog needs over their lifetime.

Energy level is the practical differentiator for most households: Miniature Schnauzer is classified as high-energy and Scottish Terrier as moderate-energy. Miniature Schnauzer needs structured daily outlets — not just a walk around the block — or it will create its own outlet, often destructively. The lower-energy option is easier to fit around desk jobs and short walks without compromising welfare.

Health watchlists differ. Miniature Schnauzer carries additional risk for pancreatitis, urinary stones, cataracts. Scottish Terrier is more notably predisposed to bladder cancer, von Willebrand disease, Scotty cramp. These aren’t guaranteed diagnoses — they’re the conditions responsible vets screen for, and they shape insurance underwriting more than most owners realize.

Grooming effort is meaningfully different: Miniature Schnauzer sheds at a minimal level, Scottish Terrier at moderate. That drives brush frequency, vacuum load, and whether the coat tolerates a week between sessions or demands daily attention during peak seasons.
