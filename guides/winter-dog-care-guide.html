Cold stiffness in an older dog belongs with the AAHA Senior Care Guidelines. A small lick of antifreeze is an emergency. Rock salt on pads is a clinic visit if the skin is cracking.

Cold weather does not add a second kind of annual exam, and it does not cancel the one already due, so puppies are examined every 3 to 4 weeks until about 16 weeks in winter the same as in July. Adult dogs need at least a yearly visit, and many dogs from about 7 years, giant breeds earlier, are examined about every six months, and stiffness in the cold is one reason those visits exist. Rock salt cracks pads. Antifreeze licked from a driveway is an emergency in small amounts. A dog who is weak, shivering, or limping on ice should be seen by a veterinarian.

Ice-melt burns and antifreeze are the winter ER bills. Use a pet-safer melt, wipe paws, and store radiator fluid where a dog cannot lick a spill. A coat helps a short-haired dog on a cold walk. A heavy-coated dog that is already warm indoors needs cooler rooms, and a coat on that dog adds heat. A wiped paw and a closed garage prevent the more common winter bills.
