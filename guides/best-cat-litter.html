Cat Litter Buying Guide

Types of Cat Litter

  • Clumping Clay: Most popular. Forms solid clumps for easy scooping. Heavy but affordable.
  • Non-Clumping Clay: Absorbs moisture but doesn't clump. Requires full replacement more often.
  • Crystal/Silica: Absorbs moisture and odors. Long-lasting but doesn't clump.
  • Natural (Corn, Wheat, Wood): Eco-friendly, often flushable. Variable clumping ability.
  • Paper: Made from recycled paper. Best for post-surgery recovery. Poor odor control.
  • Tofu/Soy: Newer option. Clumps well, flushable, virtually dustless.

Key Features to Consider

  • Odor Control: Critical for indoor litter boxes. Look for activated charcoal or baking soda.
  • Clumping: Tight clumps make scooping easier and extend litter life.
  • Dust Level: Important for cats and humans with respiratory sensitivities.
  • Tracking: Larger, heavier particles track less but may be rougher on paws.
  • Scent: Unscented is generally safer; some cats reject perfumed litters.

Litter Box Tips

The general rule is one litter box per cat, plus one extra. Scoop daily and completely change litter every 2-4 weeks. If switching litter types, transition gradually by mixing old and new to avoid box avoidance.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much litter should I put in the box?

Most cats prefer 2-3 inches of litter depth. This allows for proper digging and burying behavior. Too little litter leads to odors; too much can be wasteful and create tracking.

Can I flush cat litter?

Only certain natural litters (corn, wheat, tofu-based) are labeled as flushable. Never flush clay or crystal litters. Even flushable litters should be used cautiously with older plumbing or septic systems.

Why is my cat avoiding the litter box?

Common reasons include dirty litter box, wrong litter type, box in a stressful location, or medical issues. Try switching to unscented litter, cleaning more frequently, or adding another box in a different location. Consult your vet if problems persist.
