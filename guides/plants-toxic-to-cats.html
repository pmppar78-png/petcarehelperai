Plants Toxic to Cats

This page is educational reference material for preparing questions. A licensed veterinarian examines the animal, makes the diagnosis, and sets the treatment.

Household and outdoor plants dangerous to cats. Covers lilies (extremely toxic), poinsettias, aloe vera, and safe plant alternatives for cat owners.

True lilies can destroy a cat's kidneys

Lilies in the genera Lilium and Hemerocallis are a feline emergency. That group includes Easter lilies, Asiatic lilies, tiger lilies, Oriental lilies, and daylilies. Every part counts: petals, leaves, stamens, pollen, and the water in the vase. A cat who brushes pollen off a coat and then grooms, or who drinks from the vase, can develop acute kidney injury. The exposure that looks tiny to a person can be enough. There is no safe nibble and no safe variety inside those two groups. If a lily entered the house, assume the cat can reach it.

Early signs are vomiting, drooling, and a refusal to eat, often within a few hours. The kidney injury follows. Cats may drink more, urinate more, or urinate very little as the kidneys shut down. By the time a cat is collapsed and dehydrated, treatment is harder and sometimes unsuccessful. The useful window is the first hours, when a veterinarian can decontaminate and start intravenous fluids before urine production fails. Call an emergency clinic the moment you see chewed lily, pollen on the fur, or a knocked-over vase. Do not wait for the cat to "look sick enough."

Plants that burn the mouth and plants that are simply dangerous

Peace lilies, calla lilies, and anthuriums sit outside the Lilium and Hemerocallis groups. They contain insoluble calcium oxalate crystals that jab the mouth. Cats drool, paw at the face, and may vomit. That pain is real and deserves a call, and it is a different disease from the kidney failure of Lilium and daylilies. Dieffenbachia, philodendron, pothos, and monstera cause a similar mouth reaction. Rinse the mouth gently with water if the cat allows it and the clinic agrees, and still make the call so someone can confirm the plant name.

Sago palm is as dangerous for cats as it is for dogs. All parts, especially seeds, injure the liver. A cat who chews a sago needs the same urgent trip. Chrysanthemums, autumn crocus, oleander, and tulip or daffodil bouquets also cause serious illness. Kalanchoe can affect the heart. If the bouquet tag is still on the wrapper, photograph it. Common names overlap. "Lily" on a tag might mean a peace lily or an Easter lily, and the treatment path splits on that difference. When you are unsure, travel as if it were a true lily until an expert says otherwise.

What to do in the first minutes

Move the plant and the vase out of the home. A higher shelf is still a climb target once a cat has learned the route. Bag fallen petals and pollen-dusted tissues. Keep other cats away. Call your veterinarian or an emergency hospital and a poison hotline. Tell them the cat's weight if you know it, the time, and the plant name if you have it. Do not induce vomiting unless they instruct you. Do not give oils, milk, or human pain medicine. Acetaminophen and many human pain tablets are themselves deadly to cats.

If pollen is on the coat, the clinic may have you wipe it off with a damp cloth so grooming does not continue the exposure. Do this only if the cat is stable and they agree. A struggling cat who cannot breathe through a wrap needs the clinic to do the bath. Bring a leaf in a bag. At the hospital, care for true lily exposure often includes decontamination and aggressive intravenous fluids with monitoring of kidney values for a couple of days. There is no drugstore antidote. Asking "can it wait until morning?" is how cats lose the window. Go.

A house without lilies

The reliable prevention is refusal. Tell florists, grocery checkout staff, and relatives that lilies cannot come into a cat's home. Easter, funerals, and sympathy bouquets are the usual entry points. Offer a list of safer gifts: commercial cat grass you replace often, or flowers a veterinarian has specifically cleared, kept in a room the cat never enters. Even then, fallen petals travel on shoes. Many clinics simply say skip bouquets. That advice is easier than dialysis.

Check the yard and the neighbor's cuttings. Daylilies are common in borders and a cat who goes outside will find them. If your cat goes out, walk the block and learn what is planted, or keep the cat indoors. Indoor cats still meet lilies on kitchen tables. A closed bedroom door fails when someone airs the bouquet on the floor. Write the emergency clinic address on the fridge before a holiday weekend, when bouquets and closed primary clinics arrive together.

  • Treat Easter lilies, Asiatic lilies, tiger lilies, and daylilies as kidney emergencies for cats.
  • Include pollen and vase water as exposures.
  • Separate peace lilies, which burn the mouth, from true lilies, which injure kidneys.
  • Treat sago palm as a liver emergency in cats too.
  • Call and travel the same hour. Skip vomiting unless the lily consult tells you to proceed.
  • Bag a leaf and remove the entire plant from the home.
  • Refuse lily bouquets from florists and guests.
  • Skip acetaminophen and human pain tablets while you wait.

Pollen, vases, and the two-day fluid plan

True lily exposures often need intravenous fluids started the same day and continued while kidney values are watched. Ask how long the cat will stay and what a change in urine output means. A cat who produces little urine is in a more serious phase. Pollen on the coat continues the exposure until it is removed, so follow the clinic's instruction about wiping or bathing. Do not scrub a panicked cat at home if they told you to come in first. Peace lilies burn the mouth and still deserve an exam, and the paperwork should name the plant clearly so the kidney protocol is used only when the plant is a true lily or a daylily.

After you get home, the house rule changes the same night. Remaining bouquets leave. Tell the florist and the relatives who send sympathy flowers that lilies cannot be delivered. Daylilies in a garden bed are a risk for any cat who goes outside. If your cat goes out, walk the block or keep the cat in. A second exposure during recovery is a disaster. Put the emergency clinic's address where holiday guests can see it, because bouquets and closed daytime clinics arrive together.

Questions about lilies and other plants

Which lilies are deadly to cats?

True lilies in the Lilium group and daylilies in the Hemerocallis group. Easter, Asiatic, tiger, and Oriental lilies are examples. All parts, including pollen and vase water, can cause acute kidney injury. Call an emergency clinic immediately if contact is possible.

Is a peace lily the same emergency?

Peace lilies contain oxalate crystals that burn the mouth and cause drooling. That hurts and still deserves a veterinary call. It is a different injury from the kidney failure caused by true lilies and daylilies. If the label is unclear, go in as if the plant were a true lily.

What should I do if my cat got pollen on its fur?

Call a veterinarian or poison hotline now. They may have you gently wipe the coat so the cat stops ingesting pollen, if the cat is stable. Skip a solo attempt to make the cat vomit after a plant. True lily exposures often need intravenous fluids started the same day.
