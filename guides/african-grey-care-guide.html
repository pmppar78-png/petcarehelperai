African Grey Parrot Care Guide

Comprehensive African Grey parrot care guide covering diet, mental stimulation, talking ability, common health issues, and lifespan expectations.

Housing and Enclosure

African Grey Parrots need a large cage — minimum 36"x24"x48" — with bar spacing of 3/4 to 1 inch. Provide multiple perches of varying diameter and material to promote foot health. Include foraging toys, shreddable toys, and puzzle feeders to meet their high intelligence needs. Allow several hours of supervised out-of-cage time daily.

Diet and Nutrition

Feed a base diet of high-quality formulated pellets (60-70% of diet), supplemented with fresh fruits and vegetables daily. African Greys are prone to calcium deficiency — offer calcium-rich foods like broccoli, kale, and almonds. Avoid avocado, chocolate, caffeine, and high-fat or salty foods.

Health and Veterinary Care

African Greys are prone to feather plucking (often stress-related), calcium deficiency leading to seizures, Aspergillosis (fungal respiratory infection), and Psittacine Beak and Feather Disease (PBFD). Annual avian veterinary checkups are essential. Watch for changes in droppings, appetite, feather condition, or behavior.

Common Health Issues

  • Metabolic bone disease: Often caused by inadequate UVB lighting or calcium supplementation
  • Respiratory infections: Usually linked to incorrect temperature or humidity
  • Parasites: Both internal and external parasites can affect exotic pets
  • Nutritional deficiencies: Result from improper or monotonous diets
  • Stress-related illness: Caused by improper housing, handling, or environmental factors

Handling and Socialization

  • Allow new pets to acclimate to their enclosure before handling
  • Learn proper handling techniques specific to your pet's species
  • Start with short handling sessions and gradually increase duration
  • Wash hands before and after handling to prevent disease transmission
  • Respect your pet's signals — not all exotic pets enjoy being handled

Common Questions

Understanding this aspect of pet care helps you make informed decisions that directly affect your pet's wellbeing. Every pet is different, so observe how yours responds and adjust your approach based on what works.

Are African Grey Parrots good for first-time bird owners?

Some exotic pets are excellent for beginners (leopard geckos, corn snakes, budgies, betta fish) while others require advanced experience (chameleons, macaws, saltwater aquariums). Research thoroughly before committing.

How much do exotic pets cost to care for?

Initial setup costs (enclosure, lighting, heating) are usually the largest expense, ranging from $100-$1,000+. Ongoing costs for food, substrate, and veterinary care typically run $30-$100/month.
