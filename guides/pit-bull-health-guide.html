Quick Answers

A shelter label of pit bull is not a breed name a policy can use. Hip disease and skin allergy already under treatment are the pre-existing costs. Aortic stenosis heard before enrollment is a pre-existing heart finding. Cerebellar ataxia DNA aimed at American Pit Bull Terriers does not describe every dog with that look, and a positive result already in hand is a prior finding. What matters is documented hips and skin that are not already being treated.

A shelter label of 'pit bull' is not a registry breed, so a panel marketed for American Pit Bull Terriers does not describe every dog with that look. American Pit Bull Terrier breeders can test cerebellar ataxia and hip radiographs, and a cardiologist listens for aortic stenosis. A mixed dog from the same photograph needs hips, heart auscultation, and a history of skin allergies more than a breed SKU. Embark-style ancestry percentages do not predict hip dysplasia. Ask for the orthopedic and cardiac exam, whatever the DNA pie chart says.
