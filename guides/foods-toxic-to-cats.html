Foods Toxic to Cats

Complete list of foods dangerous or toxic to cats including lilies, onions, chocolate, alcohol, caffeine, and raw dough with emergency guidance.

Alliums, alcohol, and caffeine

Onions, garlic, leeks, chives, and shallots damage feline red blood cells. Cats are often harmed by amounts that look trivial in a shared stew, a baby-food jar, or a seasoned chicken piece. Powder and cooked forms still count. The anemia can take several days to show: pale gums, weakness, rapid breathing, and dark urine. Call a veterinarian when you discover the meal, and bring the ingredient list. Do not wait for the gums to change color. Garlic supplements sold for pets are an allium exposure and should be stopped until a clinician says otherwise.

Alcohol, including beer, wine, and cocktails left on a table, can drop a cat's blood sugar and body temperature and can depress breathing. A lick from a glass is enough to justify a call for a small cat. Caffeine in coffee, tea, energy drinks, and some sodas causes restlessness, a racing heart, tremors, and vomiting. Grounds in a trash can are concentrated. Chocolate adds theobromine to the caffeine problem. Dark chocolate and baking chocolate are the most concentrated. Milk chocolate still matters when the cat is small or the amount is a full brownie. White chocolate is mostly fat and sugar and can still upset the stomach and the pancreas.

Dough, xylitol, grapes, and bones

Raw yeast dough expands in the warm stomach and produces alcohol as it ferments. A cat or a dog who eats dough can bloat and become drunk at the same time. That is an emergency visit. Xylitol, the sweetener in some sugar-free gums and baked goods, is most famous for causing low blood sugar and liver injury in dogs. Reports in cats are fewer, and a cat who eats a sugar-free product should still be seen or discussed with a poison hotline because the downside of waiting is large and the label is right there. Grapes and raisins are a proven kidney risk in dogs. The feline picture is less mapped. Until a clinician tells you a specific incident can be watched, call and describe it. There is no benefit to experimenting.

Cooked bones splinter and pierce the gut. Raw fish as a sole diet risks thiamine deficiency, with neurologic signs that need a veterinarian. Human tuna as a daily food adds mercury and an incomplete nutrient pattern. A complete cat food is the meal. Dog food is built for dogs. Fed as a cat's diet it misses nutrients cats must eat, including the pattern of taurine and vitamin levels a feline diet is designed around. A stolen dog kibble or two is a reason to secure the bowl. A week of dog food as the only meal is a reason to call the clinic and switch back.

What to do if the food is already eaten

Remove the rest, note the time, and bag the package. Phone your veterinarian or an emergency clinic about the cat's meal. Poison hotlines can coordinate: ASPCA Animal Poison Control at 888-426-4435 and Pet Poison Helpline at 855-764-7661. Read every ingredient, especially onion powder, xylitol, raisins, and chocolate type. Skip vomiting a cat unless the food consult tells you to try. Cats are easy to aspirate, and some foods burn or swell on the way up. Do not offer milk. Many cats are lactose intolerant, and milk does not bind these toxins in any useful way.

Go in the same hour for trouble breathing, repeated vomiting, tremors, a seizure, a swollen or painful belly, or a cat who cannot stand. Pale gums and weakness in the days after an onion meal are also a go-in event. Bring the wrapper. If two cats might have eaten from the same plate, say so. Treatment may include decontamination when it is safe, fluids, blood tests, and hospital monitoring. There is no kitchen antidote for allium anemia or for theobromine. Speed and an accurate label are the tools you actually have.

A kitchen that does not invite a counter raid

Chop onions while the cat is shut out, and wipe the board into a lidded trash can. Coffee cups go into the sink. Bread dough rises in the oven or in a closed microwave, out of reach of a cat who investigates warmth. Chocolate and sugar-free gum live in a cabinet with a latch. Guests get one sentence: cats may not have people food from plates, and onion is hidden in a lot of gravies. That rule sounds fussy until you have watched a cat develop anemia on day three.

If you cook for a cat with a poor appetite, ask the clinic for a plan. Plain cooked meat or a recovery diet they name will serve you better than a seasoned leftover. Baby food must be read for onion and garlic powder before it is offered. The foods on this page fail as meals and, for several of them, fail as tastes. The complete food in the cupboard is the boring choice that keeps red cells and the heart out of the emergency story.

  • Call after onion, garlic, leek, chive, or shallot exposure, including powders.
  • Treat alcohol, caffeine, and chocolate as same-day calls.
  • Treat raw yeast dough as an emergency because it expands and produces alcohol.
  • Call about xylitol and about grapes or raisins so a clinician can judge the incident.
  • Skip cooked bones, fish-only diets, and dog food as a cat's staple.
  • Do not induce vomiting unless a veterinarian or hotline directs it.
  • Bring the package and mention every cat who had access.
  • Use a complete cat food, and read baby-food labels before a topper.

Shared plates and the days after onion

A family dinner can deliver onion in the gravy, caffeine in a spilled latte, and chocolate on a dessert plate in one evening. Walk the table with the clinic if a cat was on it. Name each food. Allium injury shows up over days as pale gums and weakness, so ask when a blood check should happen. Caffeine and theobromine show up sooner as restlessness and a fast heart. Dough that was rising on the stove can expand inside the cat and needs a same-night visit. If two cats had access, both get named. A quiet cat can be the one who ate more.

After the call, change the kitchen the same day. Lidded trash, a shut door while you chop, and baby-food jars read for garlic powder. Dog food goes back to the dog's mat, up off the floor, so it stops being the cat's buffet. Complete cat food is the recovery meal unless the veterinarian prescribes something else. Do not add milk to "soothe the stomach." Lactose often makes diarrhea worse, and it does not bind these toxins.

Questions about foods that harm cats

Why are onions and garlic dangerous for cats?

They damage red blood cells and can cause anemia over several days. Powders, broths, and cooked dishes count. Pale gums, weakness, and fast breathing are urgent. Call when the food is eaten, before those signs, and do not induce vomiting unless a veterinarian tells you to.

Can cats eat chocolate or drink a little alcohol?

Chocolate contains theobromine and caffeine, which overstimulate the heart and nervous system. Alcohol can depress breathing and drop blood sugar and temperature. Both are reasons to call a veterinarian or a poison hotline with the product and the approximate amount. Dark and baking chocolate are the most concentrated.

Is dog food toxic to cats?

A bite of dog kibble is mainly a reason to move the bowl. Dog food used as a cat's diet misses nutrients cats require, including the taurine and vitamin balance a feline food is built to provide. Switch back to a complete cat food and ask your veterinarian if the cat has been eating the wrong bag for more than a brief steal.
