Chinese Shar-Pei

Detailed guide to the best dog food for Chinese Shar-Peis based on their medium size, moderate energy level, and health needs including skin infections.

Nutritional Needs of Chinese Shar-Peis

As a medium non-sporting breed with moderate energy levels, the Chinese Shar-Pei has specific nutritional requirements that differ from other dogs.

Chinese Shar-Peis typically weigh 45-60 lbs and need approximately 800–1,200 calories per day, depending on age, activity level, and metabolism. With moderate energy levels, most Chinese Shar-Peis do well on standard feeding guidelines for their size.

Known Health Risks: Genetic screening data shows Chinese Shar-Peis have elevated rates of skin infections, entropion, Shar-Pei fever.

Health-Specific Diet Considerations

Chinese Shar-Peis are prone to several health conditions that can be managed or prevented through proper nutrition.

  • Skin Infections: Look for foods with omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids for skin and coat health
  • Entropion: Look for foods with nutrients that support entropion management
  • Shar-Pei Fever: Look for foods with nutrients that support Shar-Pei fever management

Wet Food vs Dry Food for Chinese Shar-Peis

Both wet and dry food have advantages for Chinese Shar-Peis.

Feeding Mistakes to

  • Overuse of treats: Treats should make up no more than 10% of your Chinese Shar-Pei's daily caloric intake.
  • Ignoring body condition: Use the body condition score (BCS) system to assess your Chinese Shar-Pei's weight rather than relying solely on scale numbers.

Age-Specific Nutrition Considerations

Your Chinese Shar-Pei's nutritional needs change significantly throughout their life.

Adult Chinese Shar-Peis benefit from a protein content of 22-30%.

Senior stage (7+ years): Older Chinese Shar-Peis may need fewer calories but higher-quality protein to maintain muscle mass.

Shar-Pei fever and amyloidosis are the Chinese Shar-Pei diseases, and grain-free food does not stop either. When amyloid reaches the kidneys, the veterinarian sets the kidney diet from the staging. Until then, feed a complete dog food to body condition and keep the skin folds clean. A grain allergy is uncommon in Shar-Peis and needs a veterinary diagnosis.
