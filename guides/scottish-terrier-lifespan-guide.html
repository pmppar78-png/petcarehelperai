Scottish Terrier Lifespan

Average Lifespan

The Scottish Terrier has an average lifespan of 12 yrs. Smaller breeds generally live longer, and well-cared-for Scottish Terriers often exceed average lifespan expectations.

Weighing around 18-22 lbs and lifespan of 12 yrs, the Scottish Terrier benefits from care tailored to its physical and behavioral profile. Few breeds combine steady enthusiasm with the Scottish Terrier's distinctive character quite so effectively.

Known Health Risks: Genetic screening data shows Scottish Terriers have elevated rates of bladder cancer, von Willebrand disease, Scotty cramp. Rates vary across individuals, and plenty of animals never develop the conditions associated with their breed. The real value of breed-aware veterinary care is earlier screening and faster recognition when something does appear.

Life Stages

Knowledge of breed-specific characteristics directly translates to better day-to-day care. Scottish Terriers have particular requirements based on their small size, moderate shedding level, and genetic predispositions to bladder cancer and von Willebrand disease.

Senior Care

Few breeds combine steady enthusiasm with the Scottish Terrier's distinctive character quite so effectively. Lack of physical activity affects behavior before it affects weight — restlessness and attention-seeking often precede visible fitness changes.

  • Provide 30–60 minutes of daily exercise appropriate to their energy level
  • Feed a high-quality diet formulated for small breed dogs (400–800 calories/day)
  • Maintain a 2–3 times per week grooming routine
  • Schedule breed-appropriate health screenings for bladder cancer
  • Pet insurance enrolled early typically offers the best value, covering breed-related conditions before they develop

Local Vet & Care Considerations

Routine veterinary care for Scottish Terrier Lifespan Guide varies more by region than many owners realize. Standard preventive care tends to range from $180 to $450 a year depending on local pricing, and bundled wellness plans can soften the cost if you commit to a single clinic. Urban clinics typically offer longer hours and specialist referrals but fewer in-office compounding options; rural clinics often flip that trade-off entirely. If your area has sharp humidity swings, small things like bedding materials and water-bowl placement end up mattering more than the more dramatic advice you tend to see online.
