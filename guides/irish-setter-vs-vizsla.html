Personality Overview

The Irish Setter is known for being a high-energy sporting breed with a distinctive personality. Sporting breeds like the Irish Setter are typically friendly, eager to please, and excellent with families.

With a typical weight of 60-70 lbs and lifespan of 12-15 yrs, the Irish Setter requires thoughtful care tailored to their specific breed characteristics. This guide covers everything you need to know.

Energy & Activity

The key to a happy, healthy Irish Setter is matching your care approach to their breed characteristics. High-energy breeds need physical and mental outlets every day — without them, behavioral problems like destructive chewing or excessive barking are common.

  • Provide 60–120 minutes of daily exercise appropriate to their energy level
  • Feed a high-quality diet formulated for large breed dogs (1,400–2,200 calories/day)
  • Maintain a 2–3 times per week grooming routine
  • Schedule breed-appropriate health screenings for hip dysplasia
  • Invest in pet insurance early to cover breed-specific conditions

Intelligence & Trainability

Beyond the basics, Irish Setter owners should be aware of the unique aspects of this breed. As a sporting breed, the Irish Setter has instincts and behaviors shaped by centuries of selective breeding for specific tasks.

Many experienced Irish Setter owners recommend dog sports like agility, flyball, or nosework to channel their energy productively.

Environmental enrichment plays a crucial role in your Irish Setter's well-being. Rotate toys regularly, introduce new scents and textures, and vary your walking routes to keep their mind engaged. A mentally stimulated Irish Setter is less likely to develop destructive behaviors or anxiety-related issues.

Guarding Instincts

Being proactive about your Irish Setter's care can prevent many common problems. Watch for early signs of hip dysplasia, maintain regular veterinary visits, and keep your dog at a healthy weight — obesity exacerbates nearly every health condition Irish Setters are prone to.

With proper care and attention to their breed-specific needs, your Irish Setter can live a long, healthy, and happy life of 12-15 yrs.

Establishing a consistent daily routine helps your Irish Setter feel secure and reduces stress-related behavioral problems. Include scheduled feeding times, exercise sessions, grooming, and quiet rest periods. High-energy Irish Setters especially benefit from knowing when their exercise time is coming — it helps them settle during calmer periods.

Irish Setters should receive breed-specific screening for hip dysplasia starting at 1-2 years of age, as large breeds develop structural issues early. Early detection significantly improves treatment outcomes and quality of life.

Cost of Irish Setter Ownership

Understanding the financial commitment helps you prepare for a lifetime of Irish Setter ownership:

  • Annual food costs: $600–$1,200 for high-quality dog food
  • Veterinary care: $300–$700 annually for routine visits, plus potential emergency costs
  • Grooming: $65–100 per professional session (2–3 times per week home grooming recommended)
  • Pet insurance: $50–80/month for comprehensive coverage
  • Supplies and toys: $200–$500 annually for bedding, toys, leashes, and other essentials

Is a Irish Setter right for me?

Irish Setters are large dogs with high energy and a lifespan of 12-15 yrs. They require 2–3 times per week grooming and are prone to hip dysplasia.

How much does a Irish Setter cost?

Initial costs range from $500-$3,000 for purchase/adoption, plus $1,000-$2,000 in first-year supplies and veterinary care. Annual ongoing costs are typically $1,500-$4,000 depending on health needs.

What health problems do Irish Setters have?

Irish Setters are prone to hip dysplasia, bloat, epilepsy. Regular vet checkups and breed-specific screening tests help catch these conditions early for better treatment outcomes.

How long do Irish Setters live?

The average lifespan for a Irish Setter is 12-15 yrs. Proper nutrition, regular exercise, preventive veterinary care, and maintaining a healthy weight can help your Irish Setter live to the upper end of this range.
