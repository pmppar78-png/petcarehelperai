Nutritional Needs of Lhasa Apsos

As a small non-sporting breed with moderate energy levels, the Lhasa Apso has specific nutritional requirements that differ from other dogs. Understanding these needs is key to keeping your Lhasa Apso healthy throughout their 12-15 yrs lifespan.

Lhasa Apsos typically weigh 12-18 lbs and need approximately 400–800 calories per day, depending on age, activity level, and metabolism. With moderate energy levels, most Lhasa Apsos do well on standard feeding guidelines for their size.

Known Health Risks: Genetic screening data shows Lhasa Apsos have elevated rates of kidney disease, cherry eye, luxating patella. Rates vary across individuals, and plenty of animals never develop the conditions associated with their breed. The real value of breed-aware veterinary care is earlier screening and faster recognition when something does appear.

Health-Specific Diet Considerations

Lhasa Apsos are prone to several health conditions that can be managed or prevented through proper nutrition.

  • Kidney Disease: Look for foods with controlled phosphorus and high-quality protein for kidney support
  • Cherry Eye: Look for foods with nutrients that support cherry eye management

Best Protein Sources for Lhasa Apsos

  • Chicken and turkey: Lean, easily digestible proteins ideal for most Lhasa Apsos

Foods to Avoid

Never feed your Lhasa Apso these dangerous foods.

  • Macadamia nuts and alcohol
  • Cooked bones (splintering risk)

Supplements Worth Considering

Based on Lhasa Apso-specific health concerns, these supplements may benefit your dog.

  • Glucosamine: Supports joint health as your Lhasa Apso ages

Wet Food vs Dry Food for Lhasa Apsos

Both wet and dry food have advantages for Lhasa Apsos.

  • Mixed feeding: Many Lhasa Apso owners find success combining both for balanced nutrition and variety

Feeding Mistakes to Avoid

Common feeding errors that Lhasa Apso owners make include:

  • Overuse of treats: Treats should make up no more than 10% of your Lhasa Apso's daily caloric intake. Many training treats are high in calories, so adjust meal portions accordingly.
  • Ignoring body condition: Use the body condition score (BCS) system to assess your Lhasa Apso's weight rather than relying solely on scale numbers. You should be able to feel the ribs without pressing hard.

Age-Specific Nutrition Considerations

Your Lhasa Apso's nutritional needs change significantly throughout their life.

Adult stage (1-7 years): Maintain a consistent feeding routine with measured portions. Monitor weight monthly and adjust food amounts based on activity level, seasonal changes, and body condition. Adult Lhasa Apsos benefit from a protein content of 22-30%.

Senior stage (7+ years): Older Lhasa Apsos may need fewer calories but higher-quality protein to maintain muscle mass. Senior formulas often include joint-supporting nutrients like glucosamine and chondroitin, plus antioxidants for cognitive health. Watch for changes in appetite.

How much should I feed my Lhasa Apso?

Adult Lhasa Apsos typically need 0.5–1.5 cups of high-quality food per day, split into two meals. Adjust based on your dog's activity level, age, and body condition score.

What is the best food brand for Lhasa Apsos?

Look for foods that list real meat as the first ingredient, meet AAFCO standards, and address Lhasa Apso-specific health needs like kidney disease. Brands offering small breed-specific formulas are often a good choice.

Should I feed my Lhasa Apso grain-free food?

Renal dysplasia in a Lhasa Apso is a kidney malformation, and grain-free food does not repair the kidney. When the kidneys fail, the veterinarian sets a kidney diet after staging. Until then, feed a complete dog food to body condition and plan on dental care. A grain allergy is uncommon in Lhasas and is a clinic diagnosis.
