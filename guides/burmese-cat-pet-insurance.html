Burmese Pet Insurance Overview

Pet insurance for Burmeses is particularly important given their predisposition to diabetes, HCM, head defect.

Burmese premiums are cat premiums. The breed is predisposed to diabetes mellitus, hypokalemic polymyopathy, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, and a congenital craniofacial defect in some lines.

Known Health Risks: Genetic screening data shows Burmeses have elevated rates of diabetes, HCM, head defect. Ask about diabetes, HCM, head defect at the exam for this breed. Many individual animals never show the conditions in question; when they do, a breed-literate veterinarian usually identifies them sooner.

How much does Burmese pet insurance cost?

NAPHIA's 2025 State of the Industry report, compiled by Willis Towers Watson from year-end 2024 premiums, puts US cat accident-and-illness coverage at $32.21 a month ($386.47 a year) and accident-only coverage at $9.17 a month. Enrollment age, ZIP code, deductible, reimbursement percentage, and annual limit set the quote. For a Burmese, the claims that move the quote are diabetes, hypokalemic myopathy, and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy.

Is pet insurance worth it for a Burmese?

Burmese diabetes is treated with insulin and diet that a veterinarian directs. Hypokalemic myopathy and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy are medical. The craniofacial defect is a congenital malformation.

What pre-existing conditions affect Burmese insurance?

Any condition diagnosed before enrollment is excluded. For Burmeses, common pre-existing concerns include diabetes and HCM. Early enrollment is key.
