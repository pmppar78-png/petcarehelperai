Brushes by Coat Type

  • Short, Smooth Coats (Beagles, Boxers): Rubber curry brush, bristle brush, grooming mitt
  • Short, Double Coats (Labs, Corgis): Deshedding tool, slicker brush, undercoat rake
  • Long, Silky Coats (Yorkies, Maltese): Pin brush, fine-tooth comb, detangling spray
  • Long, Double Coats (Golden Retrievers, Huskies): Slicker brush, undercoat rake, deshedding tool
  • Curly/Wavy Coats (Poodles, Doodles): Slicker brush, dematting comb, professional clippers
  • Wire Coats (Terriers, Schnauzers): Stripping knife, slicker brush, bristle brush

Grooming Frequency Guidelines

  • Brushing: 2-3 times weekly for most coats; daily for long or double coats
  • Deshedding: Weekly during shedding season; monthly otherwise
  • Nail trimming: Every 2-4 weeks or when you hear clicking on floors
  • Bathing: Monthly for most dogs; more often for smelly or dirty dogs
  • Haircuts: Every 4-8 weeks for breeds requiring trimming

Grooming Safety Tips

Never brush a dirty or dry coat - use detangling spray. Stop if you see redness or your dog shows pain. For nail trimming, only remove small amounts to avoid cutting the quick. If your dog has severe mats, see a professional groomer rather than risk cutting skin.

Common Questions

The trade-off is simple: a few hours reading about their dog behavior now versus larger bills and stress later.

How do I stop my dog from shedding so much?

You can't stop shedding entirely, but regular brushing with a deshedding tool removes loose undercoat before it falls out. Good nutrition with omega fatty acids also promotes healthy coat and may reduce excessive shedding. See a vet if shedding seems abnormal.

Nail clippers or grinder - which is better?

Both work well when used correctly. Grinders are better for dogs fearful of clipping sounds and give a smoother finish. Clippers are faster but require more precision. Many owners use both - clippers to remove length, then grinder to smooth.

My dog hates being groomed - what can I do?

Start slowly with short, positive sessions. Offer high-value treats during and after grooming. Let your dog sniff tools before using them. Choose quieter tools for sensitive dogs. If anxiety is severe, consult a professional trainer or try grooming after exercise when your dog is calm.
