Best Food for Bloodhound: Diet & Nutrition Guide

Complete guide to the best dog food for Bloodhounds based on their large size, moderate energy level, and health needs including bloat.

Nutritional Needs of Bloodhounds

As a large hound breed with moderate energy levels, the Bloodhound has specific nutritional requirements that differ from other dogs. Understanding these needs is key to keeping your Bloodhound healthy throughout their 10-12 yrs lifespan.

Bloodhounds typically weigh 80-110 lbs and need approximately 1,400–2,200 calories per day, depending on age, activity level, and metabolism. With moderate energy levels, most Bloodhounds do well on standard feeding guidelines for their size.

Health-Specific Diet Considerations

Bloodhounds are prone to several health conditions that can be managed or prevented through proper nutrition:

  • Bloat: Look for foods with smaller, more frequent meals and avoiding elevated feeding
  • Hip Dysplasia: Look for foods with glucosamine, chondroitin, and omega-3 fatty acids for joint support
  • Ear Infections: Look for foods with nutrients that support ear infections management

Many veterinary nutritionists recommend large breed-specific formulas that contain controlled calcium and phosphorus levels to support proper skeletal development.

Best Protein Sources for Bloodhounds

High-quality animal protein should be the first ingredient in any Bloodhound food. Recommended protein sources include:

  • Chicken and turkey: Lean, easily digestible proteins ideal for most Bloodhounds
  • Salmon and fish: Rich in omega-3 fatty acids for skin, coat, and joint health
  • Lamb: A good alternative for dogs with chicken sensitivities
  • Beef: High in iron and B vitamins, though some dogs may be sensitive

Foods to Avoid

Never feed your Bloodhound these dangerous foods:

  • Chocolate, caffeine, and xylitol (extremely toxic)
  • Grapes and raisins (can cause kidney failure)
  • Onions, garlic, and chives (damage red blood cells)
  • Macadamia nuts and alcohol
  • Cooked bones (splintering risk)

Wet Food vs Dry Food for Bloodhounds

Both wet and dry food have advantages for Bloodhounds:

  • Dry kibble: Better for dental health, more economical, easier to measure portions
  • Wet food: Higher moisture content (important for hydration), more palatable, easier for seniors
  • Mixed feeding: Many Bloodhound owners find success combining both for balanced nutrition and variety

Feeding Mistakes to Avoid

Common feeding errors that Bloodhound owners make include:

  • Free-feeding: Leaving food out all day leads to overeating, especially in lower-energy breeds prone to weight gain. Scheduled meals help maintain healthy weight and allow you to monitor appetite changes.
  • Sudden diet changes: Switching foods abruptly causes digestive upset. Transition over 7-10 days by gradually mixing the new food with the old.
  • Overuse of treats: Treats should make up no more than 10% of your Bloodhound's daily caloric intake. Many training treats are high in calories, so adjust meal portions accordingly.
  • Ignoring body condition: Use the body condition score (BCS) system to assess your Bloodhound's weight rather than relying solely on scale numbers. You should be able to feel the ribs without pressing hard.
  • Feeding table scraps: Human food often contains ingredients dangerous to dogs. Even safe foods add untracked calories and can encourage begging behavior.

Age-Specific Nutrition Considerations

Your Bloodhound's nutritional needs change significantly throughout their life:

Puppy stage (0-12 months): Large breed puppy formulas with controlled calcium and phosphorus are essential to prevent rapid growth that stresses developing joints. DHA supports brain development during this critical period.

Adult stage (1-7 years): Maintain a consistent feeding routine with measured portions. Monitor weight monthly and adjust food amounts based on activity level, seasonal changes, and body condition. Adult Bloodhounds benefit from a protein content of 22-30%.

Senior stage (7+ years): Older Bloodhounds may need fewer calories but higher-quality protein to maintain muscle mass. Senior formulas often include joint-supporting nutrients like glucosamine and chondroitin, plus antioxidants for cognitive health. Watch for changes in appetite that may signal underlying health issues.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I feed my Bloodhound?

Adult Bloodhounds typically need 3–5 cups of high-quality food per day, split into two meals. Adjust based on your dog's activity level, age, and body condition score.

What is the best food brand for Bloodhounds?

Look for foods that list real meat as the first ingredient, meet AAFCO standards, and address Bloodhound-specific health needs like bloat. Brands offering large breed-specific formulas are often a good choice.

Should I feed my Bloodhound grain-free food?

Unless your Bloodhound has a diagnosed grain allergy, grain-inclusive foods are generally recommended. The FDA has investigated a potential link between grain-free diets and heart disease (DCM) in dogs. Consult your veterinarian before choosing grain-free.
