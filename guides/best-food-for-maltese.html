Nutritional Needs of Malteses

As a small toy breed with moderate energy levels, the Maltese has specific nutritional requirements that differ from other dogs. Understanding these needs is key to keeping your Maltese healthy throughout their 12-15 yrs lifespan.

Malteses typically weigh 4-7 lbs and need approximately 400–800 calories per day, depending on age, activity level, and metabolism. With moderate energy levels, most Malteses do well on standard feeding guidelines for their size.

Known Health Risks: Genetic screening data shows Malteses have elevated rates of luxating patella, dental disease, collapsed trachea. Breed-level risk is population-level information; individual outcomes vary widely. The practical payoff of breed-aware veterinary care is earlier detection in the cases where risk does materialize.

Health-Specific Diet Considerations

Malteses are prone to several health conditions that can be managed or prevented through proper nutrition.

  • Collapsed Trachea: Look for foods with nutrients that support collapsed trachea management

Best Protein Sources for Malteses

  • Chicken and turkey: Lean, easily digestible proteins ideal for most Malteses

Foods to Avoid

Never feed your Maltese these dangerous foods.

  • Macadamia nuts and alcohol
  • Cooked bones (splintering risk)

Supplements Worth Considering

Based on Maltese-specific health concerns, these supplements may benefit your dog.

  • Glucosamine: Supports joint health as your Maltese ages

Wet Food vs Dry Food for Malteses

Both wet and dry food have advantages for Malteses.

  • Mixed feeding: Many Maltese owners find success combining both for balanced nutrition and variety

Feeding Mistakes to Avoid

Common feeding errors that Maltese owners make include:

  • Overuse of treats: Treats should make up no more than 10% of your Maltese's daily caloric intake. Many training treats are high in calories, so adjust meal portions accordingly.
  • Ignoring body condition: Use the body condition score (BCS) system to assess your Maltese's weight rather than relying solely on scale numbers. You should be able to feel the ribs without pressing hard.

Age-Specific Nutrition Considerations

Your Maltese's nutritional needs change significantly throughout their life.

Adult stage (1-7 years): Maintain a consistent feeding routine with measured portions. Monitor weight monthly and adjust food amounts based on activity level, seasonal changes, and body condition. Adult Malteses benefit from a protein content of 22-30%.

Senior stage (7+ years): Older Malteses may need fewer calories but higher-quality protein to maintain muscle mass. Senior formulas often include joint-supporting nutrients like glucosamine and chondroitin, plus antioxidants for cognitive health. Watch for changes in appetite.

Common Questions

How much should I feed my Maltese?

Adult Malteses typically need 0.5–1.5 cups of high-quality food per day, split into two meals. Adjust based on your dog's activity level, age, and body condition score.

What is the best food brand for Malteses?

Look for foods that list real meat as the first ingredient, meet AAFCO standards, and address Maltese-specific health needs like luxating patella. Brands offering small breed-specific formulas are often a good choice.

Should I feed my Maltese grain-free food?

A liver shunt in a Maltese is managed with a veterinary liver diet and, when chosen, surgery. Dental disease is the other common problem. Grain-free food is not that liver diet. Use the diet the veterinarian prescribes, and brush the teeth between dental procedures. A grain allergy is uncommon in Maltese and is a separate clinic diagnosis.
