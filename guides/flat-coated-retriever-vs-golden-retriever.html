Flat-Coated Retriever vs Golden Retriever: Side-by-Side

Flat-Coated Retriever and Golden Retriever look superficially similar to new owners but differ in ways that matter for daily care. Flat-Coated Retriever is larger at 60-70 lbs, while Golden Retriever typically runs 55-75 lbs. That size gap shows up in feeding volume, crate size, vehicle space, and how much joint-stress management each dog needs over their lifetime.

Both breeds share a high energy level, so the differentiator here is temperament, not exercise volume. Watch how each individual dog responds to training pressure, novelty, and time alone — that tells you more than the AKC group label.

Lifespan: Flat-Coated Retriever typically lives 8-10 yrs; Golden Retriever 10-12 yrs. Golden Retriever generally has the longer-term care window, which affects insurance math and the point at which senior diagnostics become the dominant cost line.

Health watchlists differ. Both breeds share concerns around cancer, hip dysplasia. Flat-Coated Retriever carries additional risk for bloat. Golden Retriever is more notably predisposed to heart disease. These aren’t guaranteed diagnoses — they’re the conditions responsible vets screen for, and they shape insurance underwriting more than most owners realize.

Grooming effort is meaningfully different: Flat-Coated Retriever sheds at a moderate level, Golden Retriever at heavy. That drives brush frequency, vacuum load, and whether the coat tolerates a week between sessions or demands daily attention during peak seasons.
