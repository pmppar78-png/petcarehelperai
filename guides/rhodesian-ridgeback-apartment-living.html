Rhodesian Ridgeback in an Apartment

Can a Rhodesian Ridgeback thrive in an apartment? Space needs, noise level, exercise requirements, and tips for large breed apartment living.

Apartment Suitability Score

Can a Rhodesian Ridgeback live in an apartment? With adequate daily exercise, Rhodesian Ridgebacks can adapt to apartment living, though a home with a yard is preferable.

Weighing around 70-85 lbs and lifespan of 10-12 yrs, the Rhodesian Ridgeback benefits from care tailored to its physical and behavioral profile. Below you'll find the key information organized by topic.

Space Requirements

Understanding breed tendencies equips you to anticipate needs, even as individual personalities vary. Rhodesian Ridgebacks with moderate energy levels strike a good balance between activity and relaxation.

  • Size: large (70-85 lbs)
  • Energy Level: Moderate
  • Shedding: Light
  • Common Health Issues: Hip Dysplasia, Dermoid Sinus, Hypothyroidism
  • Lifespan: 10-12 yrs

Noise Level

Care that accounts for breed predispositions leads to earlier detection and better prevention. Rhodesian Ridgebacks have particular requirements based on their large size, light shedding level, and genetic predispositions to hip dysplasia and dermoid sinus.

Work with your veterinarian to fine-tune these recommendations based on your pet's weight, activity level, and any health considerations.

Exercise Solutions

The key to a happy, healthy Rhodesian Ridgeback is matching your care approach to their breed characteristics. Activity needs are individual, not just breed-determined — age, health status, and temperament all modify the baseline.

  • Provide 30–60 minutes of daily exercise appropriate to their energy level
  • Feed a high-quality diet formulated for large breed dogs (1,400–2,200 calories/day)
  • Maintain a weekly grooming routine
  • Schedule breed-appropriate health screenings for hip dysplasia
  • Consider pet insurance while your pet is young and healthy — premiums are lower and pre-existing conditions aren't an issue

Making It Work

Breed-aware owners tend to catch things earlier, which matters. Watch for early signs of hip dysplasia, maintain regular veterinary visits, and keep your dog at a healthy weight — excess weight worsens most of the conditions Rhodesian Ridgebacks are prone to.

Rhodesian Ridgebacks should receive breed-specific screening for hip dysplasia starting at 1-2 years of age, as large breeds develop structural issues early. Screening before symptoms appear makes a meaningful difference in outcomes.
