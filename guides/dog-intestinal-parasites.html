Intestinal Parasites in Dogs

This page is educational reference material for preparing questions. A licensed veterinarian examines the animal, makes the diagnosis, and sets the treatment.

Common intestinal worms in dogs including roundworms, hookworms, whipworms, and tapeworms. Covers symptoms, deworming, and prevention protocols.

What intestinal parasites do in dogs

Intestinal parasites are organisms that live in the gut and steal blood, food, or both. The common worms are roundworms (Toxocara canis and Toxascaris leonina), hookworms (Ancylostoma caninum and Uncinaria stenocephala), whipworms (Trichuris vulpis), and tapeworms (Dipylidium caninum from fleas, and Taenia from prey). Protozoa matter just as much in practice: Giardia and coccidia in the Cystoisospora group cause a large share of puppy diarrhea. A fecal that only says "negative for worms" can miss Giardia completely if the lab method was a simple flotation.

Several of these parasites also affect people. Toxocara eggs from dog stool can cause visceral and ocular larva migrans if a child swallows dirt that was contaminated weeks earlier. Hookworm larvae can burrow into human skin and cause cutaneous larva migrans. That is why picking up stool the same day is part of treatment, and why a sandy play area where dogs defecate is a family health issue as well as a dog issue.

Which dogs carry them

Puppies acquire Toxocara and Ancylostoma from the dam, across the placenta and through the milk, before they ever walk on grass. A breeder's dewormer on one Sunday does not finish that migration. Adult dogs pick up eggs and larvae from soil, from eating stool, and from hunting. Whipworms favor dogs who use the same yard for years, because the eggs are tough and take weeks to become infective. Dipylidium shows up in a dog who has fleas, including a dog who only swallowed one flea while grooming. Kennels, shelters, dog parks with poor stool cleanup, and hunting dogs see the heaviest loads. Multi-anthelmintic-resistant Ancylostoma caninum has been confirmed by CAPC in several breeds across the United States, greyhounds among them, and those infections stay positive on drugs that used to clear hookworms.

A healthy-looking adult can still shed eggs. Absence of diarrhea does not clear a dog who lives with a toddler. Puppies, nursing dams, and dogs with chronic soft stool deserve scheduled fecal tests even when nobody has seen a worm in the yard.

Signs owners notice

Roundworms can give a puppy a pot belly, a dull coat, and spaghetti-like worms in the stool or vomit. Hookworms are the ones that cause life-threatening anemia: pale gums, black or tarry stool, weakness, and in a small puppy, collapse. Whipworms irritate the large intestine, so the stool is soft, frequent, and often streaked with mucus and fresh blood, and the dog may seem fine between bouts. Tapeworms look like rice grains or cucumber seeds stuck to the fur around the anus or on the bedding. Giardia tends to cause intermittent soft, greasy, or mucous stool and can wax and wane for weeks. Coccidia hit stressed puppies and shelter dogs with watery diarrhea. Some infected dogs show nothing at all until a fecal antigen test comes back positive.

  • Pale gums, lethargy, and dark stool in a puppy, which can be hookworm blood loss.
  • Visible worms in vomit or stool, or rice-like segments on the rear.
  • Chronic large-bowel diarrhea with mucus and straining, a whipworm pattern.
  • Soft stool that returns after a pet-store dewormer, which may mean the wrong drug or a resistant hookworm.
  • A dull puppy who is still eating, with a round belly and poor weight gain.

How a veterinarian

Intestinal parasites in dogs are identified on feces. CAPC recommends a fecal flotation, and a separate Giardia test because flotation misses a lot of Giardia. A negative fecal from one stool does not end the search when the dog still has diarrhea. Some hookworms, including multi-anthelmintic-resistant Ancylostoma caninum, survive a routine dewormer, so a repeat fecal after treatment is part of the diagnosis of whether the drug worked.

Centrifugal flotation with zinc sulfate or a similar solution is the method CAPC prefers, because whipworm eggs are dense and a passive float can miss them. Fecal antigen tests detect hookworm, roundworm, and whipworm protein even before eggs appear. A Giardia ELISA or an in-clinic Giardia antigen test is the add-on when flotation is quiet and the stool is still wrong. PCR panels are useful in shelters and in chronic cases where several organisms could be sharing the gut. A fecal egg count before and after treatment is how a clinic documents that a hookworm survived the first drug. Bloodwork joins the plan when gums are pale, because anemia changes the urgency.

Treatment categories

The dewormer has to match the parasite the fecal found. Roundworms, hookworms, whipworms, tapeworms, coccidia, and Giardia need different drugs. The veterinarian chooses the drug and how long it is given, and the follow-up fecal says whether it cleared. A dog with resistant hookworms may need a different plan than the first dewormer. Heartworm prevention is a separate drug and does not replace the fecal.

Pyrantel is a common first drug for roundworms and hookworms in puppies. Fenbendazole covers a broader set, including whipworms and, in protocols the veterinarian selects, Giardia. Praziquantel is what clears tapeworms, and it does nothing for rounds or hooks, which is why a tapeworm tablet can leave a puppy anemic from hookworms. Coccidia are treated with a drug such as sulfadimethoxine or ponazuril, chosen and timed by the veterinarian. Resistant Ancylostoma may need a combination protocol and a fecal egg count reduction test, sometimes with extra steps CAPC has outlined for confirmed multi-drug resistance. Monthly heartworm preventives often include an intestinal deworming ingredient. They still leave gaps, especially for whipworms and Giardia on some products, so the label and the fecal have to be read together.

Home care

Pick up stool the day it is passed so eggs do not sit and become infective. Stop the dog from eating feces, including the cat's, and wash outdoor bowls. Treat fleas on every pet in the home when Dipylidium was found, or the tapeworm returns with the next swallowed flea. Puppies need a series of deworming timed by the veterinarian, because larvae keep arriving from the dam and the soil after the first dose. Bring a fresh stool sample. A dried stool from yesterday's walk is a poor specimen. Keep the follow-up fecal appointment even if the stool looks perfect. Until the repeat test is negative, keep the puppy off playgrounds and away from gardens where children dig.

When it is

When to See

Pale or white gums, collapse, a bloated painful belly, black tarry stool, or a puppy who is too weak to nurse are emergencies. Hookworm anemia can kill a small puppy within a day, and it needs more than a tablet from a feed store. Profuse bloody diarrhea with vomiting and weakness can also be parvovirus, hemorrhagic gastroenteritis, or a foreign body. Those dogs need fluids and a diagnosis the same day. A single rice-like segment on an otherwise bright dog can wait for a prompt regular appointment, along with flea control.

What to ask

Common intestinal worms in dogs are clearable when the drug matches the parasite and a later fecal test is negative. CAPC recommends centrifugal flotation, antigen testing, or PCR, because whipworm eggs are dense and hookworms can be present before any eggs appear. Puppies keep acquiring roundworms and hookworms from the dam and the yard, so one dose leaves the series unfinished. CAPC has confirmed multi-anthelmintic resistant Ancylostoma caninum in several breeds across the United States, greyhounds among them, and those infections stay positive on drugs that used to clear hookworms. A resistant hookworm is the case that does not end by repeating the first tablet.

Pay for a fecal flotation or antigen test before you pay for a dewormer. CAPC drug tables are parasite-specific: a roundworm product misses giardia, and whipworms need a drug that actually covers them. The wasted money is a pet-store tablet repeated while the wrong organism remains. Puppies often need a series as larvae arrive in the gut, so ask for that series price. Heartworm disease is a separate, much larger invoice than intestinal worms.

Key Questions

Why did the first dewormer fail?

The tablet may have been the wrong family for the organism, the puppy may have been reinfected from the yard the same week, or the hookworm may be one of the multi-drug-resistant Ancylostoma strains CAPC has documented. A repeat centrifugal fecal, or a fecal egg count, tells those stories apart. Buying the same pet-store box again without that test spends money on a drug the parasite has already survived.

Can people catch worms from a puppy?

Yes for a few of them. Toxocara is the classic risk for children who swallow eggs from contaminated soil, and hookworm larvae can penetrate skin. Daily stool pickup, covering sandboxes, and handwashing after yard play cut that risk while the puppy finishes a veterinary deworming series. Giardia spread to people is possible with some types and is a reason to treat a confirmed infection and to clean the yard, even though many dog strains stay in dogs.

Does heartworm prevention replace a fecal test?

It does not. Many preventives also kill some intestinal worms, and the coverage differs by product. Whipworms, Giardia, coccidia, and resistant hookworms can persist in a dog who never misses a heartworm dose. CAPC still wants fecal testing on a schedule, with extra tests for puppies, dogs with diarrhea, and dogs whose follow-up fecal stayed positive.
