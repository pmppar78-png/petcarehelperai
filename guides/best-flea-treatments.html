Flea & Tick Treatment Buying Guide

Treatment Types

  • Oral Chewables: Easy to give, no mess, works systemically. Requires prescription. Fleas/ticks must bite to be killed.
  • Topical/Spot-On: Applied to skin between shoulder blades. Some OTC options. Can be messy, may wash off.
  • Collars: Long-lasting (months). Convenient but may cause irritation. Must fit properly.
  • Sprays/Powders: Immediate kill but short-lasting. Good for existing infestations, not prevention.

Important Safety Notes

  • Dog/Cat Specific: Many dog products are TOXIC to cats. Never use dog products on cats.
  • Weight Accuracy: Always weigh your pet and use the correct dose range.
  • Age Requirements: Most products have minimum age/weight requirements.
  • Interactions: Inform your vet of all medications your pet takes.

Our Recommendations

  • Best for Dogs (All-in-One): Simparica Trio
  • Best for Dogs (OTC): Frontline Plus
  • Best for Cats: Revolution Plus
  • Best Long-Lasting: Seresto Collar
  • Best Budget: Advantage II
  • Best Extended Dose: Bravecto (12 weeks)

Critical Safety Warning

NEVER use dog flea/tick products on cats - many contain permethrin which is fatal to cats. Always read labels carefully. If your cat is exposed to a dog product, contact your vet or pet poison control immediately. Keep treated dogs away from cats until product dries.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need flea prevention year-round?

Yes, in most climates. Fleas can survive indoors during winter, and a warm spell can trigger outdoor populations. Ticks are active whenever temperatures are above freezing. Year-round prevention is recommended by veterinarians for consistent protection.

My pet still has fleas after treatment - why?

It takes time to break the flea life cycle. Eggs in your home can hatch for 2-3 months. Continue treatment and also treat your home (vacuuming, washing bedding, environmental sprays). You should see improvement within 1-3 months of consistent treatment.

Are natural flea treatments effective?

Most natural treatments (essential oils, garlic, brewer's yeast) have not been proven effective and some can be toxic to pets. For reliable protection against flea-borne diseases and tick-transmitted illnesses, veterinarian-recommended products are strongly advised.
