Rat Terrier

Detailed guide to the best dog food for Rat Terriers based on their small size, high energy level, and health needs including luxating patella.

Nutritional Needs of Rat Terriers

As a small terrier breed with high energy levels, the Rat Terrier has specific nutritional requirements that differ from other dogs.

Rat Terriers typically weigh 10-25 lbs and need approximately 400–800 calories per day, depending on age, activity level, and metabolism. Given their high energy levels, active Rat Terriers may need 20-30% more calories than the average dog of their size.

Health Awareness: Rat Terriers show elevated breed-level risk for luxating patella, hip dysplasia, allergies.

Health-Specific Diet Considerations

Rat Terriers are prone to several health conditions that can be managed or prevented through proper nutrition.

Best Protein Sources for Rat Terriers

  • Chicken and turkey: Lean, easily digestible proteins ideal for most Rat Terriers
  • Novel proteins (venison, duck, rabbit): Excellent for Rat Terriers with food allergies

Foods to Avoid

  • Macadamia nuts and alcohol
  • Cooked bones (splintering risk)

Supplements Worth Considering

  • Glucosamine & Chondroitin: Essential for joint health, especially important for Rat Terriers prone to hip dysplasia

Wet Food vs Dry Food for Rat Terriers

Both wet and dry food have advantages for Rat Terriers.

Feeding Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overuse of treats: Treats should make up no more than 10% of your Rat Terrier's daily caloric intake.
  • Ignoring body condition: Use the body condition score (BCS) system to assess your Rat Terrier's weight rather than relying solely on scale numbers.

Age-Specific Nutrition Considerations

Your Rat Terrier's nutritional needs change significantly throughout their life.

Adult Rat Terriers benefit from a protein content of 22-30%.

Senior stage (7+ years): Older Rat Terriers may need fewer calories but higher-quality protein to maintain muscle mass.

Should I feed my Rat Terrier grain-free food?

Luxating patella in a Rat Terrier is a knee problem, and grain-free food does not seat the kneecap. A Rat Terrier stays lean on a complete dog food so the kneecap carries less mass. A grain allergy is uncommon in Rat Terriers and needs a veterinary diagnosis.
