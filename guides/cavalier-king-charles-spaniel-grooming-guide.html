Grooming Schedule

For Cavalier King Charles Spaniels, the inputs that matter most are a small frame, a moderate shedding coat, and breed-level risk for mitral valve disease and syringomyelia.

The curly-coat dry-eye pairing changes the feel in the hands. That puppy wears a harsh curl, and the eyes need the clinic's tear plan. The coat will refuse to comb like salon silk. Ordinary Cavaliers have the silk. Bathe when feathers smell or pollen cakes the trousers, wetting through, conditioning the lengths, and drying with airflow while combing so the ear fringe cannot lock into a rope. Eyes stay visible. A spaniel who cannot see your face loses the contact that makes the breed easy company.

Nails click sooner than the feathered foot suggests. Take a few at a time while the hound is already in the grooming sit. Teeth are a later evening with a finger brush, because feathering around the muzzle still hides tartar. VCA coat-care notes on this spaniel's silk line up with an ear-first routine that keeps silk from felting where the cushion rubs.

Keep a spare comb in the car. Lap dogs get held, and holding presses the ear fringe flat for the whole ride. A two-minute comb in a parking spot before you go inside saves the felt that would otherwise greet you at bedtime. Chest feathering collects food. Lift it and comb downward after meals when the hound is a messy drinker, which many of them are.

Cavalier King Charles Spaniels have moderate shedding and require 2–3 times per week brushing. Regular grooming sessions keep your Cavalier King Charles Spaniel's coat healthy and help you bond with your dog.

Between the 13-18 lbs adult size and 9-14 yrs lifespan, the Cavalier King Charles Spaniel has enough breed-specific care considerations that early familiarity with them pays off throughout ownership. Bathing

Silky ears are where the grooming on this toy spaniel actually happens. Feathering is soft, and it mats behind the ears before anywhere else, then in the armpits, the trousers, and the tail. Sit the dog between the knees, fold one ear leather back, and comb from the tips toward the skull until metal reaches skin. A knot left in that fringe holds damp against the leather and smells sour by tomorrow. Those ears get combed when the weekday number is 3 and later when it is 5, plus any morning the fringe looks twisted.

Professional Grooming Costs

Color tells someone where debris hides. Blenheim is chestnut on white, and tears rust the blaze. Tricolor puts black into that same map. Ruby is rich red, so specks show as dark dots in the feathering. Black and tan hides tear stain on the face and shows every pale strand on the saddle. Wipe the eyes before the comb so rust stays off the muzzle. Pad hair mats into pebbles. Trim only the bit that collects ice and grit, and leave the leg furnishings that make the outline read as a spaniel.
