Personality Overview

The Poodle is known for being a high-energy non-sporting breed with a distinctive personality. Their unique blend of traits makes them well-suited for the right owner and lifestyle.

Weighing around 40-70 lbs and lifespan of 12-15 yrs, the Poodle has specific care needs shaped by its genetics and build. What follows is a practical breakdown of the key factors.

Breed data gives us statistical probabilities, not certainties — but those probabilities shape smart care decisions. Poodles with high energy levels need consistent outlets for their drive and enthusiasm.

Energy & Activity

The key to a happy, healthy Poodle is matching your care approach to their breed characteristics. High-energy breeds need physical and mental outlets every day — without them, behavioral problems like destructive chewing or excessive barking are common.

  • Provide 60–120 minutes of daily exercise appropriate to their energy level
  • Feed a high-quality diet formulated for medium breed dogs (800–1,200 calories/day)
  • Maintain an occasional grooming routine
  • Schedule breed-appropriate health screenings for hip dysplasia
  • Invest in pet insurance early to cover breed-specific conditions

Stability in daily routine is particularly important during transitions: new homes, new family members, or changes in the owner's schedule. Cost of Poodle Ownership

Here is a realistic look at annual costs. Estimated annual costs for Poodle ownership:

  • Annual food costs: $400–$800 for high-quality dog food
  • Veterinary care: $300–$700 annually for routine visits, plus potential emergency costs
  • Grooming: $45–70 per professional session (occasional home grooming recommended)
  • Pet insurance: $35–55/month for comprehensive coverage
  • Supplies and toys: $200–$500 annually for bedding, toys, leashes, and other essentials
