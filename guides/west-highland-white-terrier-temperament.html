West Highland White Terrier temperament traits, personality, and behavior. What to expect from this moderate-energy terrier breed with family, kids, and other pets.

Behavioral Profile

The West Highland White Terrier is known for being a moderate-energy terrier breed with a distinctive personality. Their unique blend of traits makes them well-suited for the right owner and lifestyle.

Weighing around 13-22 lbs and lifespan of 13-15 yrs, the West Highland White Terrier benefits from care tailored to its physical and behavioral profile. Originally bred as a tenacious vermin hunter, the West Highland White Terrier brings centuries of selective breeding into the modern home.

Breed-Specific Health Profile: Research identifies skin allergies, luxating patella, liver disease as conditions with higher prevalence in West Highland White Terriers. These are population-level trends, not individual certainties. Discuss with your veterinarian which screening tests are recommended for your West Highland White Terrier.

Multi-Pet Households

Knowledge of breed-level risks helps you prioritize, but individual monitoring drives the most effective care decisions.. For West Highland White Terriers, the inputs that matter most are a small frame, a moderate shedding coat, and breed-level risk for skin allergies and luxating patella.

Activity Requirements

Originally bred as a tenacious vermin hunter, the West Highland White Terrier brings centuries of selective breeding into the modern home. Consistent daily activity, even in short sessions, contributes more to long-term health than occasional intense exercise.

Mental Stimulation Needs

Understanding your West Highland White Terrier's instinctual drives makes enrichment more effective. Rather than generic toy rotation, tailor activities to what this breed was developed to do. Working breeds benefit from task-oriented challenges; scent-driven breeds thrive with nose work; social breeds need interactive play rather than solo activities.

Watch for early signs of skin allergies, maintain regular veterinary visits, and keep your dog at a healthy weight — excess weight worsens most of the conditions West Highland White Terriers are prone to.
