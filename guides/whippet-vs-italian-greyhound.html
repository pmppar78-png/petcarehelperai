Energy & Activity

The key to a happy, healthy Whippet is matching your care approach to their breed characteristics. A sedentary lifestyle carries health risks regardless of breed predisposition — joint stiffness, weight gain, and behavioral issues increase with inactivity.

  • Provide 30–60 minutes of daily exercise appropriate to their energy level
  • Feed a high-quality diet formulated for medium breed dogs (800–1,200 calories/day)
  • Maintain a weekly grooming routine
  • Schedule breed-appropriate health screenings for heart disease
  • Consider pet insurance while your pet is young and healthy — premiums are lower and pre-existing conditions aren't an issue

Whippet vs Italian Greyhound: Side-by-Side

Whippet and Italian Greyhound look superficially similar to new owners but differ in ways that matter for daily care. Whippet is larger at 25-40 lbs, while Italian Greyhound typically runs 7-14 lbs. That size gap shows up in feeding volume, crate size, vehicle space, and how much joint-stress management each dog needs over their lifetime.

Both breeds share a moderate energy level, so the differentiator here is temperament, not exercise volume. Watch how each individual dog responds to training pressure, novelty, and time alone — that tells you more than the AKC group label.

Lifespan: Whippet typically lives 12-15 yrs; Italian Greyhound 14-15 yrs. Italian Greyhound generally has the longer-term care window, which affects insurance math and the point at which senior diagnostics become the dominant cost line.

Health watchlists differ. Whippet carries additional risk for heart disease, eye problems, anesthesia sensitivity. Italian Greyhound is more notably predisposed to dental disease, leg fractures, epilepsy. These aren’t guaranteed diagnoses — they’re the conditions responsible vets screen for, and they shape insurance underwriting more than most owners realize.

Grooming effort is meaningfully different: Whippet sheds at a light level, Italian Greyhound at minimal. That drives brush frequency, vacuum load, and whether the coat tolerates a week between sessions or demands daily attention during peak seasons.
