Great Dane vs Cane Corso: Side-by-Side

Great Dane and Cane Corso look superficially similar to new owners but differ in ways that matter for daily care. Great Dane is larger at 110-175 lbs, while Cane Corso typically runs 88-120 lbs. That size gap shows up in feeding volume, crate size, vehicle space, and how much joint-stress management each dog needs over their lifetime.

Both breeds share a moderate energy level, so the differentiator here is temperament, not exercise volume. Watch how each individual dog responds to training pressure, novelty, and time alone — that tells you more than the AKC group label.

Lifespan: Great Dane typically lives 7-10 yrs; Cane Corso 9-12 yrs. Cane Corso generally has the longer-term care window, which affects insurance math and the point at which senior diagnostics become the dominant cost line.

Health watchlists differ. Both breeds share concerns around bloat, hip dysplasia. Great Dane carries additional risk for heart disease. Cane Corso is more notably predisposed to cherry eye. These aren’t guaranteed diagnoses — they’re the conditions responsible vets screen for, and they shape insurance underwriting more than most owners realize.

Grooming effort is meaningfully different: Great Dane sheds at a moderate level, Cane Corso at light. That drives brush frequency, vacuum load, and whether the coat tolerates a week between sessions or demands daily attention during peak seasons.
