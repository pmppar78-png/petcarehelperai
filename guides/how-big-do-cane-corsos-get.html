How Big Do Cane Corsos Get? Size & Growth Guide

Cane Corsos are a large breed, reaching 88-120 lbs at full maturity. Large breeds take 12-24 months to reach their adult size, with most of their height achieved by 12 months and filling out continuing until 18-24 months.

Take the withers measurement and let rumor sit down. Male Cane Corsos stand 25 to 27.5 in. Females stand 23.5 to 26 in. Those are the registry figures. Recognition by AKC came in 2010, and those height bands are the numbers I use when a contract waves at "extra large." A male measuring 27.5 in is at the top. A female measuring 23.5 in is low and can still be correct. Arguments in kitchens are usually about pounds. The printed description talks in inches.

The fill-in lasts. Puppies of this mastiff type keep building from 12 months through 24 months, and much of the chest and skull shows up from 18 months onward to 24. A buyer at 12 months can declare the project finished and still be looking at a narrow front. Around 24 months the outline has usually arrived. Hard braking and repeated jumping wait from age 1 until age 2. The frame is still under construction then. Slick floors plus an endless bowl show up later as complaining hips.

Numbers I have actually written include 88 for a young female who was mostly leg and 120 for a finished male whose chest had dropped. Two animals, two visits. Asked how big the breed gets, I repeat 25 to 27.5 and 23.5 to 26. Paws on a baby are a poor forecast. See the parents on even footing, then follow your own puppy month after month. A photo from above makes every Corso look taller. Stand beside the dog, level the tape, and write the date. I quietly have been fooled by a fluffy adolescent neck more than once, and the second measurement a week later settled the argument. Growth from 12 months to 24 months is depth more than height, so the withers number can look stuck while the dog gets substantially harder to move in a hallway. AKC height standard for the Cane Corso is what I want cited next to the 12, 18, and 24 month marks.

Many experienced Cane Corso owners recommend a balanced mix of physical activities and brain games.

Mental stimulation is as important as physical exercise for Cane Corso. Boredom is the root cause of most destructive behavior — not disobedience.
