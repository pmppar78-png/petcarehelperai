How Big Do Irish Setters Get? Size & Growth Guide

Irish Setter full size: 60-70 lbs, large breed. Growth timeline from puppy to adult, weight chart, and when they stop growing.

Full-Grown Size

Irish Setters are a large breed, reaching 60-70 lbs at full maturity. Large breeds take 12-24 months to reach their adult size, with most of their height achieved by 12 months and filling out continuing until 18-24 months.

Plan for 60-70 lbs of animal and 12-15 yrs of companionship with an Irish Setter; the breed-specific care considerations are the kind it pays to read up on before day one. Let's examine the important details.

Growth Timeline

Understanding breed tendencies equips you to anticipate needs, even as individual personalities vary. If you own Irish Setter, plan on steady daily outlets for their energy; the breed's drive is real, and the alternatives to channeling it are worse.

  • Size: large (60-70 lbs)
  • Energy Level: High
  • Shedding: Moderate
  • Lifespan: 12-15 yrs

Care that accounts for breed predispositions leads to earlier detection and better prevention. Practical Irish Setters care is shaped by three things: large size, moderate shedding, and a known predisposition to hip dysplasia and bloat.

Male vs Female Size

The key to a happy, healthy Irish Setter is matching your care approach to their breed characteristics. High-energy breeds need physical and mental outlets every day — without them, behavioral problems like destructive chewing or excessive barking are common.

Factors Affecting Size

Informed ownership goes deeper than the basic care checklist for any breed. As a sporting breed, the Irish Setter has instincts and behaviors shaped by centuries of selective breeding for specific tasks.

Many experienced Irish Setter owners recommend dog sports like agility, flyball, or nosework to channel their energy productively.

Understanding your Irish Setter's instinctual drives makes enrichment more effective. Rather than generic toy rotation, tailor activities to what this breed was developed to do. Working breeds benefit from task-oriented challenges; scent-driven breeds thrive with nose work; social breeds need interactive play rather than solo activities.

When They Stop Growing

Care that anticipates breed-specific risks tends to lower both vet bills and avoidable health events. Watch for early signs of hip dysplasia, maintain regular veterinary visits, and keep your dog at a healthy weight — excess weight worsens most of the conditions Irish Setters are prone to.

Veterinary Care Schedule for Irish Setters

Preventive care reduces both emergency costs and disease severity over your pet's lifetime. Here is a general framework for your Irish Setter. Use this as a starting point — your vet may adjust based on individual health.

Puppy (0-1 year)Every 3-4 weeks until 16 weeks, then at 6 and 12 monthsVaccinations, deworming, spay/neuter (consult AVMA guidelines on optimal timing) consultation

Irish Setters should receive breed-specific screening for hip dysplasia starting at 1-2 years of age, as large breeds develop structural issues early. The earlier you know, the more you can do about it.
