Poodle vs Labrador Retriever: Side-by-Side

Poodle and Labrador Retriever look superficially similar to new owners but differ in ways that matter for daily care. Labrador Retriever is larger at 55-80 lbs, while Poodle typically runs 40-70 lbs. That size gap shows up in feeding volume, crate size, vehicle space, and how much joint-stress management each dog needs over their lifetime.

Both breeds share a high energy level, so the differentiator here is temperament, not exercise volume. Watch how each individual dog responds to training pressure, novelty, and time alone — that tells you more than the AKC group label.

Lifespan: Poodle typically lives 12-15 yrs; Labrador Retriever 10-13 yrs. Poodle generally has the longer-term care window, which affects insurance math and the point at which senior diagnostics become the dominant cost line.

Health watchlists differ. Both breeds share concerns around hip dysplasia. Poodle carries additional risk for progressive retinal atrophy, bloat. Labrador Retriever is more notably predisposed to elbow dysplasia, obesity. These aren’t guaranteed diagnoses — they’re the conditions responsible vets screen for, and they shape insurance underwriting more than most owners realize.

Grooming effort is meaningfully different: Poodle sheds at a minimal level, Labrador Retriever at heavy. That drives brush frequency, vacuum load, and whether the coat tolerates a week between sessions or demands daily attention during peak seasons.
