Poodle Grooming Guide

Poodle hair keeps growing, so my calendar carries a clip about every 4 to 6 weeks. Low shed does not mean low work. The coat stays on the patient until a comb or a clipper removes it, and a missed month turns feathers of hair into felt flush with the skin. I comb to bare skin before any clip, ears, armpits, and the tuck-up included. If the comb stops, the clipper is the mistaken next step. Mats come out or get clipped off with a guard that protects skin, and then the style can happen.

Feet, face, together with the tail need their own attention inside that roughly 4 to 6 week rhythm. I trim the hair between the pads so the foot can feel the floor. I clear the corner of the eyes so tears do not track into a stained rope of hair. The tail tip, if we are keeping length, gets combed most evenings with my fingers during the petting session. Ears get plucked of hair only when the clinic has shown me that this particular canal needs it. I plainly have seen infections started by enthusiastic plucking, so that step stays conservative.

Between appointments I comb on a midweek day and I repeat if this coat is long. Counting from the clip, I do a full comb in the 3-week mark and another in week 5 when the interval is running long, which puts the digits 3 and 5 on the refrigerator. Standards take longer in the tub. Toys chill faster and get dried promptly. The same rule covers both: dry to bare skin, especially the ears. VCA clipping and coat notes about the Poodle back the schedule I already keep, continuous growth and a clip that cannot slip by a month.

Grooming Schedule

Poodles have minimal shedding and require occasional brushing. While Poodles shed very little, regular grooming is still important for skin health and early detection of lumps or skin issues.

Weighing around 40-70 lbs and lifespan of 12-15 yrs, the Poodle benefits from care tailored to its physical and behavioral profile. Few breeds combine boundless energy with the Poodle's distinctive character quite so effectively.

Health Awareness: Poodles carry genetic predispositions to hip dysplasia, progressive retinal atrophy, bloat. Prevalence varies by individual, so the practical approach is a screening cadence that matches your vet's read of the breed's real-world risks. For most of these conditions, earlier identification translates directly into better management.
