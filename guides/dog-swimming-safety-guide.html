    Common Questions

    Head shaking after a lake is otitis, and cytology comes before another rinse. Standing water adds leptospirosis, and mosquitoes add the American Heartworm Society's yearly test. A life jacket is flotation. A dog who goes under needs a clinic.

    Swimming is exercise. Puppies are seen every 3 to 4 weeks until about 16 weeks, adults at least once a year, and many dogs from about 7 years, sooner in giant breeds, about every six months. Lakes and ditches are a reason to discuss leptospirosis and to run the yearly heartworm test at the preventive visit. Head shaking after a swim is a cytology visit when it starts. Near-drowning, trouble breathing, or a bloated abdomen after the beach is an emergency.

    A life jacket sized to the dog is the purchase that matters if you swim where the dog can go under. Ear infections after swimming become a cytology visit. Using leftover drops without that cytology can mean a second visit and a worse ear. Rinse and dry, and go in when the head shaking starts. Leptospirosis and heartworm prevention are the other costs the water adds if you skip them.
