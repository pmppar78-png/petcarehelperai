Dog Arthritis

Arthritis affects up to 80% of dogs over the age of 8, making it one of the most common health conditions in senior dogs. Understanding the signs and management options can help your dog maintain mobility and enjoy a better quality of life.

What is Arthritis?

Arthritis (also called osteoarthritis or degenerative joint disease) is a progressive condition where the cartilage that cushions joints breaks down, causing pain, inflammation, and reduced mobility. Unlike some conditions, arthritis cannot be cured, but it can be effectively managed.

Types of Arthritis in Dogs

  • Osteoarthritis (OA): The most common type; degenerative wear-and-tear
  • Rheumatoid arthritis: Autoimmune condition; rare in dogs
  • Septic arthritis: Caused by joint infection
  • Immune-mediated polyarthritis: Affects multiple joints due to immune dysfunction

Risk Factors

  • Age: Risk increases significantly after age 7-8
  • Size: Large and giant breeds are at higher risk
  • Obesity: Extra weight accelerates joint wear
  • Previous injury: Joint trauma increases arthritis risk
  • Hip/elbow dysplasia: Abnormal joint development leads to early arthritis
  • Genetics: Some breeds are predisposed
  • Repetitive stress: Working and athletic dogs

Symptoms of Arthritis

Dogs hide pain instinctively, so symptoms may be subtle. Watch for these signs.

Common Symptoms

  • Stiffness, especially after rest or in the morning
  • Difficulty rising from lying down
  • Reluctance to climb stairs, jump, or play
  • Limping or favoring one leg
  • Decreased activity or stamina
  • Lagging behind on walks
  • Muscle loss in affected limbs

Behavioral Changes

  • Irritability or aggression when touched
  • Reluctance to be petted in certain areas
  • Sleeping more than usual
  • Accidents in the house (difficulty getting outside)
  • Licking, chewing, or biting at joints
  • Decreased interest in activities they used to enjoy

Physical Signs

  • Swollen or warm joints
  • Audible clicking or grinding (crepitus)
  • Reduced range of motion
  • Muscle wasting (atrophy)
  • Changes in posture or gait

When to See a Vet

Schedule a veterinary appointment if your dog shows persistent stiffness, limping that doesn't improve with rest, difficulty with daily activities, or any signs of pain. Early intervention can slow disease progression and improve comfort.

Diagnosis

Your veterinarian will use several methods to diagnose arthritis.

  • Physical examination: Checking joints for pain, swelling, and range of motion
  • Gait analysis: Observing how your dog walks and moves
  • X-rays: Revealing joint changes, bone spurs, and cartilage loss
  • Joint fluid analysis: Ruling out infection or immune-mediated causes
  • Blood tests: Checking for systemic inflammation or other conditions
  • Advanced imaging: CT or MRI for complex cases

Treatment Options

Effective arthritis management typically involves multiple approaches working together.

Medications

NSAIDs (Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs)

First-line treatment for arthritis pain and inflammation.

  • Carprofen (Rimadyl)
  • Meloxicam (Metacam)
  • Deracoxib (Deramaxx)
  • Firocoxib (Previcox)
  • Grapiprant (Galliprant) - newer, targets specific pain pathways

Important: Never give human NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen) to dogs - they are toxic.

Other Pain Medications

  • Gabapentin: Nerve pain modifier, often combined with NSAIDs
  • Tramadol: Opioid-like pain relief for breakthrough pain
  • Amantadine: Helps with chronic pain sensitization

Disease-Modifying Osteoarthritis Drugs

  • Adequan (polysulfated glycosaminoglycan): Injectable medication that may help protect cartilage
  • Librela (bedinvetmab): Monthly injection targeting nerve growth factor for pain relief

Joint Supplements

While evidence varies, many pet owners and veterinarians report benefits from.

  • Glucosamine: Building block for cartilage
  • Chondroitin sulfate: Helps maintain cartilage elasticity
  • MSM (methylsulfonylmethane): Anti-inflammatory properties
  • Omega-3 fatty acids: Reduces joint inflammation
  • Green-lipped mussel: Natural source of joint-supporting compounds
  • Hyaluronic acid: Supports joint fluid viscosity

Physical Therapy and Rehabilitation

  • Hydrotherapy: Swimming or underwater treadmill for low-impact exercise
  • Therapeutic exercises: Controlled movements to maintain range of motion
  • Massage therapy: Reduces muscle tension and improves circulation
  • Laser therapy: Cold laser to reduce inflammation and pain
  • Acupuncture: May help with pain management
  • TENS therapy: Electrical stimulation for pain relief

Weight Management

Maintaining a healthy weight is one of the most important things you can do for an arthritic dog.

  • Extra weight puts additional stress on already damaged joints
  • Studies show that even modest weight loss can significantly improve mobility
  • Work with your vet to determine ideal weight and appropriate diet
  • Consider prescription weight management foods if needed

Environmental Modifications

  • Orthopedic bed: Memory foam or supportive bedding for joints
  • Non-slip surfaces: Rugs and mats on slippery floors
  • Ramps: For getting on furniture, in cars, or up stairs
  • Raised food/water bowls: Reduce neck strain
  • Pet stairs: For accessing beds or couches
  • Heated beds: Warmth soothes sore joints

Exercise Guidelines

  • Short, frequent walks rather than long, strenuous ones
  • Consistent daily exercise routine
  • Swimming or water therapy when possible
  • Avoid high-impact activities (jumping, rough play)
  • Let your dog set the pace
  • More activity on good days, less on bad days
  • Warm up with gentle movement before walks

Daily Comfort Tips

  • Warm compress before activity to loosen joints
  • Cold compress after activity if inflammation occurs
  • Gentle massage of affected areas
  • Keep nails trimmed for better traction and gait
  • Provide easy access to water (multiple locations)
  • Consider a harness with handle for mobility assistance

Newer and Alternative Treatments

  • Stem cell therapy: Injecting dog's own stem cells into joints
  • Platelet-rich plasma (PRP): Concentrated healing factors from blood
  • Extracorporeal shockwave therapy: Sound waves to promote healing
  • CBD products: Some owners report benefits; limited veterinary research
  • Adequan injections: May help protect remaining cartilage

What to Track

  • Activity levels and exercise tolerance
  • Stiffness duration in the morning
  • Limping patterns
  • Appetite and weight
  • Behavior changes
  • Response to medications

Regular Veterinary Care

  • Follow-up exams every 3-6 months for arthritic dogs
  • Blood work monitoring if on long-term NSAIDs
  • Periodic X-rays to assess progression
  • Medication adjustments as needed

Prognosis

While arthritis is progressive and cannot be cured, proper management can.

  • Significantly reduce pain and improve comfort
  • Maintain mobility and quality of life
  • Slow disease progression
  • Allow dogs to enjoy active lives for years
