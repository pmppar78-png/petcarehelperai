How Big Do Bernese Mountain Dogs Get? Size & Growth Guide

Bernese Mountain Dogs are a large breed, reaching 70-115 lbs at full maturity. Large breeds take 12-24 months to reach their adult size, with most of their height achieved by 12 months and filling out continuing until 18-24 months.

Care that accounts for breed predispositions leads to earlier detection and better prevention. Bernese Mountain Dogs sit in the large-size category, shed at a heavy level, and carry documented risk for cancer and hip dysplasia — those three factors drive most of the daily-care decisions.

Males are listed from 25 up to 27.5 inches. They carry 80-115 pounds across a typical male. Females are listed from 23 up to 26 in. They carry 70-95 pounds. I treat the distance from 70 pounds on a light female to approximately 115 pounds on a heavy male as the full window I might meet, with most dogs landing somewhere honest inside it. AKC height and weight ranges for Bernese Mountain Dogs stops the guessing that starts when a fluffy pup at eight weeks is the only dog a buyer has stood next to.

During month 1 the puppy is already substantial in bone and still nothing like the adult mass. During month 2 the coat starts to hint at the grown outline while the legs do most of the visual work. During month 6 the legs look long and the body looks light for those paws. During month 8 the chest begins to drop and the head widens. By 12 months people want to call him finished. He is tall enough to fool a camera and still light through the rib and the thigh.

Between roughly 18 and 24 months the adult mass shows up. Males especially fill chest, neck, and hindquarter and may climb toward the high end of 80 to 115 band. Females often settle inside 70 to 95 with less drama, though I once saw females take the full stretch too. I log a weight during the 1st month and during month 2 so the curve has a beginning, then again at 12, at 18, and at 24. Jumps and long hikes wait through that fill. A heavy puppy becomes a heavier adult with sore joints, and this breed already spends joint health at a high price.

Coat lies about the ribs. I part fur and use a flat hand. Ribs should be findable, and I need a waist I quietly can see when he is wet or when the undercoat has blown. Dry, fully coated, standing in winter, almost every one of them looks larger than the scale. Read height on a firm floor at the withers. The high end of ruff is theater and it adds no inches.

Buyers ask me whether a bigger pup is a better one. I need the pup whose parents sat inside those ranges and whose own curve stays steady. A youngster already looking like a full male during month 8 is one I watch at the bowl. The adult should arrive by 24 months looking fit for the old job: substantial, able to move, coat heavy, and still light enough on his feet to walk a lane. That is the size I accepted when I brought the breed home.

Factors Affecting Size

Many experienced Bernese Mountain Dog owners recommend a balanced mix of physical activities and brain games.

Mental stimulation is as important as physical exercise for Bernese Mountain Dog. Boredom is the root cause of most destructive behavior — not disobedience. Puzzle feeders, scent work, and novel experiences challenge your Bernese Mountain Dog's mind in ways that a standard walk cannot. Change up the routine regularly: the same toys and the same routes lose their enrichment value quickly.
