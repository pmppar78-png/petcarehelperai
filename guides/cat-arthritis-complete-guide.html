    Cats hide joint pain as a missed jump or a missed box edge. The AAHA Pain Management Guidelines include how that pain is recognized in cats. The veterinarian chooses the drug and the dose at the exam. AAFP/AAHA life stage guidance is why many cats from about 10 years old are already on a shorter exam interval.

    Missing a jump or hesitating at the litter box edge is a reason to examine a cat when it starts, and many cats from about 10 years old are already seen every six months so a pain plan can be adjusted. Kittens are on a different calendar, every 3 to 4 weeks until about 16 weeks. The drug and the dose are chosen at the visit. A cat who stops eating, or who cannot walk to the box, should be seen the same day.

    A stair or a softer bed is a fair purchase for a cat who is already on a pain plan. Book the pain exam before you buy furniture, so the veterinarian can choose the drug. There is no useful home dose. The recheck is what keeps you from paying for a crisis when the cat stops eating or cannot reach the box.
