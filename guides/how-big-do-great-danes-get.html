How Big Do Great Danes Get? Size & Growth Guide

Great Danes are a large breed, reaching 110-175 lbs at full maturity. Large breeds take 12-24 months to reach their adult size, with most of their height achieved by 12 months and filling out continuing until 18-24 months.

The value of breed awareness is in knowing what to watch for, not in assuming every individual will follow the statistical average.. Great Danes sit in the large-size category, shed at a moderate level, and carry documented risk for bloat and hip dysplasia — those three factors drive most of the daily-care decisions.

The tape sits at the topline, hound standing square, head level. Male height starts at 30 in and often finishes above that line. Many grown males are simply taller. Female height starts at 28 inches. I have stood a light adult bitch on the clinic scale and read 110 lb, and I have read 175 on a tall male whose chest had finished dropping. Both numbers can belong to correct dogs, because the registry is measuring height. A short back stacked with extra pounds is a different shape from the one I am feeding toward. I prefer a deep chest, a long leg, and a waist a hand can find without a search.

Youngsters fool people in the parking lot. Early on, the feet look borrowed. By month count 12 most of the leg length I am going to get is already there, and the body still looks narrow. The second project is fill. From month 18 toward 24 months into life the sternum drops, the thigh thickens, and the head finishes its planes. I quietly have seen depth keep coming after height stalled. Some were still refining at age 2 even though the withers had quit climbing around age 1. That lag is why the bowl stays measured. Kibble meant for big puppies, scooped, beats a bag that brags about a huge dog by the next holiday.

Dogs in one litter can land on either side of those height lines. I request the parents' mature inches, taken with a wicket, before I listen to a guess. A dam whose shoulder sits at 28 in and a sire well clear of 30 can throw a spread. Harlequin, mantle, fawn, brindle, blue, and black grow on one curve. The wicket ignores paint. Every month I quietly write the date, the inches, and whether I can feel ribs. A spurt followed by a pause is ordinary. Extra calories aimed at a pound target worry me, because the bone is still soft while the body is getting heavy.

Repeated stairs can wait while that fill is underway. Grass is the surface I trust under those big feet. People meet an adolescent around month 12, see the ears up in the air, and announce that growth is finished. The sternum still has dropping left. The coupling still has to catch the leg. I use the same spot every time, thumb on the shoulder blade, tape straight. Nobody leans the dog forward to steal an extra inch for the photo.

The AKC height expectations for Great Danes stays open on my phone while I explain why a pound chart is the wrong scoreboard. Withers height, males from 30 inches upward with many adults taller still, females from 28, deep chest arriving late: that is the size story I will defend. The 110 and the 175 are scale readings I quietly have seen, useful for a harness label, useless as a substitute for the tape.

Factors Affecting Size

Many experienced Great Dane owners recommend a balanced mix of physical activities and brain games.

Mental stimulation is as important as physical exercise for Great Dane. Boredom is the root cause of most destructive behavior — not disobedience. Puzzle feeders, scent work, and novel experiences challenge your Great Dane's mind in ways that a standard walk cannot. Change up the routine regularly: the same toys and the same routes lose their enrichment value quickly.

Common Questions About How Big Do Great Danes Get? Size & Growth Guide

What are the most important considerations for how big do great danes get?

Great Danes are 110 to 175 pounds and grow for 18 to 24 months. A deep chest and one huge meal are a bad pair during that window, so ask the clinic about bloat signs and about splitting meals. Weigh every month. A growth plateau is a phone call. A steady gain through the second year is the expected pattern.
