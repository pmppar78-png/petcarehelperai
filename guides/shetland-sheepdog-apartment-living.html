Shetland Sheepdog in an Apartment

Can a Shetland Sheepdog thrive in an apartment? Space needs, noise level, exercise requirements, and tips for small breed apartment living.

Apartment Suitability Score

Can a Shetland Sheepdog live in an apartment? Shetland Sheepdogs can adapt well to apartment living. Their small size and high energy levels make them more suitable for smaller spaces.

Weighing around 15-25 lbs and lifespan of 12-14 yrs, the Shetland Sheepdog requires attention to its unique health and temperament characteristics. The practical information below will help guide your decisions.

Space Requirements

Breed traits give you a general idea, but every pet has its own personality. Shetland Sheepdogs with high energy levels need consistent outlets for their drive and enthusiasm.

  • Size: small (15-25 lbs)
  • Energy Level: High
  • Shedding: Heavy
  • Common Health Issues: Collie Eye Anomaly, Hip Dysplasia, Thyroid Disease
  • Lifespan: 12-14 yrs

Noise Level

Adapting your care routine to your breed's specific needs makes a real difference. Shetland Sheepdogs have particular requirements based on their small size, heavy shedding level, and genetic predispositions to collie eye anomaly and hip dysplasia.

A conversation with your veterinarian ensures these general guidelines get adapted to your dog's unique needs, age, and overall condition.

Exercise Solutions

  • Daily exercise should total 60-120 minutes, split between physical activity and mental challenges
  • Feed a high-quality diet formulated for small breed dogs (400–800 calories/day)
  • Maintain a daily brushing grooming routine
  • Schedule breed-appropriate health screenings for collie eye anomaly
  • Consider pet insurance while your dog is young and healthy — premiums are lower and pre-existing conditions aren't an issue

Making It Work

Prevention-focused care tailored to breed characteristics reduces both health risks and long-term costs. Watch for early signs of collie eye anomaly, maintain regular veterinary visits, and keep your dog at a healthy weight — excess weight worsens most of the conditions Shetland Sheepdogs are prone to.

A stable daily routine serves as the foundation for behavioral wellness, reducing reactivity and stress responses. Set up regular times for meals, activity, grooming, and rest. High-energy Shetland Sheepdogs especially benefit from knowing when their exercise time is coming — it helps them settle during calmer periods.

Veterinary Care Schedule for Shetland Sheepdogs

Veterinary care frequency should adjust as your pet ages. Below is the recommended schedule, though your vet may adjust based on individual health for your Shetland Sheepdog. Adjust the schedule based on your vet's advice.

Puppy (0-1 year)Every 3-4 weeks until 16 weeks, then at 6 and 12 monthsVaccinations, deworming, spay/neuter (consult AVMA guidelines on optimal timing) consultation
Adult (1-7 years)AnnuallyPhysical exam, dental check, heartworm test, vaccination boosters

Shetland Sheepdogs should receive breed-specific screening for collie eye anomaly starting at 3-5 years of age or earlier if symptoms appear. Proactive testing tends to pay for itself in avoided complications.

Cost of Shetland Sheepdog Ownership

  • Annual food costs: $250–$500 for high-quality dog food
  • Veterinary care: $300–$700 annually for routine visits, plus potential emergency costs
  • Grooming: $30–50 per professional session (daily brushing home grooming recommended)
  • Pet insurance: $25–40/month for comprehensive coverage
  • Supplies and toys: $200–$500 annually for bedding, toys, leashes, and other essentials
