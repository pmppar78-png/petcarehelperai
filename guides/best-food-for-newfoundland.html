Nutritional Needs of Newfoundlands

As a large working breed with moderate energy levels, the Newfoundland has specific nutritional requirements that differ from other dogs. Understanding these needs is key to keeping your Newfoundland healthy throughout their 8-10 yrs lifespan.

Newfoundlands typically weigh 100-150 lbs and need approximately 1,400–2,200 calories per day, depending on age, activity level, and metabolism. With moderate energy levels, most Newfoundlands do well on standard feeding guidelines for their size.

Known Health Risks: Genetic screening data shows Newfoundlands have elevated rates of hip dysplasia, heart disease, bloat. Ask about hip dysplasia, heart disease, bloat at the exam for this breed. Many individual animals never show the conditions in question; when they do, a breed-literate veterinarian usually identifies them sooner.

Health-Specific Diet Considerations

Newfoundlands are prone to several health conditions that can be managed or prevented through proper nutrition.

Best Protein Sources for Newfoundlands

  • Chicken and turkey: Lean, easily digestible proteins ideal for most Newfoundlands

Foods to Avoid

Never feed your Newfoundland these dangerous foods.

  • Macadamia nuts and alcohol
  • Cooked bones (splintering risk)

Supplements Worth Considering

Based on Newfoundland-specific health concerns, these supplements may benefit your dog.

  • Glucosamine & Chondroitin: Essential for joint health, especially important for Newfoundlands prone to hip dysplasia

Wet Food vs Dry Food for Newfoundlands

Both wet and dry food have advantages for Newfoundlands.

  • Mixed feeding: Many Newfoundland owners find success combining both for balanced nutrition and variety

Feeding Mistakes to Avoid

Common feeding errors that Newfoundland owners make include:

  • Overuse of treats: Treats should make up no more than 10% of your Newfoundland's daily caloric intake. Many training treats are high in calories, so adjust meal portions accordingly.
  • Ignoring body condition: Use the body condition score (BCS) system to assess your Newfoundland's weight rather than relying solely on scale numbers. You should be able to feel the ribs without pressing hard.

Age-Specific Nutrition Considerations

Your Newfoundland's nutritional needs change significantly throughout their life.

Adult stage (1-7 years): Maintain a consistent feeding routine with measured portions. Monitor weight monthly and adjust food amounts based on activity level, seasonal changes, and body condition. Adult Newfoundlands benefit from a protein content of 22-30%.

Senior stage (7+ years): Older Newfoundlands may need fewer calories but higher-quality protein to maintain muscle mass. Senior formulas often include joint-supporting nutrients like glucosamine and chondroitin, plus antioxidants for cognitive health. Watch for changes in appetite.

How much should I feed my Newfoundland?

Adult Newfoundlands typically need 3–5 cups of high-quality food per day, split into two meals. Adjust based on your dog's activity level, age, and body condition score.

What is the best food brand for Newfoundlands?

Look for foods that list real meat as the first ingredient, meet AAFCO standards, and address Newfoundland-specific health needs like hip dysplasia. Brands offering large breed-specific formulas are often a good choice.

Should I feed my Newfoundland grain-free food?

Some Newfoundlands with dilated cardiomyopathy have had low taurine. The breed also faces subaortic stenosis and cystinuria. Cystinuria feeding is a low-purine diet the veterinarian selects, and a grain-free label is not that diet. Ask about a taurine blood test if the heart is enlarged, and keep the dog lean on the diet the clinic named. A grain allergy is uncommon in Newfoundlands and is a clinic diagnosis.
