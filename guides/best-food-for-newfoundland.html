Nutritional Needs of Newfoundlands

Newfoundlands typically weigh 100-150 lbs and need approximately 1,400–2,200 calories per day, depending on age, activity level, and metabolism. With moderate energy levels, most Newfoundlands do well on standard feeding guidelines for their size.

Wet Food vs Dry Food for Newfoundlands

Both wet and dry food have advantages for Newfoundlands.

Feeding Mistakes to

  • Overuse of treats: Treats should make up no more than 10% of your Newfoundland's daily caloric intake.
  • Ignoring body condition: Use the body condition score (BCS) system to assess your Newfoundland's weight rather than relying solely on scale numbers.

Age-Specific Nutrition Considerations

Your Newfoundland's nutritional needs change significantly throughout their life.

Adult Newfoundlands benefit from a protein content of 22-30%.

Senior stage (7+ years): Older Newfoundlands may need fewer calories but higher-quality protein to maintain muscle mass.

What is the best food brand for Newfoundlands?

Some Newfoundlands with dilated cardiomyopathy have had low taurine. The breed also faces subaortic stenosis and cystinuria. Cystinuria feeding is a low-purine diet the veterinarian selects, and a grain-free label is not that diet. Ask about a taurine blood test if the heart is enlarged, and keep the dog lean on the diet the clinic named. A grain allergy is uncommon in Newfoundlands and is a clinic diagnosis.

A Newfoundland should eat a measured large-breed diet and stay lean. Extra weight loads the hips and the heart of a dog that already overheats. Puppies need a large-breed growth food so the calories do not outrun the joints. Count the cups.
