Nutritional Needs of Papillons

As a small toy breed with moderate energy levels, the Papillon has specific nutritional requirements that differ from other dogs. Understanding these needs is key to keeping your Papillon healthy throughout their 14-16 yrs lifespan.

Papillons typically weigh 5-10 lbs and need approximately 400–800 calories per day, depending on age, activity level, and metabolism. With moderate energy levels, most Papillons do well on standard feeding guidelines for their size.

Genetic Health Considerations: The Papillon breed has documented susceptibility to luxating patella, dental disease, progressive retinal atrophy. Awareness of these predispositions is valuable for two reasons: it guides preventive screening decisions, and it helps you recognize early symptoms that might otherwise be overlooked.

Health-Specific Diet Considerations

Papillons are prone to several health conditions that can be managed or prevented through proper nutrition.

Best Protein Sources for Papillons

  • Chicken and turkey: Lean, easily digestible proteins ideal for most Papillons

Foods to Avoid

Never feed your Papillon these dangerous foods.

  • Macadamia nuts and alcohol
  • Cooked bones (splintering risk)

Supplements Worth Considering

Based on Papillon-specific health concerns, these supplements may benefit your dog.

  • Glucosamine: Supports joint health as your Papillon ages

Wet Food vs Dry Food for Papillons

Both wet and dry food have advantages for Papillons.

  • Mixed feeding: Many Papillon owners find success combining both for balanced nutrition and variety

Feeding Mistakes to Avoid

Common feeding errors that Papillon owners make include:

  • Overuse of treats: Treats should make up no more than 10% of your Papillon's daily caloric intake. Many training treats are high in calories, so adjust meal portions accordingly.
  • Ignoring body condition: Use the body condition score (BCS) system to assess your Papillon's weight rather than relying solely on scale numbers. You should be able to feel the ribs without pressing hard.

Age-Specific Nutrition Considerations

Your Papillon's nutritional needs change significantly throughout their life.

Adult stage (1-7 years): Maintain a consistent feeding routine with measured portions. Monitor weight monthly and adjust food amounts based on activity level, seasonal changes, and body condition. Adult Papillons benefit from a protein content of 22-30%.

Senior stage (7+ years): Older Papillons may need fewer calories but higher-quality protein to maintain muscle mass. Senior formulas often include joint-supporting nutrients like glucosamine and chondroitin, plus antioxidants for cognitive health. Watch for changes in appetite.

How much should I feed my Papillon?

Adult Papillons typically need 0.5–1.5 cups of high-quality food per day, split into two meals. Adjust based on your dog's activity level, age, and body condition score.

What is the best food brand for Papillons?

Look for foods that list real meat as the first ingredient, meet AAFCO standards, and address Papillon-specific health needs like luxating patella. Brands offering small breed-specific formulas are often a good choice.

Should I feed my Papillon grain-free food?

Papillon dogs do not need a grain-free diet for luxating patella or progressive retinal atrophy. The FDA investigation of diet-associated dilated cardiomyopathy collected reports of dogs eating grain-free and legume-rich foods and did not show that dropping grain protects a Papillon. A complete diet for a Papillon should meet AAFCO dog nutrient profiles, and a veterinarian should approve a switch when luxating patella or progressive retinal atrophy is already part of that dog's care.
