How Big Do Brittanys Get? Size & Growth Guide

Brittany full size: 30-40 lbs, medium breed. Growth timeline from puppy to adult, weight chart, and when they stop growing.

Full-Grown Size

Brittanys are a medium breed, reaching 30-40 lbs at full maturity. Medium breeds generally reach full size between 10-16 months.

Weighing around 30-40 lbs and lifespan of 12-14 yrs, the Brittany has specific care needs shaped by its genetics and build. Below you'll find the key information organized by topic.

Breed data gives us statistical probabilities, not certainties — but those probabilities shape smart care decisions. If you own Brittany, plan on steady daily outlets for their energy; the breed's drive is real, and the alternatives to channeling it are worse.

Care that accounts for breed predispositions leads to earlier detection and better prevention. Practical Brittanys care is shaped by three things: medium size, moderate shedding, and a known predisposition to hip dysplasia and epilepsy.

Male vs Female Size

The key to a happy, healthy Brittany is matching your care approach to their breed characteristics. High-energy breeds need physical and mental outlets every day — without them, behavioral problems like destructive chewing or excessive barking are common.

Factors Affecting Size

The details that distinguish this breed from similar breeds matter for long-term health and wellbeing. As a sporting breed, the Brittany has instincts and behaviors shaped by centuries of selective breeding for specific tasks.

Many experienced Brittany owners recommend dog sports like agility, flyball, or nosework to channel their energy productively.

Enrichment does not require expensive equipment. For Brittany, simple activities like hiding treats around the house for discovery, using a muffin tin with tennis balls over kibble, or practicing basic obedience in new locations provide effective cognitive engagement. The goal is not complexity — it is variety and appropriate challenge level.

When They Stop Growing

Knowing what to watch for gives you a real head start on breed-related problems. Watch for early signs of hip dysplasia, maintain regular veterinary visits, and keep your dog at a healthy weight — excess weight worsens most of the conditions Brittanys are prone to.

A consistent daily schedule reduces stress hormones measurably — animals that know what to expect spend less energy on vigilance and more on rest and recovery. Set up regular times for meals, activity, grooming, and rest. High-energy Brittanys especially benefit from knowing when their exercise time is coming — it helps them settle during calmer periods.

Brittanys should receive breed-specific screening for hip dysplasia starting at 3-5 years of age or earlier if symptoms appear. Catching problems early gives you more treatment options and better odds.
