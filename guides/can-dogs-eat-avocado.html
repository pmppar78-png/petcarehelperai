Can Dogs Eat

Is avocado safe for dogs? Covers persin toxicity, pit hazards, which parts are dangerous, and what to do if your dog eats avocado.

The pit, the skin, and the dip

For dogs, the dramatic avocado emergency is usually the pit. A pit is the right size to lodge in the esophagus or to block the intestine, and it is slippery enough to be swallowed whole during counter surfing. Skin can be swallowed in strips. Guacamole adds onion, garlic, lime, salt, and sometimes tomato and spices. Those add-ins matter as much as the fruit. Onion and garlic threaten red blood cells. A salty, fatty dip can also upset the pancreas. If the dog ate guacamole, tell the clinic about the seasoning and the avocado together.

Persin is a fungicidal compound in avocado leaves, skin, pit, and to a lesser extent the flesh. It is a serious toxin for many birds, and it affects some livestock. Dogs appear less sensitive to persin in the flesh than birds do, which is why a lick of plain avocado is a different story from a parrot tasting the same bite. Less sensitive does not mean you should serve avocado as a treat. The pit, the skin, and the seasoned dip carry clearer and more immediate risks, and the flesh is still a high-fat food a dog does not need.

What to watch

After a pit, watch for drooling, gagging, retching, vomiting, a stretched posture, and a refusal to eat. Those signs can mean the pit is stuck. Belly pain, repeated vomiting, or a distended abdomen can mean an intestinal blockage and should be seen the same day. After guacamole, also watch for vomiting and, over the following days, weakness or pale gums if onion was a major ingredient. A mild loose stool after a lick of plain flesh may pass with a quiet stomach and water, and you should still call if the dog is small, old, or already vomiting.

Bring any remaining fruit, the pit if it was recovered, and the ingredient list from a packaged guacamole. A photo of the amount missing helps. Skip home vomiting unless the veterinarian handling the pit says to start it. A pit that comes back up can lodge in the esophagus on the way. Oil, salt, and bread do not move a pit along safely. The clinic may recommend radiographs or ultrasound. Some pits pass with monitoring. Some need endoscopy or surgery. That decision depends on size, location, and the dog in front of them.

Birds in the same house

If a parrot, cockatiel, or other bird shared the avocado, that animal's emergency is persin, which has been linked to heart and breathing failure in companion birds. Call an avian veterinarian immediately for the bird even if the dog's issue is "only a pit." Separate the advice. Do not wait to see if the bird fluffs up. Avocado toast on a counter is a two-species hazard in a house that has both a dog and a bird. Clean the counter before either animal returns to the room.

Other pets deserve a mention on the call. A cat who licked guacamole may have taken in onion. A second dog may have taken the pit you think the first dog ate. Count the pits from the cutting board. Kitchens lose track of pits under scraps. A missing pit plus a dog who was unsupervised is enough reason to call and ask whether imaging is warranted.

Keeping avocado off the floor

Prepare avocado when the dog is in another room. Throw pits into a trash can with a lid, or into a jar you empty outside, because an open bin is a second chance to swallow one. Guacamole bowls do not stay on coffee tables during a party. Ask guests to keep plates on their laps. If you want a high-value training food, use the dog's kibble or a food the veterinarian already approved. There is no nutrient in avocado that a complete dog food is missing in a way that justifies the pit risk.

Write a one-line house rule: pits go into a lidded jar, dips stay on the high counter, and any swallowed pit triggers a call. Children can learn that rule faster than they can learn toxicology. The dog does not need a lecture. The dog needs the pit to be gone. If a swallow already happened, the useful next step is the phone, the remaining fruit, and a calm ride to the clinic the veterinarian recommends.

  • Treat a swallowed avocado pit as an obstruction risk and call a veterinarian.
  • Describe guacamole seasonings, especially onion and garlic.
  • Know that persin is a much larger crisis for birds than for dogs.
  • Do not induce vomiting for a pit unless a veterinarian says so.
  • Watch for gagging, retching, vomiting, and a painful belly.
  • Count pits so you know whether one is missing.
  • Lid the trash and keep party dips off low tables.
  • Call an avian veterinarian immediately if a bird ate any avocado.

After the imaging decision

If the clinic radiographs the belly, ask what a passing pit would look like in the stool and how many days to watch. Straining, repeated vomiting, or a sudden painful belly cancels the watch and means a return. Count pits again at home in case a second one is missing. If a bird had access to the same board, that call is about persin and should happen immediately, separate from the dog's obstruction question. Wipe the counter before either animal comes back.

After the clinic visit, recount the pits in the trash jar while everyone is still awake, in case a second pit is missing.

Counting pits after a party

Avocado pits disappear under napkins and into open bins. After a gathering, count the pits against the number of avocados you cut. A short count plus a dog who was loose in the kitchen is a reason to call and ask about imaging, even before gagging starts. Guacamole bowls add onion and garlic, so tell the clinic about the recipe if the dog ate dip. Plain flesh is a high-fat lick. A pit is an obstruction. Those are different sentences for the triage nurse, and you should say both if both happened. Bring any remaining fruit so the size of the pit can be compared with what might be missing.

If a bird shares the house, move the bird to a carrier away from the counter before you clean. Persin is a heart and breathing risk for parrots and other companion birds. The dog's obstruction question and the bird's toxin question need two calls if both species had access. Wipe the cutting board before anyone returns to the room. Future parties get a lidded jar for pits that sits on the counter only while you are standing there, then goes outside to the trash. That jar is cheaper than an endoscopic retrieval.

Questions about avocado and dogs

Can dogs eat avocado flesh?

Plain flesh is less of a persin crisis in dogs than it is in birds, and it is still a high-fat food with a dangerous pit and skin. Guacamole adds onion, garlic, and salt. Skip avocado as a treat and call a veterinarian if a pit or a bowl of dip was eaten.

What if my dog swallowed the pit?

Call a veterinarian and describe the dog's size and the pit's size. Do not induce vomiting unless they instruct you, because the pit can lodge in the esophagus. Gagging, retching, or repeated vomiting needs prompt imaging. Bring any remaining fruit.

Why is avocado worse for birds in the house?

Persin in avocado has been linked to heart and respiratory failure in parrots and other companion birds. A bird who nibbled toast or flesh needs an avian veterinarian immediately. Handle the dog's obstruction risk and the bird's persin risk as two calls.
