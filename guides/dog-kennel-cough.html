Kennel Cough in Dogs

Understanding kennel cough (infectious tracheobronchitis) in dogs. Covers symptoms, treatment, Bordetella vaccination, and when it becomes pneumonia.

Causes and Risk Factors

Multiple factors can contribute to the development of this condition.

  • Genetic predisposition: Certain breeds are at higher risk due to inherited traits
  • Age: Some conditions are more common in puppies/kittens or senior pets
  • Environmental factors: Diet, toxin exposure, stress, and living conditions play a role
  • Underlying health issues: Other conditions may increase susceptibility
  • Immune system status: Compromised immune function can predispose to disease

Symptoms to Watch For

Early detection significantly improves treatment outcomes. Watch for these signs.

  • Changes in appetite, water consumption, or weight
  • Behavioral changes including lethargy, restlessness, or hiding
  • Physical symptoms specific to the affected body system
  • Changes in bathroom habits (frequency, appearance, straining)
  • Visible discomfort, pain response to touch, or changes in posture
  • Respiratory changes, coughing, or labored breathing

When to See the Vet Immediately

Share planned diet changes with the vet before implementation — they see interactions that generic advice cannot account for.

Diagnosis

Kennel cough, canine infectious respiratory disease complex, is usually diagnosed from the cough and a recent exposure to other dogs. A bright dog with a honking cough and a normal energy level does not need a PCR panel to start care. Chest radiographs are the test when the dog is lethargic, febrile, or working to breathe, because that is how pneumonia is found. A respiratory PCR panel can name Bordetella, influenza, or other agents when the outbreak is severe or the dog is not improving.

Treatment Options

An uncomplicated kennel-cough dog often needs rest and a cough plan the veterinarian chooses. Pneumonia is different: it needs antibiotics selected for that dog, and sometimes oxygen and fluids. The veterinarian chooses the drug and the dose. Vaccines against Bordetella and canine influenza reduce risk and do not treat a dog who is already coughing.

Prevention and Management

  • Regular veterinary checkups (as recommended by the AVMA for all companion animals) for early detection (annually, or semi-annually for seniors)
  • Maintain a healthy weight through proper nutrition and exercise
  • Keep vaccinations and preventive medications current
  • Breed-specific health screenings recommended by your veterinarian
  • Provide a low-stress, enriched environment
  • Follow your vet's recommendations for diet and lifestyle modifications

Cost of Treatment

Treatment costs vary based on severity and duration.

Is this condition curable?

Most healthy adult dogs with uncomplicated kennel cough, canine infectious respiratory disease complex, improve over one to three weeks. ISCAID respiratory guidance treats that honking cough as self-limited. The picture changes in a puppy, a brachycephalic dog, or any dog that is becoming pneumonic. Pneumonia is a treatable lung infection and needs veterinary care the same day.

How can I afford treatment?

Price kennel cough as an exam and a recheck, and ask which signs would trigger chest radiographs the same day. Oxygen and hospitalization are the pneumonia invoice. Write that threshold down before a Sunday-night emergency. A vaccine and a week out of the kennel cost less than the radiograph path.
