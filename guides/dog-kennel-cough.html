Kennel Cough in Dogs

This page is educational reference material for preparing questions. A licensed veterinarian examines the animal, makes the diagnosis, and sets the treatment.

Understanding kennel cough (infectious tracheobronchitis) in dogs. Covers symptoms, treatment, Bordetella vaccination, and when it becomes pneumonia.

What kennel cough actually is

Kennel cough is the everyday name for canine infectious respiratory disease complex, a contagious airway infection that inflames the trachea and bronchi. Several agents share the credit. Bordetella bronchiseptica is the bacterium most owners have heard of. Canine parainfluenza virus, canine adenovirus type 2, canine respiratory coronavirus, and Mycoplasma cynos are frequent partners. Canine influenza viruses, H3N2 and H3N8, can look the same at the start and then hit harder. In crowded outbreaks, Streptococcus zooepidemicus has been tied to a sudden, severe pneumonia. Distemper still belongs on the list for an unvaccinated puppy who is coughing and also acting systemically ill.

The honking cough is the hallmark because the trachea is irritated and sensitive. A bright dog who eats, plays a little, and then coughs until he gags is the uncomplicated picture. Pneumonia is a different disease sitting in the same syndrome: the infection has moved into the lung, the dog is dull, and breathing becomes work.

Which dogs catch it

Any dog who shares air with other dogs can catch it. Boarding kennels, daycare, shelters, grooming salons, dog shows, training classes, and busy parks are the classic settings. The incubation period is often a few days and can stretch toward ten. Puppies, brachycephalic breeds such as French Bulldogs and Pugs, seniors, and dogs with existing heart or airway disease get into trouble faster because their airways have less reserve. A single cough in a vaccinated adult who visited daycare may stay mild. The same cough in a six-week-old puppy from a pet store is a different level of risk.

Vaccines lower the odds and the severity. They do not build a wall. Bordetella vaccines come as intranasal, oral, and injectable products, and canine influenza is a separate vaccine for dogs with a social calendar. Parainfluenza and adenovirus type 2 are already inside the core distemper combination. A dog can still cough after every one of those shots if a strain in the room was not in the syringe.

Signs owners notice

The cough is dry, harsh, and often ends in a retch that owners mistake for vomiting. Pressing the throat gently can set it off, which is something to mention, and something to stop doing once you have seen it once. Nasal discharge, if present, may be clear at first. The uncomplicated dog still wants dinner and still follows you to the kitchen. Worry starts when the cough becomes soft and wet, the appetite falls, the temperature is high, or the dog breathes faster at rest. Blue or gray gums, an elbow-out posture, and a puppy who will not get up are pneumonia signs until proven otherwise.

  • A honking cough that started a few days after daycare, boarding, or a dog park.
  • Gagging or coughing up white foam, with the dog otherwise bright.
  • Clear or cloudy nasal discharge and mild eye watering.
  • Lethargy, fever, or refusing food, which moves the problem toward pneumonia.
  • Fast breathing, effort at the chest, or a change in gum color.

How a veterinarian diagnoses kennel cough

Kennel cough, canine infectious respiratory disease complex, is usually diagnosed from the cough and a recent exposure to other dogs. A bright dog with a honking cough and a normal energy level does not need a PCR panel to start care. Chest radiographs are the test when the dog is lethargic, febrile, or working to breathe, because that is how pneumonia is found. A respiratory PCR panel can name Bordetella, influenza, or other agents when the outbreak is severe or the dog fails to improve.

The physical exam listens for crackles or harsh lung sounds and checks hydration and gum color. A complete blood count is added when the dog looks sick enough that a systemic infection is plausible. PCR on a deep swab is most useful for a kennel outbreak, a dog who is worsening on the first plan, or a case where influenza or distemper would change isolation rules. A single cough in a well dog rarely needs that panel on day one. Radiographs are the branch point: a normal chest in a bright dog supports rest, and an interstitial or alveolar pattern supports treating pneumonia.

Treatment categories

An uncomplicated kennel-cough dog often needs rest and a cough plan the veterinarian chooses. Pneumonia is different: it needs antibiotics selected for that dog, and sometimes oxygen and fluids. The veterinarian matches the drug and the course to the radiographs and the exam. Vaccines against Bordetella and canine influenza reduce risk and do not treat a dog who is already coughing.

For the bright, eating dog, the plan may be rest, a harness, humidity, and a cough suppressant if the cough is dry and the lungs are clear. Every honk does not automatically earn an antibiotic. ISCAID guidance treats many of these illnesses as self-limited in healthy adults. Antibiotics enter the plan when the dog is febrile, lethargic, producing mucopurulent discharge, or showing pneumonia on films. Doxycycline is a frequent choice when a bacterial component such as Bordetella is suspected, and the clinic sets the amount. A dog in oxygen, on intravenous fluids, or too nauseated to swallow needs a hospital. Cough suppressants are avoided when the dog needs to clear pus from the lungs. The veterinarian makes that split. You should not start a leftover antibiotic from a previous ear infection while you wait.

Home care

Isolate the dog from other dogs, including housemates if you can do it without stress, until the veterinarian says group contact is reasonable. Many dogs stay contagious while they are coughing and for a period after the cough quiets. Skip daycare, grooming, and parks during that window. Switch from a collar to a harness so pressure on the trachea does not trigger another fit. A humidifier, or a steamy bathroom with the dog sitting on the floor and the door open for air, can ease a dry cough. Offer water and normal food. Count resting breaths when the dog is asleep: a rate that keeps climbing, or breathing that looks like the abdomen is working hard, is a reason to return the same day. Wipe bowls and wash hands, and tell the clinic before you walk into a waiting room full of puppies so they can bring you in through a side path.

When kennel cough is an emergency

When to See the Vet Immediately

Most healthy adult dogs with uncomplicated kennel cough, canine infectious respiratory disease complex, improve over one to three weeks. ISCAID respiratory guidance treats that honking cough as self-limited. The picture changes in a puppy, a brachycephalic dog, or any dog that is becoming pneumonic. Pneumonia is a treatable lung infection and needs veterinary care the same day. Go to an emergency hospital if the gums look blue or gray, the dog collapses, breathing is labored at rest, or a puppy stops eating and cannot be roused. Those dogs need oxygen and radiographs, and a Sunday night is a poor time to hope the honk stays uncomplicated.

What to ask about a kennel-cough bill

Price kennel cough as an exam and a recheck, and ask which signs would trigger chest radiographs the same day. Oxygen and hospitalization are the pneumonia invoice. Write that threshold down before a Sunday-night emergency. A vaccine and a week out of the kennel cost less than the radiograph path.

Key Questions

How long should a coughing dog avoid daycare?

Keep the dog home while the cough is active and until your veterinarian clears group contact. A common window runs through the coughing period and into the days after it stops, because dogs can still shed organisms after they sound better. Sending a dog back on the first quiet morning can reseed the daycare. Tell the facility the diagnosis so they can warn other owners and check their ventilation and cleaning.

Does the Bordetella vaccine prevent every cough?

It reduces risk from Bordetella and can soften the illness. It does not cover canine influenza, and it does not cover every virus in the complex. Intranasal and oral products stimulate local immunity on a faster schedule than some injectable forms, which is why boarding facilities ask for them ahead of a stay. A dog who was vaccinated and still honks can have a strain the vaccine never claimed to stop.

When does a cough become pneumonia?

When the dog is dull, feverish, off food, or working to breathe, and when chest radiographs show lung involvement. A harsh cough in a dog who still wants breakfast is usually still airway irritation. Crackles, fast breathing at rest, and a wet cough are reasons to image the chest the same day. Streptococcus zooepidemicus and influenza are two reasons a kennel outbreak with very sick dogs needs more than cough syrup.
