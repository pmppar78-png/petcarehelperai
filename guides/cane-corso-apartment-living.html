Cane Corso in an Apartment

Can a Cane Corso thrive in an apartment? Space needs, noise level, exercise requirements, and tips for large breed apartment living.

Apartment Suitability Score

Can a Cane Corso live in an apartment? With adequate daily exercise, Cane Corsos can adapt to apartment living, though a home with a yard is preferable.

At 88-120 lbs with a 9-12 yrs lifespan, the Cane Corso has a health and temperament profile that rewards close attention rather than generic care. Let's examine the important details.

Space Requirements

Understanding breed tendencies equips you to anticipate needs, even as individual personalities vary. Cane Corsos with moderate energy levels strike a good balance between activity and relaxation.

  • Size: large (88-120 lbs)
  • Energy Level: Moderate
  • Shedding: Light
  • Common Health Issues: Hip Dysplasia, Bloat, Cherry Eye
  • Lifespan: 9-12 yrs

Care that accounts for breed predispositions leads to earlier detection and better prevention. Plan Cane Corsos care around a large body size, light shedding, and the breed's documented predisposition toward hip dysplasia and bloat.

Exercise Solutions

The key to a happy, healthy Cane Corso is matching your care approach to their breed characteristics. Mental engagement during activity sessions multiplies the benefit — a training walk where the animal practices commands is more valuable than the same distance walked passively.

  • Provide 30–60 minutes of daily exercise appropriate to their energy level
  • Feed a high-quality diet formulated for large breed dogs (1,400–2,200 calories/day)
  • Maintain a weekly grooming routine
  • Schedule breed-appropriate health screenings for hip dysplasia
  • Insurance purchased pre-diagnosis gives you the fullest set of covered conditions and the best renewal pricing.

Making It Work

Preventive care calibrated to breed profile, rather than generic pet care, reliably shifts long-term outcomes. Watch for early signs of hip dysplasia, maintain regular veterinary visits, and keep your dog at a healthy weight — excess weight worsens most of the conditions Cane Corsos are prone to.
