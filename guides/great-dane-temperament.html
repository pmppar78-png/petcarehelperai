Great Dane Temperament & Personality Guide

The lamp died because a tail at chest height cleared the end table on the way to the kitchen. Living beside a male whose withers cleared 30 inches rearranged the furniture more than the schedule. He wanted whichever room I was using, and he arrived in a few strides. Females I have handled near the 28 inch minimum still fill a hallway when they turn around. Plenty of grown dogs stand taller than those minimums, and the taller ones lean. The lean is affection with a shoulder behind it. I teach a stand-back, palm flat on the ribcage, before a guest reaches the rug.

What I have lived with is a long watch and a short burst. The driveway gets noticed. One deep bark rolls out, then the dog checks my face to learn whether the person belongs. Play is real, and it stays clumsy until it has edges. I hold a tug low so the neck stays level, and I stop the game when the feet start to skate. Toy 1 is a soft tug saved for evening. Toy 2 is a stuffed animal that gets put away, so the game has a start and a finish. A harsh shout shuts these dogs down faster than it teaches anything. The cue stays quiet. The pay is the toy, or the chance to follow me to the door.

Coat color never sorted character in my house. A gold coat, a striped coat, a dilute gray-blue, a solid black, a patched white, and a dark-blanket mantle all wanted the same patch of rug behind my heels. The short coat lets me feel a shiver when the floor is cold, and a sweater on a January night stops the pacing that comes with that shiver. Once the house goes quiet they settle hard. Mine slept with his spine along the couch and his nose aimed down the hall, still on duty inside the dream.

Strangers go better when practice happens while the hound is young and the human stays calm. We sit in the clinic lot. People pass. He gets to watch, which suits a body built for a long look, and then he gets to leave. Steering a giant into a hug from a stranger teaches him that people corner him. The AKC breed page on the Great Dane sketches the large, people-focused dog I have been feeding. I trust that outline, and I trust the palm-on-chest lesson the day a delivery driver steps inside.

Hip and Joint Health Management

Gastric Dilatation-Volvulus (GDV) Prevention

Bloat, technically gastric dilatation-volvulus (GDV), represents a life-threatening surgical emergency with mortality rates between 10-33% even with treatment. As a large breed with a deep chest conformation, the Great Dane carries elevated GDV risk. A landmark Purdue University study identified key risk factors: feeding from elevated bowls (contrary to earlier recommendations), eating one large meal daily, rapid eating, and a fearful temperament. Evidence-based prevention includes feeding 2-3 smaller meals daily, restricting vigorous exercise for 60-90 minutes after eating, and discussing prophylactic gastropexy with your veterinarian — a procedure that can be performed during spay/neuter surgery and reduces GDV risk by over 90%.

What are the most important considerations for great dane temperament?

A Great Dane is a short-lived giant that is often affectionate and clumsy. It needs room and a stomach plan. Dilated cardiomyopathy, bloat, and osteosarcoma are the three medical facts under the sweet temperament.
