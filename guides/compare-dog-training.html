What to Look for in Online Training

Red Flags to Avoid

  • Dominance-based methods: "Alpha" rhetoric, physical corrections
  • Quick fix promises: "Train your dog in 7 days!"
  • Aversive tools required: Shock collars, prong collars
  • No credentials: Trainers without verifiable experience
  • Outdated methods: Based on debunked science

Green Flags to Seek

  • Positive reinforcement: Reward-based training methods
  • Realistic timelines: Honest about training duration
  • Force-free approach: No pain or intimidation
  • Qualified instructors: Certifications (CPDT-KA, IAABC, etc.)
  • Updated content: Based on current behavioral science
  • Community support: Forums, groups, or Q&A access

When to Seek In-Person Help

Online training may not be sufficient for serious behavioral issues like aggression, severe anxiety, or fear-based behaviors. If your dog shows signs of aggression toward people or animals, resource guarding with biting, or extreme fear reactions, please consult a certified veterinary behaviorist or certified applied animal behaviorist (CAAB) in person.

Tips for Success with Online Training

Set Yourself Up for Success

  • Watch first, then train: Preview lessons before practicing with your dog
  • Prepare your supplies: Have treats, clicker, leash ready before starting
  • Short sessions: 5-10 minute training sessions multiple times daily
  • Quiet environment: Start in low-distraction settings
  • Keep a log: Track progress and challenges
  • Be consistent: Train daily, even if just for a few minutes

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Moving too fast before dog masters current step
  • Training when dog is tired, hungry, or over-excited
  • Skipping foundation skills to jump to advanced behaviors
  • Inconsistency between family members
  • Giving up after initial challenges
