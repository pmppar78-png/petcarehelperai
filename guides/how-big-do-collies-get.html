How Big Do Collies Get? Size & Growth Guide

Collie full size: 50-75 lbs, large breed. Growth timeline from puppy to adult, weight chart, and when they stop growing.

Full-Grown Size

Collies are a large breed, reaching 50-75 lbs at full maturity. Large breeds take 12-24 months to reach their adult size, with most of their height achieved by 12 months and filling out continuing until 18-24 months.

Weighing around 50-75 lbs and lifespan of 12-14 yrs, the Collie benefits from care tailored to its physical and behavioral profile. Read on for the specifics that matter most.

Growth Timeline

No two animals are identical, but breed-level health and temperament data provides actionable guidance for owners. Collies with moderate energy levels strike a good balance between activity and relaxation.

  • Size: large (50-75 lbs)
  • Energy Level: Moderate
  • Shedding: Heavy
  • Common Health Issues: Collie Eye Anomaly, Hip Dysplasia, Bloat
  • Lifespan: 12-14 yrs

The key to a happy, healthy Collie is matching your care approach to their breed characteristics. Consistent daily activity, even in short sessions, contributes more to long-term health than occasional intense exercise.

  • Provide 30–60 minutes of daily exercise appropriate to their energy level
  • Feed a high-quality diet formulated for large breed dogs (1,400–2,200 calories/day)
  • Maintain a daily brushing grooming routine
  • Schedule breed-appropriate health screenings for collie eye anomaly
  • Insurance purchased pre-diagnosis gives you the fullest set of covered conditions and the best renewal pricing.

Factors Affecting Size

Informed ownership goes deeper than the basic care checklist for any breed. As a herding breed, the Collie has instincts and behaviors shaped by centuries of selective breeding for specific tasks.

Many experienced Collie owners recommend a balanced mix of physical activities and brain games.

Understanding your Collie's instinctual drives makes enrichment more effective. Rather than generic toy rotation, tailor activities to what this breed was developed to do. Working breeds benefit from task-oriented challenges; scent-driven breeds thrive with nose work; social breeds need interactive play rather than solo activities.

Preventive care reduces both emergency costs and disease severity over your pet's lifetime. Here is a general framework for your Collie. These are baseline recommendations.

Collies should receive breed-specific screening for collie eye anomaly starting at 1-2 years of age, as large breeds develop structural issues early. Screening before symptoms appear makes a meaningful difference in outcomes.

Cost of Collie Ownership

Ownership costs vary by region, health status, and lifestyle. These ranges reflect national averages for Collie ownership.
