Irish Setter Pet Insurance Overview

Pet insurance for Irish Setters is particularly important given their predisposition to hip dysplasia, bloat, epilepsy.

Irish Setter premiums are dog premiums. Gastric dilatation-volvulus, hip dysplasia, idiopathic epilepsy, and progressive retinal atrophy are the claims. Megaesophagus and CLAD occur in the breed as well.

Known Health Risks: Genetic screening data shows Irish Setters have elevated rates of hip dysplasia, bloat, epilepsy. Breed-linked risks describe populations, not prognoses; many individual pets never encounter the issues their breed is associated with. A veterinarian who knows the breed profile simply catches problems earlier when they do surface.

Best Time to Insure Your Irish Setter

Enroll your Irish Setter as early as possible — ideally as a puppy or kitten. Pre-existing conditions are never covered, so insuring before health issues develop is critical. Irish Setters are prone to hip dysplasia, which can develop as early as 1-2 years of age.

How much does Irish Setter pet insurance cost?

NAPHIA's 2025 State of the Industry report, compiled by Willis Towers Watson from year-end 2024 premiums, puts US dog accident-and-illness coverage at $62.44 a month ($749.29 a year) and accident-only coverage at $16.10 a month. Enrollment age, ZIP code, deductible, reimbursement percentage, and annual limit set the quote. For an Irish Setter, the claims that move the quote are idiopathic epilepsy, progressive retinal atrophy, and gastric dilatation-volvulus.

Is pet insurance worth it for an Irish Setter?

Idiopathic epilepsy in an Irish Setter is medication, and progressive retinal atrophy is not surgical. Gastric dilatation-volvulus is the emergency operation. Megaesophagus is feeding management from an elevated position. Hip dysplasia remains its own orthopedic choice.

What pre-existing conditions affect Irish Setter insurance?

Any condition diagnosed before enrollment is excluded. For Irish Setters, common pre-existing concerns include hip dysplasia and bloat. Early enrollment is key.
