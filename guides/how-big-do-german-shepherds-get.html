How Big Do German Shepherds Get? Size & Growth Guide

This page is educational reference material for preparing questions. A licensed veterinarian examines the animal, makes the diagnosis, and sets the treatment.

German Shepherd full size: 50-90 lbs, large breed. Growth timeline from puppy to adult, weight chart, and when they stop growing.

Full-Grown Size

German Shepherds are a large breed, reaching 50-90 lbs at full maturity.

Leg length gets a tape at approximately 9 months and at approximately 13 months, with the checkpoint people expect at approximately 12 months. Chest and thigh get a fresh look at approximately 18 months and another at approximately 24 months. Leg length often shows up before the ribcage and thigh fill. A roommate who looks finished at approximately 12 months frequently puts on substance from 18 months into 24 months. That fill is bone and muscle if the work and the cup are right, and it is fat if the cup is a guess. The dense double coat camouflages a thickening waist. Hands go through the coat to the ribs.

Herding condition is the outline inside those numbers: able to move, reserved and athletic, with a job. A soft, inactive dog high in the male range is a different animal from a working dog at the same pounds. Weigh once each month while height is changing fast, or every two weeks, checkpoint 2, if the puppy is a heavy eater or a picky one losing outline. Watch the shoulder angulation as the chest drops, because a pretty stack in a photo can hide a narrow puppy who will still fill the 65 to 90 male line or stay nearer the 50 lb female floor. People also arrive quoting 9 yr through 13 yr beside the size chart. Separate the conversations. Size is the tape and the scale. Years are a different page. AKC stature and weight on the German Shepherd gives the male line and the female line to copy onto the growth card. Crate length has to include a tail that reaches toward the hock, measured for the adult size expected, or the dog spends nights curled against the door.

German Shepherd adults typically weigh 50-90 lbs and live 9-13 yrs; the practical breed-specific considerations are the kind worth knowing going in, not figuring out later. The following sections address key owner concerns.

Day-to-day care gets easier once the routine matches what the breed was bred for. Plan German Shepherds care around a large body size, heavy shedding, and the breed's documented predisposition toward hip dysplasia and degenerative myelopathy.

Male vs Female Size

The male line on the card says height 24 to 26 in with a weight of 65 lb through 90 lb. The female line says height 22 to 24 in with a weight of 50 lb through 70 lb. The outer weight band mutter when someone says "medium" is 50 lb through 90 lb, which is a huge spread and a reason to meet the parents. A 50 pound female and a 90 pound male do not take the same crate, the same car seat, or the same amount of food. Height overlaps in the middle: a tall female at 24 in and a short male at 24 inches can stand eye to eye and still be built differently through the chest.

Factors Affecting Size

Many experienced German Shepherd owners recommend dog sports like agility, flyball, or nosework to channel their energy productively.

The connection between enrichment and behavior is well-documented in veterinary behavioral science. A German Shepherd without adequate mental engagement will find ways to occupy itself — and owners rarely appreciate the results. Invest in variety: rotate toys on a weekly cycle, introduce new textures and objects, and provide opportunities for species-appropriate problem-solving.

When They Stop Growing

Stability in daily routine is particularly important during transitions: new homes, new family members, or changes in the owner's schedule. During these periods, maintaining as much consistency as possible in feeding, exercise, and sleep patterns supports adaptation. Set up regular times for meals, activity, grooming, and rest. High-energy German Shepherds especially benefit from knowing when their exercise time is coming — it helps them settle during calmer periods.
