Nutritional Needs of Bichon Frises

As a small non-sporting breed with moderate energy levels, the Bichon Frise has specific nutritional requirements that differ from other dogs. Understanding these needs is key to keeping your Bichon Frise healthy throughout their 14-15 yrs lifespan.

Bichon Frises typically weigh 12-18 lbs and need approximately 400–800 calories per day, depending on age, activity level, and metabolism. With moderate energy levels, most Bichon Frises do well on standard feeding guidelines for their size.

Known Health Risks: Genetic screening data shows Bichon Frises have elevated rates of allergies, bladder stones, luxating patella. Statistics about breed risk do not forecast any single pet's future. They simply justify attentive, breed-aware veterinary care that catches issues early if and when they arise.

Health-Specific Diet Considerations

Bichon Frises are prone to several health conditions that can be managed or prevented through proper nutrition.

  • Bladder Stones: Look for foods with nutrients that support bladder stones management

Best Protein Sources for Bichon Frises

  • Chicken and turkey: Lean, easily digestible proteins ideal for most Bichon Frises
  • Novel proteins (venison, duck, rabbit): Excellent for Bichon Frises with food allergies

Foods to Avoid

Never feed your Bichon Frise these dangerous foods.

  • Macadamia nuts and alcohol
  • Cooked bones (splintering risk)

Supplements Worth Considering

Based on Bichon Frise-specific health concerns, these supplements may benefit your dog.

  • Glucosamine: Supports joint health as your Bichon Frise ages

Wet Food vs Dry Food for Bichon Frises

Both wet and dry food have advantages for Bichon Frises.

  • Mixed feeding: Many Bichon Frise owners find success combining both for balanced nutrition and variety

Feeding Mistakes to Avoid

Common feeding errors that Bichon Frise owners make include:

  • Overuse of treats: Treats should make up no more than 10% of your Bichon Frise's daily caloric intake. Many training treats are high in calories, so adjust meal portions accordingly.
  • Ignoring body condition: Use the body condition score (BCS) system to assess your Bichon Frise's weight rather than relying solely on scale numbers. You should be able to feel the ribs without pressing hard.

Age-Specific Nutrition Considerations

Your Bichon Frise's nutritional needs change significantly throughout their life.

Adult stage (1-7 years): Maintain a consistent feeding routine with measured portions. Monitor weight monthly and adjust food amounts based on activity level, seasonal changes, and body condition. Adult Bichon Frises benefit from a protein content of 22-30%.

Senior stage (7+ years): Older Bichon Frises may need fewer calories but higher-quality protein to maintain muscle mass. Senior formulas often include joint-supporting nutrients like glucosamine and chondroitin, plus antioxidants for cognitive health. Watch for changes in appetite.

Common Questions

How much should I feed my Bichon Frise?

Adult Bichon Frises typically need 0.5–1.5 cups of high-quality food per day, split into two meals. Adjust based on your dog's activity level, age, and body condition score.

What is the best food brand for Bichon Frises?

Look for foods that list real meat as the first ingredient, meet AAFCO standards, and address Bichon Frise-specific health needs like allergies. Brands offering small breed-specific formulas are often a good choice.
