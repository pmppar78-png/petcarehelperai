Macaw Care Guide

Comprehensive macaw care covering housing requirements, diet, socialization, health issues, and the commitment of owning a large parrot.

Housing and Enclosure

Macaws are giant parrots, and the cage is architecture. A medium macaw such as a military or a severe macaw needs at least something in the range of 36 inches deep, 48 inches wide, and 60 inches high, with heavier bar stock and spacing around 1 to 1.5 inches. Large macaws, including blue-and-gold, scarlet, and green-wing, need still more room, often a flight the size of a small room or an outdoor aviary with a sheltered indoor area. Mini macaws such as a Hahn's or a noble macaw are smaller and still too strong for a cockatiel cage. Measure the bird you intend to keep, then buy the cage for the adult wingspan.

Bars have to be heavy enough that a macaw cannot bend them, and toys have to be stainless or very thick hardwood. A macaw will destroy a "large parrot" toy sold for an African grey in an afternoon, and the broken clip becomes a swallowed foreign body. Perches should be thick natural branches, replaced as they are chewed, so the feet have different diameters. The cage stays out of the kitchen. Nonstick fumes, self-cleaning ovens, and aerosol sprays kill macaws. The noise will fill the rest of the house anyway. Scarlets and blue-and-golds call at a volume that neighbors hear. If your housing cannot absorb that sound, choose a different parrot.

Out-of-cage time is daily and supervised. A macaw on top of a cage, with a play stand and a foraging project, is the normal picture. Ceiling fans, open doors, and other pets are the injuries. A bird light can help day length indoors. Dietary fat and calcium are nutrition questions for an avian veterinarian. A reptile UVB-and-dusting routine is the wrong manual for a macaw, and a cuttlebone sized for a budgie will be a toothpick this bird ignores.

Diet and Nutrition

Pellets formulated for large parrots can be about half to two-thirds of the diet, with the rest made of vegetables, fruit, and nuts. Macaws use more fat than a budgie or a cockatiel. Unsalted nuts, including almonds, walnuts, and the palm nuts some species are built to crack, belong in the bowl in measured amounts. A cup of nuts on top of a seed mix is too much, and a fat-free salad with no nuts is too little for many macaws. Vegetables every day: peppers, squash, leafy greens, and beans cooked plain. Fruit is welcome and still sugary.

Seed-only diets fail macaws the way they fail other parrots, with vitamin gaps and fatty liver, even though the bird looks busy shelling sunflower. Convert with an avian veterinarian's advice if the bird is older and stubborn. Fresh water in a bowl that can survive a dunking. Foraging is mandatory. Whole nuts in the shell, a durable puzzle, and food scattered through a destroyable box give the beak a job on something you can replace. Pull spoiled produce. A macaw will keep eating a fermented mango out of stubbornness.

Weight belongs on a scale that can handle a kilogram-plus bird, recorded monthly. A macaw can lose muscle under the feathers while still cracking nuts. Chips, chocolate, avocado, caffeine, and alcohol stay out. People food at the table teaches a bird to lunge at every plate, and the lunge of a macaw is a hospital visit.

Health and Veterinary Care

Proventricular dilatation disease, psittacine beak and feather disease, feather destruction, and wasting syndromes are the serious macaw problems. Undigested food in the droppings, weight loss, and a personality change need an avian veterinarian. A new seed brand leaves the disease untouched. Feather plucking has medical and social causes. A macaw left alone in a back room will sometimes destroy its chest feathers, and a medical workup still comes first so you do not train a sick bird to "just stop." Atherosclerosis and reproductive disease show up in middle-aged birds on fatty, bored diets.

Annual exams are the floor. Many macaw owners schedule bloodwork because these birds hide pain. Find an avian veterinarian with equipment for a large parrot, including anesthesia experience, before you need a broken blood feather or a bite wound on a family member. Macaw bites are crushing. Training is a health practice. A bird that steps up, goes to a station, and accepts a towel is a bird you can help. A bird that no one can handle will suffer longer in any emergency.

Quarantine and testing matter if you already keep parrots. PBFD and PDD are reasons to know the source of a baby macaw and to keep papers. Lifespan runs for decades, often 40 to 60 years or longer depending on species and care. The veterinary plan has to imagine that timeline.

The Commitment

  • Confirm the adult size and the decibel level of the exact species before you visit a breeder or a rescue.
  • Install a cage you cannot bend by hand, with perches and toys rated for macaws.
  • Feed pellets, vegetables, and a measured nut portion, and make the bird work for part of it.
  • Schedule daily social time. A macaw is a flock animal with a voice that will collect the flock if you hide.
  • Put an avian veterinarian and a successor caregiver in writing.

A well-matched macaw plays hard, cracks a nut, and talks or calls and then settles. A silent bird on the cage floor, a sudden plume of blood from a broken feather, or a chest going bald in a week needs help the same day. These are extraordinary birds and a poor impulse purchase. The cage, the noise, and the decades are the actual decision.

Daily Work for a Large Beak

A macaw needs a job before noon. Set out a whole nut in the shell, a hardwood toy you expect to lose, and a vegetable skewer, then leave the room long enough for the bird to work. Come back for training while the energy is still useful: step-up, target, and a station on the cage top. End the session while the bird is still willing. A macaw that learns screaming brings you faster than stepping up will use that lesson for years.

Shower or mist on a schedule the bird accepts. Many macaws open their wings under a lukewarm spray and preen for an hour afterward. Skip the spray on a cold day in a draft. Dry the room. A hair dryer set hot is a burn risk on wet feathers. Check the feet when you change a chewed perch. A flat, pink spot means the diameter was wrong or the bird stood in one place too long. Replace the branch with a thicker, uneven limb and mention the foot at the next avian visit.

Decide where the bird goes if you travel. A macaw is a poor candidate for a last-minute pet sitter who has only met cockatiels. Write the diet, the veterinarian's number, and the foods that are forbidden, and introduce the sitter while you are still home. The cage, the noise, and the successor are one decision. A bird this large and this long-lived deserves that paperwork in the first month, while the novelty is still doing some of the work.

Quick Answers

Are macaws good for first-time bird owners?

No for most households. Macaws need a very large, heavy-gauge cage, a loud-tolerant home, a pellet-and-nut diet, daily training, and an avian veterinarian, for a lifespan that can run for decades.

How much does macaw care cost?

The cage and stand are often the price of a major appliance, and stainless toys plus an avian exam add more before the first year ends. Food, replacement wood, and veterinary care continue for as long as the bird lives, which may be the rest of your life.
