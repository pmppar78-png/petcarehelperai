Best Food for Old English Sheepdog: Diet & Nutrition Guide

Complete guide to the best dog food for Old English Sheepdogs based on their large size, moderate energy level, and health needs including hip dysplasia.

Nutritional Needs of Old English Sheepdogs

As a large herding breed with moderate energy levels, the Old English Sheepdog has specific nutritional requirements that differ from other dogs. Understanding these needs is key to keeping your Old English Sheepdog healthy throughout their 10-12 yrs lifespan.

Old English Sheepdogs typically weigh 60-100 lbs and need approximately 1,400–2,200 calories per day, depending on age, activity level, and metabolism. With moderate energy levels, most Old English Sheepdogs do well on standard feeding guidelines for their size.

Health-Specific Diet Considerations

Old English Sheepdogs are prone to several health conditions that can be managed or prevented through proper nutrition:

  • Hip Dysplasia: Look for foods with glucosamine, chondroitin, and omega-3 fatty acids for joint support
  • Hypothyroidism: Look for foods with nutrients that support hypothyroidism management
  • Cataracts: Look for foods with nutrients that support cataracts management

Many veterinary nutritionists recommend large breed-specific formulas that contain controlled calcium and phosphorus levels to support proper skeletal development.

Best Protein Sources for Old English Sheepdogs

High-quality animal protein should be the first ingredient in any Old English Sheepdog food. Recommended protein sources include:

  • Chicken and turkey: Lean, easily digestible proteins ideal for most Old English Sheepdogs
  • Salmon and fish: Rich in omega-3 fatty acids for skin, coat, and joint health
  • Lamb: A good alternative for dogs with chicken sensitivities
  • Beef: High in iron and B vitamins, though some dogs may be sensitive

Foods to Avoid

Never feed your Old English Sheepdog these dangerous foods:

  • Chocolate, caffeine, and xylitol (extremely toxic)
  • Grapes and raisins (can cause kidney failure)
  • Onions, garlic, and chives (damage red blood cells)
  • Macadamia nuts and alcohol
  • Cooked bones (splintering risk)

Wet Food vs Dry Food for Old English Sheepdogs

Both wet and dry food have advantages for Old English Sheepdogs:

  • Dry kibble: Better for dental health, more economical, easier to measure portions
  • Wet food: Higher moisture content (important for hydration), more palatable, easier for seniors
  • Mixed feeding: Many Old English Sheepdog owners find success combining both for balanced nutrition and variety

Feeding Mistakes to Avoid

Common feeding errors that Old English Sheepdog owners make include:

  • Free-feeding: Leaving food out all day leads to overeating, especially in lower-energy breeds prone to weight gain. Scheduled meals help maintain healthy weight and allow you to monitor appetite changes.
  • Sudden diet changes: Switching foods abruptly causes digestive upset. Transition over 7-10 days by gradually mixing the new food with the old.
  • Overuse of treats: Treats should make up no more than 10% of your Old English Sheepdog's daily caloric intake. Many training treats are high in calories, so adjust meal portions accordingly.
  • Ignoring body condition: Use the body condition score (BCS) system to assess your Old English Sheepdog's weight rather than relying solely on scale numbers. You should be able to feel the ribs without pressing hard.
  • Feeding table scraps: Human food often contains ingredients dangerous to dogs. Even safe foods add untracked calories and can encourage begging behavior.

Age-Specific Nutrition Considerations

Your Old English Sheepdog's nutritional needs change significantly throughout their life:

Puppy stage (0-12 months): Large breed puppy formulas with controlled calcium and phosphorus are essential to prevent rapid growth that stresses developing joints. DHA supports brain development during this critical period.

Adult stage (1-7 years): Maintain a consistent feeding routine with measured portions. Monitor weight monthly and adjust food amounts based on activity level, seasonal changes, and body condition. Adult Old English Sheepdogs benefit from a protein content of 22-30%.

Senior stage (7+ years): Older Old English Sheepdogs may need fewer calories but higher-quality protein to maintain muscle mass. Senior formulas often include joint-supporting nutrients like glucosamine and chondroitin, plus antioxidants for cognitive health. Watch for changes in appetite that may signal underlying health issues.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I feed my Old English Sheepdog?

Adult Old English Sheepdogs typically need 3–5 cups of high-quality food per day, split into two meals. Adjust based on your dog's activity level, age, and body condition score.

What is the best food brand for Old English Sheepdogs?

Look for foods that list real meat as the first ingredient, meet AAFCO standards, and address Old English Sheepdog-specific health needs like hip dysplasia. Brands offering large breed-specific formulas are often a good choice.

Should I feed my Old English Sheepdog grain-free food?

Unless your Old English Sheepdog has a diagnosed grain allergy, grain-inclusive foods are generally recommended. The FDA has investigated a potential link between grain-free diets and heart disease (DCM) in dogs. Consult your veterinarian before choosing grain-free.
