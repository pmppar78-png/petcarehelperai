    A change in the box is a medical sign, and the Cornell Feline Health Center material on feline lower urinary tract disease is the reading that explains straining, blood, and a male cat who produces no urine. AAFP and AAHA feline life stage guidelines explain why urine volume is rechecked with bloodwork as cats age.

    A self-cleaning litter box can log visits and still hide a change in urine volume. Adult cats are examined about once a year, and cats from about 10 years old are often examined every six months so kidney disease and diabetes show up on bloodwork before the waste drawer looks different. Kittens still need a clinic visit every 3 to 4 weeks until about 16 weeks for vaccines. A male cat straining over an empty drawer needs a clinic the same day, because a urethral blockage can kill within a day or two.

    An automatic box is a one-time equipment purchase, and it does not remove the cost of a blocked cat. A male cat who strains and produces no urine needs an emergency visit the same day. Skipping that visit to save the fee is how a blockage becomes a longer hospitalization. The box log is useful only if someone still looks at it.
