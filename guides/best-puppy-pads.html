Puppy Pad Buying Guide

Types of Puppy Pads

  • Disposable: Convenient, single-use. Most common choice. Various absorbency levels available.
  • Washable: Reusable fabric pads. Eco-friendly and cost-effective long-term. Require laundry.
  • Real Grass: Hydroponically grown grass. Natural feel helps outdoor transition. Subscription-based.
  • Synthetic Grass: Reusable artificial turf systems. Long-term solution with waste trays.

Key Features to Consider

  • Size: Match to your dog's size and aim accuracy. Larger is better for beginners.
  • Absorbency: More layers = more absorbency. Heavy wetters need premium options.
  • Leak-Proof Backing: Essential to protect floors. Look for sealed edges.
  • Attractant: Pheromone scents encourage use. Helpful for training but not always effective.
  • Odor Control: Carbon or baking soda helps neutralize smells.

Potty Training Tips

Place pads in consistent locations. Praise immediately when your puppy uses the pad. Clean accidents with enzymatic cleaner to remove scent markers. Gradually move pads toward the door if transitioning to outdoor potty. For puppies, expect to use pads every 1-2 hours initially.

Questions Owners Ask

When can I stop using puppy pads?

Most puppies can transition fully to outdoor elimination by 4-6 months if given frequent outdoor access. Gradually move pads closer to the door, then outside. Some owners keep pads for emergencies or apartment living. Senior dogs may need pads permanently.

How often should I change puppy pads?

Change disposable pads after each use for hygiene, or at minimum once daily. Some premium pads can handle 2-3 uses. For washable pads, wash every 1-3 days depending on use. Never leave soiled pads out longer than necessary.

My puppy chews or plays with the pads - what can I do?

Use a pad holder with a frame that covers edges. Try grass pads which are less tempting. Redirect with appropriate toys. Some puppies outgrow this behavior. If ingested, the gel material can be harmful - contact your vet.
