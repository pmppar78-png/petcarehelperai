Questions Owners Ask

Where can I learn more?

Prepare the cat before the baby arrives: a closed door, the sound of a recording, and a vertical place that is off the crib. CDC and pediatric guidance say to keep cats out of the crib. A cat that stops using the box or stops eating after the baby comes home needs a clinic, because stress and illness look alike.

How often should I take my pet to the vet?

The cat should be current on an exam before a baby comes home, including vaccines, parasites, and anything that would make handling less safe. Kittens in that house are seen every 3 to 4 weeks until about 16 weeks. Adult cats need about a yearly exam. Cats from about 10 years old are often seen every six months. A cat who starts urinating on baby laundry needs a urinalysis. Scratching or biting that escalates is a reason to talk with the veterinarian soon, with the litter box still available.

How can I save money on pet care?

The expensive outcome is a bite, a cat who starts urinating on baby laundry because the box was moved, or a urinary obstruction missed while the house is chaotic. Keep the litter boxes, and have the cat examined before the baby arrives so you are not doing vaccines and a workup in the first week. You do not need a room full of new cat furniture for the baby's sake. You need the box and the exam.
