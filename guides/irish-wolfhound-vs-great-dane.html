Personality Overview

The Irish Wolfhound is known for being a moderate-energy hound breed with a distinctive personality. Their unique blend of traits makes them well-suited for the right owner and lifestyle.

Weighing around 105-180 lbs and lifespan of 6-8 yrs, the Irish Wolfhound benefits from care tailored to its physical and behavioral profile. What follows is a practical breakdown of the key factors.

Irish Wolfhound vs Great Dane: Side-by-Side

Irish Wolfhound and Great Dane look superficially similar to new owners but differ in ways that matter for daily care. Great Dane is larger at 110-175 lbs, while Irish Wolfhound typically runs 105-180 lbs. That size gap shows up in feeding volume, crate size, vehicle space, and how much joint-stress management each dog needs over their lifetime.

Lifespan: Irish Wolfhound typically lives 6-8 yrs; Great Dane 7-10 yrs. Great Dane generally has the longer-term care window, which affects insurance math and the point at which senior diagnostics become the dominant cost line.

Health watchlists differ. Both breeds share concerns around heart disease, bloat. Irish Wolfhound carries additional risk for osteosarcoma. Great Dane is more notably predisposed to hip dysplasia. These aren’t guaranteed diagnoses — they’re the conditions responsible vets screen for, and they shape insurance underwriting more than most owners realize.

Which one fits your household?

If you have limited exercise time, a small yard, or regularly leave the dog alone for full workdays, weigh the Great Dane more heavily on the exercise axis. If joint-disease genetics are a concern, the health row above matters more than size alone. Talk to breed-specific rescue groups for both breeds before committing — the people rehoming these dogs see the real-world behavior, not the breed-club brochure.

The middle years of an Irish Wolfhound's life are when subtle health shifts begin to appear — slightly slower recovery after exercise, a preference for softer resting spots, or minor changes in appetite.
