Grooming Schedule

Practical Boston Terriers care is shaped by three things: small size, light shedding, and a known predisposition to brachycephalic syndrome and cataracts.

Nails stay short so the little jumps he loves land square. Ears stand open. Look, smell, and wipe only the surface can see. Eyes get a damp cotton sweep from the inner corner outward, especially when staining tracks down into the face fold. Teeth crowd behind the short muzzle. A small canine toothbrush across the canines feels clumsy and earns its place. The VCA care handout on the Boston Terrier is the sheet to hand people who assume a short coat means pockets stay full and the dog stays untouched.

Open the white chest after a walk. Grass stain hides in that bib, and a stained bib left damp overnight spreads trouble toward the folds. A quiet rinse at the sink beats a week of face-rubbing on the sofa. The used cotton goes in the trash where can see the job happened.

Boston Terriers have light shedding and require weekly brushing. Regular grooming sessions keep your Boston Terrier's coat healthy and help you bond with your dog.

Bathing

The jacket is short. The wrinkles are the chore that matters. Cotton rounds live by the sink for the fold over the nose and the folds beside the mouth. After a drink, after breakfast, and after any roll outdoors, dry those folds until the cotton comes away clean. A wet crease on a brachycephalic face is how the itch gets started. The short muzzle parks that crease close to the eyes and the teeth, so the hand stays light and quit the moment he pulls away.

Black-and-white hair sheds short strands and picks up skin oil. A hound glove, or a cloth that is barely damp, handles the shine on the black and the mud on the white. Baths stay uncommon.
