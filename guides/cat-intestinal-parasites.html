Intestinal Parasites in Cats

This page is educational reference material for preparing questions. A licensed veterinarian examines the animal, makes the diagnosis, and sets the treatment.

Common worms in cats including roundworms, tapeworms, hookworms, and coccidia. Covers symptoms, deworming protocols, and indoor cat prevention.

Different worms, different routes into the cat

Intestinal parasites in cats are a set of organisms that happen to share a fecal sample. Roundworms, hookworms, tapeworms, coccidia, Giardia, and Tritrichomonas foetus do not respond to one kitchen remedy, and a cat can carry more than one at a time. Toxocara cati, the common roundworm, reaches kittens through the mother's milk, and it reaches hunters through prey and through eggs that have sat in soil. A pot-bellied kitten with a dull coat and sometimes a worm in the vomit is the textbook picture. Adult indoor cats can still carry a few. Hookworms, including Ancylostoma species, attach to the small intestine and are less famous in cats than in puppies, yet they still cause diarrhea, and some species are the ones that migrate in human skin. People pick those larvae up from contaminated soil, which is why a sandbox should be covered and why a garden used as a litter area is a poor idea.

Tapeworms arrive by a different door. Dipylidium caninum comes from swallowing a flea that carries the larval tapeworm. Owners see rice-like segments on the stool, on the fur under the tail, or on a favorite blanket. Those segments crawl when fresh. Taenia tapeworms come from hunting rodents. A fecal flotation often misses tapeworms, so the segment you saw is better evidence than a single negative float. Coccidia, usually Cystoisospora, cause diarrhea in kittens and in crowded groups. Giardia is a protozoan that sheds intermittently and needs a test aimed at it, an antigen test or a proper fecal method. Tritrichomonas foetus causes chronic large-bowel diarrhea, often a cow-pat consistency, in young cats from catteries and shelters. A drug plan aimed at roundworms leaves it in place, and a Giardia treatment often fails against it too. The diagnosis is a PCR the veterinarian sends, because a routine float was never designed to find it.

What the fecal test can and cannot say

The Companion Animal Parasite Council recommends fecal flotation for roundworms, hookworms, and coccidia, plus a separate Giardia test when diarrhea is on the table. Tapeworm segments are a visual diagnosis. One negative fecal does not exclude parasites in a cat who still has diarrhea, because shedding pauses. Bloodwork does not name these worms. A kitten with diarrhea still deserves a float even when an antigen snap for a virus was already run. Bring a fresh sample, the same day if you can, in a clean container. A sample that sat in a warm car all afternoon is a worse test. If the household has several cats and a shared box, the veterinarian may ask you to confine one cat overnight so the sample belongs to the sick individual.

Toxoplasma gondii is the parasite people worry about during pregnancy. Cats usually shed oocysts for a short window after a first infection, often after hunting, and a random fecal may miss that window entirely. The larger human exposures in many households are undercooked meat and soil, which a physician can discuss. Practical cat steps are still worth taking: keep the cat indoors and away from prey, scoop the box daily because oocysts need time to become infectious, and let someone else scoop if you are pregnant. A veterinarian can explain your cat's actual risk. Do not start a human antibiotic because a friend had toxoplasmosis.

Signs worth acting on

  • A pot belly, poor weight gain, or a worm in vomit or stool in a kitten.
  • Rice segments on the rear or on bedding, which means a tapeworm and usually a flea problem.
  • Diarrhea that is watery, mucous, or bloody, especially in a newly adopted cat.
  • A young cat with chronic soft stool that has already "been dewormed" once at a shelter.
  • Visible worms in a cat who also has fleas, anemia, or a dull coat.

Kittens can fade quickly

A kitten with diarrhea who stops eating, becomes cold, or looks pale should be seen the same day. Heavy worm burdens and coccidia dehydrate small bodies. Do not use a dog dewormer dose you found online, and do not give a cattle or horse product. The safe drug, the amount, and the interval are different for a two-pound kitten. Bring the adoption paperwork that lists what was already given so the veterinarian does not stack the same drug.

Treatment follows the organism

Roundworms and hookworms in kittens need repeated treatments. Larvae migrating in the body are missed by a single dose, which is why a veterinarian spaces treatments through early kittenhood and then sets a prevention plan. The drug and the calendar are written for that kitten's weight. Adults who hunt or who live with children may stay on a regular deworming or a monthly product the veterinarian prefers, plus fecal checks. Tapeworms need a drug that actually kills tapeworms, and they return if fleas are still in the house or if the cat keeps hunting. Flea control for every pet, plus washing the bedding, is part of Dipylidium treatment. Coccidia need their own medication. Giardia treatment is a veterinary choice of drug plus serious litter-box hygiene: scoop often, wash the box, and keep the cat's rear clean so the cat does not swallow cysts groomed off the fur. Tritrichomonas is the organism that punishes a guess. A roundworm dewormer leaves it in place, and the drug a veterinarian may select for it has a narrow safety margin. Do not buy that drug from an unregulated source or split a tablet meant for another species.

Recheck fecals after treatment when the veterinarian asks, especially if diarrhea continues. A cat who is still sick may have inflammatory bowel disease, a food intolerance, or a second parasite the first test missed. Deworming forever without a diagnosis is how Tritrichomonas and diet problems get a six-month delay. Indoor cats who never hunt and never meet fleas have a lower risk, and they still deserve a fecal when diarrhea starts. Raw diets raise the chance of Salmonella and of parasites. That is a reason to tell the veterinarian exactly what the cat eats.

Keeping the next infection out

Cover sandboxes, pick up feces from the yard, control fleas, and keep cats indoors if you want the smallest parasite load. Wash hands after the litter box. New cats and kittens should be tested and treated before they share a box with a resident who is clear. A shelter "already dewormed" note is a start, and it is a weak promise that coccidia, Giardia, and migrating roundworm larvae are finished. Ask your veterinarian where this cat should be in a prevention calendar six months from now. Children who play in soil should use ordinary handwashing. That advice belongs next to the pediatrician, and the cat's deworming belongs next to the veterinary record.

What parasite care costs

A fecal flotation and a Giardia test are small fees next to a week of diarrhea in a kitten who then needs fluids. The dewormer itself is often inexpensive. PCR for Tritrichomonas costs more and saves months of the wrong drug. Flea control for a multi-pet home is a recurring cost and the actual cure for flea tapeworms. Ask the clinic to price the fecal, the medication, and a recheck sample as one plan so you know when to bring the second stool.

Questions about worms and protozoa

Why did my cat get tapeworms after a normal deworming?

Many routine dewormers used for roundworms do not kill tapeworms. Dipylidium also returns as long as the cat swallows fleas, and Taenia returns if the cat keeps catching rodents. Tell the veterinarian you saw rice segments so the drug matches the worm, and put every pet in the home on flea control. A negative fecal flotation is common with tapeworms, so the segment you saved in a bag is useful evidence.

Does one deworming cover a new kitten?

One dose misses roundworm larvae that are still migrating and will reach the intestine later. Veterinarians repeat treatments through kittenhood and then decide on a maintenance plan. Coccidia and Giardia need different drugs from the roundworm dose. Bring any shelter record so those products are counted. A kitten who is cold, pale, or no longer eating should be seen the same day, because diarrhea dehydrates a small body fast.

Can indoor cats still carry parasites?

Yes. Fleas get inside, mice get inside, and a new kitten can bring coccidia or Giardia to a shared box. A cat who has always lived indoors and never hunts has a lower risk, and a fecal is still the right test when stool changes. Tritrichomonas in particular travels with young cats from group housing and then looks like a food problem for months. Ask for the test that fits the history you actually have.
