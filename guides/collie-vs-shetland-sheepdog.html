Collie vs Shetland Sheepdog: Breed Comparison

Collie vs Shetland Sheepdog — detailed comparison of size, temperament, exercise needs, health, and costs to help you choose the right breed.

Personality Overview

The Collie is known for being a moderate-energy herding breed with a distinctive personality. Herding breeds are intelligent, alert, and may try to herd family members, especially children.

Weighing around 50-75 lbs and lifespan of 12-14 yrs, the Collie needs care that accounts for its particular breed traits. Here's what the evidence and breed data show.

With Family Members

Individual variation exists within every breed, but documented breed traits provide a solid foundation for care planning. Collies with moderate energy levels strike a good balance between activity and relaxation.

  • Size: large (50-75 lbs)
  • Energy Level: Moderate
  • Shedding: Heavy
  • Common Health Issues: Collie Eye Anomaly, Hip Dysplasia, Bloat
  • Lifespan: 12-14 yrs

With Other Pets

Knowledge of breed-level risks helps you prioritize, but individual monitoring drives the most effective care decisions.. Collies have particular requirements based on their large size, heavy shedding level, and genetic predispositions to collie eye anomaly and hip dysplasia.

Preventive veterinary care, following AAHA guidelines of annual exams for adults and biannual exams for seniors, enables earlier detection of breed-related conditions. Given the breed's health tendencies, proactive screening is important for Collie.

Energy & Activity

The key to a happy, healthy Collie is matching your care approach to their breed characteristics. Lack of physical activity affects behavior before it affects weight — restlessness and attention-seeking often precede visible fitness changes.

  • Provide 30–60 minutes of daily exercise appropriate to their energy level
  • Feed a high-quality diet formulated for large breed dogs (1,400–2,200 calories/day)
  • Maintain a daily brushing grooming routine
  • Schedule breed-appropriate health screenings for collie eye anomaly
  • Invest in pet insurance early to cover breed-specific conditions

Intelligence & Trainability

Several breed-specific considerations deserve attention beyond routine care protocols. As a herding breed, the Collie has instincts and behaviors shaped by centuries of selective breeding for specific tasks.

Many experienced Collie owners recommend a balanced mix of physical activities and brain games.

The connection between enrichment and behavior is well-documented in veterinary behavioral science. A Collie without adequate mental engagement will find ways to occupy itself — and owners rarely appreciate the results. Invest in variety: rotate toys on a weekly cycle, introduce new textures and objects, and provide opportunities for species-appropriate problem-solving.

Guarding Instincts

Breed-aware care means adjusting your monitoring based on known risks — not waiting for symptoms that may indicate advanced disease. Watch for early signs of collie eye anomaly, maintain regular veterinary visits, and keep your dog at a healthy weight — excess weight worsens most of the conditions Collies are prone to.

The single most impactful thing owners can do for lifespan: maintain healthy body weight and stay current on preventive veterinary care..

Consistent daily structure — including predictable meal times, exercise, and rest periods — reduces anxiety and supports behavioral stability. Set up regular times for meals, activity, grooming, and rest. Even moderate-energy breeds thrive with predictable schedules.

Collies should receive breed-specific screening for collie eye anomaly starting at 1-2 years of age, as large breeds develop structural issues early. Early detection significantly improves treatment outcomes and quality of life.

Cost of Collie Ownership

An honest cost assessment prevents financial surprises that can compromise care. Here is what to budget for Collie ownership:

  • Annual food costs: $600–$1,200 for high-quality dog food
  • Veterinary care: $300–$700 annually for routine visits, plus potential emergency costs
  • Grooming: $65–100 per professional session (daily brushing home grooming recommended)
  • Pet insurance: $50–80/month for comprehensive coverage
  • Supplies and toys: $200–$500 annually for bedding, toys, leashes, and other essentials
