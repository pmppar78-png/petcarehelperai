Collie vs Shetland Sheepdog: Side-by-Side

Collie and Shetland Sheepdog look superficially similar to new owners but differ in ways that matter for daily care. Collie is larger at 50-75 lbs, while Shetland Sheepdog typically runs 15-25 lbs. That size gap shows up in feeding volume, crate size, vehicle space, and how much joint-stress management each dog needs over their lifetime.

Energy level is the practical differentiator for most households: Collie is classified as moderate-energy and Shetland Sheepdog as high-energy. Shetland Sheepdog needs structured daily outlets — not just a walk around the block — or it will create its own outlet, often destructively. The lower-energy option is easier to fit around desk jobs and short walks without compromising welfare.

Health watchlists differ. Both breeds share concerns around collie eye anomaly, hip dysplasia. Collie carries additional risk for bloat. Shetland Sheepdog is more notably predisposed to thyroid disease. These aren’t guaranteed diagnoses — they’re the conditions responsible vets screen for, and they shape insurance underwriting more than most owners realize.
