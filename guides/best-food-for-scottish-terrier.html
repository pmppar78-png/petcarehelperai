Nutritional Needs of Scottish Terriers

As a small terrier breed with moderate energy levels, the Scottish Terrier has specific nutritional requirements that differ from other dogs. Understanding these needs is key to keeping your Scottish Terrier healthy throughout their 12 yrs lifespan.

Scottish Terriers typically weigh 18-22 lbs and need approximately 400–800 calories per day, depending on age, activity level, and metabolism. With moderate energy levels, most Scottish Terriers do well on standard feeding guidelines for their size.

Genetic Health Considerations: The Scottish Terrier breed has documented susceptibility to bladder cancer, von Willebrand disease, Scotty cramp. Awareness of these predispositions is valuable for two reasons: it guides preventive screening decisions, and it helps you recognize early symptoms that might otherwise be overlooked.

Wet Food vs Dry Food for Scottish Terriers

Both wet and dry food have advantages for Scottish Terriers.

  • Mixed feeding: Many Scottish Terrier owners find success combining both for balanced nutrition and variety

Feeding Mistakes to Avoid

Common feeding errors that Scottish Terrier owners make include:

  • Overuse of treats: Treats should make up no more than 10% of your Scottish Terrier's daily caloric intake. Many training treats are high in calories, so adjust meal portions accordingly.
  • Ignoring body condition: Use the body condition score (BCS) system to assess your Scottish Terrier's weight rather than relying solely on scale numbers. You should be able to feel the ribs without pressing hard.

Age-Specific Nutrition Considerations

Your Scottish Terrier's nutritional needs change significantly throughout their life.

Adult stage (1-7 years): Maintain a consistent feeding routine with measured portions. Monitor weight monthly and adjust food amounts based on activity level, seasonal changes, and body condition. Adult Scottish Terriers benefit from a protein content of 22-30%.

Senior stage (7+ years): Older Scottish Terriers may need fewer calories but higher-quality protein to maintain muscle mass. Senior formulas often include joint-supporting nutrients like glucosamine and chondroitin, plus antioxidants for cognitive health. Watch for changes in appetite.

When the household genuinely understands this layer of Best Food For Scottish Terrier care, daily decisions stop feeling like emergencies. Because each pet is its own animal, treat any general guideline as a starting point and refine from there.

How much should I feed my Scottish Terrier?

Adult Scottish Terriers typically need 0.5–1.5 cups of high-quality food per day, split into two meals. Adjust based on your dog's activity level, age, and body condition score.

What is the best food brand for Scottish Terriers?

Look for foods that list real meat as the first ingredient, meet AAFCO standards, and address Scottish Terrier-specific health needs like bladder cancer. Brands offering small breed-specific formulas are often a good choice.

Should I feed my Scottish Terrier grain-free food?

Scottish Terrier dogs do not need a grain-free diet for transitional cell carcinoma of the bladder. The FDA investigation of diet-associated dilated cardiomyopathy collected reports of dogs eating grain-free and legume-rich foods and did not show that dropping grain protects a Scottish Terrier. A complete diet for a Scottish Terrier should meet AAFCO dog nutrient profiles, and a veterinarian should approve a switch when transitional cell carcinoma of the bladder is already part of that dog's care.
