Cat Overgrooming (Psychogenic Alopecia)

Understanding excessive grooming and hair loss in cats. Covers stress triggers, medical vs behavioral causes, treatment options, and environmental enrichment.

Bald skin is a clue, and psychogenic alopecia is the last label

Cats spend a large part of the day grooming. Overgrooming is the point where that habit removes hair, leaves stubble, and sometimes breaks the skin. The belly, the groin, the inner thighs, and the forelegs are the zones owners notice first, because those areas are easy for a tongue to reach in private. The hair shaft is broken off. A gentle tug does not pull a clump the way some hormonal diseases do in dogs. A trichogram, a few hairs under the microscope, shows frayed tips and confirms the cat is barbering. Psychogenic alopecia, the idea that stress alone is driving the licking, is what remains after fleas, mites, fungus, allergy, pain, and hormone disease have been looked for. It is a poor first guess. Cats with real skin disease are labeled "stressed" every week, and the itch continues.

Film the cat at night or when the house is quiet. Many owners swear the cat is never licking, then a camera shows a half hour of work on the same patch. An Elizabethan collar can be a short diagnostic trial if the veterinarian suggests it: hair that regrows under the collar was being removed by the tongue. The collar does not identify why. It only proves the tongue was the razor. Leaving a collar on for months while the itch or the pain continues is unkind, and some cats stop eating or drinking in a collar that was the wrong size.

Medical causes to clear before anyone says behavior

  • Fleas, including a single flea on a cat with flea allergy, producing miliary scabs and frantic licking.
  • Mites: Demodex gatoi is itchy and easy to miss on scrapings, and Notoedres, Cheyletiella, and Otodectes can drive face, neck, or body itch.
  • Ringworm, a fungus, which can look like barbered patches and needs a culture or PCR.
  • Food allergy or environmental allergy, often overlapping with the skin-allergy patterns described for eosinophilic plaques and miliary dermatitis.
  • Pain: arthritis in a spine or hip, bladder discomfort, anal-sac disease, dental pain, or feline hyperesthesia with rippling skin.
  • Hyperthyroidism in an older cat, which can include restlessness and excessive grooming along with weight loss.

The workup is ordinary dermatology, and it is worth doing in order. A flea comb and a real flea-control trial come first, because allergy to flea saliva is common and indoor cats still meet fleas. Skin scrapings, hair plucks, and tape preparations look for mites. A fungal culture or PCR looks for dermatophyte. Cytology of any scab or moist patch looks for bacteria and yeast that moved in after the tongue broke the skin. If the pattern still looks allergic, a diet trial with a prescription novel or hydrolyzed food, with no flavored medicines and no treats, is the veterinarian's tool. Blood tests sold as allergy panels do not prove that a cat's environment is the cause. Pain gets a physical exam, sometimes radiographs, and a urinalysis if the cat is also visiting the litter box more often. A cat who overgrooms the belly and also strains to urinate needs the bladder checked before a behavior drug is discussed.

Licking plus a sick cat

Open sores, a cat who stops eating, a swollen face, or any straining in the litter box should be seen promptly. A male cat who licks the penis and produces little or no urine may be blocked, which is an emergency described in the litter-box guide and needs an emergency hospital. Do not put essential oils, tea tree products, human hydrocortisone, or a dog's steroid cream on the bald skin. Cats groom the product off and swallow it. Several of those items are toxic in this species.

When the medical list is actually clear

If parasites are treated, the fungus culture is negative, the diet trial was strict, pain has been considered, and the cat is still barbering a symmetrical pattern, then a behavioral diagnosis becomes reasonable. Conflict with another cat, a move, a new baby, a schedule that leaves the cat alone and under-stimulated, and a litter box or feeding station that another cat guards are common backgrounds. The fix is the conflict and the environment: separate resources, vertical space, play that ends in a meal, and a truce between cats that a behavior-savvy veterinarian or a certified behavior consultant can design. Medication such as an anti-anxiety drug is sometimes added. The veterinarian chooses the drug and the dose after the medical workup, and it is a poor substitute for flea control that was never tried. Recheck the skin. A cat on a behavior drug who still has mites will not grow a normal coat.

Punishment, spraying with water, and yelling add fear and can increase grooming. So can a collar with a bitter spray that the cat then licks into a larger area of irritation. Track a weekly photo of the same patch in the same light so you and the veterinarian can see regrowth. Note whether the other animals in the home are itchy too, because a contagious mite or ringworm rarely stops at one cat. A dog who suddenly scratches or a second cat with a bald belly belongs in the same appointment. Hair cycles are slow. A month with no change may mean the cause is still in the house, and a month with stubble returning is progress.

What a fair workup costs

Flea control for every pet in the home is the cheapest meaningful step and the one most often skipped because nobody saw a flea. Cytology, scrapings, and a fungal test sit in the middle. A strict diet trial costs food for eight to twelve weeks, which the veterinarian sets, and it fails if you add treats. Behavior drugs and referral sit at the end of the sequence. Spending on a supplement while the cat still has fleas buys another bald month.

Questions about overgrooming

How do I know the cat is licking the hair out?

Barbered areas show short, broken stubble, and the cat may look guilty only because you walked in. A trichogram shows split or frayed hair tips. Video at night catches sessions owners miss. Hair that grows back while a veterinarian-fitted collar stops the tongue was being removed by grooming. You still need the reason, from fleas and mites through pain and allergy, before anyone calls it stress.

Can stress be the whole story?

Stress can keep a lick cycle going, and it can be the main driver once the medical list is negative. It is a weak explanation for the first visit. Flea allergy, Demodex gatoi, ringworm, food allergy, bladder pain, and hyperthyroidism all make cats lick until they are bald. Ask for that workup. A behavior drug chosen afterward can help a cat whose skin disease has truly been ruled out.

What should I put on the bald spots tonight?

Leave them free of oils, human creams, and dog products. Clean any open sore only if the veterinarian has told you what to use. An emergency visit is wiser than a home potion if the skin is raw, the cat is male and straining, or the face is swelling. Bring a photo of the patch and a note about every other pet in the house, including whether they are on flea control.
