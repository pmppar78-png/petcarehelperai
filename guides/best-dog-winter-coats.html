    Where can I learn more?

    Insulation is the coat. Ethylene glycol on a driveway is Merck Veterinary Manual's poisoning chapter, and a lick of antifreeze is an emergency in small amounts. Ice-melt burns on pads are a clinic visit. Neither problem is solved by a size chart.

    How often should I take my pet to the vet?

    A coat helps a thin or short-haired dog hold heat, and ice-melt on the paws is still a reason to look at the feet. Puppies are examined every 3 to 4 weeks until about 16 weeks, adults at least yearly, and many dogs from about 7 years, giant breeds earlier, about every six months. A limp after walking on ice should be seen. Antifreeze licked from a spill is an emergency in a very small amount. The coat does not change either of those timelines.
