Rottweiler vs Cane Corso: Side-by-Side

Rottweiler and Cane Corso look superficially similar to new owners but differ in ways that matter for daily care. Cane Corso is larger at 88-120 lbs, while Rottweiler typically runs 80-135 lbs. That size gap shows up in feeding volume, crate size, vehicle space, and how much joint-stress management each dog needs over their lifetime.

Both breeds share a moderate energy level, so the differentiator here is temperament, not exercise volume. Watch how each individual dog responds to training pressure, novelty, and time alone — that tells you more than the AKC group label.

Lifespan: Rottweiler typically lives 8-10 yrs; Cane Corso 9-12 yrs. Cane Corso generally has the longer-term care window, which affects insurance math and the point at which senior diagnostics become the dominant cost line.

Health watchlists differ. Both breeds share concerns around hip dysplasia. Rottweiler carries additional risk for osteosarcoma, heart disease. Cane Corso is more notably predisposed to bloat, cherry eye. These aren’t guaranteed diagnoses — they’re the conditions responsible vets screen for, and they shape insurance underwriting more than most owners realize.

Grooming effort is meaningfully different: Rottweiler sheds at a moderate level, Cane Corso at light. That drives brush frequency, vacuum load, and whether the coat tolerates a week between sessions or demands daily attention during peak seasons.
