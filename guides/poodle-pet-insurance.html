Poodle Pet Insurance Overview

Pet insurance for Poodles is particularly important given their predisposition to hip dysplasia, progressive retinal atrophy, bloat.

Poodle premiums on this page are dog premiums for hip dysplasia, progressive retinal atrophy, and gastric dilatation-volvulus. The standard-size dog is the one that bloats. Addison's disease is covered on the Standard Poodle page.

Breed-Specific Health Profile: Research identifies hip dysplasia, progressive retinal atrophy, bloat as conditions with higher prevalence in Poodles. These are population-level trends, not individual certainties. Discuss with your veterinarian which screening tests are recommended for your Poodle.

Best Time to Insure Your Poodle

Enroll your Poodle as early as possible — ideally as a puppy or kitten. Pre-existing conditions are never covered, so insuring before health issues develop is critical. Poodles are prone to hip dysplasia, which can develop at any point in their life.

How much does Poodle pet insurance cost?

Poodle insurance cost here means a dog premium, not a single size band. The NAPHIA 2025 US dog accident-and-illness average for 2024 is $62.44 a month ($749.29 a year), accident-only is $16.10 a month, compiled by Willis Towers Watson, and enrollment age, ZIP code, deductible, reimbursement percentage, and annual limit move the quote. Progressive retinal atrophy adds no surgical line item. Bloat does.

Is pet insurance worth it for a Poodle?

Progressive retinal atrophy in a Poodle is not a surgery. Gastric dilatation-volvulus is emergency surgery, mostly a risk in the standard-sized dog. Hip dysplasia may be medical or a hip replacement. Those three are not interchangeable.

What pre-existing conditions affect Poodle insurance?

Any condition diagnosed before enrollment is excluded. For Poodles, common pre-existing concerns include hip dysplasia and progressive retinal atrophy. Early enrollment is key.
