Managing Pet Allergies During Allergy Season

Just like humans, dogs and cats can suffer from seasonal allergies that cause significant discomfort. Understanding the types of allergies, recognizing symptoms, and knowing your treatment options can help you keep your pet comfortable throughout the year.

Types of Pet Allergies

Pet allergies generally fall into three main categories, each with distinct causes and treatment approaches.

Environmental Allergies (Atopy)

Environmental allergies, also called atopic dermatitis or atopy, occur when your pet's immune system overreacts to airborne allergens like pollen, mold spores, dust mites, and grasses. These allergies are often seasonal but can become year-round in pets with multiple sensitivities.

Food Allergies

Food allergies develop when the immune system reacts to specific proteins in your pet's diet. Common culprits include beef, chicken, dairy, wheat, and eggs. Unlike environmental allergies, food allergies cause consistent symptoms year-round regardless of season.

Flea Allergy Dermatitis (FAD)

Flea allergy dermatitis is an allergic reaction to flea saliva. Even a single flea bite can trigger intense itching and skin irritation in sensitive pets. This is one of the most common allergies in both dogs and cats.

Environmental Allergy Symptoms

Recognizing allergy symptoms early allows for faster relief and prevents secondary complications like skin infections.

Common Signs in Dogs

  • Excessive scratching, licking, or chewing at skin
  • Red, inflamed, or irritated skin
  • Recurrent ear infections
  • Paw licking and chewing (especially between toes)
  • Hot spots (acute moist dermatitis)
  • Hair loss or thinning coat
  • Watery or irritated eyes
  • Sneezing or nasal discharge
  • Rubbing face on carpet or furniture
  • Skin thickening or darkening in chronic cases

Common Signs in Cats

  • Excessive grooming leading to hair loss
  • Miliary dermatitis (small scabby bumps under fur)
  • Scratching at face, ears, and neck
  • Respiratory symptoms (sneezing, wheezing)
  • Watery eyes
  • Chin acne
  • Eosinophilic plaques or granulomas
  • Symmetrical hair loss on belly or legs

Common Environmental Allergens

Knowing which allergens affect your pet helps you take targeted prevention measures.

Pollen

Tree pollen (spring), grass pollen (late spring/summer), and weed pollen (late summer/fall) are major triggers. Different plants release pollen at different times, which is why some pets have symptoms only during certain seasons.

Grass

Direct contact with grass can cause contact allergies in addition to pollen-related reactions. Dogs that lie on grass or run through tall grass may develop belly rashes and itchy paws.

Mold Spores

Mold thrives in damp conditions and peaks during humid weather, rainy seasons, and fall when leaves decompose. Indoor mold from bathrooms, basements, and houseplants can cause year-round symptoms.

Dust Mites

These microscopic creatures live in bedding, carpets, and upholstery. While present year-round, they proliferate in warm, humid conditions and can worsen symptoms when heating systems circulate dust in winter.

Seasonal Allergy Timeline

Understanding when different allergens peak helps you prepare and protect your pet.

Spring (March - May)

  • Tree pollen season begins (oak, birch, cedar, maple)
  • Early grass growth starts releasing pollen
  • Mold spores increase with spring rains
  • Flea populations begin to grow

Summer (June - August)

  • Grass pollen peaks
  • Weed pollen begins (ragweed starts late summer)
  • High humidity increases mold growth
  • Peak flea and tick season
  • More outdoor time means more allergen exposure

Fall (September - November)

  • Ragweed and other weed pollen peaks
  • Mold spores increase from decomposing leaves
  • Dust mite allergens increase as heating begins
  • Flea populations remain active until first hard frost

At-Home Allergy Management

Many allergy symptoms can be reduced with consistent home care practices.

Wiping Paws After Walks

Pollen and allergens collect on your pet's paws during outdoor time. Wipe all four paws with a damp cloth or pet-safe wipe after every walk or outdoor session. Pay special attention to between the toes where allergens accumulate.

Bathing Frequency

Regular bathing removes allergens from your pet's coat and provides relief from itching. During peak allergy season, weekly baths may be beneficial. Use a gentle, hypoallergenic or oatmeal-based shampoo to avoid further irritation. Medicated shampoos containing chlorhexidine or ketoconazole can help if secondary infections develop.

Air Purifiers

A high-quality HEPA air purifier removes airborne allergens from your home. Place purifiers in rooms where your pet spends the most time, especially sleeping areas. Change filters regularly according to manufacturer recommendations.

Air Purifiers for Pet Allergies

Consider a HEPA air purifier designed for homes with pets. Look for models with activated carbon filters that also remove pet odors and dander. Popular options include HEPA air purifiers on Amazon.

Keeping Windows Closed

During high pollen days, keep windows closed and use air conditioning instead. Check local pollen forecasts and limit outdoor time during peak pollen hours (typically early morning and late afternoon).

Regular Washing of Bedding

Wash your pet's bedding weekly in hot water to remove accumulated allergens and dust mites. Use fragrance-free, hypoallergenic detergent. Consider covering pet beds with allergen-proof covers. Vacuum regularly using a vacuum with a HEPA filter, including areas where your pet sleeps and plays.

Diet Support for Allergies

Nutrition plays an important role in supporting skin health and managing allergy symptoms.

Omega Fatty Acids

Omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids support skin barrier function and have natural anti-inflammatory properties. Fish oil supplements are an excellent source of omega-3s. Look for supplements specifically formulated for pets with EPA and DHA.

Omega Fatty Acid Supplements

High-quality fish oil supplements can help support your pet's skin health. Options like Grizzly Salmon Oil or Nordic Naturals Omega-3 Pet are popular choices for dogs and cats.

Limited Ingredient Diets

If food allergies are suspected alongside environmental allergies, a limited ingredient diet can help identify triggers while reducing overall inflammatory burden on the immune system.

Hypoallergenic Pet Foods

Prescription and limited ingredient diets can help pets with food sensitivities. Consider options like Hill's Science Diet Sensitive Skin, Royal Canin Hypoallergenic, or Natural Balance Limited Ingredient.

Probiotics

A healthy gut microbiome supports immune function. Probiotic supplements formulated for pets may help modulate immune responses and support overall health in allergic pets.

When to See the Vet

While mild allergies can often be managed at home, certain situations require professional veterinary care.

See Your Vet If:

  • Symptoms are severe or significantly affecting quality of life
  • Home management isn't providing adequate relief
  • You notice signs of secondary infection (odor, discharge, hot spots)
  • Ear infections are recurring
  • Your pet is losing significant amounts of hair
  • Scratching is causing open wounds
  • Symptoms are new or suddenly worse
  • Your pet is also experiencing digestive issues
  • Over-the-counter antihistamines aren't helping

Online Vet Consultations

Not sure if your pet's symptoms warrant an in-person visit? Consider a virtual consultation with Pawp or Airvet to get expert guidance on whether your pet needs allergy testing or prescription treatment.

Allergy Testing Options

Identifying specific allergens allows for targeted treatment and avoidance strategies.

Intradermal Skin Testing

Considered the gold standard for environmental allergy testing, this involves injecting small amounts of allergens under the skin and observing reactions. It's typically performed by veterinary dermatologists and is most accurate for formulating immunotherapy.

Serum (Blood) Allergy Testing

A blood sample is analyzed for antibodies to various allergens. While less invasive than skin testing, results may be less specific. Still useful for identifying allergens and guiding immunotherapy.

Elimination Diet Trials

For suspected food allergies, an 8-12 week elimination diet using a novel protein or hydrolyzed diet is the only reliable way to diagnose food allergies. This requires strict adherence with no treats, table scraps, or flavored medications.

Treatment Options

Several prescription and over-the-counter options can help manage allergy symptoms.

Antihistamines

Over-the-counter antihistamines like Benadryl (diphenhydramine), Zyrtec (cetirizine), and Claritin (loratadine) may provide relief for mild allergies. Effectiveness varies between pets, so you may need to try different options. Always consult your vet for proper dosing and to ensure safety with your pet's other medications.

Apoquel (Oclacitinib)

This prescription medication targets specific itch pathways and provides rapid relief, often within 24 hours. It's FDA-approved for dogs and effective for both short-term flares and long-term management. Requires veterinary prescription and monitoring.

Cytopoint

An injectable treatment that neutralizes itch-causing signals. Each injection provides 4-8 weeks of relief in most dogs. It's a biological therapy with minimal side effects, making it suitable for long-term use. Administered by your veterinarian.

Immunotherapy (Allergy Shots or Drops)

Based on allergy testing results, a customized serum containing small amounts of your pet's specific allergens is administered over time to desensitize the immune system. Available as injections or sublingual drops. Takes 6-12 months to see full effect but offers the potential for long-term resolution rather than just symptom management.

Medicated Shampoos and Topicals

Prescription shampoos containing chlorhexidine, ketoconazole, or phytosphingosine help manage secondary bacterial or yeast infections. Mousse, sprays, and leave-on conditioners can provide additional relief between baths.

Corticosteroids

Prednisone and other steroids provide rapid, effective relief but are typically reserved for short-term use due to potential side effects with long-term administration. May be used for severe flares while other treatments take effect.

Food Allergies vs. Environmental Allergies

Distinguishing between these allergy types helps guide appropriate treatment.

Signs Suggesting Food Allergies

  • Year-round symptoms that don't change with seasons
  • Gastrointestinal symptoms (vomiting, diarrhea, gas) alongside skin issues
  • Symptoms began after a diet change
  • Ear infections are the primary symptom
  • Young age at symptom onset (food allergies can develop at any age but often appear earlier)
  • Poor response to medications that help environmental allergies

Signs Suggesting Environmental Allergies

  • Clear seasonal pattern to symptoms
  • Symptoms improve indoors or with air conditioning
  • Paws, armpits, and groin are primarily affected
  • Symptoms started between ages 1-3 (typical for atopy)
  • Good response to antihistamines or Apoquel/Cytopoint
  • No digestive symptoms

Note: Many pets have both food and environmental allergies, making diagnosis more challenging. Your vet may recommend addressing both simultaneously.

Flea Allergy Dermatitis

Flea allergy is an allergic reaction to proteins in flea saliva. Pets with flea allergy dermatitis (FAD) can experience severe itching from just one or two flea bites.

Recognizing Flea Allergy

  • Intense itching, especially at the base of the tail, lower back, and thighs
  • Hair loss in affected areas
  • Small red bumps or scabs
  • Hot spots (secondary infections from scratching)
  • You may not see fleas (they're eaten during grooming or jump off)
  • Flea dirt (black specks that turn red when wet) may be visible

Treatment and Prevention

The only effective treatment is strict, year-round flea prevention. A single flea can cause weeks of misery in an allergic pet.

Flea Prevention Products

Year-round flea prevention is essential for pets with flea allergies. Effective options include Bravecto (lasts 12 weeks), NexGard (monthly chewable), Simparica Trio, and Revolution Plus for cats. Ask your vet which product is best for your pet.

Environmental Flea Control

  • Treat ALL pets in the household, even if they don't go outside
  • Wash all pet bedding in hot water weekly
  • Vacuum frequently, especially in areas where pets sleep
  • Consider professional pest control for severe infestations
  • Treat yard and outdoor areas if your pet spends time outside

Year-Round Allergy Management

Allergies are typically a lifelong condition requiring ongoing management. These strategies help minimize flares throughout the year.

Create an Allergy Management Plan

  • Keep a symptom diary to identify patterns and triggers
  • Work with your vet to develop both maintenance and flare-up protocols
  • Have medications on hand before peak seasons
  • Schedule vet visits before allergy season for preventive care

Maintain Skin Barrier Health

  • Continue omega fatty acid supplementation year-round
  • Use gentle, moisturizing shampoos to avoid stripping natural oils
  • Consider ceramide-containing topical products
  • Address any skin infections promptly before they worsen

Reduce Environmental Allergen Load

  • Use HEPA air purifiers in main living areas
  • Replace HVAC filters regularly with HEPA-rated options
  • Keep humidity below 50% to discourage dust mites and mold
  • Vacuum and dust frequently
  • Consider hard flooring instead of carpet in pet areas

Maintain Year-Round Flea Prevention

Even in winter months, continue flea prevention. Fleas can survive indoors year-round, and a single infestation can take months to fully eliminate.

Regular Veterinary Check-ups

Schedule regular appointments to monitor your pet's allergies and adjust treatment as needed. Early intervention prevents minor symptoms from becoming major problems.
