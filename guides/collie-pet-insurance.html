Collie Pet Insurance Overview

Pet insurance for Collies is particularly important given their predisposition to collie eye anomaly, hip dysplasia, bloat.

Collie premiums are dog premiums. Collie eye anomaly, MDR1 drug sensitivity, and dermatomyositis are the breed diseases. Hip dysplasia is the joint claim.

Health Awareness: Predispositions seen in Collies include collie eye anomaly, hip dysplasia, bloat. Many individuals go their whole lives without expressing these conditions, but the ones that matter are usually more manageable when caught on a screening visit rather than during a crisis.

Best Time to Insure Your Collie

Enroll your Collie as early as possible — ideally as a puppy or kitten. Pre-existing conditions are never covered, so insuring before health issues develop is critical. Collies are prone to collie eye anomaly, which can develop as early as 1-2 years of age.

How much does Collie pet insurance cost?

NAPHIA's 2025 State of the Industry report, compiled by Willis Towers Watson from year-end 2024 premiums, puts US dog accident-and-illness coverage at $62.44 a month ($749.29 a year) and accident-only coverage at $16.10 a month. Enrollment age, ZIP code, deductible, reimbursement percentage, and annual limit set the quote. For a Collie, the claims that move the quote are collie eye anomaly, dermatomyositis, and MDR1 drug sensitivity.

Is pet insurance worth it for a Collie?

Collie eye anomaly is a congenital eye malformation without a curative operation. Dermatomyositis is immune-mediated skin and muscle disease treated medically. MDR1 is a list of drugs to avoid. Hip dysplasia, if clinical, is the claim that may reach a femoral head ostectomy or a hip replacement.

What pre-existing conditions affect Collie insurance?

Any condition diagnosed before enrollment is excluded. For Collies, common pre-existing concerns include collie eye anomaly and hip dysplasia. Early enrollment is key.
