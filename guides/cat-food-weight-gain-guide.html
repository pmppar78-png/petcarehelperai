Weight Gain in Underweight Cats

How to help an underweight cat gain weight safely. High-calorie foods, appetite stimulants, and when weight loss indicates illness.

Key Information

What You Need

Free-feeding a bowl of dry food is how many indoor cats put on fat. Scoop two meals and pick the dish up after twenty minutes. Switching to a richer food without shrinking the scoop is a common reason the waist vanishes between exams.

    Start with the problem this page is about: an underweight cat, higher-calorie food, and the chance that weight loss is illness. A veterinarian has to look for the cause before an appetite stimulant or a richer diet is useful. The cat guides and local clinic listings linked above are the next stop after that exam.

    A healthy adult cat is usually examined once a year. Kittens are seen more than once in the first year. Cats about seven and older are often examined twice a year. An underweight cat that is still losing weight should be seen now, not held for the next routine visit.

    Weigh the cat on the same scale each week so a slow loss shows up early. Ask the clinic for an estimate before blood tests or imaging. Skipping that exam to buy richer food often costs more once the illness behind the weight loss has gone further.

    Get Pet Care

    A thin cat that is otherwise bright can gain weight on extra meals of a complete food, with a gram target from the clinic. A cat that is thin and also vomiting, drinking more, or walking away from the bowl is a medical case first. Hyperthyroidism and kidney disease both strip weight. Weigh the cat weekly. Add calories with the usual food before you add a supplement.

    Treats and the second cat's leftovers belong in the day's calorie count.
