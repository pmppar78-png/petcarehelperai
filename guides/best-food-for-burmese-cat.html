Nutritional Needs of Burmeses

Burmeses typically weigh 8-12 lbs and need approximately 200–350 calories per day, depending on age, activity level, and metabolism. Given their high energy levels, active Burmeses may need 20-30% more calories than the average cat of their size.

Known Health Risks: Genetic screening data shows Burmeses have elevated rates of diabetes, HCM, head defect.

Health-Specific Diet Considerations

Burmeses are prone to several health conditions that can be managed or prevented through proper nutrition.

  • Diabetes: Look for foods with low-glycemic, high-protein formulas for blood sugar management
  • Head Defect: Look for foods with nutrients that support head defect management

Best Protein Sources for Burmeses

  • Chicken and turkey: Lean, easily digestible proteins ideal for most Burmeses

Foods to Avoid

Wet Food vs Dry Food for Burmeses

Both wet and dry food have advantages for Burmeses.

Feeding Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overuse of treats: Treats should make up no more than 10% of your Burmese's daily caloric intake.
  • Ignoring body condition: Use the body condition score (BCS) system to assess your Burmese's weight rather than relying solely on scale numbers.

Age-Specific Nutrition Considerations

Your Burmese's nutritional needs change significantly throughout their life.

Adult Burmeses benefit from a protein content of 22-30%.

Senior stage (7+ years): Older Burmeses may need fewer calories but higher-quality protein to maintain muscle mass.

Common Questions

Diabetes, potassium, and the heart

Diabetes is common in the Burmese. A diabetic cat eats a high-protein, low-carbohydrate wet food the veterinarian chooses, with calories set against the glucose curve. The breed also has genetic hypokalemic myopathy, a low-potassium muscle disease the veterinarian treats. Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy is reported in Burmese cats, and the screening test is an echocardiogram. A grain-free label does not set the insulin plan or thicken a normal heart wall into a healthy one.
