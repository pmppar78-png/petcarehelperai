Questions Owners Ask

Think of this as the knowledge layer that most pet owners skip and later wish they had started with. Small tweaks based on how your pet actually reacts usually beat rigid adherence to a template.

Where can I learn more?

AAHA weight-management guidelines and the pet's body-condition score, on a 9-point scale, are the tools. The clinic should set a goal weight and a calorie number. A rib check and a waist, felt monthly, tell you if the plan is working. The bag's feeding chart is a starting range for an intact, active adult. The clinic's calorie number overrides that chart for a heavy or neutered pet.

How often should I take my pet to the vet?

Body condition is scored at each clinic visit, so weight gain shows up within months. Puppies and kittens are weighed every 3 to 4 weeks until about 16 weeks. Adult dogs and cats need about one exam a year where the portion is compared with that score. Many senior dogs from about 7 years, giant breeds earlier, and senior cats from about 10 are seen every six months, often enough to bend a weight trend. Fast gain plus heavier drinking needs bloodwork. A weight-loss drug and its dose, if one is even appropriate, come from the veterinarian.

How can I save money on pet care?

The costly disease is the one extra weight brings: diabetes, cruciate tears, and earlier arthritis. Measure the cup. Cut the treats to a tenth of calories. A kitchen scale costs less than a weight-loss clinic package, and you can still ask the clinic for the calorie target.
