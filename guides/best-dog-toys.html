Types of Dog Toys

  • Chew Toys: Satisfy the natural urge to chew. Choose based on your dog's chewing intensity.
  • Fetch Toys: Balls, frisbees, and sticks for active outdoor play.
  • Puzzle/Interactive: Mental stimulation through treat-dispensing challenges.
  • Tug Toys: Great for bonding and energy release through interactive play.
  • Squeaky Toys: Trigger prey drive with satisfying sounds. Not for aggressive chewers.
  • Comfort Toys: Soft plush toys for carrying and cuddling.

Choosing by Chew Style

  • Light Chewers: Plush toys, soft rubber, rope toys
  • Average Chewers: KONG Classic, nylon bones, quality rubber
  • Power Chewers: Goughnuts, KONG Extreme, heavy-duty nylon
  • Destroyers: Goughnuts MAXX, solid rubber with guarantee

Toy Safety Tips

Always supervise play with new toys. Discard toys when they show significant wear, have loose parts, or can be broken into pieces. Choose appropriate sizes - toys shouldn't be small enough to swallow. Rotate toys to maintain interest and extend toy life.

How many toys does my dog need?

Most dogs do well with 3-5 toys available at a time, rotated weekly to maintain interest. Have a variety of types: one chew toy, one fetch toy, one puzzle toy, and one comfort/tug toy.

My dog destroys every toy - what should I get?

Look for toys specifically designed for "extreme" or "power" chewers. Goughnuts, KONG Extreme (black), and Nylabone DuraChew are good options. Avoid plush toys, thin rubber, and anything with small parts.

Are rope toys safe?

Rope toys are generally safe for supervised play and tug. However, if your dog shreds and swallows the fibers, they can cause intestinal blockages. Remove rope toys when frayed and never leave them for unsupervised chewing.
