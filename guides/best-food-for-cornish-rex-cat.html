Best Food for Cornish Rex Cats: Nutrition Guide

Top rated cat food for Cornish Rex cats. Diet recommendations for their short coat, high energy needs, and common concerns like HCM.

Nutritional Needs of Cornish Rexs

As a small cat breed with high energy levels, the Cornish Rex has specific nutritional requirements that differ from other cats. Understanding these needs is key to keeping your Cornish Rex healthy throughout their 11-15 yrs lifespan.

Cornish Rexs typically weigh 5-10 lbs and need approximately 400–800 calories per day, depending on age, activity level, and metabolism. Given their high energy levels, active Cornish Rexs may need 20-30% more calories than the average cat of their size.

Known Health Risks: Genetic screening data shows Cornish Rexs have elevated rates of HCM, luxating patella, hyperthyroidism. Breed-level risk is population-level information; individual outcomes vary widely. The practical payoff of breed-aware veterinary care is earlier detection in the cases where risk does materialize.

Health-Specific Diet Considerations

Cornish Rexs are prone to several health conditions that can be managed or prevented through proper nutrition.

  • Hyperthyroidism: Look for foods with nutrients that support hyperthyroidism management

Best Protein Sources for Cornish Rexs

  • Chicken and turkey: Lean, easily digestible proteins ideal for most Cornish Rexs

Foods to Avoid

Never feed your Cornish Rex these dangerous foods.

  • Macadamia nuts and alcohol
  • Cooked bones (splintering risk)

Supplements Worth Considering

Based on Cornish Rex-specific health concerns, these supplements may benefit your cat.

  • Glucosamine: Supports joint health as your Cornish Rex ages

Wet Food vs Dry Food for Cornish Rexs

Both wet and dry food have advantages for Cornish Rexs.

  • Mixed feeding: Many Cornish Rex owners find success combining both for balanced nutrition and variety

Feeding Mistakes to Avoid

Common feeding errors that Cornish Rex owners make include:

  • Overuse of treats: Treats should make up no more than 10% of your Cornish Rex's daily caloric intake. Many training treats are high in calories, so adjust meal portions accordingly.
  • Ignoring body condition: Use the body condition score (BCS) system to assess your Cornish Rex's weight rather than relying solely on scale numbers. You should be able to feel the ribs without pressing hard.

Age-Specific Nutrition Considerations

Your Cornish Rex's nutritional needs change significantly throughout their life.

Adult stage (1-7 years): Maintain a consistent feeding routine with measured portions. Monitor weight monthly and adjust food amounts based on activity level, seasonal changes, and body condition. Adult Cornish Rexs benefit from a protein content of 22-30%.

Senior stage (7+ years): Older Cornish Rexs may need fewer calories but higher-quality protein to maintain muscle mass. Senior formulas often include joint-supporting nutrients like glucosamine and chondroitin, plus antioxidants for cognitive health. Watch for changes in appetite.

Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy Screening

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) is the most common cardiac disease in cats and carries particular significance for Cornish Rex owners. For Cornish Rex cats, echocardiographic screening remains the primary detection method, as breed-specific genetic markers have not yet been validated. The American College of Veterinary Internal Medicine recommends echocardiographic screening beginning at 1-2 years of age and repeating annually or biennially for breeds with documented HCM predisposition. Left ventricular wall thickness exceeding 6mm on M-mode echocardiography is the diagnostic threshold.

Common Questions

How much should I feed my Cornish Rex?

Adult Cornish Rexs typically need 0.5–1.5 cups of high-quality food per day, split into two meals. Adjust based on your cat's activity level, age, and body condition score. Active Cornish Rexs may need up to 30% more.

What is the best food brand for Cornish Rexs?

Look for foods that list real meat as the first ingredient, meet AAFCO standards, and address Cornish Rex-specific health needs like HCM. Brands offering small breed-specific formulas are often a good choice.
