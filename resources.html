Pet Care Resources

Educational information and resources to help you care for your pet's health and well-being.

Emergency Pet Care Resources

Animal Poison Control

ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center: (888) 426-4435

Available 24/7 for assistance with potential pet poisoning emergencies. A consultation fee may apply.

Finding Emergency Veterinary Care

  • Keep your regular veterinarian's emergency contact information readily available
  • Research local 24-hour emergency veterinary hospitals before an emergency occurs
  • Program emergency contacts into your phone
  • Ask your veterinarian for after-hours emergency recommendations

When to Seek Emergency Care

Contact emergency veterinary services immediately if your pet experiences:

  • Difficulty breathing or choking
  • Severe bleeding or trauma
  • Suspected poisoning or toxin ingestion
  • Seizures or loss of consciousness
  • Inability to urinate or defecate despite attempts
  • Eye injuries
  • Severe vomiting or diarrhea
  • Heatstroke symptoms

Note: This is educational information only. Always consult with a veterinarian for your pet's specific situation.

Pet Safety & Preparedness

Pet Emergency Preparedness Checklist

  • Maintain up-to-date identification tags and microchip registration
  • Keep current photos of your pet for identification purposes
  • Prepare an emergency supply kit with at least 3-5 days of pet food and water
  • Include medications and medical records in your emergency kit
  • Have a pet first aid kit available
  • Know your evacuation plan and pet-friendly shelters in your area
  • Keep a secure carrier or leash accessible

Home Safety Tips

  • Store medications, cleaning products, and chemicals out of pet reach
  • Keep toxic foods away from pets (chocolate, grapes, onions, xylitol, etc.)
  • Secure electrical cords and remove small objects that could be choking hazards
  • Research which houseplants are toxic to pets
  • Ensure windows and balconies are secure
  • Keep trash cans covered and inaccessible

Travel Safety

  • Never leave pets unattended in vehicles
  • Use proper restraints or carriers when traveling
  • Research pet-friendly accommodations in advance
  • Bring familiar items to reduce stress
  • Keep identification tags and microchip information current

Mental Health & Grief Support for Pet Owners

Understanding the Human-Animal Bond

The relationship between people and their pets is a significant emotional connection. Loss of a pet or concerns about pet health can deeply affect mental well-being.

Pet Loss Support Resources

Many veterinary schools and organizations offer pet loss support hotlines and counseling:

  • Association for Pet Loss and Bereavement (aplb.org)
  • Cornell University Pet Loss Support Hotline
  • University of California, Davis Pet Loss Support Hotline
  • Ohio State University Honoring the Bond Support Line

Coping with Pet Illness or Loss

  • Allow yourself to grieve; pet loss is a legitimate form of loss
  • Seek support from understanding friends, family, or support groups
  • Consider speaking with a counselor who understands pet loss
  • Take time before deciding whether to welcome a new pet
  • Honor your pet's memory in meaningful ways

Supporting Children Through Pet Loss

  • Be honest and age-appropriate in explanations
  • Allow children to express their feelings
  • Include children in memorial activities if appropriate
  • Answer questions openly and patiently
  • Reassure children that their feelings are valid

How to Find a Qualified Veterinarian

What to Look For

  • State licensure and proper credentials
  • Hospital accreditation (AAHA accreditation is a mark of quality)
  • Clean, well-organized facilities
  • Friendly, knowledgeable staff
  • Clear communication about treatments and costs
  • Emergency care availability or referral arrangements
  • Convenient location and hours

Questions to Ask When Choosing a Veterinarian

  • What are your hours of operation?
  • How do you handle after-hours emergencies?
  • What services do you provide in-house vs. through referral?
  • What is your approach to preventive care?
  • How do you communicate test results and treatment plans?
  • What payment options do you accept?
  • Do you have experience with my pet's species or breed?

Specialist Referrals

Your primary care veterinarian may refer you to a veterinary specialist for complex conditions. Board-certified specialists have completed additional training in areas such as:

  • Internal medicine
  • Surgery
  • Cardiology
  • Dermatology
  • Oncology
  • Ophthalmology
  • Emergency and critical care

Building a Good Relationship with Your Veterinarian

  • Schedule regular wellness examinations
  • Be honest about your pet's behavior and symptoms
  • Ask questions if you don't understand something
  • Follow treatment plans and medication schedules
  • Communicate concerns promptly
  • Keep your contact information current

Additional Considerations

Responsible Pet Ownership

  • Research breed or species needs before acquiring a pet
  • Commit to providing proper nutrition, exercise, and veterinary care
  • Ensure proper training and socialization
  • Spay or neuter pets unless breeding responsibly
  • Comply with local licensing and vaccination requirements
  • Plan for your pet's care in case of emergency or life changes

Pet Insurance Considerations

Pet insurance can help manage unexpected veterinary costs. When researching options, consider:

  • Coverage details and exclusions
  • Deductibles and reimbursement rates
  • Pre-existing condition policies
  • Whether your preferred veterinarians are covered
  • Waiting periods for coverage to begin

This information is educational only. Research multiple options and read policy details carefully before making insurance decisions.
