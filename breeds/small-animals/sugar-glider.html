A night animal that glides, and screams

Sugar gliders weigh about 4 to 5 ounces. Captive life is often 12 to 15 years, which is a long commitment for an animal that is awake when you want to sleep. They need a tall cage, well beyond a hamster tub, with branches and a pouch. The diet has to be built for calcium. A fruit-only menu produces hind-limb paralysis from metabolic bone disease.

They bond to their group. Hands are a slow project, not a day-one cuddle. The smell is real. The barking at night is real. Apartment neighbors may not find it charming.

Calcium, law, and a vet who has seen one

A glider that drags a back leg, or that stops eating the prepared diet and only licks fruit, needs a diet overhaul and often a vet. Ordinary cat-and-dog clinics may decline them. ASPCA pet care notes are a start for small mammals, not a glider recipe. Appetite loss is the small-mammal not-eating page.

Sugar glider questions

Can I keep one sugar glider?

A lone glider needs hours of direct contact every night or it self-mutilates. Two or more compatible gliders are the usual plan. One glider plus a busy human who is asleep at night is not a colony.

Are they legal?

Not in every state. California, Hawaii, and Alaska restrict or ban them, and some cities do too. Check before you buy. A breeder who ships does not write your state law.

Are they flying squirrels?

No. Sugar gliders are marsupials, Petaurus breviceps. North American flying squirrels are a different animal. This page is the glider.
