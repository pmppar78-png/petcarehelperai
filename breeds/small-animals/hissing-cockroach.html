Madagascar Hissing Cockroach Overview

The Madagascar hissing cockroach is Gromphadorhina portentosa, a wingless insect from Madagascar. Size on the card is 2-3 inches. Lifespan is 2-5 years. They do not fly. The adults you buy are the large, heavy-bodied roaches, and they stay on the ground and on the bark you give them. A hissing cockroach in a room is an escape, not a moth that took off. The card rates them docile and hardy, at beginner difficulty, which is about a contained colony, not about an insect that can be left loose.

Males carry bumps, the horns, on the pronotum, the shield behind the head. Those horns are how you tell a mature male from a female, and males use them when they shove each other. Females lack that pair of bumps. The hiss is not a rattle and not a warning from a mouthpart. They hiss by forcing air through the spiracles along the body. A sudden hiss when you open the lid is that air, from an animal that cannot fly away. Nymphs are smaller copies of the adults, not a different pest species that appeared in the bin.

They want warmth and humidity, and they want a tight lid. A dry, cool tank slows them and cracks the conditions the card's hardy label assumes. Ventilation matters, but a screen with a gap, a lid that sits askew, or a cable hole is enough for a wingless roach that climbs. They do not need height for flight because they have no wings to use. They do need the lid latched. A colony of 2-3 inch adults on a kitchen floor is the failure the tight lid is there to prevent.

Nymphs hatch as small versions of the same animal and grow through molts until they reach that 2-3 inch adult size. A tiny roach in the colony is not a German cockroach infestation and not a sign that a pest species got in, as long as it matches the hissing cockroach shape. Do not spray the enclosure with a household bug killer because you saw small ones. You will kill the animals you meant to keep. If a roach of a different shape shows up in the house, that is a separate problem from the nymphs in the tank.

The temperament line, docile and hardy, fits animals that tolerate being looked at and that breed in a simple setup. It does not mean children should squeeze a 2-3 inch roach, and it does not mean the colony can live in an open plastic bin. Males will still wrestle. A startled adult will still hiss through those spiracles. Beginner difficulty assumes you can hold a temperature and a lid. The origin is Madagascar. These are not the roaches that arrive in a grocery bag, and they should not be released into a building or a yard to prove the point.
