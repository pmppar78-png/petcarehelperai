A burrowing squirrel, not a cartoon

Prairie dogs weigh about 1 to 3 pounds and can live 8 to 12 years if they are not wild-caught and stressed into an early death. They dig, they chew, they bark, and they need a large enclosure or a room you are willing to surrender. Hay and grasses are the diet. Seed mixes and junk food make fat, sick animals.

They can carry fleas and, in wild populations, plague. That is a reason for a vet who understands the risk, not a reason to panic at a captive-bred animal and also not a reason to treat one like a hamster from a fair. Most people should not keep them.

Teeth, heat, and a law book

Dental disease and obesity are the captive problems. A prairie dog that stops eating needs an exotic vet quickly. Do not release one outdoors. A pet prairie dog is not a native restoration project. ASPCA guidance on exotic pets is the cautious starting point. Appetite loss is the small-mammal page.

Prairie dog questions

Are prairie dogs legal?

Not everywhere. Some states ban them or require permits, partly because of plague and other diseases. Check state and city rules before you believe a seller.

Can they be handled?

Some captive-bred animals tolerate their own person. They bite, they are not domesticated dogs, and a wild-caught prairie dog is a bad idea and often an illegal one.

Do they need company?

They are highly social. A single prairie dog in a cage is a poor life. Most private homes cannot provide a group and the space they use to dig.
