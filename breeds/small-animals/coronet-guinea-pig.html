Coronet Guinea Pig Overview

The Coronet Guinea Pig is a medium (1.5-2.5 lbs) guinea pig that makes a wonderful pet for those with some small animal experience.

Coronet Guinea Pigs thrive with companionship and are best kept with compatible cage mates.

A Coronet guinea pig is a long-haired cavy with a single rosette crest on the forehead. The crest is one neat rosette over the eyes, and the rest of the long coat falls back over the body. Printed figures here are a medium adult of 1.5 to 2.5 pounds, a life of 5 to 7 years, moderate activity, intermediate care, and at least 7.5 square feet of floor. They are meant to live as a pair. The hair is the reason care sits at intermediate. The species underneath is still a guinea pig.

Both the crest and the rear coat need grooming. The forehead rosette mats into a knot if it is ignored, and the long hair at the back picks up hay, bedding, and damp bedding from the floor. A daily comb of those two zones prevents the kind of felt that has to be cut off later. Bathing a guinea pig to fix mats is a poor idea. They chill easily, and a wet long coat stays wet. Clip only when a mat is already tight, and keep scissors off the skin.

Training Advice

Vitamin C is not optional. Coronets cannot synthesize it any more than a short-haired cavy can. Hay, pellets, and vegetables are the diet line, and the vegetables plus fortified pellets are where the vitamin has to come from every day. Hay is still unlimited, because the teeth and the gut did not change when the coat got long. A silky rear end is not a sign that the diet can shift toward fruit and treats.

Grooming Essentials

Keep them in pairs, with at least the 7.5 square feet this card names, and more if two long coats are sharing a hide. Moderate activity means they still run and popcorn. A pen full of furniture and no open floor makes a long-haired cavy both bored and dirtier, because the coat drags through every wet corner. Choose a companion that will not chew the crest into a stump. Watch introductions. Long hair can hide a nip until the skin under it is already angry.
