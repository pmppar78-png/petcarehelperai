American Rabbit Overview

The American Rabbit is a large (9-12 lbs) rabbit that makes an excellent pet for beginners and families.

American Rabbits thrive with companionship and are best kept with compatible cage mates.

The American rabbit on this page is a domestic breed, blue or white, not a wild cottontail pulled from a yard. Cottontails are a different kind of rabbit, with different parasites, a different stress response, and no business in a pet hutch. This card lists a large adult, 9 to 12 pounds, a lifespan of 8 to 12 years, moderate activity, and a beginner care level. Eight to twelve years is a long stretch next to the giant breeds whose cards run shorter, so the pen and the hay budget have to last.

Mandibular teeth grow continuously. The lower cheek teeth and the incisors do not stop at adulthood, and a mouth with nothing hard to grind wears into points, abscesses, and a rabbit that cannot chew. Grass hay is the feed that does that grinding. Pellets and vegetables sit beside the hay. They do not replace it. A diet of mostly pellets and carrots is how a 9 to 12 pound rabbit arrives at the clinic with a wet chin and a jaw that hurts.

Some American Rabbits do great on standard kibble; others need a different approach due to allergies, sensitivities, or individual metabolism. Work with your vet to find what works, and be willing to adjust as your American Rabbit's needs change with age.
