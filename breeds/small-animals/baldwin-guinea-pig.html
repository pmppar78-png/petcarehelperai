Baldwin Guinea Pig Overview

The Baldwin Guinea Pig is a medium (1.5-2.5 lbs) guinea pig that makes a wonderful pet for those with some small animal experience.

Baldwin Guinea Pigs thrive with companionship and are best kept with compatible cage mates.

A Baldwin guinea pig is a hairless cavy, still a guinea pig in every system that matters, with bare skin instead of a coat to buffer the room. This card lists a medium adult, 1.5 to 2.5 pounds, a lifespan of 5 to 7 years, moderate activity, intermediate care, and a minimum of 7.5 square feet. Social needs are a pair. Hairless does not mean low maintenance, and it does not mean the animal can be housed like a reptile on sand.

They need warmth. A cool floor, a drafty hutch, or a room that a coated cavy tolerates will chill bare skin. They also need skin care. The skin can burn and it can dry out. Direct sun through a window, a heat lamp too close to the body, and rough dusty bedding are the usual ways that happens. Cracks, flakes, and red patches are a husbandry problem to fix and, if the skin breaks open, a vet visit. Do not smear random human lotions on a cavy and call it skincare.

A conversation with your exotic veterinarian ensures these general guidelines get adapted to your Baldwin Guinea Pig's unique needs, age, and overall condition.

Training Advice

The diet is still hay, pellets, vegetables, and vitamin C. Hairless cavies cannot make vitamin C either. The missing coat does not change the liver. Unlimited grass hay is the bulk of the food, fortified pellets cover a measured share, and vegetables have to carry vitamin C every day. A Baldwin that is fed like a hairless rat, on seeds and scraps, will fail the same way any other guinea pig fails when the vitamin is missing: rough coat is not the clue, because there is no coat. Look at appetite, joints, and energy instead.

Grooming Essentials

Keep them with another guinea pig. The card says pairs, and 7.5 square feet is the minimum printed for the enclosure, which gets tight once two bodies and a hide are in it. Give them more floor if the room allows. Moderate activity still means they move, popcorn, and need space to turn around without standing on each other. A solitary Baldwin in a small tub is not warmer in any way that matters. It is only more bored, and boredom does not protect skin.

Temperament & Personality

Bedding should be soft and clean. Urine scald shows up fast on skin with no hair to wick it, so the wet spots have to leave the cage daily. Five to seven years of that routine, plus vitamin C and a warm room, is the real care level behind the intermediate label. They are guinea pigs that you can see in detail. The skin is not a fashion choice that cancels hay, a companion, or the vitamin they cannot synthesize.
