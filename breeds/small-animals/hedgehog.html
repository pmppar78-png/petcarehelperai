Hedgehog Overview

The African Pygmy Hedgehog kept as a pet is actually a hybrid between the Four-toed Hedgehog and the Algerian Hedgehog. These charming creatures are covered in approximately 5,000-7,000 quills on their back, which they raise when frightened by rolling into a tight ball.

Unlike the European hedgehog (which is a wild animal not suitable as a pet), African Pygmy Hedgehogs have been domestically bred as pets since the 1980s. They're known for their cute faces, entertaining wheel-running, and unique "anointing" behavior where they create foamy saliva and spread it on their quills when encountering new smells.

Legal Considerations

Important legal restrictions:

  • Illegal: California, Hawaii, Georgia, Pennsylvania (some exceptions), New York City, Washington D.C.
  • Restricted: Arizona, Maine, New Jersey - require permits
  • Check First: Always verify local regulations
  • Breeders: Purchase from reputable breeders, not wild-caught

Housing Requirements

Hedgehogs need appropriate housing with temperature control:

Enclosure Size

  • Minimum: 2 x 4 feet floor space
  • Options: Large storage bins, C&C cages, or commercial enclosures
  • Avoid: Wire-bottom cages (feet can get caught)
  • Height: At least 12 inches (hedgehogs can climb)
  • Lid: Ventilated but secure

Essential Equipment

  • Wheel: 12+ inch solid-surface wheel (CRITICAL)
  • Hiding House: Enclosed hide for sleeping
  • Bedding: Fleece liners, paper bedding (no cedar/pine)
  • Food/Water: Shallow dishes or bottle
  • Heat Source: Ceramic heat emitter or heating pad
  • Thermometer: To monitor temperature

Temperature is Critical

Hedgehogs must be kept between 72-80°F (22-27°C). Below 70°F, they can attempt hibernation, which is often fatal for domesticated hedgehogs. Above 85°F can cause heat stress. Always use a thermometer and have a reliable heat source.

Diet & Nutrition

Hedgehogs have specific dietary needs:

Primary Diet

  • High-Quality Cat Food: Main staple - dry, high-protein, low-fat
  • Requirements: 30-35% protein, 10-15% fat, high fiber
  • Portion: 1-2 tablespoons daily (adjust for weight)
  • Hedgehog Food: Some commercial options available but quality varies

Supplemental Foods

  • Insects: Mealworms, crickets, dubia roaches (treats)
  • Cooked Meat: Plain chicken, turkey
  • Vegetables: Small amounts of cooked carrots, green beans
  • Fruits: Occasional small pieces of apple, banana
  • Eggs: Scrambled or hard-boiled (occasional)

Foods to Avoid

  • Dairy products (lactose intolerant)
  • Raw meat or eggs
  • Grapes and raisins (toxic)
  • Onions, garlic, avocado
  • Nuts and seeds
  • Citrus fruits

Common Health Issues

Hedgehogs face several health concerns:

Wobbly Hedgehog Syndrome (WHS)

  • What: Degenerative neurological disease (similar to MS)
  • Signs: Progressive paralysis starting in rear legs, wobbling
  • Cause: Genetic - no cure or prevention
  • Prognosis: Progressive, ultimately fatal

Cancer

  • Prevalence: Very common in hedgehogs over 3 years
  • Types: Often oral, digestive, or reproductive cancers
  • Signs: Weight loss, lumps, changes in eating

Other Concerns

  • Obesity: Very common - monitor weight closely
  • Dental Disease: Tartar buildup, tooth loss
  • Mites: Skin parasites causing quill loss
  • Respiratory Issues: From drafts or poor ventilation
  • Hibernation Attempts: From cold temperatures - can be fatal

Handling & Taming

Hedgehogs can be tamed with patience:

Temperament

  • Variable: Personalities range from friendly to prickly
  • Defensive: Ball up and raise quills when scared
  • Nocturnal: Most active at night
  • Solitary: Don't need or want companions

Handling Tips

  • Approach calmly and speak softly
  • Scoop from underneath rather than grabbing
  • Let them uncurl naturally - don't force
  • Handle regularly to maintain tameness
  • Evening handling when naturally waking
  • Wear a shirt with your scent to acclimate them

Understanding Quills

  • Quills are modified hairs, not barbed
  • Relaxed hedgehog has flat quills - safe to touch
  • Raised quills indicate fear/stress
  • Quilling (losing quills) normal in juveniles

Behavior & Activity

Understanding hedgehog behavior:

Common Behaviors

  • Anointing: Creating foamy saliva and spreading on quills (normal!)
  • Wheel Running: They run MILES nightly
  • Balling Up: Defensive posture
  • Pooping While Running: Common on wheels (messy!)
  • Huffing: Annoyed/defensive sound

Activity Pattern

  • Nocturnal: Sleep during day, active at night
  • Wheel: Essential - may run 5+ miles nightly
  • Exploring: Enjoy supervised floor time

Is a Hedgehog Right for You?

Hedgehogs Are Great For:

  • Night owls comfortable with nocturnal pets
  • Those wanting a unique, quiet pet
  • People who don't need a cuddly companion
  • Owners who can maintain proper temperatures
  • Those without other pets that might harm them

Hedgehogs May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Those in states where they're illegal
  • Anyone wanting a daytime, interactive pet
  • Young children (quills, nocturnal, may bite)
  • Those unable to maintain warm temperatures
  • People expecting a cuddly pet

Related Species to Consider

If you're interested in Hedgehogs, you might also consider:

  • Syrian Hamster - Nocturnal, easier care
  • Skinny Pig - Unique appearance, social
  • Gerbil - More active daytime option
