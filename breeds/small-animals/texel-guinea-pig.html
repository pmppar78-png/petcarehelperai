Texel Guinea Pig Overview

The Texel Guinea Pig is a medium (1.5-2.5 lbs) guinea pig that makes a wonderful pet for those with some small animal experience.

Texel Guinea Pigs thrive with companionship and are best kept with compatible cage mates.

A texel guinea pig is a long-haired guinea pig with a curly coat. It is not a texel sheep, and it is not a silkie. The sheep is a livestock breed that shares a name. The silkie, or sheltie, is a guinea pig whose long hair is straighter and sweeps back off the face. The texel curls. Size on the card is medium, 1.5-2.5 lbs. Lifespan is 5-7 years. Care level is intermediate because of that coat. People who search the name and expect either a sheep or a smooth longhair will mismanage the hair in front of them.

The curls mat. High grooming need is the whole intermediate rating. A texel that is combed once a week and otherwise left to look charming will felt. Mats pull on the skin, collect urine, and hide sores. The curl is tighter than a silkie's wave, so a routine copied from a silkie is often not enough. Work the coat down to the skin often enough that you never discover a mat by accident. Bathing a felted curl makes the mat worse. Cut the problem out only if you can do it without the skin, or have someone do it who has done a texel before. This is not a sheep shear on a schedule. It is a guinea pig that mats.

Housing Requirements

  • Space: 7.5 sq ft minimum - always provide the largest enclosure possible.
  • Type: C&C cage or large commercial enclosure with solid flooring.
  • Bedding: Paper-based or fleece bedding; avoid cedar and pine.
  • Enrichment: Tunnels, hidey houses, and hay racks.
  • Temperature: 65-75°F ideal; avoid drafts and direct sunlight.
  • Cleaning: Spot-clean daily; full cleaning weekly.

Diet & Nutrition

  • Primary Diet: Hay, pellets, vegetables, vitamin C.
  • Vitamin C: Guinea pigs cannot produce their own vitamin C. Supplement with bell peppers, kale, or vitamin C tablets daily.
  • Fresh Foods: Leafy greens daily; limited fruit as treats.
  • Avoid: Iceberg lettuce, potatoes, onions, and foods high in sugar.

Common Health Issues

  • Scurvy (Vitamin C Deficiency): Causes lethargy, rough coat, swollen joints. Supplement vitamin C daily.
  • Respiratory Infections: Common in guinea pigs; signs include sneezing, labored breathing.
  • Bumblefoot: Painful foot infection from rough or wire surfaces. Use solid, clean flooring.
  • Obesity: Overfeeding treats and under-exercising leads to obesity in most small animals.

  • Exercise: Floor time in safe, enclosed area.

Training Advice

The diet is hay, pellets, vegetables, and vitamin C. The curls do not change the fact that a guinea pig needs vitamin C and needs hay in the pen all day. Fruit juice in a curly coat is how mats start in earnest. Keep treats small and the hay constant. A texel at 1.5-2.5 lbs is easy to overfeed because the coat adds visual bulk. Weigh the animal. A dense coat over a thin body is the opposite problem, and you will not see it if you only look at the silhouette. Intermediate care is the comb and the scale, plus the ordinary cavy diet, for 5-7 years.

  • Start early: Begin socialization and basic training as soon as your Texel Guinea Pig comes home. The first few months are a critical learning period.
  • Positive methods: Reward desired behaviors with treats, praise, or play. Positive reinforcement is proven more effective and humane than punishment-based methods.
  • Socialization: Expose your Texel Guinea Pig to various people, animals, sounds, and environments in a positive way during the early socialization window.
  • Keep sessions short: Training sessions of 5-15 minutes are most effective. End before your Texel Guinea Pig becomes bored or frustrated.
  • Consistency matters: Use the same commands and rules across all family members. Inconsistency confuses your Texel Guinea Pig and slows learning.

Grooming Essentials

Social needs are social, and the card says to keep them in pairs. Space is 7.5 sq ft minimum. That minimum is tight once two curly coats are sharing it, and a pair is what the social line is asking for. One texel alone in 7.5 sq ft meets the floor and misses the pair. Two texels need enough room to pass, and both need the grooming. A cagemate will not keep the curls open. Activity is moderate. They are not a high-drive animal whose coat you can ignore because they are busy. They are a medium guinea pig that sits in its own hair.

  • Brushing: Regular brushing removes loose hair, distributes natural oils, and prevents matting.
  • Bathing: Bathe your Texel Guinea Pig every 4-8 weeks or as needed using a species-appropriate shampoo. Overbathing strips natural oils from the coat and skin.
  • Nail care: Trim nails every 2-4 weeks. Keep styptic powder on hand in case of bleeding.
  • Dental hygiene: Brush teeth several times weekly using pet-safe toothpaste.
  • Ear cleaning: Check ears weekly for redness, odor, or discharge. Clean with a veterinarian-approved ear cleaner as needed.

Living Environment
