Short-Tailed Opossum Overview

The short-tailed opossum is Monodelphis domestica, a small solitary marsupial from South America. It is not a sugar glider, and it is not a Virginia opossum. Size on the card is 4-6 inches. Lifespan is 4-8 years. There is no gliding membrane, no colony, and no life in the rafters of a North American house. People meet the name opossum and picture the Virginia animal, or they meet a marsupial and picture a sugar glider. This card is the small, short-tailed species. The 4-6 inch body is the scale.

Four to 8 years is a short life compared with a cat. The card's 4-8 year range is the one to plan on. Do not stretch it to a cat's lifespan because the animal is a mammal that can learn your routine. Difficulty is intermediate. A 4-6 inch marsupial that may only live to the low end of 4-8 years does not have many seasons in which to recover from the wrong diet or the wrong housing. Intermediate means you set the enclosure up correctly before the animal arrives, not after a month of experiments.

They do not have a pouch. Nothing about this species matches the picture of a mother with a belly pocket full of young. Females of this species lack that pouch. If you wanted a marsupial you could watch carry joeys in a pocket, this is the wrong animal, and a sugar glider is a different wrong animal. Do not prod the belly looking for a pouch that the species does not have. Curiosity on the card is the animal's trait, not an invitation to examine it like a specimen.

Temperament is curious and solitary. Solitary housing is the plan. One short-tailed opossum to an enclosure. They are not a colony pet, and they are not happier in a pair the way sugar gliders are. Housing two together because both are small marsupials is how animals get bitten and stressed. Curious means they investigate the cage and your hand. It does not mean they want to live in a pocket or sleep in a group. A hide, and enough room to move at night, matters more than a companion.

They can bite. A 4-6 inch animal has a mouth that can break skin, and a solitary marsupial does not owe you handling. Intermediate care includes a plan for an animal that would rather be in the hide. Use a cup or a short tunnel to move one if it does not step onto a hand. Do not pull it out by the tail. Children who need a pet that tolerates grabbing are looking at the wrong card. Curious animals still bite when they are restrained. The short life does not make a bite harmless.

Temperament & Behavior

The diet is insectivore and omnivore. Insects and other animal protein matter. A fruit cup meant for a sugar glider, or a seed mix meant for a hamster, is not this diet. Offer a varied menu built around that insectivore and omnivore need, and keep water available. Do not free-feed sweets because the animal is only 4-6 inches and seems to burn it off. Origin is South America. You do not need a rainforest room. You do need a secure lid, because a curious marsupial will leave through a gap a jar could not.

Is a Short-Tailed Opossum Right for You?

Short-Tailed Opossum May Be Great For:

  • A keeper who will house Monodelphis alone, with an exotic vet already identified
  • Someone who accepts a short life, often a few years, and a strong scent
  • An aquarist-level husbandry person who will not house them with sugar gliders

Short-Tailed Opossum May Not Be Ideal For:

  • A child who wants a pocket pet to carry around
  • Two males, or a male and female unless you are prepared for breeding
  • Anyone who was told a short-tailed opossum is a low-smell hamster

Whether a Short-Tailed Opossum fits your life comes down to a few practical questions. How much time can you realistically spend on exercise, grooming, and training each day? Is your living space suitable? Can you afford both routine care and the occasional surprise vet bill over the next 4-8 years? If the honest answers line up, a Short-Tailed Opossum can be a genuinely good match. If they don't, there is no shame in choosing a different small animal — or waiting until your circumstances change.
