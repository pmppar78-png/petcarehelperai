Recommended for Degus

A brief conversation with your exotic veterinarian before a Degu diet change adds an. Buy a pellet labeled for degus, or a high-fiber hay-based pellet that does not list molasses, honey, or dried fruit in the first ingredients. Hay is the food. A yogurt drop, a raisin, or a fruit mix is the usual reason a degu develops cataracts and diabetes. By the time the eye clouds, the sugar has already done the damage.

A Chilean rodent that should not see a raisin

Degus weigh about 6 to 10 ounces and often live 6 to 9 years. They are diurnal enough to be awake when you are, which is rarer than hamster marketing suggests. They chew. A plastic cage is a snack. Multi-level space, hay, and safe wood are the housing. The tail skin can slip off if you grab it. Scoop them up instead.

They bark and whistle. They are not a quiet child's first pet, and they are not a hamster with a longer life. By the time a degu's lens turns cloudy, the sugary food that caused it has already been eaten. Fruit and sweet treats do not belong in the cage.

Teeth and blood sugar

Incisors and cheek teeth overgrow when the diet is soft. A degu that drools or drops food needs a dental exam under anesthesia by someone who treats exotic mammals. Diabetes shows up as drinking, weight loss, and cloudy eyes. Merck is the clinical reference. A degu off its hay is the appetite page.

Degu questions

Can a degu eat fruit?

Almost none. Sugar is how degus become diabetic and develop cataracts. Hay, a measured pellet made for them, and the occasional plain vegetable are the diet. A yogurt drop is not a treat. It is a problem.

Do they need company?

Yes. Keep a same-sex pair or a group that was raised together. A single degu is a welfare failure unless a vet has a reason.

What is the dust bath for?

Coat health, a few times a week, not a pool they live in. Take the dust out after they bathe so it does not become a litter box.
