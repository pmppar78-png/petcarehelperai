Lifespan

Land hermit crabs are often sold as a few dollars of beach souvenir. The honest setup is a tank with deep substrate for molting, heat, humidity high enough that the gills work, a pool of dechlorinated fresh water, and a pool of marine salt water. Both pools. A saltwater aquarium and a dry sponge both miss the animal. These crabs need humid air and two pools.

Painted shells can be toxic and too small. Offer natural shells. Food is a mix of plants, protein, and calcium. One pellet cannot be the only food for years. They need darkness and quiet while they molt, which can take weeks.

Molts and shells

A crab out of its shell is in crisis. A crab that smells of decay is already dead. Do not rinse them in tap water for fun. ASPCA exotic-pet cautions apply. There is no honest version of this animal as a pocket pet for a weekend.

The two species shops sell

The photograph on this page is a Caribbean hermit crab, Coenobita clypeatus, the purple pincher. Shops also sell the Ecuadorian, Coenobita compressus, which is smaller, darker, and usually more active. Purple pinchers have a large purple or burgundy claw and a rounder shell opening. Ecuadorians are the ones stuffed into a tiny painted shell at a beach stall. Both are land crabs with modified gills. Neither is a marine hermit you can drop in a saltwater aquarium, and neither is the desert animal a dry souvenir cage pretends it is. The 2 to 6 inch line on the table is shell-and-body length after years of molts. The crab in the shop is smaller.

Heat, humidity, and substrate

Aim for about 75 to 85°F and humidity around 80 percent. Read both on a thermometer and a hygrometer. Do not guess from a warm lid. A heat mat on the side or back, controlled by a thermostat, beats a lamp that cooks the shell. The substrate is play sand mixed with coconut fiber, moist enough to hold a tunnel the way sandcastle sand does, and deep enough that the largest crab can bury: at least 6 inches, or about three times the height of its shell, whichever is more. A 29- to 40-gallon tank is a fair start for a small group. Ten gallons per crab is crowded. The lid has to close. They climb silicone, mesh, and the power cord.

Two pools, both easy to climb out of, both deep enough to wet the shell. One is dechlorinated fresh water. The other is salt water mixed with a marine aquarium salt. Table salt, iodized salt, and the salt sold for freshwater fish are the wrong minerals. Change the water when it films over. A crab that spends all day in the fresh pool is often short of humidity in the air, or short of salt water it trusts.

Greens, protein, and calcium

Offer a varied plate and take uneaten fresh food out the same day: leaf greens, unsalted cooked egg, a bit of plain fish or shrimp, unsweetened coconut, leaf litter, and a cuttlebone or crushed oyster shell for calcium. A commercial hermit-crab food can be part of that plate. It cannot be the only thing in the tank for a decade. They eat at night. A full dish in the morning does not prove they were fed well. Skip citrus oils, pesticides on beach wood, and anything salted or spiced.

Molting and a crab out of its shell

A buried crab is often molting. The questions below already say not to dig it up. A surface molt, or tankmates pulling at a soft crab, is the exception: move the molter into a small isolation tub with the same substrate, heat, and humidity, and leave the shed skin. They eat that skin for the minerals. Taken for a school display, that skin is missing when the next shell needs the minerals. After a molt they are soft for days. Extra empty natural shells, in several openings slightly larger than the one they wear, are equipment.

A crab that has left its shell is in crisis. Raise the humidity, dim the tank, and offer intact natural shells. Do not pull, poke, or rinse the abdomen under tap water. A fishy or rotten smell means the crab is dead. Painted shells and shells with a gloss coat can be toxic and are often too small. A crab that will not switch may need the painted shell cracked carefully by someone who has done it, with a better shell already in the tank. Do not soak the painted shell in nail-polish remover. Lethargy on the surface when the hygrometer reads dry is a husbandry emergency the same day. Most dog-and-cat clinics will not see a hermit crab. Ask an exotic practice before you buy whether anyone on staff will. There is no safe home dose of anything in the fish aisle for this.

Handling

Skin oils, soap, and a fall onto a table take legs off. Watch them at night. If you must move one, coax it into a cup rather than pinching the shell and shaking. Do not tap the shell to make it come out. That is a threat, and the crab answers by hiding until it is too dry to hide.

Enrichment

Cholla, cork, and a piece of mopani that has been soaked and scrubbed give a climb. Leaf litter gives a place to disappear. A wheel is a rodent tool and does not belong here. The enrichment that prevents fights is the spare-shell pile. Without it they evict each other, and an eviction on dry sand kills the loser.

Costs in October

A US shopper in October 2026 can expect a 29- to 40-gallon tank from about $50 to $150, sand and coconut fiber $30 to $60, a thermostat and heat mat $40 to $70, marine salt and dechlorinator under $20, and a handful of natural shells $15 to $30. Food after that is about $10 to $20 a month. The crab is often $5 to $15. That price is the warning. You are buying an animal that was collected, plus a tank it will need for ten years or more if you do this right. The souvenir cage at the register is why the lifespan on the table and the lifespan in the average bedroom diverge.

Hawaii and collection

Hawaii's Department of Agriculture lists hermit crabs among the animals prohibited for private possession. The page is the Plant Quarantine animal guidelines. On the mainland there is no single federal pet ban that covers Coenobita in a living room, and cities can still say no. Taking crabs off a public beach can violate local harvest rules. Do not collect a "free" crab on vacation. Captive breeding of land hermit crabs is uncommon in ordinary retail. If a seller says the crabs were bred in captivity, ask who the breeder is. A shrug means they were shipped from a wholesaler who bought them from a collector.

Suitability

They are social. A child's weekend pet, or a classroom animal that goes home in June in a plastic box, dies of the dry trip. One crab in a dry kit is the failure mode. A small group in a humid tank, with spare shells, suits an adult who will check a hygrometer. Other pets should not have access to the lid. A cat will bat a shell off the table. Beginners who have already kept a reptile with a thermostat can learn this. Beginners who want something that lives in a painted bucket cannot.

Before you buy

The tank should already be warm and humid, with deep substrate and both water pools, before any crab is purchased. At the shop, every crab should be in a shell, none of the shells painted shut, and the display itself humid. A fishy smell is a reason to leave the whole tank. Ask which species you are buying and write it down. Count spare shells into the cart. Isolate new crabs in a simple tub with the same heat and humidity for a few weeks if you already have a colony, because a new arrival can bring mites and a shell fight.

Hermit crab questions

How long do hermit crabs live?

The long end is 10 years and more, sometimes decades. Most pet crabs die in months from a dry tank, tap water, or a shell glued shut. The lifespan is a husbandry score.

Do they need friends?

They are social. A group in a large tank does better than one crab in a souvenir cage. They also fight over shells. Keep extra empty shells in several sizes.

What if my crab buries itself?

It may be molting. Do not dig it up. A molt is when they are soft and easily killed by a helpful owner. Isolate a molting crab only if the others are chewing on it.

What temperature and humidity do land hermit crabs need?

About 75 to 85°F and humidity around 80 percent, with a thermostat and a hygrometer. They breathe with modified gills. A dry tank kills, often within a month, a crab that could have lived for years.

Why are there two water dishes?

One is dechlorinated fresh water and one is mixed with marine aquarium salt. Table salt and freshwater aquarium salt are the wrong recipe. Both pools need a way to climb out.

Can I take a hermit crab off the beach?

Do not. Local harvest rules often forbid it, and Hawaii prohibits private possession of hermit crabs. Shop crabs are usually wild-caught already. Buy none until the tank is running.
