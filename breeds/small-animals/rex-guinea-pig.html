Rex Guinea Pig Overview

A rex guinea pig is a guinea pig with a short, dense coat produced by a rex gene, and it is not a rex rabbit. The coat has rosettes, the swirls where the hair changes direction. Size on the card is 1.5-2.5 lbs. . Origin is South America. Temperament is friendly and social. Difficulty is beginner. The rabbit called a rex is a different species with a velvet coat and no vitamin C requirement. A rex guinea pig is still Cavia porcellus. The gene changed the hair. It did not turn the animal into a rabbit.

Because the coat is short and dense, the skin is more exposed than on a long-haired cavy. Rosettes part the hair and leave little windows of skin. Wire floors, rough edges, and strong sun reach that skin faster than they reach a coated breed that hides it. Look at the rosettes when you handle the pig, not only at the back. Bald patches, scabs, or a sore under a swirl are easy to miss if you only pet the plush direction of the coat. Beginner care includes that look. The coat does not need the daily combing a Peruvian needs, and it still needs eyes on the skin.

The diet is the guinea pig diet, whatever the rex gene did to the hair. They need vitamin C because guinea pigs do not make it. Hay is the staple that keeps the teeth and the gut moving. Pellets made for guinea pigs, plus vegetables, are the usual way to cover vitamin C without inventing a dose in this paragraph. A rex rabbit pellet, a rabbit mix, or a vitamin C plan copied from a rabbit will not do. Fruit is a treat. A sticky coat on a short rex is less dramatic than on a longhair, and the missing vitamin C is just as serious. Five to 7 years is a long time to feed the wrong species' food.

Friendly and social means they want company of their own kind. A single rex guinea pig in a cage next to a rex rabbit is not a social life. Pair them with another guinea pig, with enough floor that the two can pass each other. Activity is not listed as a separate high-energy job on this card. They still move, popcorn, and use the space you give them. A carrier-sized cage is a poor match for a 1.5-2.5 lb animal you intend to keep 5-7 years. The short coat is not a reason to shrink the pen.

They handle ordinary household temperatures and suffer in heat. A rex coat does not add the heat tolerance people imagine when they hear short hair. Shade, airflow, and water still matter on a hot day. The dense coat can also hide a wet belly if the bedding is soaked. Friendly handling should include a hand under the belly so you notice damp skin before it scalds.

Temperament & Behavior

Costs on the card are initial setup $100-$500+, animal purchase $10-$500+, annual food and bedding $150-$500, and annual vet care $50-$200. The annual food range has to buy hay for 5-7 years, not a sack of mixed seed. Vitamin C is in the diet, not in a once-a-year purchase. The vet range is a planning number. A guinea pig that stops eating still needs a clinic that will see cavies, and that visit does not wait because the difficulty line says beginner. Setup money is the pen and the hay storage. It is not a rabbit hutch with a wire floor.

Read the animal as a short-coated guinea pig with rosettes. The rex gene made the coat dense and left the skin easier to see and easier to injure. It did not make a rex rabbit, and it did not cancel vitamin C or hay. Friendly and social, beginner, South America, 1.5-2.5 lbs, 5-7 years: those are the card. The welfare additions are the exposed skin, a solid floor, and a companion of the same species. Pet the rosettes backward once in a while so you are looking at skin, not only at plush.

Housing & Environment Requirements

Guinea pigs need a minimum 7.5 square feet of living space (more is better). They are social animals and do best in pairs or small groups.

Diet & Nutrition

Unlimited timothy hay, daily vitamin C-rich vegetables (guinea pigs cannot produce their own vitamin C), quality pellets, and fresh water. Bell peppers and dark leafy greens are excellent choices.

A well-formulated food that meets your Rex Guinea Pig's specific needs is better than a rotation of trendy diets. Focus on protein quality, calorie appropriateness for your Rex Guinea Pig's size and activity level, and avoiding ingredients your Rex Guinea Pig does not tolerate well. .

Common Questions About Rex Guinea Pigs

Are Rex Guinea Pigs good pets for children?

With proper adult supervision, Rex Guinea Pigs can be appropriate pets for responsible children.

How much space do Rex Guinea Pigs need?

Rex Guinea Pigs require an appropriately sized habitat that meets their specific needs. A minimum of 7.5 square feet for one guinea pig, with additional space for each additional pig. C&C (cubes and coroplast) cages are popular for providing ample space at reasonable cost.

How long do Rex Guinea Pigs live?

Rex Guinea Pigs typically live 5-7 years with proper care.

A Rex guinea pig has a short, dense, curly coat that still needs checking for skin problems, plus the same non-negotiables as any cavy: unlimited hay, daily vitamin C, and a companion. The Rex coat does not protect them from scurvy. A pig that hops instead of walks, or drools, needs a dental and vitamin check. Keep the cage large and the bedding dry so the short coat does not sit in ammonia.

The Rex Guinea Pig has a particular way of winning people over through sheer expressiveness — the low rumbling purr when you hit the right spot behind the ears, the explosive popcorning leap that signals pure contentment, the insistent wheek that greets you the moment you open the refrigerator. Their dense, velvety coat feels unlike any other guinea pig variety and invites the kind of casual handling that both animal and owner seem to enjoy equally. Keepers who establish a consistent daily routine report that their Rex pigs become remarkably trusting and will actively seek lap time rather than merely tolerating it.
