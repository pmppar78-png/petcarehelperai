Dumbo Rat Overview

The Dumbo Rat is a small-medium (0.5-1.5 lbs) rat that makes an excellent pet for beginners and families.

Dumbo Rats thrive with companionship and are best kept with compatible cage mates.

A dumbo rat is a fancy rat, Rattus norvegicus, with ears set low and wide on the sides of the head. It is not a smaller species, not a dwarf rat, and not a mouse with large ears. This card lists a small to medium adult, 0.5 to 1.5 pounds, a lifespan of 2 to 3 years, high activity, beginner care, and a minimum of 2 cubic feet per rat. That weight range is the ordinary pet-rat range. A dumbo that stays well under it is not a teacup variety. It is a young rat or a thin one.

Care matches other pet rats. The ear placement does not change the lungs or the mammary tissue. Respiratory disease is common in the species, and so are tumors, especially mammary tumors. A new lump, a wheeze, red staining around the nose, or weight loss needs a vet who will treat rats. Pet-shop drops are not a plan, and this page will not invent a dose. The low ears still have to be checked for tears and for mites, but they are not a different organ system.

Housing Requirements

  • Space: 2 cu ft per rat minimum - always provide the largest enclosure possible.
  • Type: Multi-level wire cage with solid platforms.
  • Enrichment: Hammocks, ropes, tunnels, and foraging opportunities.

Common Health Issues

  • Respiratory Disease: Extremely common in rats (mycoplasma). Signs: sneezing, porphyrin (red discharge around eyes/nose).
  • Tumors: Both benign (mammary) and malignant tumors are common, especially in females.
  • Bumblefoot: Foot infections from wire floors. Use solid platforms and clean bedding.

Training Advice

They are highly social. The card calls for pairs or groups. Females often do better in pairs, settling with another female more readily than a pair of adult males that were raised apart. Males still need company. A lone dumbo is not a low-drama choice. It is a rat missing the species it was built to sleep beside. Introductions belong on neutral ground. Dropping a new adult into a lived-in cage is how a beginner-labeled pet draws blood.

Grooming Essentials

Feed lab blocks as the base and add fresh food beside them. A seed mix as the whole diet lets a rat pick the fatty pieces and ignore the rest. Two cubic feet per rat is the printed minimum, and a pair needs that allowance twice, plus height for hammocks and a solid floor. Wire mesh under the feet causes bumblefoot. High activity means climbing, chewing, and a cage that is redecorated often. Beginner care assumes you can meet that, not that the animal occupies a corner like a fish.
