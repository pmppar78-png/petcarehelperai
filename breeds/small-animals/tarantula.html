Diet & Nutrition

Research species-specific dietary needs.

A tarantula's food is feeder insects of an appropriate size, offered on a slow schedule, not a shelf diet scored by coat and stools. This animal has an exoskeleton, not a coat, and it will fast for long stretches, especially before a molt. Steady body condition means a rounded abdomen that is not shriveled and not dragging hugely. Good energy, for a tarantula, is a spider that still takes prey and still returns to its hide, not one that runs a wheel. You do not need an expensive formulated gel if appropriate insects are available. Remove uneaten prey so it cannot injure a molting spider. If the abdomen shrinks and the spider refuses food outside a clear pre-molt, ask a veterinarian who sees invertebrates. Do not invent a supplement dose.

A Tarantula tends to reveal the payoff of this kind of attention gradually, rather than in a single dramatic moment.

Are Tarantulas good pets for children?

Tarantulas are better suited for older children or teens who can understand and meet their specific care requirements.

How much space do Tarantulas need?

House a tarantula in a secure enclosure built for its lifestyle, with more floor or more height than the smallest display cube, and with a lid that locks rather than rests. Terrestrial and fossorial species need horizontal space and substrate. Arboreal species need vertical space and anchor points. Ventilation must match the species' moisture needs. Always exceed the tightest minimum, because a 10-inch legspan is not a 3-inch spiderling, and adults of long-lived species spend years in the box you first chose. Do not use an open-topped critter keeper. The habitat requirement is species-specific and closed.

How long do Tarantulas live?

Tarantulas typically live 5-30 years with proper care. Consider the time commitment before acquiring any pet.

What veterinary care do Tarantulas need?
