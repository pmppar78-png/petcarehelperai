Rex Rabbit Overview

The Rex Rabbit is a medium (7.5-10.5 lbs) rabbit that makes an excellent pet for beginners and families.

Rex Rabbits thrive with companionship and are best kept with compatible cage mates.

The rex rabbit on this page is the plush, short-coated rabbit, including the standard rex, which is larger than a mini rex. Size is medium, 7.5-10.5 lbs. . A mini rex is the small breed at a few pounds and a longer listed life. Do not use that other card's weight or its 8-12 year line here. This rabbit is the bigger velvet one. Guard hairs are reduced, so the coat feels like velvet rather than like ordinary rabbit fur with a soft undercoat. That missing guard hair is the breed and the reason the coat is thin.

The coat does not need clipping. There is no wool to block the gut and no mane to wrap. What the coat needs is a floor that does not abrade it. A solid floor is the housing detail that matches reduced guard hairs. Wire rubs the thin coat and then rubs the skin, and a 7.5-10.5 lb rabbit puts real weight on the hocks while it sits. Sore hocks on a rex start as bald pink spots and get worse if the rabbit is left on metal. Bedding or a resting mat on a solid surface is the fix. A carpet the rabbit can catch a nail in is not.

Training Advice

A medium rabbit of 7.5-10.5 lbs fills a 4x2 ft pen and still needs exercise beyond it. The standard rex is not a mini rex that will be content in a smaller box because both say rex. Activity is moderate. . A compatible rabbit can share the pen if they are introduced with room to separate. The velvet is not a reason to house the animal alone in a display cage. Five to 6 years, the lifespan on the card, is the length of that housing decision.

Grooming Essentials

The diet is hay, pellets, and vegetables. Hay is the main feed, the same as for any rabbit, because the gut and the teeth did not change when the guard hairs did. A rex that lives on pellets gets heavy inside the 7.5-10.5 lb range and then carries that weight on thinner coat and vulnerable hocks. Greens are the vegetable portion. Fruit is occasional. A rabbit that stops eating hay, or that stops eating entirely, needs a veterinarian the same day. The velvet coat will not tell you the gut has slowed. The droppings and the hay rack will.

The coat feels sturdy because it is dense, and it is still a thin coat over a medium-sized rabbit. Do not shave a rex for summer.
