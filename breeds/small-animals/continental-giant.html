Continental Giant Rabbit Overview

The Continental Giant Rabbit is a very large (12-25 lbs) rabbit that makes a wonderful pet for those with some small animal experience.

A Continental giant is a very large domestic rabbit of the UK type, doing a similar job to a Flemish giant and not a wild hare. Hares have longer legs, a different build, and no place in a pet pen. This card lists a very large adult, 12 to 25 pounds, a lifespan of 4 to 7 years, moderate activity, intermediate care, and a very large enclosure. Use those figures. Do not borrow the Flemish weight range or the Flemish lifespan and paste them onto this breed. The top of this card is heavier, and the years printed here are 4 to 7.

The pen has to match a body that can reach 25 pounds. A very large enclosure means the rabbit can lie flat, turn, and take a few hops, not a hutch sized for a 4 pound Dutch. Moderate activity does not shrink the animal. A giant that sits all day still occupies a giant's floor when it stretches. Nail care matters more as the weight climbs. Overgrown claws change how a heavy rabbit stands and add pressure to the hocks. Trim them before they curl, and keep the flooring solid so the feet are not standing on wire.

Training Advice

Hay, pellets, and vegetables are the diet, with hay as the bulk. A 12 to 25 pound rabbit eats a serious amount of grass hay, and the teeth still grow without it. Pellets are a measured portion. A free-choice pellet bin plus no hay produces a heavy rabbit with a sore mouth. Social needs are listed as social. Add another rabbit only with a real introduction, and do not pair a giant with a dwarf that can be injured in a scuffle over a bowl.
