Two pounds and a decade

Netherland dwarfs weigh about 1.1 to 2.5 pounds and often live 10 to 12 years. They are energetic and not especially interested in being held. Hay is most of the diet. A seed-and-carrot habit will grow the wrong teeth and stop the gut. They need space to run that looks absurd next to their size, because a small animal still has legs.

Neuter and spay. Uterine cancer is common in female rabbits that are left intact, and intact males spray and fight. A pair can work after surgery and a proper introduction. A pair of strangers dumped together is a fight.

A short face is a dental plan

Incisors that curl and molars that spur are the breed tax. Some need repeated dentals for life. Budget that before you fall for the face. GI stasis is the other emergency, same as in any rabbit. ASPCA covers rabbit feeding in plain language. A dwarf that will not eat hay by evening is the not-eating page.

Netherland dwarf questions

Are Netherland dwarfs good for kids?

Usually no. They are fragile, quick, and often nippy. An adult who wants a small rabbit can do well. A child who wants to carry a bunny around will break one or get bitten.

Why are their faces so short?

The dwarf face crowds the teeth. Malocclusion is common. A rabbit that drops pellets or has a wet chin needs the molars and the incisors examined.

Ask whether the parents needed a dental

Netherland dwarfs were developed in the Netherlands in the early 1900s from small Polish-type rabbits, and the quick facts already give the 1.1 to 2.5 pound body and the 10 to 12 year life. The short face crowds the incisors and the cheek teeth, and malocclusion is common enough that some rabbits need repeated dentals for life. A breeder should be able to say whether the parents have had incisor or molar trims. Hay is the food that wears teeth and keeps the gut moving. A dwarf that drops pellets or shows a wet chin needs the mouth examined. Uterine adenocarcinoma is common in unspayed female rabbits, so the spay is a health plan as well as a behavior plan.
