Jersey Wooly Rabbit Overview

The Jersey wooly is a small wool rabbit breed from the United States, and it is not a guinea pig. Size on the card is 2.5-3.5 lbs. Lifespan is 7-10 years. Temperament is gentle and docile. Difficulty is listed as beginner. The coat is wool, which is the part those labels leave for you to discover after the rabbit is home. A guinea pig of a similar weight is a different animal with a different diet. This one is a rabbit, and the wool does not change that.

Beginner, gentle, and docile describe the handling, not the coat. A Jersey wooly still has to be groomed. The wool mats, picks up bedding, and gets swallowed during a bath the rabbit gives itself. Swallowed wool is wool block. A mass of coat in the gut is a reason the rabbit stops eating, and a wool rabbit that is behind on grooming is the one that grows that mass. Brushing and removing loose wool is maintenance on a schedule, for the whole 7-10 years, not a show-day extra.

Hay comes first. The diet of a Jersey wooly is the diet of a rabbit. Unlimited grass hay is the staple. Pellets are measured. Vegetables are a side, not the meal. Wool is not a reason to switch the animal to a guinea pig pellet or to a vitamin C supplement meant for a cavy. A rabbit that lives on soft food and treats and only nibbles hay is the rabbit whose gut slows. The wool makes that worse if the rabbit is also eating its coat. Hay is still the first thing in the pen each day.

A rabbit that stops eating needs a vet the same day. That rule is not softer because the card says beginner, and it is not slower because the temperament says docile. A Jersey wooly that refuses hay, sits hunched, or produces no droppings may be in wool block or in gut stasis, and small rabbits go downhill in hours. Do not wait overnight because the breed is only 2.5-3.5 lbs and looks quiet. Quiet is the problem. The same-day visit is the response the size demands.

The cost lines are initial setup $100-$500+, animal purchase $10-$500+, annual food and bedding $150-$500, and annual vet care $50-$200. Read the annual vet line against a 7-10 year life, not against a single checkup. Wool block and dental work blow past a $50-$200 year when they happen. The food and bedding range has to cover unlimited hay for a decade, plus the grooming tools the wool requires. A $10-$500+ purchase price is the rabbit. It is not the grooming and it is not the hay.

Temperament & Behavior

House a Jersey wooly as a rabbit. The pen has to let a 2.5-3.5 lb animal hop, with a solid floor and hay in reach. Wool holds heat. A dense coat in a hot room is a risk even though the body is small. Do not shave the rabbit as a summer shortcut and then skip the hay because it looks less like a wool breed. Do not house it with a guinea pig. The guinea pig can be sat on, bullied, or fed the wrong pellet, and it still will not be company of the right species.

Seven to 10 years, the card's 7-10 year lifespan, is a long time to keep a coat you chose because it looked soft. Gentle and docile are real. They do not cancel grooming, wool block, or the rule that a rabbit off its hay is a same-day patient. The animal is a small wool rabbit from the United States, 2.5-3.5 lbs, not a guinea pig and not a pet that can live on pellets. Hay first, then the brush, then the vet if the appetite stops.

Housing & Environment Requirements

Provide an appropriately sized habitat for your Jersey Wooly Rabbit. Rabbits need at minimum a 4x their body length enclosure with daily free-roaming time in a rabbit-proofed area. Provide hay, hiding spots, and toys.

Diet & Nutrition

Unlimited timothy hay should make up 80% of the diet, supplemented with fresh leafy greens daily and a small amount of quality pellets. Fresh water always available.

What you feed your Jersey Wooly Rabbit matters more than most owners realize. Diet affects energy, coat condition, digestive health, immune function, and even behavior. There is no single "best food" — the right choice depends on your Jersey Wooly Rabbit's age, activity level, any health conditions, and how they respond to specific ingredients. Expect some trial and adjustment, especially in the first year.

Learning to read a pet food label takes five minutes and will serve you for the life of your Jersey Wooly Rabbit. Look at the guaranteed analysis for protein and fat percentages that match your Jersey Wooly Rabbit's needs.

Grooming & Maintenance

Regular grooming includes daily brushing for long-haired breeds, nail trimming every 4-6 weeks, and scent gland cleaning. Spot-clean the litter area daily and deep clean weekly.

Is a Jersey Wooly Rabbit Right for You?

Jersey Wooly Rabbit May Be Great For:

  • A keeper who will groom a small wool rabbit and watch for wool block and GI stasis
  • Someone who wants a small rabbit and will not house it with a giant breed
  • An owner with a rabbit-savvy vet and a hay-first diet

Jersey Wooly Rabbit May Not Be Ideal For:

  • A first rabbit bought for a child's Easter
  • Anyone who will skip grooming because the rabbit is small
  • A wire-floor cage
  • Plan the adult size, the vet, and the daily work for the jersey wooly before it comes home.

Owning a Jersey Wooly Rabbit is a commitment measured in years, not months. The enthusiasm of the first few weeks fades, and what remains is a daily routine of feeding, exercise, grooming, and vet visits. If that sounds like a satisfying rhythm rather than a burden, you are probably in a good position to move forward. If it sounds exhausting, it is worth reconsidering.

People who live with a Jersey Wooly Rabbit tend to develop a deep appreciation for the breed's personality — the gentle, docile nature becomes part of the household's rhythm. That bond does not happen overnight, but it builds steadily when care is consistent and expectations are grounded.
