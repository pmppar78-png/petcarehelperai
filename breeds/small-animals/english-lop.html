English Lop Rabbit Overview

The English Lop Rabbit is a large (9-11 lbs) rabbit that makes a wonderful pet for those with some small animal experience. Their moderate activity level and social - pairs social nature make them engaging and entertaining companions.

The English lop is the lop with very long ears, a large domestic rabbit whose ears are the breed and the injury risk. It is not a small dwarf lop, not a Holland lop, and not a mini lop that stayed a little leggy. This card lists a large adult, 9 to 11 pounds, a lifespan of 5 to 7 years, moderate activity, intermediate care, and a minimum of 4 by 2 feet. The ears can trail and spread in ways a compact lop never will. Housing that ignores that spread is how the ears get hurt.

Ears injure easily. They are thin-skinned, easy to cut on a rough edge, and slow to heal when a crate door shuts on them. Check the pen for gaps at floor level, exposed wire ends, and any lid that drops. A rabbit this size will shake its head and slap an ear into a wall. Blood along the ear margin is a vet visit, not a stain to watch for a week. Five to seven years is short enough that a chronic ear wound uses up a real fraction of the life.

Diet & Nutrition

The ears can be stepped on. That happens when people, children, or another animal move through a tight pen while the lop is resting with the ears flung out. It also happens when the rabbit is on the floor of a busy room. The 4 by 2 foot minimum on this card is already modest for a 9 to 11 pound body with ear leather spread beside it. Give more floor if you can. Do not stack furniture in the only open space and leave the ears nowhere to lie.

Do not overthink your English Lop Rabbit's diet to the point of paralysis.

Training Advice

Ear canals need checking. The canal under a heavy lop ear stays warm and poorly aired, and wax, odor, or head shaking means the ear needs a look, not a cotton swab driven downward. This page does not include a drug dose. A rabbit vet can see the canal. A keeper's job is to notice the head tilt, the scratch, and the smell early. Long ears are not a funnel you clean like a water bottle.

Grooming Essentials

Diet is hay, pellets, and vegetables, with hay unlimited so the teeth and the gut keep up. Social needs are pairs. Introduce a partner carefully. A scuffle that would bruise a short-eared rabbit can tear an English lop ear. Moderate activity means they need exercise that does not turn into a chase across a room full of chair legs. Intermediate care is the ears plus the ordinary large-rabbit work, held for the 5 to 7 years on the card.

Cost of Ownership

Some English Lop Rabbit owners spend well below these figures; others spend significantly more due to health issues or premium product choices. The smartest financial move is setting up an emergency fund early — even a modest one — so an unexpected vet bill does not become a crisis.

Budgeting for an English Lop Rabbit is easiest once you understand the spending curve. The first twelve months absorb the bulk of startup costs — an appropriate enclosure, bedding, an initial vet checkup, feeding supplies, and enrichment accessories. Beyond that, you are primarily covering food, bedding, routine health checks, and enrichment supplies, which are far more predictable and manageable.
