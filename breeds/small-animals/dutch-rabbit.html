Dutch Rabbit Overview

The Dutch Rabbit is a small-medium (3.5-5.5 lbs) rabbit that makes an excellent pet for beginners and families.

Dutch Rabbits thrive with companionship and are best kept with compatible cage mates.

The Dutch rabbit is a domestic breed marked with a white belt and colored cheeks, not a nationality of wild rabbit and not any rabbit that happens to live in the Netherlands. A brown cottontail does not become Dutch because of geography. This card lists a small to medium adult, 3.5 to 5.5 pounds, a lifespan of 5 to 8 years, moderate activity, beginner care, and a minimum of 4 by 2 feet. The belt, the cheeks, and the colored hindquarters are the pattern. A rabbit without that white saddle is a different breed or a mix.

Hay is the diet that matters. Pellets and vegetables are on the card beside it, and they stay in supporting roles. The teeth keep growing for the whole 5 to 8 years. A Dutch rabbit that lives on pellets and sweet mixes still weighs only a few pounds, so a molar spur or a gut slowdown shows up fast. Unlimited grass hay, a small pellet portion, and greens the rabbit actually eats are the ordinary bowl. Treats are not a personality.

Training Advice

Space is the 4 by 2 foot minimum, which is a floor a 3.5 to 5.5 pound rabbit can lie flat on. Smaller is not acceptable because the breed is called small-medium. They still stretch, hop, and need a solid surface. Wire floors hurt a light rabbit as surely as a heavy one. The card wants them kept as pairs or in a small group. Bond rabbits to rabbits. A guinea pig is the wrong roommate: different language, different diet, and a body that loses a kick.

Grooming Essentials

Activity is moderate and the care level is beginner only when the hay and the floor are already in place. They need time outside the pen in a rabbit-proofed room. Cords, baseboards, and houseplants are all on the menu if the room is not ready. Colored cheeks and a white belt make them easy to photograph and easy to mistake for a hardy toy. They are a small domestic rabbit with a full set of rabbit rules.
