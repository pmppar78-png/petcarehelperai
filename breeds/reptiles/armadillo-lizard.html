A spiny ring from the Karoo

Armadillo lizards are Ouroborus cataphractus, formerly Cordylus cataphractus. They commonly reach 15 to 25 years, care is intermediate, and they are docile, slow, and social. The social part is real: they live in groups in rock crevices. The slow part ends when a predator, or a hand, arrives and the lizard seizes its own tail.

A 20 to 40 gallon tank with rock crevices, humidity of 30 to 50 percent, a basking spot near 100°F, and a cooler side in the 70s fits the species. UVB is part of the basking setup. They are insect eaters. Calcium and a varied bug list matter more than a fancy bowl. Water can be a dish plus an occasional light mist. A constantly wet tank is the wrong climate for a lizard from dry South African rock.

Captive-bred groups only

These lizards are protected and were hit hard by collection. Buy captive-bred, keep any paperwork, and do not add a wild animal to a group. Give the group space. One male with several females is the usual breeder pattern. Two pushy males in a small tank will fight even if the species is called social. Handling is optional. Many keepers never do it. The tail-bite display means stop.

A thin lizard that will not eat insects needs a check for bullying first, then a vet. ARAV lists clinics.

Armadillo lizard questions

Why does it bite its tail?

Defense. A threatened armadillo lizard grabs its tail and turns into a spiny ring. Let go of the idea of pulling the tail free. Give it cover and wait.

Can they live together?

Often yes, which is unusual. They are social. A group still needs more than one hide, more than one food spot, and no crowding.

Mostly insects. In the wild they eat a lot of termites. Crickets and roaches, calcium-dusted, are the captive stand-in. Variety helps.

Ouroborus cataphractus is a 3 to 4 inch girdled lizard from South Africa. The 15 to 25 year lifespan is the surprise in a palm-sized animal. Temperament is docile, social, and slow-moving, which is why people try to keep a small group. Social still means each lizard needs a retreat and a place on the heat. A 20 to 40 gallon long tank is the quick facts size. Floor space matters more than height. Stack flat rocks into stable crevices the lizards can wedge into, and cement or brace anything that could slide.

Insects for a lizard that rolls into a ring

. Small crickets, roaches, and similar prey, dusted with calcium, are the meals. Feed where you can see who eats. In a group, one bold lizard will take every cricket while a shyer animal fades in a crevice. Scatter food or tong-feed the ones that hang back. They are slow, so a feeding response is easy to misread as tameness. Support the body if you lift one. The tail and the spiny scales are the defense, and the famous ring posture is a shield, so prying a rolled lizard open is a good way to break spines.

Metabolic bone disease is the watch-item for a basking insect eater this long-lived. A soft jaw, tremors, or a lizard that stops climbing the rock pile needs a reptile veterinarian and a check of the UVB distance and the calcium dust. Captive-bred groups settle better than imports. Buy animals that already eat, and ask how many can honestly share the tank you built. A 15-year social lizard in a bare ten-gallon is a husbandry failure even if the species looks calm on the day you bring it home.
