Venom, stress, and a quiet cage

Rear fangs deliver venom meant for small prey. A chewing bite on a person can swell and hurt. Keep hands away from the head. These snakes also die of stress when the cage is busy, bare, or wrong. Captive-bred animals are uncommon. Imported adults are a welfare problem.

Any bite that swells, or a snake that hangs limp and refuses food, needs a doctor for you and a reptile vet for the snake. Clinic lists start at ARAV.

Vine snake questions

Which vine snake is sold as a pet?

Usually an Asian vine snake, Ahaetulla, or a New World vine snake, Oxybelis. They are different genera. One size range has to stand in for several species, so ask the seller for the scientific name.

Are vine snakes venomous?

They are rear-fanged. The venom subdues lizards and can cause local swelling in people. Do not let one chew on a hand. They are a specialist snake for keepers who already understand a rear-fanged bite.

Will they eat mice?

Usually no. The diet is lizards and frogs. That is a hard, ethically messy menu, and it is why these snakes die in ordinary collections.
