A group of thin, rear-fanged snakes

Vine snake on a price list might be an Asian Ahaetulla or a New World Oxybelis. Both are long, skinny, day-active snakes with pointed heads. They run about 3 to 6 feet, they often live 8 to 12 years, and the care is advanced. They live in branches, and a rough grab injures them. None of that is one species with one correct temperature. Get the scientific name before you trust a single setup.

They need a tall cage with branches, temperatures around 75 to 85°F, and humidity around 60 to 70 percent as a starting band. Airflow matters. A sealed fog box grows mold on a snake that cannot climb away from it. The diet is the real barrier: lizards and frogs. Feeding wild lizards is illegal in places and cruel as a system. Some keepers use captive-bred anoles. Many snakes never switch to rodents. If you cannot accept that diet, do not buy the snake.

Venom, stress, and a quiet cage

Rear fangs deliver venom meant for small prey. A chewing bite on a person can swell and hurt. Keep hands away from the head. These snakes also die of stress when the cage is busy, bare, or wrong. Captive-bred animals are uncommon. Imported adults are a welfare problem.

Any bite that swells, or a snake that hangs limp and refuses food, needs a doctor for you and a reptile vet for the snake. Clinic lists start at ARAV.

Vine snake questions

Which vine snake is sold as a pet?

Usually an Asian vine snake, Ahaetulla, or a New World vine snake, Oxybelis. They are different genera. One size range has to stand in for several species, so ask the seller for the scientific name.

Are vine snakes venomous?

They are rear-fanged. The venom subdues lizards and can cause local swelling in people. Do not let one chew on a hand. They are not a beginner bite lesson.

Will they eat mice?

Usually no. The diet is lizards and frogs. That is a hard, ethically messy menu, and it is why these snakes die in ordinary collections.
