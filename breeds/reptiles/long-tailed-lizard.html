Long-tailed lizards are about 10 to 12 inches, mostly tail, and often live 5 to 8 years in a tall enclosure of 20 gallons or more. An active, flighty insect eater needs 75 to 85°F on a thermostat, humidity around 60 to 70 percent, UVB, and small insects with calcium.

Long-Tailed Lizard Habitat Essentials

A tall planted tank and a UVB tube match a small basking lizard that spends its length in the tail. Small feeders dusted with calcium, offered where you can see them eaten, are the diet that goes with 75 to 85°F and 60 to 70 percent humidity.

Mostly tail, very fast

Long-tailed grass lizards in the pet trade are usually Takydromus sexlineatus. They are about 10 to 12 inches, they often live 5 to 8 years, and the care is beginner. The body is small. The speed is not. They bolt at a shadow. A tall 20-gallon tank with branches, 75 to 85°F, and humidity around 60 to 70 percent suits them.

UVB and a basking spot keep the bones in order. Small crickets and other small insects, calcium-dusted, are the diet. They drink from droplets. Mist the leaves. A tight lid matters because a gap is an exit. House them where you can watch and not where children are expected to hold them.

Groups only if you are watching

Keepers sometimes keep several in a large planted cage. That works until one animal monopolizes the basking spot or the food. Plenty of cover and more than one warm perch reduce the bullying. Wild-caught shipments are the usual source and a common reason these lizards arrive thin. Eat-and-watch is the right use. A handling collection is the wrong one.

A lizard that stops hunting needs a temperature check first, then the appetite guide. Reptile vets are listed at ARAV.

Long-tailed lizard questions

Why is the tail so long?

10 to 12 inches, and most of that is tail. Grabbing the tail makes it break off. Scoop from below if you must move the lizard, or better, guide it into a cup.

Do they like handling?

No. They are active and flighty. They are a watch-them lizard. Chasing one around a room is how tails and toes get hurt.

Small insects

Small insects. Crickets that are too large get refused or cause a problem. Dust with calcium and offer UVB.

A tall tank for a mostly-tail lizard

Long-tailed lizards on this page are small, about 10 to 12 inches, and most of that length is tail. Care is beginner. Temperament is active and flighty. A tall enclosure of 20 gallons or more, with branches and cover, matches how they move. A tight lid is mandatory. They are fast, and the tail can break if you grab. The cage is for watching. Handling stays a rare cup-move for cage maintenance.

Small insects and the jaw

The diet is small insects. Pinhead to small crickets and similar prey, dusted with calcium, are the meals. Gut-load them. Offer only what is eaten, because leftover crickets nip a sleeping lizard. One male per visible space if they display at each other. A flighty group in a bare tank spends the day hiding and losing weight.

A soft jaw or a bent limb on a Long-Tailed Lizard is the metabolic bone disease sign to set next to UVB distance and the 75-85°F basking spot. A soft jaw, a kink, or a lizard that misses branches means UVB or calcium failed. Five to eight years is short, and a lizard that stops hunting for several days needs a reptile veterinarian while it still has weight. The tail is most of the animal and most of the injury list. Build the tall tank, set 75 to 85°F, and keep your hands out.
