Box Turtle Habitat Essentials

The pen supplies that matter are a secure wall, soil for burrowing, and a soak dish a 5 to 7 inch turtle can leave. Respiratory noise or bubbles at the nose are the health sign to act on when a woodland-style box turtle is kept too cool and dry for that 60 to 80 percent humidity.

Several turtles, one common name

Box turtle in a shop can mean an eastern box turtle, a three-toed, a Gulf Coast, a Florida, or an ornate. All are Terrapene. Common pets land around 5 to 7 inches and 30 to 50 years or more. A hinged shell that closes is the shared trick. The moisture they need is not shared. Ornate box turtles want a drier pen than a three-toed from a wet woodland.

They eat insects, worms, greens, and a little fruit. They really are omnivores. Dog food as the whole diet makes them fat and sick. Offer calcium, UVB or outdoor sun, and temperatures from about 70 to 85°F with a warmer bask. Humidity of 60 to 80 percent fits the woodland species. Drop it for an ornate, which has its own page and a drier range.

A pen on the ground, and a legal animal

A 4 by 2 foot indoor box is the minimum listed. An outdoor pen with shade, a moist burrow, and walls sunk into the soil is the setup these turtles actually use. They are escape artists and they overheat in a glass tank in the sun. Care level is intermediate because of the lifespan and the species mix, not because they are aggressive.

Wild box turtles are protected in many states. A turtle from the roadside belongs on the roadside, headed the way it was going. Captive-bred only. Swollen eyes, a soft shell, or a turtle that will not eat is a case for a reptile vet via ARAV.

Box turtle questions

Which box turtle is this page about?

Several. Eastern, three-toed, Gulf Coast, Florida, and ornate box turtles are all sold under the short name. Humidity and winter care are not the same for each.

Can I keep a box turtle I found outside?

In much of the United States it is illegal to take a wild box turtle home. Wild animals also carry parasites and often fail in a tank. Look for a captive-bred turtle with papers.

Do box turtles need water?

They are not pond turtles. They need a shallow dish they can soak in and climb out of, plus damp soil for burrowing. Deep water is a drowning risk.

A pen on the ground

Box turtle is a common name for several Terrapene species. Adults on this page are about 5 to 7 inches and often live 30 to 50 years or more. Care is intermediate. Temperament is shy and personable, which describes a turtle that learns a feeding spot and still panics if it is grabbed from above. The enclosure is about 4 by 2 feet indoors or outdoors. More floor is better. Walls should be smooth enough that a climber cannot walk over them, and outdoor pens need a cover because raccoons dig.

Keep a gradient of 70 to 85°F. Indoors, a basking lamp on a thermostat should warm one end while the other stays cooler inside that band. Outdoors, sun and shade do the same job if the turtle can choose. These animals bask. UVB from a reptile tube indoors, or unfiltered sunlight outdoors, is daily. A window filters out the wavelengths the shell needs. Humidity of 60 to 80 percent suits the woodland species people usually mean. Soil they can burrow into, kept damp below and drier on top, is the shelter. A shallow soak dish that the turtle can exit prevents drowning. Deep pools are a bad idea for a turtle that walks more than it swims.

Insects, plants, and breathing

The diet is omnivore: insects, vegetation, and fruit. Earthworms, snails where they are safe, and other invertebrates can lead the protein side. Greens and other plants fill the bowl. Fruit is a smaller share. Calcium on the insects, and a cuttlebone some turtles nibble, supports the shell. Feed on a schedule you can see, because a shy turtle hides food and then lives on leftovers that spoil.

Recheck 70-85°F and humidity 60 to 80 percent the same day at an adult size around 5 to 7 inches. Bubbles at the nostrils, a wheeze, or a turtle that stretches the neck and gapes needs a veterinarian who sees chelonians. Cold, dry housing is a common cause. Metabolic bone disease and a soft shell also follow when UVB and calcium are skipped, so the lamp is prevention. Protected wild box turtles should stay in the woods. Buy captive-bred animals and check the law before the sale. A 40-year turtle in a 4 by 2 foot pen is a housing plan, a humidity plan, and a winter plan with heat and light.
