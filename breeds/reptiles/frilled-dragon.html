The frill is a stop sign

Frilled dragons are Chlamydosaurus kingii, the big-headed arboreal lizards of northern Australia and southern New Guinea. Adults are about 24 to 36 inches, tail included. They usually last 10 to 20 years, and care is intermediate to advanced. The famous ruff opens when the animal is afraid. A pet that displays every time you walk in is a pet that is not settled.

A fair cage is at least 4 by 2 by 4 feet and should be taller than a terrestrial lizard's tank. Give branches that can hold an adult, a basking surface in the 90s to around 100°F, a cooler zone, UVB, and humidity of 40 to 70 percent. They are insect eaters first. Some take greens or a little fruit. Calcium is mandatory. A water dish and misting give them a drink. The lid and the door have to be secure. A frightened frilled dragon bolts.

Captive-bred, and a quiet room

Wild export from Australia is not the legal source of pets. Buy captive-bred. Give the lizard height and a retreat it can reach without climbing through your hands. Short, predictable sessions beat a daily chase around the living room. Two adults in one cage is a dominance problem. House them singly unless you have a reason and the space.

A frill that stays half-open, a limp jaw, or a lizard off its insects is a medical or a UVB problem. Clinics are listed at ARAV.

Frilled dragon questions

When does the frill open?

When the lizard is startled or threatened. The flash is a warning display. A frilled dragon that flashes you wants distance. Step back and give the lizard the space the display is asking for.

Are they handleable?

They are nervous and flighty, and some tame with patience. Many stay nervous. A fall from shoulder height injures them. Keep handling low and over a soft surface if you do it at all.

What size cage?

At least 4 by 2 by 4 feet, and taller than a ground lizard's tank. They are arboreal. A long low tank is the wrong shape.

Height for a lizard that flares

Temperament is nervous and flighty, and some individuals tame with patience. The enclosure minimum is 4 by 2 by 4 feet and vertical. They need height to climb and room to turn without striking the glass when they startle. A frill display is a warning. The lizard wants distance. Step back and let the animal settle on a high perch.

Temperatures of 80 to 100°F cover the basking gradient. Put the basking lamp on a thermostat and read the branch with an infrared thermometer. The cool lower zone should stay inside the stated range so a nervous lizard can get off the heat without leaving the cage's comfort band. These are basking lizards. A UVB tube over the upper perches, replaced on the maker's schedule, is required. Humidity of 40 to 70 percent should cycle: a bit of moisture, then drying, with airflow in a tall cage. A water bowl and mist on leaves give them something to drink.

Insects and a soft jaw

The diet is primarily insects. Roaches and crickets, gut-loaded and dusted with calcium, are the meals. A flighty dragon may only eat when the room is quiet. Tong-feed if the strike is wild, and keep hands out of the path. Captive-bred animals settle better than imports. One lizard per cage unless you have a very large setup and a plan for the animal that never gets the hot perch.

Metabolic bone disease is the health watch-item because frilled dragons bask. A soft jaw, tremors, or a lizard that misses branches needs a reptile veterinarian and a check of UVB distance and calcium. Respiratory noise in a tall, stagnant, cold cage is the other failure mode, so the thermostat stays on the basking branch. Build the 4 by 2 by 4 foot vertical space before the lizard arrives. A nervous adult in a short tank spends its life against the lid.
