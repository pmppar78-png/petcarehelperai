Wood Turtle Habitat Essentials

A pen with soil, a filtered water area, and UVB or sun over a warm spot matches a 5 to 9 inch wood turtle's 65 to 80°F range. Bubbles at the nose or a week without food are respiratory and appetite signs for a veterinarian who sees chelonians; this species is protected first.

Protected first, pet second

Wood turtles, Glyptemys insculpta, are the brown, sculpted-shell turtles of northeastern streams and woods. They are known for stomping the ground to bring up worms, and for learning a pen faster than people expect. They are also one of the more protected turtles in North America. Collection, sale, and often possession of wild animals are illegal. This page is husbandry for a protected turtle, with wild collection left out.

If you already keep a legal, documented captive, the husbandry is semi-aquatic. Plan a 4 by 2 foot space, temperatures about 65 to 80°F, and humidity around 60 to 70 percent. They walk as much as they swim. Half land, half clean water, and a ramp out. Care is intermediate because the water quality and the cool temperature band are easy to get wrong.

Worms, plants, and a filter

They eat insects, worms, greens, and a little fruit, which is a real omnivore diet. Fish as a staple is a poor idea. UVB over the land side and a filter on the water side are the equipment that matters. A turtle that basks all day with shut eyes, or that skips food, needs a reptile vet. Finding one starts at ARAV.

Wood turtle questions

Can I keep a wood turtle I found?

No. Wood turtles are protected, and taking one from the wild is illegal across their range. Leave it where it is, and do not post the spot.

How long do wood turtles live?

40 to 60 years. That span is one reason wild adults cannot be replaced by a pet-shop hatchling.

Do wood turtles need a pond?

They need both. A land area for walking and a clean water area deep enough to soak, with an easy climb-out. A dry box or a bare aquarium both fail them.

A legal turtle's semi-aquatic pen

Temperament is intelligent and active. Husbandry on this page is for people who already hold a legal captive-bred animal and need the temperatures and the diet right.

The enclosure is about 4 by 2 feet and semi-aquatic. Give land with soil and cover, plus a water area they can enter and leave, with a filter. Temperatures of 65 to 80°F are cool compared with tropical water turtles. A basking lamp on a thermostat can warm one end while the rest of the pen stays in the band. They use sun outdoors when the season allows. UVB indoors over the warm land spot matters because they bask. Humidity of 60 to 70 percent should keep the land from drying to dust. A deep, warm tropical pool is the wrong model. They walk, they soak, and they need both.

Worms, plants, and breathing

The diet is omnivore: insects, vegetation, and fruit. Earthworms are the famous item. Calcium supports the shell. Feed where you can see an intelligent turtle that will learn the routine and also hide food. A secure cover matters. They climb and they remember exits. Outdoor time needs raccoon-proof walls.

Hear a click from a Wood Turtle and book a reptile veterinarian, then read the 65-80°F gradient again and humidity 60 to 70 percent. Bubbles at the nose, a wheeze, or a turtle that stops eating for a week needs a veterinarian who sees chelonians. Cold water with no warm land spot is a common setup error. A soft shell means UVB and calcium were skipped, so the lamp stays in the indoor plan. Forty to sixty years of a 5 to 9 inch protected turtle is a 4 by 2 foot semi-aquatic pen at 65 to 80°F, kept legal for the whole life of the animal.
