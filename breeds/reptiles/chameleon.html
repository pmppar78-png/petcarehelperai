Pick a species before you pick a cage

This page is the chameleon family as it shows up in pet shops, not one animal. Veiled, panther, and Jackson's chameleons are the common three, and each has a more specific page or a more specific need. Figure 4 to 24 inches and 3 to 10 years, because a tiny Brookesia and a large veiled male are both chameleons. Care runs from intermediate to advanced. They do poorly with a cage mate, and they stress easily. Both of those points hold across the group.

Screen cages, not glass boxes, are the usual home. The minimum listed is 2 by 2 by 4 feet, which is a veiled or panther adult size and nonsense for a pygmy. UVB, a basking branch, and water delivered as drips or mist are shared. Temperatures of 70 to 85°F are only a middle. Jackson's want cooler air. Veileds use a hotter bask. Copying one setup onto the other species is how chameleons die with a full cricket cup below them.

Insects, one animal, no outdoor release

They eat insects. Gut-load those insects and dust calcium. A fruit cup on its own fails as a veiled-chameleon diet and fails as a Jackson's diet too. House one chameleon where it cannot see another male. Handling is a stress for most of them. Watch the colors and the eyes instead. Do not release an unwanted pet. Several species have already become outdoor problems in warm climates.

Eyes sunk in, a lizard on the floor, or a week of missed feeds is urgent. Use the reptile appetite guide and a clinic from ARAV.

Chameleon questions

Which chameleon should I buy?

Name the species first. Veiled chameleons tolerate heat better. Jackson's need a cooler room. Panthers sit between them and vary by locale. A shop label that only says chameleon leaves the keeper without a usable care sheet.

Can two chameleons share a cage?

No. They are solitary and easily stressed. A pair in one screen cage is a fight or a courtship you did not plan, followed by a neglected animal.

How long do chameleons live?

Figure 3 to 10 years, because the species differ. Short lives are normal. A cage that is wrong makes them shorter.

The shared enclosure start is a screen cage around 2 by 2 by 4 feet. Glass tanks stagnate. Branches should run horizontally and diagonally so the animal can move between a warmer basking branch and a cooler retreat. The printed range is 70 to 85°F and is species dependent. Put the basking lamp on a thermostat and measure the branch, because a small chameleon overheats faster than the room thermometer suggests. These are basking lizards. A linear UVB tube over the screen, at the distance the bulb maker lists, is required. Humidity of 50 to 80 percent is also species dependent. Mist or drip so the chameleon can drink, then let the cage dry. Airflow is part of hydration.

Insects, one animal, and the jaw

The diet is insects. Gut-load them and dust calcium. A fruit cup is a poor stand-in for a veiled chameleon and a poor stand-in for a Jackson's chameleon. House one animal where it cannot see a rival male. Handling stresses most of them. Watch the colors at rest and the eyes. Released pets have already become outdoor problems in warm climates.

Metabolic bone disease is the health watch-item because chameleons bask. A wavy jaw, a weak grip, or tremors mean UVB or calcium failed. Eyes closed by day, a perch on the floor, and missed meals are urgent. Females of several species lay eggs even without a male and can become egg-bound. Have a lay bin and a reptile veterinarian before that week. The 2 by 2 by 4 foot screen cage is only a fair start if the species you named actually fits it. A tiny species and a large veiled do not share one finished setup.
