Chameleon Overview

Chameleons are among the most fascinating and unique reptiles available in the pet trade. Known for their color-changing abilities, independently moving eyes, projectile tongues, and zygodactylous feet (toes fused in groups of two and three), they're unlike any other lizard. However, their specific care requirements make them challenging pets.

Chameleons are primarily arboreal and need tall, well-ventilated enclosures with careful attention to humidity, temperature gradients, and UVB lighting. They're solitary animals that become stressed by handling and other chameleons.

Natural Habitat & Origin

Chameleons are found in various habitats:

Common Pet Species

  • Veiled Chameleon (Chamaeleo calyptratus): Yemen/Saudi Arabia - hardiest, good for intermediates
  • Panther Chameleon (Furcifer pardalis): Madagascar - colorful, moderately hardy
  • Jackson's Chameleon (Trioceros jacksonii): East Africa - live-bearing, three horns
  • Carpet Chameleon (Furcifer lateralis): Madagascar - smaller, shorter-lived

Natural Environment

  • Habitat: Forest canopies, bushland, sometimes montane regions
  • Behavior: Strictly arboreal, solitary
  • Activity: Diurnal
  • Climate: Varies from tropical to semi-arid depending on species

Enclosure Setup & Requirements

Chameleons have specific enclosure needs:

Enclosure Type & Size

  • Type: SCREEN enclosure essential for ventilation
  • Minimum: 18x18x36" for smaller species
  • Standard: 24x24x48" for most species
  • Large species: 36x24x48" or larger
  • Never: Glass tanks (poor ventilation, stagnant air)

Essential Furnishings

  • Numerous horizontal and diagonal branches at various levels
  • Live plants (pothos, ficus, schefflera, hibiscus)
  • Basking branch within appropriate distance of heat lamp
  • NO water dish (chameleons won't drink from standing water)
  • Drainage system or tray for water collection

Ventilation is Critical

Chameleons are susceptible to respiratory infections from stagnant air. Screen enclosures with good air flow are essential. Glass enclosures, even with screen tops, are generally unsuitable.

Temperature & Lighting

Temperature and UVB needs vary by species:

Temperature Requirements

ZoneTemperature
Basking Spot85-90°F (29-32°C) - varies by species
Ambient72-78°F (22-26°C)
Nighttime60-70°F (16-21°C) - drop important for many species

Species-Specific Temperatures

  • Veiled: Tolerate higher temps, basking to 90°F
  • Panther: Moderate temps, basking 82-88°F
  • Jackson's: Cooler temps preferred, basking 80-85°F

UVB Lighting (ESSENTIAL)

  • Type: T5 HO 5.0 or 6% linear UVB
  • Placement: Inside or on top of screen, spanning 1/2-2/3 enclosure length
  • Duration: 10-12 hours daily
  • Replace: Every 6 months

Humidity & Hydration

Chameleons have unique hydration needs:

Humidity Requirements

  • Daytime: 50-60%
  • After misting: Spike to 80-100%
  • Drying period: Must dry out between mistings

Hydration Methods

  • Misting: 2-3+ times daily for 2-5 minutes
  • Automatic misting system: Highly recommended
  • Dripper: Drip system on plants for drinking
  • Note: Chameleons drink droplets from leaves, not standing water

Hydration Systems

ZooMed ReptiRain - Automatic misting system | ExoTerra Dripper - Drip system for drinking

Diet & Feeding

Chameleons are insectivores requiring varied diets:

Feeder Insects

  • Staples: Crickets, dubia roaches, black soldier fly larvae
  • Variety: Silkworms, hornworms, mantids, stick insects
  • Treats: Waxworms, superworms (sparingly)
  • Avoid: Wild-caught insects, fireflies (toxic)

Feeding Schedule

  • Juveniles: Daily, as many appropriately-sized insects as they'll eat
  • Adults: 3-5 insects every other day
  • Method: Cup feeding or free-range in enclosure

Supplementation

  • Calcium without D3: Every feeding
  • Calcium with D3: 2x monthly
  • Multivitamin: 2x monthly (alternating with D3)
  • All-in-one option: Repashy Calcium Plus ICB

Health Issues & Common Problems

Chameleons are prone to several health issues:

Metabolic Bone Disease (MBD)

From inadequate UVB and/or calcium. Signs: weak grip, curved limbs, inability to climb, tremors. Prevention is key.

Respiratory Infections

From poor ventilation, high humidity without drying, or incorrect temperatures. Signs: gaping, wheezing, mucus, puffed throat.

Dehydration

Very common and dangerous. Signs: sunken eyes, lethargy, loss of appetite, wrinkled skin. Increase misting duration and frequency.

Egg Binding (Females)

Females produce eggs even without males. Without proper laying site, eggs can become stuck. Provide lay bin for all female veiled and panther chameleons.

Stress

Signs: dark colors, closed eyes during day, gaping, hiding. Caused by handling, other chameleons, reflections, or improper husbandry.

Handling & Temperament

Chameleons are NOT handling pets:

  • Stress sensitivity: Handling causes significant stress
  • Observation animals: Best enjoyed from a distance
  • Necessary handling: For health checks, enclosure cleaning only
  • Approach: From below, let them climb onto hand
  • Signs of stress: Hissing, gaping, dark colors, puffing up
  • Individual variation: Some tolerate handling better than others

Breeding Information

Breeding chameleons requires careful planning:

  • Sexual maturity: 8-12 months for most species
  • Introduction: Brief, supervised pairings only
  • Aggression: Males can injure females if left together
  • Egg-laying species: Veiled, panther - need laying bins
  • Live-bearing: Jackson's chameleon
  • Clutch size: 20-80+ eggs depending on species
  • Incubation: 6-12 months for some species

Is This Reptile Right for You?

Chameleons Are Great For:

  • Experienced keepers who enjoy observation over handling
  • Those who can invest in proper equipment (misting, lighting)
  • People fascinated by unique reptile behavior
  • Keepers with time for daily feeding and monitoring

Chameleons May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Beginners to reptile keeping
  • Those wanting a handleable pet
  • People unable to maintain misting schedules
  • Anyone expecting a hardy, forgiving species
  • Those on a tight budget
