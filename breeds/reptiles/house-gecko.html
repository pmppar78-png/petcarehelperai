Two geckos, one common name

House gecko here covers the Mediterranean house gecko, Hemidactylus turcicus, and the common house gecko, Hemidactylus frenatus. They can live 5 to 10 years, care is beginner, and they are shy, fast, and nocturnal. They stick to glass, hunt insects at night, and have spread with people around the world. A pet tank should not become the way they spread further. Do not release them.

A vertical 10 to 20 gallon tank at 75 to 85°F, with humidity of 50 to 70 percent, is enough to start. They do not need a desert bake. They need cover, a tight lid, and calcium-dusted insects. A small water dish and a light mist give them something to drink. UVB is less critical than for a day gecko and still reasonable at a low level. Nighttime heat should not come from a bright white lamp that ruins the hunt.

Watch them after the lights go down

Handling is how you lose the tail or the gecko. Feed at dusk. Several females may share a large tank. Two males usually fight. Eggs get glued into crevices. Check before you throw away cork bark. Wild-caught groups arrive with parasites. Captive-bred is kinder if you can find it.

A gecko that sits on the floor in daylight and ignores crickets is sick or cold. Start with the not-eating page and a clinic from ARAV.

House gecko questions

Two species share this page: the Mediterranean house gecko, Hemidactylus turcicus, and the common house gecko, H. frenatus. Both are small nocturnal insect eaters. Confirm which one is in the cup.

Do they chirp?

Yes. Both species make small calls, mostly at night. The sound is normal. A gecko that is silent, thin, and stuck on the floor is the one to check.

Can children hold them?

They are shy and fast, and the tail drops if they are grabbed. The honest use is a vertical tank you watch after dark.

Night hunting in a warm vertical tank

House gecko here covers the Mediterranean house gecko, Hemidactylus turcicus, and the common house gecko, H. frenatus. Adults are about 3 to 5 inches and can live 5 to 10 years. Care is beginner. Temperament is shy, fast, and nocturnal. They stick to glass and hunt insects after dark. A vertical 10 to 20 gallon tank with a tight lid is the start. Cover, cork, and a climbing surface matter more than open floor. Two males usually fight. Several females may share a larger tank. Eggs get glued into crevices, so check cork before you throw it out.

Hold 75 to 85°F. If the room is cooler, add a low heat source on a thermostat and keep it from blasting a white light through the night. They need darkness to hunt. A low UVB tube is optional daylight and is less critical than it is for a day gecko. It can stay on a day cycle at a low level. A desert basking chart does not fit these small nocturnal geckos. Humidity of 50 to 70 percent, a small water dish, and a light mist give them something to drink.

Insects and stuck toes

. Calcium-dusted crickets and other small prey, offered at dusk, match a 3 to 5 inch gecko. Remove uneaten prey that can chew on a sleeping lizard. Handling is how the tail drops or the gecko escapes. The honest use is a tank you watch after the lights go down. Do not release them. Both species already spread with people, and a pet tank should not be the next route outdoors.

Weigh the latest House Gecko (Mediterranean Gecko) shed against 75-85°F and humidity 50 to 70 percent, including whether the eye caps left with the skin. Stuck skin on toes is common when humidity sits under 50 percent for weeks. Recheck the 75 to 85°F band and see a reptile veterinarian if the animal stays down. Chirping at night is normal. Silence plus a thin body is the combination to investigate. Five to ten years is the span to plan, even though the lizard is small.
