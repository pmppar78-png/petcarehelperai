A genus that wants a true desert setup of its own

Uromastyx on a shop tag might be an ornate, a Mali, an Egyptian, or something rarer. Adult size spans about 10 to 30 inches. They commonly reach 15 to 25 years or more, and care is intermediate. All of them are dry, hot, herbivorous lizards with a spiked tail they swing. That shared plan is real. A single cage size still does not fit a 10-inch ornate and a large Egyptian equally well. The minimum listed is 4 by 2 by 2 feet. A big species needs more.

Basking surface temperature has to be genuinely hot, often well above 110°F and toward the 120s, inside the 80 to 130°F gradient. Measure the rock with a temperature gun. Humidity of 10 to 35 percent means no misting system and no soaked substrate. They dig. Slate and a deep dry dig box both get used. The diet is greens, seeds, and lentils, with calcium. Insects are not the staple. UVB is required.

One lizard, captive-bred

They are generally docile and still a one-lizard animal in its own cage. A tail whip is a defense the lizard uses when it is alarmed. Buy captive-bred and get the species name in writing. Wild-caught uromastyx arrive dehydrated and stay wild. A colorful adult male is territorial toward other males even through glass. Give them a visual barrier.

A uromastyx that stays cold, looks puffy, or stops eating greens is often a temperature or a humidity problem first. If fixing the bask does not fix the lizard, call a vet from ARAV.

Uromastyx questions

Which uromastyx is the common pet?

Ornate, Mali, and Egyptian uromastyx are the usual three, and they do not grow to the same size. Adults run 10 to 30 inches, because the genus is wide. Ask for the species.

Do they eat bugs?

They are herbivores. Lentils, seeds, and greens are the diet. A cricket habit is the wrong picture of this lizard, even if a baby snaps at a moving pellet.

How low should humidity be?

A damp tank causes respiratory and skin infections. Skip the mister. Give a hot, dry basking rock.

Very hot, very dry, all plants

Uromastyx is a genus from North Africa, the Middle East, and South Asia. Size runs about 10 to 30 inches because species differ. Care is intermediate. Temperament is generally docile, and they are still housed alone. A tail whip is a defense. The enclosure minimum is 4 by 2 by 2 feet, deeper and longer for the big species. They want a true desert setup: rock, a burrow, and a basking surface that gets honestly hot. A generic tropical lizard tank is the wrong build.

Put the basking lamp on a thermostat and read the surface with an infrared thermometer. The cool burrow has to stay available so the lizard can leave a 130°F rock. These are extreme basking lizards. High-output UVB over the hot zone, at the manufacturer's distance, replaced on schedule, is mandatory. Humidity of 10 to 35 percent is low. Wet substrate and frequent misting cause skin and respiratory problems in this genus. A water dish can be offered and will often be ignored. Many drink from food. Vegetables should be fresh enough to carry moisture without turning the cage damp.

Greens and a soft jaw

The diet is herbivore. Suitable greens, seeds, and flowers, with calcium, are the meals. Insects are a minor item at most for many species and a problem if they become the staple. Confirm the species. Give a visual barrier.

Metabolic bone disease in Uromastyx shows up when UVB or calcium dusting slips. The basking target on this page is 80-130°F at an adult size around 10 to 30 inches. A soft jaw or swollen limbs in a desert herbivore means UVB or calcium failed despite the heat. A reptile veterinarian should see a lizard that stops eating greens while the basking surface still reads in the high end of the 80 to 130°F gradient. Fifteen to twenty-five years of a docile uromastyx is a 4 by 2 by 2 foot minimum, species by species, hot and dry on purpose.
