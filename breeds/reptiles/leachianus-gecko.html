The biggest gecko, often the loudest

Leachianus geckos, Rhacodactylus leachianus, come from New Caledonia. Care is intermediate. Temperament is vocal and, for many lines, handleable. Some localities would rather bite. Ask the breeder about the parents' attitudes before you pay locality prices.

They want a tall cage, at least about 18 by 18 by 36 inches, with cork hollows they can cram into. Temperatures of 72 to 80°F are correct. Baking them like a bearded dragon is a common mistake. Humidity of 60 to 80 percent, good airflow, a commercial crested-gecko diet, and insects make the menu. A water dish and a mist give them something to drink. They are nocturnal enough that the show starts at dusk.

Thirty years is the expensive part

A hatchling is a cheap-looking animal next to the adult it becomes, and the adult may outlast the shelf you bought. Plan the 20 to 30 year span. Handle calmly if the individual allows it. Quit when it growls and gapes. Crested geckos and gargoyles are the smaller relatives. A leachianus will bully or eat a smaller Rhacodactylus. House them alone unless you are an experienced breeder with a plan.

Leachianus gecko questions

How big is a leachianus gecko?

Locality decides it. Island animals can be around 8 to 10 inches. Grande Terre animals are larger, and some pass 14 inches. Length runs 8 to 17 inches. That is the spread, not one animal.

Do they need high heat?

No. Keep temperatures around 72 to 80°F. A hot reptile room overheats them. Room temperature with a slight gradient is the usual goal.

Why is my leachianus growling?

The species is vocal. Barks and growls are advertising or annoyance. A gecko that growls and gapes wants space, quiet, and the door left alone.

A tall cool cage and a fruit diet

Leachianus geckos are the large New Caledonian species, about 8 to 17 inches depending on locale, with a 20 to 30 year lifespan. Care is intermediate. Temperament is vocal and handleable. The enclosure is about 18 by 18 by 36 inches and arboreal. Larger locales need the roomy end of that advice, with cork tubes they can hide in completely. A gecko that growls and gapes wants space. Barks and growls are advertising or annoyance, and they are normal.

Temperatures of 72 to 80°F should be the room or a lightly assisted cage on a thermostat. Overheating is the common failure, the same pattern as other New Caledonian geckos. A fluorescent over Leachianus Gecko can mark day and night while the thermostat holds 72 to 80°F and humidity 60 to 80 percent. A desert basking chart does not fit a nocturnal gecko that wants the 70s. Humidity of 60 to 80 percent comes from misting that dries back. Stagnant soak rots cork and can bother the skin. Airflow in a tall cage is part of the humidity plan.

Fruit mix, insects, and toes

The diet is a fruit-based complete gecko diet plus insects. Mix the diet fresh and remove it before it ferments. Dust insects with calcium. Locale size changes how much food disappears in a night, so weigh the gecko every few weeks. Handling can work with a calm adult if you support the body and stop when the gecko gapes. Thirty years is a long time to listen to a bark at night. Plan the room for that sound.

The Leachianus Gecko tail tip and the spectacles should come off in the same shed inside 72 to 80°F across 20 to 30 years. Stuck skin on toes and tail tips shows up when humidity sits under 60 percent or the gecko never finds a damp cork tube. A swollen digit needs a reptile veterinarian before the toe is lost. A gecko that stops taking the fruit mix and loses weight needs the same clinic, with the 72 to 80°F readings written down. Big, vocal, and handleable still means a tall cage and a thermostat that keeps the top from baking.
