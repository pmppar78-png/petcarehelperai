Giant Day Gecko Overview

The Giant Day Gecko (Phelsuma grandis) is one of the most visually spectacular gecko species kept in captivity. Native to Madagascar, these diurnal geckos display brilliant emerald green coloration with red markings and blue eye rings. As the largest of the Phelsuma genus, they can reach impressive sizes of 10-12 inches.

Unlike most gecko species, Giant Day Geckos are active during daylight hours, making them excellent display animals. Their vibrant colors and active nature provide endless entertainment. However, their fragile, tear-prone skin makes them primarily look-don't-touch pets, best suited for keepers who appreciate observation over handling.

Handling Caution

Giant Day Geckos have extremely fragile skin that can tear easily when handled. They are best kept as display animals and should only be handled when absolutely necessary. Their skin will regenerate but handling should be minimized.

Natural Habitat

Giant Day Geckos originate from tropical Madagascar:

  • Geographic Range: Northern Madagascar, also introduced to other islands
  • Environment: Tropical rainforests, plantations, and human settlements
  • Arboreal: Lives on trees, bamboo, and buildings
  • Climate: Warm and humid with seasonal variations
  • Activity: Diurnal baskers, hunting insects and lapping nectar

Enclosure Setup

Creating a proper environment is essential for these active geckos:

Enclosure Size

  • Single Adult: 24x18x36" minimum (height is crucial)
  • Pair: 36x18x36" or larger recommended
  • Type: Glass terrarium with screen top for ventilation
  • Orientation: Vertical setup is mandatory

Substrate Options

  • Bioactive: ABG mix with drainage layer (ideal)
  • Coconut Fiber: Maintains humidity well
  • Eco Earth: Budget-friendly option
  • Moss Layer: Sphagnum moss on top helps humidity

Decor and Furnishings

  • Bamboo Poles: Favorite climbing and basking surfaces
  • Live Plants: Pothos, bromeliads, snake plants, orchids
  • Cork Bark: Tubes and flats for hiding
  • Horizontal Branches: At various heights for basking
  • Background: Cork or foam background for climbing

Temperature & Lighting

Proper lighting is crucial for these diurnal geckos:

Temperature Requirements

  • Basking Spot: 90-95°F (32-35°C)
  • Warm Zone: 82-86°F (28-30°C)
  • Cool Zone: 75-80°F (24-27°C)
  • Night Drop: 70-75°F (21-24°C) acceptable

Lighting Requirements

  • UVB: ESSENTIAL - 5.0-10.0 UVB bulb required for vitamin D3 synthesis
  • Basking Light: Halogen or incandescent for heat
  • Photoperiod: 12-14 hours in summer, 10-12 hours in winter
  • LED Plant Lights: Beneficial for live plants

UVB Requirement

Unlike nocturnal geckos, Giant Day Geckos REQUIRE UVB lighting. Without it, they will develop metabolic bone disease. Replace UVB bulbs every 6-12 months as output diminishes.

Humidity Requirements

Maintaining proper humidity cycles is essential:

  • Daytime: 50-65% humidity
  • Morning/Evening Spike: 70-80% after misting
  • Misting Schedule: 2-3 times daily with dechlorinated water
  • Drying Period: Allow enclosure to dry between mistings
  • Water Source: Mist leaves and glass; they drink droplets

Diet & Nutrition

Giant Day Geckos are omnivores with diverse dietary needs:

Fruit/Nectar Diet

  • Commercial CGD: Pangea, Repashy - 60% of diet
  • Fruit Purees: Mango, papaya, banana (occasional)
  • Honey/Nectar: Small amounts as treats
  • Frequency: Offer fresh CGD every 2-3 days

Insects

  • Crickets: Appropriately sized, gut-loaded
  • Dubia Roaches: Excellent nutrition
  • Black Soldier Fly Larvae: High calcium
  • Frequency: 2-3 times per week
  • Dusting: Calcium every feeding, D3 twice weekly

Feeding Tips

  • Use magnetic feeding ledges near basking spots
  • Feed insects in the morning when geckos are active
  • Remove uneaten insects to prevent stress

Common Health Issues

Be aware of these health concerns:

Metabolic Bone Disease (MBD)

  • Cause: Lack of UVB, calcium, or D3
  • Signs: Weak limbs, kinked tail/spine, tremors
  • Prevention: Proper UVB and supplementation

Skin Tears

  • Cause: Rough handling, sharp decor, stress
  • Signs: Visible skin loss, raw patches
  • Treatment: Keep clean, anti-bacterial ointment, will heal

Egg Binding

  • Cause: Calcium deficiency, no laying site
  • Signs: Swollen abdomen, lethargy, straining
  • Prevention: Proper calcium, cork bark laying sites

Respiratory Infections

  • Cause: Poor ventilation, cold temperatures
  • Signs: Wheezing, mucus, lethargy
  • Treatment: Veterinary antibiotics required

Handling & Temperament

Understanding their nature prevents stress and injury:

Temperament

  • Skittish: Quick to flee when startled
  • Curious: Will investigate and watch keepers
  • Can Bite: Bites can draw blood but are rare
  • Individual Variation: Some become somewhat hand-tame

Handling Guidelines

  • Minimize Contact: Handle only when necessary
  • Technique: Cup gently, never grab or squeeze
  • Skin Care: Wet hands are gentler on their skin
  • Best Practice: Encourage to walk onto hand rather than grabbing

Breeding Giant Day Geckos

Breeding is achievable for intermediate keepers:

Sexing

  • Males: Larger, broader head, visible femoral pores, hemipenal bulges
  • Females: Smaller, calcium sacs visible on neck, no bulges
  • Maturity: 12-18 months of age

Breeding Process

  • Conditioning: Slight cooling period (optional)
  • Pairing: Introduce female to male's territory carefully
  • Egg Laying: Adhesive eggs laid in pairs on surfaces
  • Egg Sites: Cork bark, bamboo tubes, or specially made boxes
  • Incubation: 45-70 days at 78-82°F
  • Hatchling Care: House separately, same care at smaller scale

Is a Giant Day Gecko Right for You?

Giant Day Geckos Are Great For:

  • Keepers wanting a stunning display animal
  • Those who enjoy observing natural behaviors
  • Bioactive terrarium enthusiasts
  • People wanting a diurnal (daytime active) gecko
  • Intermediate keepers ready for UVB requirements

Giant Day Geckos May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Those wanting a handleable pet
  • Beginners unfamiliar with UVB and humidity management
  • Keepers wanting a low-maintenance reptile
  • Those with limited space for tall enclosures
  • Children who want to hold their pet

Related Species to Consider

If you're interested in Giant Day Geckos, you might also consider:

  • Gargoyle Gecko - More handleable, nocturnal
  • Tokay Gecko - Nocturnal, similarly sized
  • Mourning Gecko - Smaller day gecko species
  • House Gecko - Similar tropical requirements
