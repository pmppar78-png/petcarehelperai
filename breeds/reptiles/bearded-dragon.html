Bearded Dragon Overview

The Bearded Dragon, affectionately known as "beardie," is one of the most beloved reptile pets worldwide. Native to the arid regions of Australia, these charismatic lizards are known for their docile temperament, interactive personalities, and the distinctive "beard" of spiny scales under their chin that they can puff out when threatened or displaying.

Bearded dragons are diurnal (active during the day), making them perfect for keepers who want to observe their pet's activities. They're known for their dog-like personalities, often recognizing their owners and displaying behaviors like arm waving and head bobbing.

Natural Habitat & Origin

Bearded dragons are native to the deserts, scrublands, and woodlands of central Australia:

  • Semi-arid climate: Hot days, cooler nights, low humidity
  • Open terrain: Rocky outcrops, fallen timber, and sparse vegetation
  • Basking behavior: Spends hours basking on rocks and branches
  • Diurnal activity: Active during daylight, sleeps at night
  • Note: Export from Australia is prohibited; all pet beardies are captive-bred

Enclosure Setup & Requirements

Bearded dragons require spacious enclosures with proper temperature gradients:

Tank Size

  • Juveniles (0-6 months): 40-gallon minimum
  • Sub-adults (6-12 months): 75-gallon minimum
  • Adults: 120-gallon / 4x2x2 feet minimum
  • Ideal: Larger is always better - 6x2x2 feet provides excellent space

Substrate Options

  • Recommended: Reptile carpet, slate tile, paper towels, excavator clay
  • Bioactive: Organic topsoil/sand mix (for adults with proper husbandry)
  • Avoid for juveniles: Loose substrates due to impaction risk

Essential Furnishings

  • Basking platform/branch under heat lamp
  • Hide on cool side
  • Climbing branches and rocks
  • Shallow water dish (many beardies won't drink from dishes)
  • Food dish for salads

Temperature & Lighting

Proper temperature and UVB lighting are critical for bearded dragon health:

Temperature Requirements

ZoneTemperature
Basking Spot100-110°F (38-43°C)
Warm Side90-95°F (32-35°C)
Cool Side75-85°F (24-29°C)
Nighttime65-75°F (18-24°C)

UVB Lighting (ESSENTIAL)

Bearded dragons REQUIRE strong UVB lighting for proper calcium metabolism and overall health:

  • Tube lights: T5 HO 10.0 or 12% UVB, spanning 2/3 of enclosure length
  • Distance: 10-12 inches from basking spot (without screen) or 6-8 inches (with screen)
  • Duration: 12-14 hours per day
  • Replace: Every 6-12 months (UVB output diminishes)

UVB is Non-Negotiable

Without adequate UVB, bearded dragons will develop metabolic bone disease (MBD), a painful and often fatal condition. Do not skip or skimp on proper UVB lighting.

Essential Lighting Products

ZooMed ReptiSun T5 HO 10.0 - Gold standard UVB | ExoTerra Solar Glo - Combined heat and UVB

Humidity & Water

Bearded dragons are desert animals requiring low humidity:

  • Ambient humidity: 30-40%
  • Hydration: Many beardies prefer drinking from movement (dripping water, baths)
  • Baths: Weekly lukewarm baths help with hydration and shedding
  • Misting: Not generally recommended, can raise humidity too high

Diet & Feeding

Bearded dragons are omnivores with changing dietary needs as they age:

Diet Ratio by Age

AgeInsectsVegetables
Juvenile (0-6 months)70-80%20-30%
Sub-adult (6-12 months)50%50%
Adult (12+ months)20-30%70-80%

Appropriate Feeder Insects

  • Staples: Dubia roaches, crickets, black soldier fly larvae (nutrigrubs)
  • Occasional: Superworms, hornworms, silkworms
  • Treats only: Waxworms, mealworms (hard to digest)
  • Size rule: No larger than the space between their eyes

Vegetables & Greens

  • Daily staples: Collard greens, mustard greens, turnip greens, butternut squash
  • Occasional: Bell peppers, carrots, green beans, snap peas
  • Treats: Fruits (berries, mango) - sparingly due to sugar content
  • Avoid: Lettuce (low nutrition), spinach (binds calcium), avocado (toxic)

Supplementation

  • Juveniles: Calcium with D3 at every feeding, multivitamin 2x weekly
  • Adults: Calcium with D3 3-4x weekly, multivitamin 1x weekly
  • With proper UVB: Can alternate calcium with and without D3

Health Issues & Common Problems

With proper care, bearded dragons are robust, but watch for these conditions:

Metabolic Bone Disease (MBD)

The most common and serious condition, caused by inadequate UVB and/or calcium. Signs include soft jaw, kinked spine/tail, tremors, lethargy, and difficulty walking.

Impaction

Intestinal blockage from substrate ingestion or improper feeding. Signs: bloating, lethargy, lack of bowel movements, dragging back legs.

Respiratory Infections

From improper temperatures or humidity. Signs: wheezing, mucus around nose/mouth, gaping, lethargy.

Parasites

Internal parasites common in captive beardies. Annual fecal exams recommended. Signs: weight loss despite eating, runny stool.

Yellow Fungus Disease

Serious fungal infection requiring immediate veterinary care. Signs: yellow/brown patches on skin that spread.

Brumation

Natural dormancy period (not illness). Beardies may sleep more, eat less during winter months. Normal if dragon maintains weight and is otherwise healthy.

Handling & Temperament

Bearded dragons are known for being one of the most handleable reptiles:

  • Acclimation: Allow 1-2 weeks to settle before regular handling
  • Approach: From the side, not above (predators come from above)
  • Support: Always support all four legs and the body
  • Duration: Can handle for extended periods once acclimated
  • Body language: Black beard and puffing = stressed; arm waving = submission/acknowledgment
  • Temperature: Provide warmth during handling, return when they get cool

Breeding Information

Breeding bearded dragons requires preparation and commitment:

  • Sexual maturity: 18-24 months, though they can breed younger (not recommended)
  • Sexing: Males have larger heads, hemipenal bulges, and femoral pores
  • Brumation: Cooling period helps trigger breeding behavior
  • Clutch size: 15-30 eggs per clutch, multiple clutches per season
  • Egg laying: Provide lay box with moist substrate
  • Incubation: 55-75 days at 82-86°F

Is This Reptile Right for You?

Bearded Dragons Are Great For:

  • Those wanting an interactive, personable reptile
  • Families with children (supervised handling)
  • People who enjoy daytime activity from their pets
  • Keepers who want a handleable species
  • Those prepared for long-term commitment (10-15 years)

Bearded Dragons May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Limited space (adults need 4+ foot enclosures)
  • Tight budgets (proper lighting is expensive)
  • Those unwilling to feed live insects
  • People who travel frequently
  • Anyone not prepared for daily salad prep
