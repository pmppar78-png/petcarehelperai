A room-sized snake with a legal fence around it

Burmese pythons, Python bivittatus, are among the largest snakes in captivity. The quick facts list 12 to 18 feet, 20 to 30 years, and expert care. An adult female is a heavy, strong animal that eats rabbits and needs more than a bedroom closet. The 8 by 4 by 4 foot minimum on the quick facts is a juvenile description. The adult needs a room built as a cage, with a thermostat you can trust and a door that locks from the outside.

In the United States they are injurious wildlife under federal law. You cannot legally import them or move them across state lines. States and cities add their own bans. A cheap hatchling at a show can be an animal you cannot legally rehome later. That trap is how large pythons get dumped, including into the Everglades.

Heat, food, and a second person

Temperatures of 78 to 90°F and humidity around 60 percent match the table. Large rats and rabbits are the adult diet. Feed in a way that does not teach the snake that a person is the prey item. Two adults should be present to handle a large Burmese. A bite or a wrap from an animal this size is an emergency, not a story.

Respiratory infections and inclusion body disease are reasons to have a reptile vet before you need one. ARAV is the directory. This species is a poor choice for almost every private home.

Burmese python questions

Are Burmese pythons legal in the United States?

They are listed as injurious wildlife. Import is banned, and transporting one across state lines is a federal problem. Some states also ban possession. Check before you believe a seller.

How big do they get?

The quick facts list 12 to 18 feet. Females are often the giants. A hatchling is not a preview of the adult or of the room it will need.

Are they safe because they seem calm?

The temperament line says docile but powerful. Calm is not harmless. Adults have killed keepers. Never handle a large Burmese alone, and never put one around a neck.
