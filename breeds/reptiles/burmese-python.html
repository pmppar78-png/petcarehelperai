Burmese pythons reach about 12 to 18 feet, often live 20 to 30 years, and need an enclosure of at least 8 by 4 by 4 feet with 78 to 90°F on a thermostat and humidity around 60 percent. A docile but powerful expert-level snake eats large rats and rabbits; a low UVB tube is optional daylight, and the heat plan stays inside that stated band.

A room-sized snake with a legal fence around it

Burmese pythons, Python bivittatus, are among the largest snakes in captivity. A typical adult is 12 to 18 feet, they can live 20 to 30 years, and care is expert. An adult female is a heavy, strong animal that eats rabbits and needs more than a bedroom closet. The 8 by 4 by 4 foot minimum is a juvenile description. The adult needs a room built as a cage, with a thermostat you can trust and a door that locks from the outside.

In the United States they are injurious wildlife. Import is still illegal, and so is shipping them to Hawaii, the District of Columbia, or U.S. territories. After USARK v. Zinke in 2017, federal law does not ban moving them between states in the lower 48. States and cities still add their own possession bans. A cheap hatchling at a show can be an animal you cannot legally keep where you live. That trap is how large pythons get dumped, including into the Everglades.

Heat, food, and a second person

They need about 78 to 90°F and humidity around 60 percent. Large rats and rabbits are the adult diet. Feed in a way that does not teach the snake that a person is the prey item. Two adults should be present to handle a large Burmese. A bite or a wrap from an animal this size is an emergency, a real event for the people in the room.

Respiratory infections and inclusion body disease are reasons to have a reptile vet before you need one. ARAV is the directory. This species is a poor choice for almost every private home.

Burmese python questions

Are Burmese pythons legal in the United States?

Length runs 12 to 18 feet. Females are often the giants. A hatchling is a poor preview of the adult and of the room that adult will need.

Are they safe because they seem calm?

They are often called docile, and they are still powerful. Calm is not harmless. Adults have killed keepers. Never handle a large Burmese alone, and never put one around a neck.

Heat for a snake that fills a room

Burmese pythons are very large snakes, about 12 to 18 feet, often living 20 to 30 years. Care is expert. Temperament is described as docile but powerful, which means a calm animal can still injure a person by weight, a bite, or a wrap. Females are often the giants. A hatchling's size is a poor preview of the adult or of the room. The enclosure minimum is 8 by 4 by 4 feet, and many adults need more. Doors, locks, and the frame have to survive a snake that can push. Check the law before you buy. Several places restrict or ban this species.

Hold about 78 to 90°F with a thermostat on every heat source. The warm hide and the cool end should both be measured, because a room-sized cage develops cold corners. Guard lamps so the snake cannot lie on a bulb. Humidity around 60 percent supports shedding without turning the floor into a swamp. A large water tub that the snake can soak in will be emptied and refilled as a chore, not as an occasional rinse. A fluorescent over Burmese Python can mark day and night while the thermostat holds 78-90°F. A desert basking chart does not belong on a giant python. Stay inside the 78 to 90°F band.

Large prey and two people

The diet is large rats and rabbits. Prey size follows the snake's girth, with long gaps between meals for a big adult. Tongs, a consistent routine, and a second adult in the room are the minimum. One person should not be the only handler of a large Burmese. A bite or a wrap at this size is an emergency.

Recheck 78-90°F the same day at an adult size around 12 to 18 feet. Mucus, bubbling at the mouth, or open-mouth breathing after a cool, damp night needs a reptile veterinarian who can see a giant snake. Transport is part of the plan: a secure bin, a helper, and a clinic that has agreed in advance. Incomplete sheds mean the 60 percent humidity failed. Fix the air and the soak tub. An expert animal is a housing problem, a legal problem, and a medical-access problem for the entire 20 to 30 years.
