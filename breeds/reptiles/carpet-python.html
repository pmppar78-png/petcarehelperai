Carpet python usually means Morelia spilota: jungle, coastal, inland, diamond, and the animals sold as crosses. Bredl's python is a close relative that people lump in. Adult length is 6 to 10 feet because the forms differ. Lifespan runs about 15 to 20 years for this Large (6-10 ft) animal at 75-88°F in a 4x2x4 ft (arboreal) enclosure. Care is intermediate. They are active, partly arboreal, and stronger than a ball python of the same age.

Temperatures of 75 to 88°F and humidity around 50 to 60 percent are a fair range. A higher humid hide helps a jungle carpet shed. A diamond from drier country does not need a swamp. The cage is 4 by 2 by 4 feet. Treat that as a starting label. A 10-foot snake needs a longer room. Sturdy perches, a locked door, and a water bowl big enough to soak in are the useful extras.

Carpet python questions

How big do carpet pythons get?

It depends which carpet. Jungle and coastal carpets are often 6 to 8 feet. Some diamond and other large forms reach 8 to 10. Size lands around 6 to 10 feet. That is the spread, not one animal.

Do carpet pythons need a tall cage?

They use branches and they also cruise the floor. Give them 4 by 2 by 4 feet. A large adult needs more length than that minimum.

Are they aggressive?

They are semi-arboreal and active. Babies can be nippy. Captive-bred adults usually calm down with steady handling. Feeding response stays strong.

Branches at carpet-python height

Carpet python covers several Morelia that share a look and differ in adult size. The quick facts band is large, about 6 to 10 feet, with a 15 to 20 year lifespan and intermediate care. Temperament is semi-arboreal and active. Get the species or locality on the invoice before you freeze the cage size. The listed enclosure is 4 by 2 by 4 feet and arboreal, which means sturdy horizontal perches with a real branch diameter. An empty vertical space leaves nowhere to rest. Bolt the branches. A heavy snake will pull down a suction-cup vine. Floor space still matters, because carpets use both the branch and the ground.

Temperatures of 75 to 88°F should be a gradient the snake can choose by moving along a perch or dropping to the floor. Put heat on a thermostat and guard lamps so a looping body cannot touch the bulb. The warm end can sit toward 88°F. The cool end stays nearer 75°F. Lighting on the Carpet Python cage is a timer problem. The heat problem is 75-88°F and humidity 50 to 60 percent. A desert basking chart with an extreme surface temperature does not fit this python. Humidity of 50 to 60 percent is moderate. A water bowl and a humid hide during shed are enough in most houses. Ventilate a tall cage so the top stays breathable.

Rats and a shed you inspect

Rats and mice, thawed and tong-fed, are the diet. Size prey to the snake and keep a calendar, because an active carpet will keep taking food a sedentary snake would refuse. Juveniles can be defensive. Many adults settle with predictable handling. Feed away from the time you open the door for maintenance. .

After each cycle, look at the eye caps and the tail. A torn shed means the 50 to 60 percent humidity was too low, or the snake had no place to soak. Respiratory noise, open-mouth breathing, or a snake that stays on the floor and misses strikes needs a reptile veterinarian. Name the carpet you own, because a small locality and a large coastal animal do not share a finished adult cage even when both are sold as carpet pythons.
