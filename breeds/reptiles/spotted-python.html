Blotches, a small body, a real lifespan

Spotted pythons are Antaresia maculosa, from eastern Australia. Adults are about 2 to 4 feet. The quick facts list 15 to 20 years, a calm temperament, and beginner difficulty. They are one of the snakes people recommend when a ball python feels too likely to skip meals. They still need heat you can measure and a cage they cannot leave.

A warm hide in the high 80s, a cooler side around 78°F, and a water bowl cover the daily setup. Add a moist box when the eyes go blue. Floor space for an adult is a 36-inch cage or a 40-gallon breeder. Hatchlings eat small mice. Adults eat a larger mouse. They do not need rats to prove they are pythons.

Captive-bred only

Australia does not export this species for the pet trade. The animals in US and European cages are captive-bred. A wild-sourced adult is a red flag. Handle gently after they are feeding. They can be food-sharp. Wash your hands so you do not smell like a mouse.

Respiratory noise or a crooked shed that repeats is a husbandry or a medical problem. A reptile vet through ARAV can tell which.

Spotted python questions

How is a spotted python different from a Children's python?

Spotted pythons usually show a stronger blotched pattern and are a separate species, Antaresia maculosa. Children's pythons are A. childreni. Both stay small. Buy the one named on the receipt.

Are spotted pythons beginners?

The quick facts say beginner. A captive-bred animal on thawed mice, with a thermostat, is a reasonable first snake. The life is still 15 to 20 years.

Do they need a tall cage?

They climb a little and live mostly on the floor. A long cage with a couple of branches is more useful than a tall empty tank.
