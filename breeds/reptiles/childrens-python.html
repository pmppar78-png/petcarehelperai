A small python with an old name

Children's pythons, Antaresia childreni, are the little brown pythons of northern Australia. Adults are about 2.5 to 4 feet and often live 20 to 30 years. A captive-bred animal is calm enough for a first snake. A wild one is not, and you should not have one.

They need a secure cage with a warm side in the upper 80s, a cool side in the 70s, and a water bowl. Humidity can sit near ordinary room levels, with a moist hide at shed time. They use a branch and they also sit on the floor. A 40-gallon or a 3-foot tub suits an adult. Hatchlings are tiny and do better in a small tub until they are eating reliably. The species is nocturnal enough that a red night check shows more snake than a noon visit.

Weigh the latest Children's Python shed against the thermostated range on this page, including whether the eye caps left with the skin. A moist hide at the right week produces a shed in one piece, including the tail tip and the eye caps. A crooked, repeated shed means that hide dried out. Respiratory noise in a snake this small is easy to miss. If you hear a click or see the mouth held open, book a reptile veterinarian and recheck the warm hide.
