Captive-bred and well fed, not fat

Imports arrive dehydrated and wild. Captive-bred juveniles take handling better and still need tongs at feeding time. A skink that is round like a sausage is overfed. A skink whose hips suddenly show is underfed or ill. Offer food, watch the body, and keep the lid locked. They push.

Schneider's skink questions

Are Schneider's skinks friendly?

They are active and inquisitive. Many captive-bred animals learn that a hand means food. They are still fast, and they bite fingers that smell like insects.

What temperatures do they need?

Give them a gradient of about 80 to 100°F, a hot basking spot, and a cooler side they can choose.

Do they need high humidity?

A fair range is 40 to 50 percent. They still use a moist burrow. Wet walls and a soaked floor cause scale problems.

Insects, fruit, and the jaw

The diet is insects and some fruit. Dust calcium on the prey and offer plants or fruit as a side, not as the only calories. They can get fat if the bowl is always full of sweet food. Feed on a schedule and let them hunt. An inquisitive skink learns the tongs. That is useful. Keep the hand that smells like an insect out of the strike path until you know the individual.
