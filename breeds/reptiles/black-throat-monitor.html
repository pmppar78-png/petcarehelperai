A large monitor with a long memory

Black-throat monitors, typically Varanus albigularis microstictus, are big African lizards. Adults are about 5 to 7 feet, and the enclosure listed here is 8x4x4 ft minimum at 80-130°F (gradient). They commonly reach 20 to 25 years, and care is advanced. They are intelligent. Some become calm around a familiar keeper. Experience before the hatchling

A black-throat is not the next step after a leopard gecko. It is a specialist animal with a 20-year span and housing you build before it arrives. Captive-bred babies are the ones to consider. Imported adults are dangerous and often sick. Do not free-roam one for entertainment. Floors are full of toxins, falls, and exits.

Black-throat monitor questions

How big do black-throat monitors get?

Size lands around 5 to 7 feet. That is an adult that needs an enclosure of at least 8 by 4 by 4 feet, and more length is better. A baby cage is a temporary crate.

Are they friendly?

They are intelligent and capable of being tamed. Intelligence includes learning that people mean food. Tame does not mean a 6-foot lizard is safe loose in a house.

What meals suit a black-throat monitor?

Whole prey and insects, in measured meals. Variety and a measured meal beat a constant hopper of fatty food.

A room with a measured hot surface

Black-throat monitors are large Varanus, about 5 to 7 feet, with a 20 to 25 year lifespan and advanced care. The quick facts enclosure is 8 by 4 by 4 feet at the minimum. Many adults need more once the tail and the climbing are real. Intelligent animals that can be tamed still need walls, a lock, and a floor that can be cleaned. Build the room before the hatchling, because a baby in a glass tank teaches the wrong habits and then outgrows the furniture in a few seasons.

Temperature is an 80 to 130°F gradient. Air in the cool end can sit near 80°F. The basking surface, read with an infrared thermometer, is what climbs toward the high end. Put the basking lamp on a thermostat with the probe on that surface. A lamp on a timer alone will overshoot on a hot day and leave the animal cold on a cold one. Give shade and a retreat so a tame monitor can leave the heat. These lizards bask. A high-output UVB tube over the hot zone, at the distance the manufacturer prints, has to be replaced on schedule. Glass lids block it.

Humidity of 60 to 70 percent is higher than a savannah monitor setup. A large water tub they can soak in, changed often, helps, and the rest of the room should not stay swampy. Whole prey and insects are the diet. Vary the prey size to the lizard's girth, dust or gut-load the insects, and keep rodents from becoming the only meal if the animal is already heavy. Feed with tools. A tamed black-throat is still strong enough to injure the person holding the food.

Bone disease in a lizard that looks fine

Metabolic bone disease is the watch-item because this species basks. A soft jaw, tremors, swollen limbs, or a monitor that stops climbing needs a veterinarian who has treated varanids. By the time the jaw looks rubbery, the UVB distance or the diet has been wrong for a while. Weigh the animal and photograph the body condition every month so a slow decline is visible.

Handling, if you do it, happens after the animal knows the room and the person. Support the body. Do not lift by the tail. An intelligent monitor remembers rough housing. The same memory makes a consistent routine useful: same door, same feeding place, same retreat that is never used as a grab spot. If the room cannot hold 8 by 4 by 4 feet of heated, UVB-lit space with a soak tub, the species does not fit the house.
