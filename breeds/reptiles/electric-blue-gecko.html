A three-inch gecko with a conservation file

Electric blue day geckos are Lygodactylus williamsi. Males are the brilliant blue animals. Females are greenish bronze. Adults are about 3 inches. They usually last 5 to 10 years, the care is intermediate, and they are for watching, not holding. The intermediate part is real. The larger story is that this species was nearly emptied from the Kimboza forest for pet cages. It is critically endangered and on CITES Appendix I. A wild-caught animal is not a bargain. It is a problem.

Captive-bred geckos still need a vertical tank, UVB, heat with a basking spot, and humidity that cycles into the 60s and higher during the day with drying time. They drink droplets. They eat tiny insects and a bit of fruit puree, with calcium. Males display at each other through glass. Cover the sides between cages or house them where they cannot see a rival.

Papers, plants, and no grabbing

Ask a breeder how long the line has been captive. Keep the CITES paperwork. A planted cage with bamboo is both hiding cover and drinking surface. Handling is how tiny toes get broken. If the colony produces babies, the plan for those babies is part of owning the adults. Releasing any of them is illegal and useless to the forest they came from.

A gecko that fades, stops eating, or cannot climb needs a vet who has seen small day geckos. Start at ARAV.

Electric blue gecko questions

Why are electric blue geckos controversial?

They come from a tiny forest in Tanzania and were stripped from the wild for the pet trade. They are critically endangered and listed on CITES Appendix I. Captive-bred animals with paperwork are the only defensible pet.

Are they a handling pet?

No. They are active and display-only. They are tiny, fast, and territorial. Watch the blue. Keep your hands out.

Can two males share a tank?

No. Males fight. A pair or a small female group in a planted vertical tank is the usual limit, and even females need enough cover.
