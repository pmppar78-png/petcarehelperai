Sudan plated lizards are Broadleysaurus major, the big plated lizards of East and southern Africa. Adults are about 15 to 24 inches, tail included. They usually last 15 to 20 years or more, and care is beginner to intermediate. They are shy until they decide the room is safe. Then many become the lizard that walks onto a shoe.

An adult cage should be at least 4 by 2 by 2 feet. They dig. Deep soil, a basking rock at about 100 to 110°F on the surface, and a cool side in the mid 70s are the setup. Humidity of 40 to 60 percent is the air. UVB over the bask is necessary. Insects, greens, and occasional fruit are the diet. They will eat more protein than a tortoise should. They still need the plants. One animal, or a carefully watched pair in a much larger pen. Secure the lid. They are strong.

Captive-bred, and a place to vanish

Give a hide the lizard can reach without crossing open floor every time. Confidence comes from that retreat, not from daily handling sessions that drag it into the open. Captive-bred babies tame more reliably than wild adults. Toes shed badly if the moist hide is missing. Check digits when you do handle.

A plated lizard that stops eating and stays buried for a long stretch in the warm season needs the appetite page and possibly a vet from ARAV.

Plated lizard questions

Do plated lizards tame down?

They are shy at first and often tame later. Food from tongs is the usual path. A newly imported adult may never want hands.

How hot is the basking spot?

Air on the warm side can sit in the 80s while the rock surface reaches about 100 to 110°F. Measure the rock, not just the air.

They are omnivores

They are omnivores. Insects are the main item, with greens and a little fruit. Calcium and UVB belong with that menu.

Plated lizards, Broadleysaurus major, are heavy-bodied lizards from East and Southern Africa, about 15 to 24 inches, often living 15 to 20 years or more. A plated lizard of 15 to 24 inches (38-60 cm) lives at 75-85°F (24-29°C), basking to 100-110°F, eats insects and plants, and needs at least 4x2x2 feet as an adult. That care is beginner to intermediate. Temperament is shy at first, and many tame well and become docile once feeding is predictable. The adult enclosure is at least 4 by 2 by 2 feet. They need floor space, a burrow or cork flat, and a basking rock that will not tip. . They are stronger than a nervous first week suggests.

Ambient temperatures of 75 to 85°F, with basking up to about 100 to 110°F, are the gradient. Put the basking lamp on a thermostat and read the rock with an infrared thermometer. The cool end must stay available so a shy lizard is not forced to sit on a surface that is too hot. . UVB over the hot end, replaced on schedule, is required. Humidity of 40 to 60 percent should leave a slightly damp retreat for sheds and a drier basking surface. A swampy floor rots belly scales.

Insects, plants, and the jaw

. Insects, dusted with calcium, plus vegetables and a little fruit for animals that take them, match a plated lizard better than a cricket-only bowl. Feed where you can see the shy ones eat. They learn tongs. That is useful and still a bite if your hand smells like a roach. One animal per cage unless the space is large and you can see both bask.

If a Sudan Plated Lizard stops climbing or the jaw feels soft, remeasure UVB at the 100 to 110°F spot before the next shed cycle across 15 to 20 years. A soft jaw, swollen limbs, or a lizard that stops climbing onto the hot rock needs a reptile veterinarian and a check of the UVB distance. Fifteen to twenty years in a 4 by 2 by 2 foot cage is a docile adult only if the 100 to 110°F surface and the UVB were there while the lizard was still shy.
