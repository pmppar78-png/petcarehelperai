Savannah Monitor Habitat Essentials

Strong Savannah Monitor care plans prioritize enclosure conditions, stress reduction, and scheduled health observation instead of generic mammal care routines.

The cheap baby is a big, hot project

Savannah monitors are Varanus exanthematicus. Adults are about 3 to 5 feet. They can live 10 to 15 years, care is advanced, and a patient keeper with enough space can tame one. They are sold as inexpensive hatchlings. The adult needs an enclosure at least 8 by 4 by 4 feet, a basking surface that can reach the high end of an 80 to 130°F gradient, and humidity around 40 to 60 percent with a humid burrow. That is not a 40-gallon starter kit with a longer bulb later.

Wild savannah monitors eat mostly insects and other invertebrates. Captive lizards fed a stream of rodents become obese, with fatty liver and kidney disease, and they die years early. Insects, snails where you can offer them safely, and occasional whole prey are the menu. UVB, deep substrate for digging, and a thermostat you check with a temperature gun are the equipment that matters.

Tame is not the same as safe

A well-socialized adult can be calm. The bite and the tail are still serious. Children are not handlers for this species. Buy captive-bred if you buy one at all, and only after the adult cage exists. Wild-caught imports are parasite cases with a worse attitude. This is a poor choice for most households.

A monitor that stops eating or looks bloated needs a reptile vet, not another rat. ARAV is the directory. The not-eating page covers the early signs.

Savannah monitor questions

Are savannah monitors insect eaters?

In the wild they eat mostly invertebrates, plus the occasional whole prey item. A diet of fatty rodents is the common way these lizards get obese and die early.

How long do they really live?

10 to 15 years. Many pets never approach it because the cage is small and the food is wrong. Treat 10 to 15 as the span of a correct setup.

Is a baby savannah monitor a beginner pet?

No. Care is advanced. The baby in a dealer bin becomes a strong 3 to 5 foot lizard that needs an 8 by 4 by 4 foot enclosure at a minimum.
