Red-footed tortoises reach about 10 to 14 inches, often live 30 to 50 years or more, and need a 4 by 2 foot enclosure or an outdoor pen at 75 to 90°F on a thermostat with humidity around 70 to 80 percent. Red-Footed Tortoise Habitat Essentials

A humid hide, a UVB tube, and a thermostat for the 75 to 90°F gradient are the gear that matches a red-foot's forest climate. A soft shell is the metabolic bone disease sign when that UVB or the calcium in a varied diet is missing.

A forest tortoise that needs humid heat

Red-footed tortoises, Chelonoidis carbonarius, are the South American tortoises with red or orange scales on the legs. They are more social and more humid-loving than a Russian or a sulcata. The beginner label is fair for a person who will keep a 4 by 2 foot enclosure or an outdoor pen, hold humidity near 70 to 80 percent, and feed a mixed diet. It is not fair for a dry box and a pile of iceberg.

Temperatures about 75 to 90°F, with a basking spot. UVB indoors. They do poorly in a cold house. A humid hide and a soak schedule matter while they are growing, or the shell lifts into pyramids.

Greens, fruit, and a little protein

Dark greens and weeds are the base. Fruit can be a real part of the week, which is the opposite of the Mediterranean tortoises. A small amount of low-fat animal protein shows up in wild diets and in many keeper menus. Do not turn that into dog food as the staple. Shell rot follows a filthy, soaking substrate. Keep the surface clean even when the air is moist.

Nasal bubbles, a soft shell, or a tortoise that stops eating needs a clinic that sees chelonians. ARAV lists them. Use the not-eating page and the checker.

Red-footed tortoise questions

How are red-foots different from Russian tortoises?

Red-foots are larger, from humid South American forest, and they eat a broader diet that can include fruit and a little animal protein. Russians are dry-climate grazers. Do not copy one care sheet onto the other.

What humidity do they need?

About 70 to 80 percent, with a warm side and a humid hide. A bone-dry tortoise table pyramids the shell and dehydrates them.

Can red-foots live together?

Often better than most tortoises, if the pen is large and males are not constantly mounting. A cramped group still produces bitten shells. Watch the animals you actually have.

Humid forest heat

Red-footed tortoises are medium chelonians, about 10 to 14 inches, often living 30 to 50 years or more. Care is listed as beginner and is still a humid, warm pen for decades. Temperament is friendly and social, which is why people try groups. Social works only with space, matched animals, and more than one feeding spot. A bully at the warm end will fade the others. The enclosure is 4 by 2 feet or an outdoor pen, and bigger is the real adult plan. Walls should stop climbing and pacing.

Temperatures of 75 to 90°F need a thermostat indoors. A warm basking end and a cooler, humid retreat should both be measured at shell height. These tortoises bask, and they also need moisture. UVB or real sun is daily. Humidity of 70 to 80 percent is the forest part of the setup. A dry desert table that suits a Russian tortoise will leave a red-foot with a rough shell and a respiratory risk. Use a humid hide, moist substrate in part of the pen, and ventilation so the air is damp and fresh.

Fruit, greens, and protein

The diet is fruit, vegetables, and protein. That mix is broader than a grazing Mediterranean tortoise. Fruit is a real share for this species and still should not be the only item. A little appropriate protein, on a schedule, rounds the week. Calcium and UVB support the shell. A shallow water dish they can exit, cleaned often, matters in a humid pen.

Watch a Red-Footed Tortoise for trembling or a rubbery jaw, and check that the UVB still reaches the 75-90°F basking site across 30 to 50 years. A soft shell or weak limbs in a basking tortoise means the lamp or the calcium failed even though the cage felt tropically wet. Bubbles at the nose need a chelonian veterinarian the same week, because a humid animal kept too cool still gets respiratory disease. Thirty to fifty years of a friendly 10 to 14 inch tortoise is a 4 by 2 foot start with 75 to 90°F and 70 to 80 percent humidity already solved.
