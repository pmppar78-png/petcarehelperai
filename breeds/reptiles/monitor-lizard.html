A genus of large, demanding lizards

Monitor lizard means Varanus. The animals in the pet trade run from ackies around 2 feet to water monitors, crocodile monitors, and other species that become enormous. Adults run 2 to 8 feet or more, they often live 15 to 25 years or more, and care is advanced. Temperature, humidity, and diet depend on the species. An 80 to 130°F gradient and 40 to 80 percent humidity are ranges that contain opposites. Each species needs its own measured setup, checked on a schedule.

What the genus shares is intelligence, a strong feeding response, and a need for heat you measure on the basking surface. Whole prey or insects, depending on the species, beat a can of dog food. The enclosure minimum of 8 by 4 by 4 feet fits some medium monitors and is a joke for a large adult. Identify the species before you believe a tank size. A baby with no scientific name is a risk and a poor purchase.

Tame, powerful, and housed in their own locked room

Many monitors learn their keeper. They also learn the feeding routine and they can hurt people and other pets. Salmonella precautions are ordinary handwashing. Loose time on a bed is a bad plan. Build the adult enclosure, find a vet who will see a monitor, and only then choose a species whose adult size you can live with. Ackies, savannahs, and black-throats are different projects with their own pages.

Appetite loss in a monitor is easy to miss because people expect a fast. The not-eating guide and ARAV are the practical tools.

Monitor lizard questions

Which monitor is this page about?

No single species. Ackies, savannahs, black-throats, water monitors, and others are all Varanus. A typical adult is 2 to 8 feet or more, because the genus is that wide.

Is there one monitor setup?

No. An ackie can live in a large dedicated cage. A water monitor or a large tree monitor needs a room. Eight by four by four feet is a minimum for many species and too small for some.

Are any monitors beginner pets?

As a group, care is advanced. A few smaller species are kept by careful beginners who built the adult cage first. A mystery hatchling labeled only monitor is how people end up with an animal they cannot house.

What the genus shares is intelligence, a strong feeding response, and heat you measure on the basking surface. Put that lamp on a thermostat and read the surface with an infrared thermometer. These are basking lizards. UVB over the hot zone, at a strength and distance suited to the species, has to be replaced on schedule. A low tube that would suit a crepuscular gecko is too weak for a desert monitor, and a scorching setup that suits a savannah is too dry for a humid forest species. Whole prey or insects, depending on the species, are the diet. Dog food is a poor staple.

Bone disease in a smart lizard

Metabolic bone disease is the health watch-item because monitors bask. A soft jaw, tremors, or swollen limbs mean UVB or calcium failed for that species' setup. Respiratory infections follow cold, stale, or wrong-humidity housing and need a veterinarian who has seen varanids. Tame is a training outcome in some species. Powerful is the body you still have to house. An intelligent monitor remembers rough handling and also learns a feeding routine. Use tools for food.

Build for the adult named on the receipt. A 15 to 25 year animal at 8 by 4 by 4 feet is already a room project, and the larger species are livestock-scale. If the seller cannot give you the scientific name, the temperature, and the adult size, you cannot set the thermostat or the UVB. That information is the care sheet. The genus page is only the warning that those numbers are not interchangeable.
