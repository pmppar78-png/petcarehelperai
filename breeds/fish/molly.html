Molly Fish Overview

Mollies are one of the most popular livebearing aquarium fish, beloved for their hardiness, variety of colors and fin types, and ease of breeding. These adaptable fish can thrive in freshwater, brackish, and even full marine conditions, making them incredibly versatile for different aquarium setups.

Available in numerous colors including black, white, gold, dalmatian, and orange, as well as different fin types like sailfin, lyretail, and balloon, mollies offer something for every aquarist. They're excellent community fish that add activity and personality to any peaceful tank.

Natural Habitat & Origin

Mollies are native to freshwater and brackish waters of the Americas:

  • Native Range: Southern United States through Central America to South America
  • Habitat: Streams, rivers, coastal areas, and brackish estuaries
  • Environment: Often found in areas with hard, alkaline water
  • Species: Multiple species contribute to aquarium mollies

Their natural tolerance for brackish conditions is why aquarium mollies often benefit from some salt in their water.

Tank Requirements & Setup

Mollies need adequate space for their active swimming:

Tank Size

  • Minimum: 20 gallons for a small group
  • Sailfin Mollies: 30+ gallons (larger fish)
  • Ratio: 1 male to 2-3 females
  • Groups: Keep in groups of 4 or more

Essential Equipment

  • Heater: Maintain stable warm temperatures
  • Filter: Good filtration with moderate flow
  • Lighting: Standard aquarium lighting
  • Optional: Aquarium salt (1 tbsp per 5 gallons)

Decoration & Plants

  • Live Plants: Java fern, vallisneria, hornwort
  • Hiding Spots: Important for females and fry
  • Substrate: Gravel or sand
  • Open Space: Swimming room appreciated

Water Parameters

Mollies prefer hard, alkaline water:

Salt Recommendation

Mollies often thrive with the addition of aquarium salt (1 tablespoon per 5 gallons). This helps prevent disease and mimics their natural brackish habitat. Ensure tank mates are salt-tolerant.

Diet & Feeding

Mollies are omnivores with a preference for plant matter:

Primary Foods

  • Flake Food: High-quality tropical flakes
  • Algae: Spirulina flakes, algae wafers
  • Vegetables: Blanched zucchini, spinach, peas
  • Frozen Foods: Brine shrimp, bloodworms (treats)
  • Live Foods: Daphnia, brine shrimp (occasional)

Feeding Guidelines

  • Feed 2-3 times daily in small amounts
  • Include vegetable matter regularly
  • Avoid overfeeding
  • Variety ensures optimal health

Popular Molly Varieties

  • Black Molly: Solid black coloration
  • White/Silver Molly: Bright white or silver
  • Dalmatian Molly: White with black spots
  • Gold/Orange Molly: Bright golden-orange
  • Sailfin Molly: Large dorsal fin (P. latipinna/velifera)
  • Lyretail Molly: Forked, lyre-shaped tail
  • Balloon Molly: Rounded body shape
  • Platinum/Creamsicle: Various color morphs

Behavior & Temperament

Mollies are active, social fish:

  • Active Swimmers: Constantly moving throughout tank
  • Peaceful: Generally non-aggressive
  • Social: Prefer groups of their own kind
  • Males: May chase females persistently
  • Algae Grazers: Nibble on algae throughout the day

Compatibility with Other Fish

Compatible Tank Mates

  • Other livebearers (guppies, platies, swordtails)
  • Corydoras catfish
  • Peaceful tetras
  • Rasboras
  • Peaceful gouramis
  • Snails

Incompatible Species

  • Aggressive cichlids
  • Fin nippers
  • Large predatory fish
  • Fish requiring very soft, acidic water

Breeding Information

Mollies are prolific livebearers:

  • Livebearers: Give birth to free-swimming fry
  • Gestation: 60-70 days
  • Fry Per Batch: 20-100 depending on female size
  • Frequency: Can give birth every 4-6 weeks
  • Fry Care: Provide plants or breeding boxes for protection
  • Fry Food: Crushed flakes, baby brine shrimp

Common Health Issues

Molly Disease (Shimmies)

  • Symptoms: Rocking motion, clamped fins, not swimming
  • Causes: Poor water conditions, wrong parameters
  • Treatment: Water changes, add aquarium salt, correct parameters

Ich

  • Symptoms: White spots on body
  • Treatment: Raise temperature, add salt, ich medication

Fin Rot

  • Symptoms: Deteriorating fins
  • Treatment: Clean water, aquarium salt, antibacterial if needed

Is This Fish Right for You?

Mollies Are Great For:

  • Beginners wanting hardy, colorful fish
  • Community tank enthusiasts
  • Those interested in livebearer breeding
  • Brackish tank setups
  • Hard water aquariums

Mollies May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Soft, acidic water setups
  • Those not wanting population growth
  • Very small tanks
  • Tanks with sensitive soft-water fish

Related Species

  • Guppy - Smaller, equally colorful livebearer
  • Platy - Similar care, different appearance
  • Swordtail - Distinctive tail extension
  • Neon Tetra - Peaceful tank mate option
