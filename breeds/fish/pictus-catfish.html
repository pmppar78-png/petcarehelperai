Pictus Catfish Overview

The Pictus Catfish is a medium (5 in) freshwater species that is well-suited for hobbyists with some experience. With a lifespan of 8-10 years, this peaceful species requires a minimum tank size of 55 gal.

Pictus Catfish thrive in water temperatures of 72-78°F with a pH range of 5.8-6.8. As an omnivore, they accept a wide variety of foods.

Pimelodus pictus is a medium catfish that reaches about 5 inches and lives 8 to 10 years. Night-active 5-inch cats need 55 gallons of floor. That number surprises people who met the fish as a 2-inch juvenile in a dealer tank. They are nocturnal, they are schooling, and they carry sharp pectoral and dorsal spines that tangle in a net and puncture skin if you grab them. Intermediate care is about those spines, the school, and the mouth, not about an exotic pH trick alone.

The card calls them peaceful omnivores. They are peaceful toward fish they cannot swallow. Anything small enough to fit in the mouth, including neon tetras and young shrimp, is food after dark. Stock them with fish that stay larger than that gape: bigger tetras, rainbowfish, peaceful cichlids of a similar build. Do not add them to a nano community and hope the nocturnal habit means they will not hunt.

Water Quality Warning

Always cycle your tank fully before adding fish. Use a quality test kit to monitor ammonia, nitrite, and nitrate levels. Never add Pictus Catfish to an uncycled or newly set up tank.

Diet & Feeding

  • Primary Diet: Omnivore.
  • Foods: High-quality flakes/pellets, frozen brine shrimp, mysis shrimp, and blanched vegetables.
  • Variety: Rotate different food types for balanced nutrition.

Pictus Catfish Tank Essentials

Water is warmer-cool and acidic compared with a rift-lake tank: 72 to 78°F and pH 5.8 to 6.8. That pH band is narrow. A tank buffered to 8.0 for livebearers is outside it. Ammonia and nitrite at 0 ppm, nitrate under 20 ppm, and a soft sand or smooth gravel bottom protect the barbels. They forage all night. A rough pile of crushed coral will wear those whiskers down and the fish will stop finding food.

Common Health Issues

  • Ich (White Spot Disease): White spots on body and fins. Raise temperature gradually and use ich medication.
  • Parasites: Internal and external parasites; quarantine new additions.

Small adjustments based on what you observe often yield the biggest improvements.

Compatibility

  • Temperament: Peaceful - pairs well with other peaceful community species.
  • Tank Mates: Other peaceful community fish of similar size.
  • Avoid: Large aggressive species that may bully or eat them.

Tank Maintenance Essentials

Use protocols as a default and adjust when your pet's particular needs push in a different direction.

  • Water changes: Perform 20-30% partial water changes weekly.
  • Filter maintenance: Rinse filter media in old tank water monthly.
  • Algae control: Some algae is normal, but excessive growth indicates nutrient imbalance. Adjust lighting duration and consider adding algae-eating species.
  • Water testing: Test ammonia, nitrite, nitrate, and pH at least weekly. Keep a log to track trends and catch problems early.

Behavior & Temperament

Pictus Catfish are characterized by a peaceful disposition that influences their care requirements and compatibility.

  • General Disposition: Peaceful nature that defines daily interactions
  • Social Behavior: Specific social needs that owners should understand and accommodate
  • Activity Patterns: Natural activity cycles that influence care scheduling

Several pictus cats belong together. A single pictus spends the day wedged behind equipment and never shows the spotted pattern in the open. Several fish in 55 gallons use the length at night and rest together under driftwood by day. Feed sinking pellets, frozen bloodworms, and chopped prawn in the evening so the midwater fish do not take every bite. The spines mean you net them with a container or a fine mesh, not a coarse nylon bag that locks the spine and tears the fish.

They are not upside-down catfish and they are not a pleco. They will not clean glass, they do not swim inverted as a lifestyle, and they will not stay in a 20-gallon tank for an 8-year lifespan. Plan the 55 gallons, the acidic water, and tankmates with enough body mass to stay off the menu. A pictus that is out in the day, gasping, is usually a water-quality problem. A pictus that vanishes until lights-out is behaving normally.

Work on the simple inputs first and let your own observations tell you where to add nuance.
