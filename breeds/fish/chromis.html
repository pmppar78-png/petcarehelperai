Green Chromis Overview

The Green Chromis is a small (3 in) saltwater species that is an excellent choice for both beginners and experienced aquarists.

Green chromis is the shop name for a small reef damselfish, almost always Chromis viridis, sold as a peaceful school. The card lists them at about 3 inches, with a lifespan of 8 to 15 years, in 72 to 82°F, pH 8.1 to 8.4, and a specific gravity of 1.020 to 1.025. A school needs at least 30 gallons. Nitrate on this card is under 10 ppm, tighter than a fish-only tank usually aims for. They are omnivores and they are peaceful only while the group is big enough to behave like a school instead of a pecking order.

Buy a group, not a pair. Two or three green chromis in a 30-gallon tank sort themselves into a bully and a victim, and the victim fades, stops eating, and dies. Seven or more, added on the same day, spread that aggression out. A fish added later to replace a loss gets picked on. If you have to replace one, rearrange the rock when you add the newcomer so the old territories are gone. The beginner care level assumes you already know that. The label on the tank rarely says it.

Behavior & Temperament

They feed in open water on zooplankton. A small marine pellet and frozen mysis or brine shrimp, fed in the water column, match that. Nori clipped to the glass feeds the tang and the snails. It does not feed this fish. A chromis with a pinched belly in a reef that looks well stocked is usually losing the race to faster fish, or it is the odd one out in a group that is too small. Watch the school eat. The fish that hangs under the overflow and never comes out is the one to separate, not the one to wait on.

They jump when the school startles, and a 3-inch fish fits through a slot that looks too small. Mesh the top. They also ride the current and will end up stuck against an intake if the strainer is missing. Color tells you how the group is doing. Bright blue-green fish that swim together are settled. Olive, brown, or patchy fish are stressed, newly shipped, or being harassed. Give a new group a week of stable salinity before you judge the color. Specific gravity that swings between water changes will mute them faster than a dim light will.

Eight to 15 years is a long time for a fish that costs less than a takeaway meal. The 30-gallon minimum holds a young school and gets small once the fish are full size and sharing the tank with larger angels or tangs. They use the upper water. Put bottom-dwelling gobies under them rather than another midwater school in the same 30 gallons. They are not blue damsels. A damsel bought as a starter fish often becomes the tyrant of the tank. A real school of green chromis should not, if the group was large enough from the first day.

Cost of Ownership

The details matter, but so does the big picture — keep both in view as you develop your approach.

Green chromis sold for marine tanks are schooling damselfish that need a group, open swimming room, and stable salt water around 72 to 78 F. They harass each other when the group is too small or the tank is a bare 20 gallons. Feed a marine pellet and frozen mysis, and quarantine new arrivals.
