Overview

Rabbit Snails (Tylomelania species) are unique freshwater snails from the ancient lakes of Sulawesi, Indonesia. Named for their rabbit-like faces with long, droopy "antennae" and wrinkled skin, these large, elongated snails have become increasingly popular in the aquarium hobby.

Unlike most aquatic snails, Rabbit Snails are livebearers, giving birth to fully-formed miniature snails. They come in stunning color varieties including chocolate, orange, yellow, and gold, with various shell patterns. Their slow, deliberate movements and distinctive appearance make them fascinating aquarium inhabitants.

Understanding the full scope of Rabbit Snail care requires appreciating the biological and behavioral complexity of this species. As a 2-5 inches (5-12 cm) aquatic animal with a typical lifespan of 1-3 years, the Rabbit Snail has evolved specific physiological adaptations that directly influence how they should be kept in captivity. Their natural habitat—characterized by specific water chemistry, flow patterns, and ecological relationships—provides the blueprint for successful aquarium husbandry. Experienced aquarists consistently note that Rabbit Snail thrive when keepers replicate these natural conditions as closely as possible, rather than simply meeting minimum survival parameters.

The Rabbit Snail's behavioral repertoire extends well beyond what casual observers might expect. These fish exhibit complex social hierarchies, territorial behaviors, and feeding strategies that become increasingly apparent in well-maintained aquarium environments. Their well-balanced disposition means that tank mate selection requires careful consideration—not all community fish are compatible, and individual personality variation means that even within the same species, behavioral differences can be significant. Keepers who invest time in observing their Rabbit Snail's natural behaviors are better equipped to identify stress indicators, illness onset, and social conflict before these issues escalate into serious problems.

Natural Habitat

Rabbit Snails come from a very specific environment: Your aquatic veterinarian and experienced Rabbit Snail owners can offer perspective tailored to your situation.

  • Origin: Endemic to ancient lakes of Sulawesi, Indonesia
  • Environment: Lake shores with sandy/muddy substrates
  • Water: Warm, alkaline, mineral-rich water
  • Diet: Detritus, algae, decaying vegetation

Tank Requirements

Rabbit Snails need warm, alkaline conditions.

  • Tank Size: 20+ gallons (they're large and have significant bioload)
  • Substrate: Sand strongly preferred for burrowing and feeding
  • Temperature: Warmer than most snails (76-86°F)
  • Filtration: Good filtration with moderate flow
  • Plants: May eat soft-leaved plants; Java fern and Anubias safer
  • Decor: Rocks, driftwood, and hiding spots

Temperature Critical

Rabbit Snails require warmer water than most freshwater snails. Temperatures below 74°F can cause lethargy, poor appetite, and health decline. Keep heater reliable and consistent.

Diet & Feeding

Rabbit Snails are hearty eaters with big appetites.

  • Algae Wafers: Staple food; they love them
  • Sinking Pellets: Fish pellets and bottom-feeder foods
  • Blanched Vegetables: Zucchini, spinach, cucumber, carrots
  • Algae: Will graze but not efficient algae control
  • Calcium: Essential - cuttlebone, calcium blocks, or supplements
  • Protein: Occasional bloodworms or shrimp pellets

Note: Rabbit Snails may eat soft-leaved aquarium plants if hungry. Keep well-fed to protect plants.

Compatibility

Rabbit Snails are peaceful but have specific needs.

Good Tankmates

  • Peaceful tropical fish
  • Shrimp (compatible with Sulawesi species)
  • Other peaceful snails (Mystery, Nerite)
  • Corydoras and peaceful catfish
  • Small plecos

Avoid

  • Loaches (snail predators)
  • Puffers (eat snails)
  • Crayfish (may attack)
  • Assassin Snails (may target juveniles)
  • Large, aggressive cichlids

Breeding

Rabbit Snails are unique livebearers: Your aquatic veterinarian and experienced Rabbit Snail owners can offer perspective tailored to your situation.

  • Sexing: Separate sexes; males have slightly different shell shape
  • Live Birth: Give birth to fully-formed baby snails
  • Gestation: 4-6 weeks
  • Offspring: Usually 1-3 babies at a time
  • Baby Size: Born relatively large (pea-sized)
  • Frequency: Every 4-6 weeks when conditions are good
  • Population: Slow breeding prevents overpopulation

Breeding Note

Unlike prolific pest snails, Rabbit Snails reproduce slowly. This makes them ideal for aquarists who don't want snail population explosions. Babies are immediately independent and will graze alongside adults.

Health Issues

Common Rabbit Snail health concerns: Leaning into Rabbit Snail-specific detail, instead of one-size-fits-all advice, consistently yields better results.

  • Shell Erosion: From soft/acidic water; maintain proper pH and calcium
  • Temperature Stress: Lethargy in cool water
  • Parasites: Can carry internal parasites
  • Starvation: Large snails need regular feeding
  • Mantle Collapse: Serious condition; often from stress or poor water

Signs of Health

  • Active movement and exploration
  • Strong appetite
  • Smooth shell without erosion
  • Extended antenna and mantle
  • Regular breeding (indicates comfort)
