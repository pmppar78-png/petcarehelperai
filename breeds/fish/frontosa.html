Frontosa Cichlid Overview

Frontosa are Cyphotilapia frontosa, a deep-water cichlid from Lake Tanganyika. Adults reach 12 to 14 inches and live 15 to 25 years. This page starts them at 75 gallons. Temperament is listed as a peaceful giant. They are peaceful the way a large predator is peaceful toward fish it does not bother to hunt. They eat small fish. Difficulty is intermediate to advanced, mostly because they grow slowly, live a long time, and need Tanganyikan water rather than a community mix.

They come from deep, dim water. The tank should be a long one with open sand, large rock caves, and light that is not a noon glare. They are not mbuna. A pile of small rubble and a crowd of aggressive rock-dwellers is the wrong layout and the wrong company. Hard, alkaline water, the kind Tanganyika actually is, is the chemistry. Soft South American water will not grow a frontosa out to a healthy 12 inches. Do not keep them with tetras, rasboras, shrimp, or any fish that fits in a 12-inch mouth. The peaceful label has a size limit, and the limit is the gape.

Growth is slow. A juvenile that looks manageable for years is still on the way to 12 to 14 inches and may live past 20. The 75-gallon minimum is a young-adult tank and a tight one for a group. Frontosa are calmer in a group than alone, and a group of adults needs more length than 75 gallons provides. Plan the larger tank while the fish are small. They will not stay small because the current tank is small. They will stall, and a stalled frontosa is not the same thing as a healthy small adult.

Feed a meaty sinking pellet and frozen or fresh seafood. They are not herbivores and they are not algae control. A diet of feeder fish is a parasite risk and a poor staple. They feed in a deliberate way and they feed low and midwater. Fast, aggressive tankmates will take the meal. The right neighbors, if any, are other Tanganyikan fish too large to swallow, in a tank that can hold everyone at adult size. A synodontis catfish is a common choice. A school of neon tetras is a snack.

The bar count varies by locality. Burundi fish are associated with six bars, other populations with seven. The count is not a health indicator. A hump on the head develops with age, more in males. It is not a flowerhorn kok and it does not need special food to force it. Fifteen to 25 years means this is a fish you can still have when the rest of the original stocking is a memory. Intermediate to advanced care is the water, the patience, and the refusal to add small dither fish. They do not need dither. They need depth, dim light, and no one they can eat.

Temperament & Behavior

A frontosa that hangs at the surface or pines in a bright, shallow, busy tank is in the wrong place. Drop the light, give it a cave it can enter at 14 inches, and keep the pH alkaline. They are poor jumpers compared with an arowana, and they are still large enough to displace a lid if something spooks them. Cover the tank. A 15-year-old fish on the floor is a bad end to a slow life. Buy captive-bred juveniles if you can. Wild deep-water fish take the move harder, and the lake does not need the extra pressure.

Frontosa are a big, slow Tanganyikan cichlid, and the variables that matter are alkaline water, a 75-gallon or larger tank, and new fish that have been watched before they join a group. Adults reach 12 to 14 inches and can live 15 to 25 years. They are peaceful only by the standard of large cichlids. Anything that fits in the mouth is still a meal, so tiny tankmates are food, not dither. Feed a measured carnivore-leaning ration at lights-down, when these fish actually hunt, and keep ammonia and nitrite at 0 ppm. A brand of buffer does less than a finished cycle and a cave for each big head in the group.

Housing & Environment Requirements

A minimum tank size of 75+ gallons is recommended for Frontosa Cichlids. Provide appropriate substrate, hiding places, and peaceful tankmates. A quality filtration system and heater are essential.

Diet & Nutrition

Feed Frontosa Cichlids a varied diet appropriate for their species. Overfeeding leads to water quality issues. Vary the diet to ensure complete nutrition.

Common Health Issues

Ich, fin rot, bacterial infections, and stress show on frontosa as white spots on a dark body, eroded fins, or a fish that will not leave the cave at feeding time. They are intermediate to advanced largely because the adult is huge and the group is a long commitment, not because the juvenile is fragile in the shop. Keep the 75-gallon tank clean and do not chill them. Quarantine new frontosa for a few weeks in hard, alkaline water. A soft-water hospital tank is the wrong room for a Tanganyikan fish and will stress it into the symptoms you hoped to avoid. No dose is printed on this card. If the fish declines, ask a veterinarian rather than salting a 75-gallon tank by guess.

For Frontosa, prevention is always better than treatment.

Beginner-Friendly Rating: Intermediate-Advanced

Frontosa Cichlids are suitable for keepers with some experience.

Have the large tank, the rock caves, and the filter in place before you buy a frontosa. A juvenile hump is not the adult. Twelve to 14 inches and 15 to 25 years means the setup range on the card, about 100 to 500 dollars or more, is often only a down payment on a tank that can hold a group. Talk to keepers who have kept them past the first upgrade. They will mention deep-looking rock, dimmer light, and the fish that disappeared into a neighbor's mouth. Read that as stocking advice. A peaceful giant still needs a home sized for the giant.

Socialization & Enrichment

Build caves and open sand in the Tanganyikan pattern, with rock that will not collapse when a 12-inch fish leans on it. Frontosa hover near cover and come out on their own schedule. Rearranging the pile every week destroys the map and stresses a fish you bought for its calm. Add a cave if a new animal has nowhere to stand. Leave a stable structure alone otherwise. Sharp plastic is a cut waiting for a startled giant. The habitat is heavy rock and room, in 75 gallons at the absolute floor and more if you keep a real group.

Feed frontosa at dusk, a sinking pellet or a prawn they can take near the cave, and let them hunt rather than chasing flakes at the surface in bright light. Frozen or fresh meaty foods from a clean source match a fish that eats smaller animals in the lake. The enrichment and the health check are the same moment. A frontosa that will not come out for a prawn it usually takes has left its baseline. Test the water while the fish is still willing to show itself. A 14-inch cichlid off food, deep in the rock, is a difficult patient.

Where to Find Frontosa Cichlids

Buy frontosa from a shop that keeps them in hard alkaline water and can show you a fish eating, or from a seller with a live-arrival guarantee and a record you can read. Prices on the card run about 5 to 100 dollars, and a juvenile at the low end still becomes the same 12 to 14 inch adult. Refuse thin fish and any tank sprinkled with white spot. Quarantine in similar chemistry to the display. A peaceful giant that arrives sick costs the yearly upkeep, about 200 to 500 dollars on this card, in problems before you have enjoyed the hump.

Common Questions About Frontosa Cichlids

Master this layer of Frontosa care and everything from feeding to vet visits becomes more predictable. Some iteration is normal, a Frontosa tends to signal clearly when something fits and when it does not.

What size tank do Frontosa Cichlids need?

Target a 75+ gallons tank at minimum for a Frontosa Cichlids. The larger footprint provides the stability and flexibility that small tanks tend to lack.

What fish are compatible with Frontosa Cichlids?

With their peaceful giant temperament, Frontosa Cichlids are compatible with many community fish of similar size and temperament. Good tankmates include other peaceful species that share similar water parameter requirements. Always research specific compatibility before adding new species to your aquarium.

How often should I feed Frontosa Cichlids?

Feed Frontosa Cichlids 1-2 times daily, offering only what they can consume within 2-3 minutes. Fasting one day per week can benefit digestive health.

Are Frontosa Cichlids good for beginners?

Frontosa Cichlids are suitable for fishkeepers with some experience.

Owning Frontosa Cichlids is a commitment that spans their full lifespan of 15-25 years. Aquariums require daily attention, weekly maintenance, and ongoing expense. Many fishkeeping communities have members willing to help in emergencies.
