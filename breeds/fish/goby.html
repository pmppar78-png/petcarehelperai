Overview

Gobies are one of the largest fish families, with thousands of species ranging from tiny nano fish to robust sand-sifters. They're beloved in the marine aquarium hobby for their peaceful nature, interesting behaviors, and symbiotic relationships with pistol shrimp. Many species are perfect for nano reefs and beginner marine tanks.

From the hovering firefish to the industrious sand-sifting gobies, this diverse group offers something for every aquarist. Their small size, hardy nature, and entertaining behaviors make them staples in reef aquariums worldwide.

Natural Habitat

Gobies inhabit diverse marine environments:

  • Range: Tropical and temperate seas worldwide
  • Environment: Sand flats, coral reefs, rubble zones
  • Behavior: Bottom-dwelling; many burrow or hover near substrate
  • Symbiosis: Many species partner with pistol shrimp

Popular Species

Many goby species are kept in aquariums:

  • Firefish Goby (Nemateleotris magnifica): White/red gradient; hovers mid-water; 3 inches
  • Yellow Watchman Goby (Cryptocentrus cinctus): Yellow; pairs with pistol shrimp; 4 inches
  • Diamond Goby (Valenciennea puellaris): Sand-sifter; 6 inches; keeps sand clean
  • Clown Goby (Gobiodon spp.): Tiny at 1.5 inches; lives in coral branches
  • Neon Goby (Elacatinus oceanops): Blue stripe; cleaner fish; 2 inches
  • Engineer Goby (Pholidichthys leucotaenia): Eel-like; burrows; 13 inches

Tank Requirements

Requirements vary by species type:

  • Tank Size: 10 gallons for tiny species; 30-55+ for sand-sifters
  • Reef Safe: Most species are completely reef safe
  • Substrate: Fine sand essential for sand-sifting species
  • Burrows: Provide caves or allow room for burrow construction
  • Lid: Essential for firefish and other jumpers
  • Filtration: Standard reef filtration

Diet & Feeding

Most gobies are carnivorous:

  • Staple Food: Frozen mysis shrimp, brine shrimp
  • Prepared Foods: Marine pellets (some species accept these)
  • Live Foods: Copepods, amphipods from refugiums
  • Sand-Sifters: Filter food from sand; need active sand beds
  • Feeding Challenge: Some are shy feeders; target feed if needed

Feeding Schedule: Feed 2 times daily; small amounts.

Goby-Shrimp Symbiosis

One of the most fascinating behaviors in marine aquariums:

  • Partners: Watchman gobies pair with pistol shrimp
  • Relationship: Shrimp digs/maintains burrow; goby acts as lookout
  • Communication: Goby alerts shrimp to danger via tail signals
  • Observation: Fascinating to watch their partnership
  • Pairing: Not all goby/shrimp combinations work; research compatibility

Behavior & Personality

Gobies display varied behaviors:

  • Bottom Dwellers: Most stay near substrate
  • Hovering: Firefish hover mid-water near bolt holes
  • Burrowing: Many excavate and maintain burrows
  • Sand-Sifting: Some continuously filter sand for food
  • Timid: Many are shy initially; need hiding spots
  • Jumping: Firefish especially are notorious jumpers

Compatibility

Gobies are peaceful community fish:

Compatible Tank Mates

  • Most peaceful reef fish
  • Clownfish, chromis, anthias
  • Blennies (usually)
  • Wrasses (non-aggressive species)
  • Corals and invertebrates
  • Pistol shrimp (for watchman types)

Incompatible Species

  • Aggressive fish that bully them
  • Other gobies of same species (usually)
  • Large predatory fish
  • Very competitive feeders (may outcompete shy gobies)

Breeding

Some gobies breed readily in aquariums:

  • Neon Gobies: Relatively easy to breed
  • Clown Gobies: Can spawn in aquariums
  • Spawning: Usually in burrows or on surfaces
  • Male Care: Males typically guard eggs
  • Larvae: Small; require rotifers and phytoplankton

Common Health Issues

Gobies are generally hardy:

  • Marine Ich: Common parasite; quarantine prevents introduction
  • Jumping: Many species jump; secure lids essential
  • Starvation: Shy feeders may not compete well for food
  • Stress: From lack of hiding spots or aggression
  • Sand-Sifter Issues: May starve in tanks without sufficient sand fauna

Is This Fish Right for You?

Gobies Are Great For:

  • Reef aquarium enthusiasts
  • Nano reef keepers (smaller species)
  • Those wanting the goby-shrimp symbiosis
  • Peaceful community tanks

Gobies May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Tanks without tight-fitting lids (jumpers)
  • Aggressive fish communities
  • Bare-bottom tanks (for sand-sifters)
  • Highly competitive feeding environments
