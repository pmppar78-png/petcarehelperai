Harlequin Rasbora Overview

The Harlequin Rasbora is a small (2 in) freshwater species that is an excellent choice for both beginners and experienced aquarists.

Harlequin Rasboras thrive in water temperatures of 72-81°F with a pH range of 6.0-7.5.

The rasbora this card describes is the harlequin, a small peaceful schooling fish of about 2 inches with a lifespan of 5 to 8 years. Harlequins need 10 gallons for a small school, not for three fish. Care is beginner, the diet is omnivore, and the temperament is peaceful. The field mark is the black wedge on the rear half of the body, larger in males, which is why a loose school in a planted tank looks sharper than a single fish in a bare bowl.

Trigonostigma heteromorpha is the usual scientific name behind that wedge. They come from soft, tannin-stained water in Southeast Asia, and the card's numbers match a gentle version of that: 72 to 81°F and pH 6.0 to 7.5. Ammonia and nitrite need to read 0 ppm. The wedge stays sharp when nitrate remains under 20 ppm. A 10-gallon tank works for a small school if it is planted and already cycled. It does not work as a starter aquarium that is still clouding in week one, and it does not work if the school is three fish.

Tank Size

Behavior & Temperament

Harlequin Rasbora are characterized by a peaceful disposition that influences their care requirements and compatibility.

Keep enough of them that the group moves together. A harlequin that is alone clamps and loses the reason you bought the pattern. They spawn, in the wild and in a well-set tank, on the underside of broad leaves. You do not need to breed them to keep them, but a few Cryptocoryne or similar leaves give the school somewhere to hover. Flow should be gentle. A powerhead aimed through a 10-gallon tank exhausts a 2-inch fish.

They are omnivores that take fine flake, micro pellets, and frozen baby brine or cyclops. Large floating pellets meant for cichlids are the wrong size. Feed a pinch they can finish. Bigger, faster tankmates will steal a slow feeding, so choose small peaceful neighbors: other small rasboras, peaceful tetras that can live at this pH, and calm bottom fish. Tiger barbs and restless danios turn the school into a group that hides in the plants.

Beginner care here means stable water inside those ranges, a real school, and a lid, because even a small rasbora will jump through a feeding hole. The 5-to-8-year lifespan is realistic for a harlequin that was not cooked above 81°F and was not kept in a nitrate bath over 20 ppm. If the fish in the bag lack the wedge and the shop called them rasboras anyway, you may have a different species. Stock to the fish you can see, but do not ignore this card's 2-inch, 10-gallon, peaceful schooling frame when the fish is actually a harlequin.
