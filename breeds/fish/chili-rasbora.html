Chili Rasbora Overview

Chili rasboras are Boraras brigittae, a nano cyprinid that stays about 0.7 inches and lives 4 to 8 years if the tank suits a fish that small. The minimum is 5 gallons. Temperament is a peaceful nano fish, and the difficulty line runs from beginner to intermediate. The intermediate half is the food, the company, and the fact that a 0.7-inch fish disappears in a busy community. They are not a schooling tetra you can drop in with barbs.

Boisterous barbs belong in another aquarium. A tiger barb, a rosy barb, even a fast zebra danio in a small tank, outcompetes them for food and keeps them pinned in the plants. The right tank is planted, dim, and quiet: other Boraras, a few ember tetras if the tank is larger than the minimum, and shrimp that will not be eaten because the rasbora's mouth cannot take an adult shrimp. Five gallons works for a species group. It does not work as a community.

They come from the blackwater peat swamps of Borneo. Soft, acidic, tannin-stained water and a dark substrate are what turn a pale orange fish into the red one on the bag. Hard tap water keeps them alive for a while and keeps them washed out. Fine leaf litter and a gentle filter are more useful than a strong current. They hover in a loose group near the surface and the upper plants, not on the gravel. A school of eight or more is when that hovering looks like a species instead of a few lost specks.

The mouth takes powder food, crushed flake, and tiny live or frozen foods such as baby brine shrimp and cyclops. A standard community flake is too big. If you feed only what your larger fish eat, the chilies starve in a tank that looks well fed. Four to 8 years is a long life for a fish under an inch, and it depends on that small food showing up often. They have a fast metabolism. A long weekend with an automatic feeder set for big pellets is not a feeding plan.

Males are slimmer and redder, females rounder with less color. Spawning in a mature planted tank is possible and the eggs are scattered in fine plants. The adults will eat them. If you want to see fry, they need a separate heavily planted tank, not a promise from the display. Care is beginner-level only when the tank was built for them: stable soft water, no nippy neighbors, and food they can physically eat. A chili rasbora in a barb tank is an intermediate problem you created at the fish store.

Temperament & Behavior

The common mix-up is with mosquito rasboras, Boraras brigittae versus B. merah and the other tiny Boraras. Brigittae has the dark wedge along the side and the intense red when it is kept correctly. If the shop cannot tell them apart, the care on this page still fits the group: tiny, shy, planted, and kept away from any fish that treats a 0.7-inch neighbor as a snack or a punchline. A 5-gallon tank still needs a lid and a sponge over the intake, because a fish this small rides out through a cable hole or disappears into the filter. Nitrate kept low, with no ammonia and no nitrite, is what lets one reach the 4-year end of the life span and maybe the 8. They are a species tank. They are not a dither fish.

Housing & Environment Requirements

A minimum tank size of 5+ gallons is recommended for Chili Rasboras. Provide appropriate substrate, hiding places, and peaceful tankmates. A quality filtration system and heater are essential.

Diet & Nutrition

Feed Chili Rasboras a varied diet appropriate for their species. Use micro-pellets, crushed flakes, and small frozen foods like baby brine shrimp. Overfeeding leads to water quality issues. Vary the diet to ensure complete nutrition.

Common Health Issues

Common health issues in Chili Rasboras include ich (white spot disease), fin rot, bacterial infections, and stress-related conditions. Maintaining excellent water quality is the best prevention. Quarantine new fish for 2-4 weeks before adding to the main tank. Have aquarium salt and broad-spectrum medications on hand for emergencies.

Grooming & Maintenance

Remove uneaten food promptly. Trim live plants as needed. Monitor equipment function daily. Replace filter media according to manufacturer recommendations.

Socialization & Enrichment

Rearranging decorations periodically provides novelty. For Chili Rasboras specifically, consider their natural habitat when designing your aquascape - this helps encourage natural behaviors and reduces stress.

Feeding enrichment is another important aspect. Live and frozen foods provide hunting stimulation that dry foods cannot. This knowledge helps you detect health issues early and optimize care.

How often should I feed Chili Rasboras?

Feed Chili Rasboras 1-2 times daily, offering only what they can consume within 2-3 minutes. Fasting one day per week can benefit digestive health.
