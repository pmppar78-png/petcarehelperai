Cleaner Shrimp Overview

The Cleaner Shrimp is a small (2-3 in) saltwater invert invertebrate that is an excellent choice for both beginners and experienced aquarists. With a lifespan of 2-3 years, this peaceful species requires a minimum tank size of 10 gal.

Cleaner Shrimps thrive in water temperatures of 72-82°F with a pH range of 8.1-8.4. As an omnivore, they accept a wide variety of foods.

With that baseline in hand, individual calls on food, exercise, preventive care, and enrichment follow more naturally

The cleaner shrimp on this page is a saltwater invert, not a freshwater ghost shrimp and not an Amano. The animal most shops mean is the skunk cleaner, Lysmata amboinensis, white with a red stripe, about 2 to 3 inches long, living 2 to 3 years. The smallest reef on this card is 10 gallons. Keep the reef at 72 to 82°F and pH 8.1 to 8.4, specific gravity 1.020 to 1.025, nitrate under 10 ppm, and ammonia and nitrite at zero. Temperament is peaceful. Diet is omnivore, which means they do more than pick at fish.

Tank Size

Water Quality Warning

Saltwater species are sensitive to parameter fluctuations. Use an RO/DI unit for water changes and test regularly. Never add Cleaner Shrimps to an uncycled or newly set up tank.

Diet & Feeding

  • Primary Diet: Omnivore.
  • Foods: High-quality flakes/pellets, frozen brine shrimp, mysis shrimp, and blanched vegetables.
  • Feeding Schedule: Feed 2-3 times weekly or allow natural grazing.
  • Variety: Rotate different food types for balanced nutrition.

Cleaner Shrimp Tank Essentials

What matters most is consistency in the basics while staying alert to signals that something needs adjustment.

They set up a cleaning station and will pick dead skin and parasites off fish that choose to sit still. That behavior is real and it is not a treatment. A cleaner shrimp will not cure an ich outbreak, and it will not replace a quarantine tank. Fish that are hungry, or fish that eat shrimp, will eat the cleaner. Wrasses, hawkfish, and large predators are a bad roommate. A peaceful reef fish that ignores ornamental shrimp is the company that works.

Common Health Issues

  • Marine Ich (Cryptocaryon): White spots on body and fins. Treat with copper-based medication in quarantine tank.
  • Marine Velvet: Dusty gold appearance; highly contagious and often fatal if untreated.
  • Molting Issues: Ensure proper water parameters and nutrition during molting.

Compatibility

  • Temperament: Peaceful - pairs well with other peaceful community species.
  • Tank Mates: Other peaceful community fish of similar size.
  • Avoid: Large aggressive species that may bully or eat them.

Diet and Nutrition Tips

Owners who use these specifics to calibrate their care programme — not as background reading but as operational defaults — report fewer surprises over the long term.

  • Primary diet: Feed a high-quality staple food formulated for your Cleaner Shrimp's dietary type (herbivore, omnivore, or carnivore). Look for foods with whole ingredients and minimal fillers.
  • Variety ensures complete nutrition and encourages natural foraging.
  • Feeding frequency: Feed small amounts 1-2 times daily.
  • Avoid overfeeding: Overfeeding is the most common nutritional mistake in fishkeeping. It leads to obesity, water pollution, and secondary health problems.

Tank Environment and Stimulation

  • Water parameters: Maintain consistent temperature, pH, and hardness levels appropriate for Cleaner Shrimp. Test water weekly and perform regular partial water changes.
  • Filtration: Use a quality filter rated for your tank size.

Tank Maintenance Essentials

  • Water changes: Perform 20-30% partial water changes weekly.
  • Filter maintenance: Rinse filter media in old tank water monthly.
  • Algae control: Some algae is normal, but excessive growth indicates nutrient imbalance. Adjust lighting duration and consider adding algae-eating species.
  • Water testing: Test ammonia, nitrite, nitrate, and pH at least weekly. Keep a log to track trends and catch problems early.

Living Environment

  • Compatible tankmates: Research compatibility before adding new species. Aggression, different water parameter requirements, and size mismatches are common problems.

Feed them. Leftover mysis, a bit of mussel, and pellets that reach the bottom are the omnivore diet in practice. A shrimp that only gets what it can clean off healthy fish slowly starves in a tank with a light parasite load. They are scavengers with a party trick, not obligate cleaners. A shrimp that stays hidden and never comes out to a feeding stick is either molting or being hunted. Give it a rock crevice it can actually defend.

Molting is normal. An empty shell on the sand, with a soft shrimp hiding nearby, means leave it alone. The new shell hardens in a day or two. Pulling the shrimp out to inspect it during a molt is how you kill it. Lysmata are simultaneous hermaphrodites, and a pair may carry green eggs under the tail in a mature reef. Raising those larvae takes separate equipment and a food culture. Eggs on a cleaner are not a promise of baby shrimp in the display. They are also not a sign that the shrimp is sick.

Copper, and most medications aimed at marine ich, kill shrimp. A reef that may need copper later is a bad place to make a cleaner shrimp the centerpiece. Two to 3 years is the life on the card. They are listed as beginner inverts because a peaceful reef with stable salinity keeps them without much fuss. The beginner mistakes are copper, a hawkfish, and assuming the shrimp can live on parasites alone. A 10-gallon tank works for one or a pair and leaves little room for a fish that might eat them.
