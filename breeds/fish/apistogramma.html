Apistogramma Overview

With a lifespan of 3-5 years, this semi-aggressive species requires a minimum tank size of 20 gal.

Apistogrammas thrive in water temperatures of 72-86°F with a pH range of 5.0-7.0.

Apistogramma is a genus of small South American cichlids, not one species with a single correct temperature. Most of the fish sold under the name stay 2 to 3 inches and live 3 to 5 years. The table's 72 to 86°F span is that wide because a cooler-water borellii and a warmer-water cacatuoides got printed on one card. Care level is intermediate. A 20-gallon tank is the listed minimum, and a long footprint matters more than height for a fish that lives on the sand.

pH on the card runs from 5.0 to 7.0. Blackwater species, including many agassizii, fade and stop spawning in hard tap water. Some line-bred cockatoo cichlids, A. cacatuoides, tolerate closer to neutral. If the label says only Apistogramma, ask which species is in the bag before you set the heater. Fine sand, leaf litter, and a cave the female can fill with her body are the furniture. A bare tank with plastic plants gives her nowhere to put a brood, and the male spends his time displaying at the glass.

Temperament is semi-aggressive, and almost all of that aggression is about sex and a cave. A female guarding eggs turns yellow and black and will drive the male off. The group that works in 20 gallons is one male and two or three females, not two males. Two males in a small tank fight until one stops eating. Any dither fish have to accept the same soft, acidic water. A barbs-and-goldfish community cannot be their room.

Tank Size

  • Minimum: 20 gal
  • Type: A warm, soft-water planted tank. Apistogramma pairs want caves and leaf litter, and 20 gallons is a starting point for one pair, not a mixed cichlid show tank.

Diet & Feeding

  • Primary Diet: Omnivore.

Apistogramma Tank Essentials

They are omnivores that do better on small meaty foods than on large cichlid sticks. Baby brine shrimp, frozen daphnia, and a pellet they can chew match a 2-inch mouth. Males are the fish on the price tag: tall dorsal spines on a cockatoo, a spear tail and a cheek stripe on an Agassiz's. Females look plain until they spawn, and then they are the brightest animal in the tank. Buying only the male is why he never quite colors up.

Observing your Apistogramma's response to different food types also provides useful health information, as reduced interest in normally preferred foods is often an early indicator of illness.

Behavior & Temperament

Apistogramma are characterized by a semi-aggressive disposition that influences their care requirements and compatibility.

  • General Disposition: Semi-Aggressive nature that defines daily interactions

A dark cave and soft water are what produce a spawn. The female fans the eggs. Fry need microworms or newly hatched brine shrimp, and any danio in the tank will eat them. Three to 5 years is a short life next to a big Central American cichlid, so a hollow-bellied import has already used part of it on a breeder rack. These are not dwarf cichlids for a goldfish tank or a rift-lake tank. Both of those run outside the pH on this card.

Shops mix the names. A Macmaster's, a cockatoo, and an Agassiz's do not want the same hardness, and a German blue ram is not in this genus at all. Set the heater for the species you actually bought, somewhere inside the 72 to 86°F band, instead of parking it at the top because the card allows it. Hot water and a tired filter is how a fish listed for 3 to 5 years dies in the first one. Nitrate under 20 ppm, ammonia and nitrite at 0 ppm, and a cave she can defend are the practical test.

Cost of Ownership

Apistogramma are dwarf cichlids from soft, acidic South American streams. Many species do well as a pair in a 20-gallon tank with leaf litter, caves, and a temperature in the mid to upper 70s Fahrenheit. The female often guards a cave of fry while the male holds a wider territory.
