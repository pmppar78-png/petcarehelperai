Green Terror Cichlid Overview

Green terrors are Andinoacara rivulatus, a large South American cichlid, not a peaceful acara and not a community fish. Adults reach 8 to 12 inches and live 12 to 15 years. House an adult in at least 75 gallons. Temperament is semi-aggressive, which understates a full-grown pair. Difficulty is intermediate. The fish on the true green terror is the one with the gold or white edge on the tail and fins, the saum. A juvenile is silver-green and does not yet show why the adult needs a tank of its own.

They dig, they claim caves, and they grow into fish that bite hard. A 75-gallon tank is the floor for one adult or a young pair and it is a battleground if you add tetras, angelfish, or smaller cichlids and hope the semi-aggressive line will cover it. Stock as if the animal will be a foot long and territorial. Robust tankmates of similar size, in a tank longer than the minimum, can work if you are willing to separate them later. Shrimp, small catfish, and plants rooted in the open sand will not work. The plants come up. The shrimp get eaten.

A pair that spawns becomes more aggressive, not less. They clean a rock, guard the eggs, and patrol a wider circle than they did the week before. This is the same pattern as other large cichlids and it is not a phase you can decorate away. Give them a cave that cannot be dug out from under the rockwork. Stack stone on the glass, not on sand you hope will hold. A rockfall in a 75-gallon tank is a broken panel waiting to happen. Green terrors move gravel by the mouthful.

Feed a cichlid pellet and meaty frozen foods, and do not feed feeder fish as a staple. They are greedy and the bioload of a 12-inch cichlid in 75 gallons is the maintenance. Intermediate care means you can keep up with that waste and you have a plan for aggression. It does not mean a special pH ritual. Ordinary neutral to slightly acidic water suits them. The heat can sit in a normal tropical band. The difficulty is the body and the temper, not a narrow KH target.

Twelve to 15 years is a long time to keep a fish that may not tolerate the tankmate you bought in year two. Buy the green terror for a species tank, not as a surprise centerpiece. Do not house them with oscars, flowerhorns, or Jack Dempseys unless the aquarium is far beyond 75 gallons and you already know how you will separate a loser. Oscar, flowerhorn, and Dempsey are the same class of problem: big, mean, and messy. One green terror, a solid cave, a filter that can take the waste, and no fish small enough to be practice prey is a coherent setup.

Temperament & Behavior

The gold saum is the field mark people argue about in shops. If the fish is young, the edge may be faint. A mislabeled blue acara stays smaller and calmer. If the animal in front of you is already past 6 inches, thick through the head, and charging the glass, it is not an electric blue acara and it does not belong in a 30-gallon tank. They will also take a finger if you are careless during a water change near a nest. Expect a spawn if a male and a female share a cave, and decide what happens to the fry before the first eggs, not after a decade of unplanned broods.

Green terrors eat like the big cichlids they are, out in the open, and they remember who fed them. An 8 to 12 inch Andinoacara in 75 gallons or more should come forward for a pellet, a prawn, or an earthworm, then go back to digging. Scatter part of the meal if you keep a pair, or the larger fish takes all of it and the smaller one fades. They are semi-aggressive. Feeding time is when that shows up, especially once they have a spawning site. Do not keep tiny tetras in the same water and call the missing ones a mystery. Live and frozen foods are useful. They are also how a terror learns to bite fingers if you hand-feed carelessly. Use tongs, and watch whether both fish still eat. A terror that will not rise for a prawn has left a very obvious baseline.

Housing & Environment Requirements

A minimum tank size of 75+ gallons is recommended for Green Terror Cichlids. Provide appropriate substrate, hiding places, and compatible tankmates. A quality filtration system and heater are essential.

Diet & Nutrition

Feed Green Terror Cichlids a varied diet appropriate for their species. Overfeeding leads to water quality issues. Vary the diet to ensure complete nutrition.

Portions for an 8 to 12 inch omnivore are larger than for a ram and still finite. A cichlid pellet, earthworm, or market shrimp, eaten in a few minutes at a temperature appropriate for a South American cichlid, then removed, keeps a 75-gallon tank from becoming a nitrate factory. The card budgets about 50 to 150 dollars a year for food. That is enough if you are not dumping a handful because the fish begs. Begging is normal in a green terror. Obesity and filthy water are the usual result of believing it. Feed juveniles more often than a settled 12-inch adult. Twelve to 15 years of overfeeding is a long time to carry a swollen fish in a tank you cannot easily rehome.

A pellet day and a meaty day give you a way to read a green terror's appetite. This species will eat almost anything, so a refusal is meaningful. If a fish that usually inhales an earthworm turns away, darkens, and stays in a cave, stop shopping for new foods and test the water. Variety still matters over 12 to 15 years, because a single cheap pellet is a thin diet for a large omnivore, but the third new jar in a week is not medicine. Note what was eaten and by which fish of the pair. A semi-aggressive cichlid that suddenly shares, or suddenly will not, is telling you about dominance as well as health.

Common Health Issues

Ich, fin rot, bacterial infections, and stress from constant fighting are the health problems that show on green terrors. White spot on a big green body, ragged fins after a spat, and a fish pinned behind the filter are the usual views. Prevention is space, 75 gallons or more, a pair rather than a pile of adults, and water changes that match the waste of an 8 to 12 inch cichlid. Quarantine new fish for a few weeks. Catching a sick terror out of a decorated 75-gallon tank is a wet, destructive afternoon. Do not pre-dose salt or a broad medicine. Nothing on this card is a dose. If the fish declines in clean water, a veterinarian who will see a large cichlid is the next step.

The feeding approach is the exam. A healthy green terror should be bold, green and bright, fins out, taking food from tongs or from the surface without thrashing the whole tank. Your baseline is that confidence. A fish that hides, breathes fast, develops holes or white grains, or stops eating while its partner still comes forward has left it. Look at both animals. Semi-aggressive pairs can injure each other, and the injury looks like disease if you only glance. Catch the change while the fish still eats and still comes to the glass. A 12-inch cichlid off food under a pile of rock is a hard rescue, and this page will not hand you a treatment recipe.

Quarantine a green terror in a cycled tank big enough that an 8-inch fish can turn around, with a cave and a tight lid, before it meets your pair. They are semi-aggressive and they dig, so a small hospital bowl is both cruel and useless as an observation. Several weeks of normal appetite tells you whether you are importing ich into a 75-gallon system you do not want to tear apart. Do not medicate on arrival because large cichlids look tough. If spots appear, they stay in quarantine and a veterinarian sets the plan. Adding a new terror straight to a breeding pair is how the new fish gets killed and how any disease it carried gets distributed.

Grooming & Maintenance

Remove uneaten food promptly. Trim live plants as needed. Monitor equipment function daily. Replace filter media according to manufacturer recommendations.

Beginner-Friendly Rating: Intermediate

Green Terror Cichlids are suitable for keepers with some experience.

Before you buy a green terror, have 75 gallons or more cycled, filtered, and lidded, with a plan for the fish it will eat or beat. They are intermediate, semi-aggressive, and 12 to 15 years long. The card's setup range of about 100 to 500 dollars or more will not always build that tank, so treat it as a floor and talk to keepers who have housed an adult pair. They will mention digging, divorces between pairs, and tankmates that vanished. A beginner community thread will tell you the juvenile looks fine. The juvenile is not the fish you are agreeing to keep.

Socialization & Enrichment

Give a green terror sand or fine gravel it can dig, a cave, and wood, and expect the planting to be rearranged. Rooted plants become debris. Tie hardy plants to rock if you want any green left. Do not rebuild the aquascape every weekend. A semi-aggressive pair treats every rebuild as a new border war. Move one dangerous sharp ornament out. Leave the spawning cave where the pair put it. The useful habitat in 75 gallons or more is open floor for digging and a sight-line break, not a delicate aquascape you will resent the fish for ruining.

Toss an earthworm or a prawn into the open and let a green terror hunt it, then hand a pellet with tongs if you want the interaction without the fingers. That hunt is the enrichment for a big omnivore, and it shows you whether the pair is still bold. Live foods from a clean source work. Feeder fish from a crowded tank are a parasite delivery system and a bad habit. If the terror will not move for a worm, believe the fish and test the water. On a cichlid of 8 to 12 inches, appetite is rarely subtle. The day it becomes subtle is the day you pay attention.

Where to Find Green Terror Cichlids

Buy green terrors from a shop that keeps large cichlids in clean, covered tanks and will show you a feeding, or from a seller with a live-arrival guarantee. About 5 to 100 dollars on the card buys a juvenile more often than a proven adult pair. Pay for a fish that eats and has intact fins, not for the biggest discount in a bruised tank. Quarantine it. A semi-aggressive species that arrives already sick becomes your problem in a tank that is hard to break down. Ask what else was in the shop tank. If the answer is a pile of dead tankmates, choose another fish.

Plan for a large cichlid's upkeep. Setup about 100 to 500 dollars or more, the fish about 5 to 100 dollars, yearly costs about 200 to 500 dollars, and food about 50 to 150 dollars, for an animal that needs 75 gallons and can live 12 to 15 years. Those yearly figures assume you actually change water and replace media on a filter sized for an 8 to 12 inch omnivore. People who buy the juvenile cheap later pay in emergency upgrades and in fish the terror has killed. Add a spare heater and a spare impeller to the year. Semi-aggressive and long-lived means the equipment decision is part of the livestock decision.

A green terror in 75 gallons has more thermal mass than a ram tank, which makes heater failure sneakier. The water cools overnight in a cold room, the fish darkens and stops coming up for food, and ich shows up a day later when you thought the heater had only just died. A stuck-on heater is slower to notice too, until the fish is gasping and the temperature is well above a normal tropical range. Read a thermometer you can see without moving the lid a big cichlid will hit. Keep a second heater that can hold 75 gallons, and aeration if the filter stops. An air pump in a power cut supplies oxygen to a large, oxygen-hungry fish. It does not remove the ammonia a 12-inch cichlid produces while the canister is off. Be ready to change water when the power returns.

Buy the canister, the lid, and the heater as if a 12 to 15 year semi-aggressive cichlid will test them, because it will. Green terrors move decor, splash, and produce waste that clogs cheap filters. The fish may sit at the low end of a 5 to 100 dollar range as a juvenile and still live in a 75-gallon system that costs far more to replace than the animal. A lid that lifts lets a startled terror onto the floor. A filter you cannot get parts for becomes a crisis in year six. Spend on serviceable equipment and replace a noisy pump while the pair is still eating. The tankmates that survive this species, and the mature filter that keeps up with it, are the value you are protecting.

What size tank do Green Terror Cichlids need?

A Green Terror Cichlids does best with at least 75+ gallons — bigger water volumes give you more stable chemistry, extra swim space, and flexibility when you want to add tankmates.

What fish are compatible with Green Terror Cichlids?

With their semi-aggressive temperament, Green Terror Cichlids should be kept with robust tankmates that can hold their own. Avoid small, delicate fish that could be bullied or eaten. Research specific compatibility before adding any new fish. Always research specific compatibility before adding new species to your aquarium.

How often should I feed Green Terror Cichlids?

Feed Green Terror Cichlids 1-2 times daily, offering only what they can consume within 2-3 minutes. Fasting one day per week can benefit digestive health.

Are Green Terror Cichlids good for beginners?

Green Terror Cichlids are suitable for fishkeepers with some experience.
