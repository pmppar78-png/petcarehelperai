Celestial Pearl Danio (Galaxy Rasbora) Overview

Celestial pearl danios are Danio margaritatus, the fish shops also call a galaxy rasbora. The rasbora nickname is a leftover from the year they were described. They are danios, family Cyprinidae, and they are not zebra danios. Adults are about 1 inch and live 3 to 5 years. A tank of 10 gallons or more, densely planted, is the minimum that lets a school behave like itself. Temperament is a peaceful nano fish. In a bare tank with fast roommates they simply vanish into a corner and stop showing the spots you bought them for.

They come from small, vegetated pools in Myanmar, and the aquarium fish today are mostly captive-bred. The wild pools are cooler and quieter than a discus tank or a Lake Malawi setup. Keep them out of hot, hard, alkaline water. A planted community in the low to mid 70s, with gentle flow and a dark substrate, is the look that brings the pearly spots and the orange-red on the males out of hiding. A strong powerhead pins a 1-inch fish to the glass. Turn the flow down or baffle it.

Males carry more red on the belly and a busier pattern of pearly spots. Females are rounder and plainer, with a dark little mark at the vent that is easier to see in a clear cup than in the tank. A school of eight or more, with both sexes, is when the males display instead of sulk. Six males and no females will spar in a way the peaceful label did not prepare you for. The fighting is small, but a beaten male stops eating, and at this size you notice only when he is already thin.

The mouth is tiny. Crushed flake, a micro pellet, and frozen baby brine shrimp or cyclops are the foods that fit. A community wafer meant for a pleco is irrelevant to them. Feed in the planted midwater where they actually hunt. If danios or barbs share the tank, the celestial pearls get nothing. They should not share with boisterous barbs at all. A tiger barb is a different kind of animal, and a 1-inch shy danio is not a dither fish for it.

They jump. A lid, or a full cover of floating plants plus a lid, belongs on the tank. The floating plants also dim the light, which is what gets a school out of the stems and into the open. Three to 5 years is a short life, and it is easy to waste the first months in a tank that is too bright, too fast, and too empty. They are listed as beginner fish. That is fair in a quiet planted 10-gallon tank and unfair in a community that was stocked for color and speed.

Temperament & Behavior

Do not buy them as a substitute for zebra danios. Danio rerio is larger, faster, happier in cooler open water, and perfectly willing to nip a slow neighbor. Celestial pearls are the opposite animal that happens to share a genus. A shop that labels them galaxy rasboras is selling the same Danio margaritatus. A shop that offers them as food for an angelfish has misunderstood both fish. One inch is the adult size, not a juvenile measurement they will grow out of.

Galaxy rasboras feed like tiny midwater hunters, not like goldfish waiting under a flake shaker. A one-inch celestial pearl danio picks at micro foods among plants, and in a 10-gallon or larger tank the food has to be small enough to fit that mouth. Scatter powdered fry food, baby brine, or a crushed fine pellet across the planting so the whole peaceful group gets some. Dumping a pinch in one spot lets two bold fish eat and the rest fade. Watch who is out at feeding time. A CPD that no longer leaves the moss when brine shrimp hit the water is telling you about health or harassment, even though the species is listed as peaceful. Live and frozen bits, spread out, keep the hunting behavior that dry dust alone eventually dulls.

Housing & Environment Requirements

A minimum tank size of 10+ gallons is recommended for Celestial Pearl Danio (Galaxy Rasbora). Provide appropriate substrate, hiding places, and peaceful tankmates. A quality filtration system and heater are essential.

Diet & Nutrition

Feed Celestial Pearl Danio (Galaxy Rasbora) a varied diet appropriate for their species. Use micro-pellets, crushed flakes, and small frozen foods like baby brine shrimp. Overfeeding leads to water quality issues. Vary the diet to ensure complete nutrition.

Portions for a one-inch omnivore are smaller than most keepers think, and the mess lands in a tank that may be only 10 gallons. Feed a fine pellet or crushed flake they can finish in a minute, plus baby brine or microworms a few times a week, and siphon what reaches the substrate. The card budgets about 50 to 150 dollars a year for food, which covers quality micro food if you are not buying large cichlid pellets that rot uneaten. Overfeeding is the usual way nitrate climbs and a 3 to 5 year nano fish loses condition. Underfeeding is the quiet way a shy fish in the back of the school starves while the front rank looks fat. Watch both ends of the group.

One jar of flakes will not carry a galaxy rasbora that lives by picking varied tiny prey off leaves. Rotate a micro pellet, frozen baby brine or daphnia, and an occasional live food from a clean source. Appetite is your early-warning light on a fish only an inch long. If a celestial pearl danio that used to spark across the front ignores a favorite brine feeding, test the water and count the school before you change brands. Refusal is often the first visible illness in a nano fish whose color is already subtle. Write down what they ate. Memory is a poor log for a fish this small.

Common Health Issues

Ich, fin rot, bacterial infections, and stress are the problems that actually show on a fish this small, usually as flicking, clamped tiny fins, white grains, or a school that has stopped coming out. Water quality is the prevention. A 10-gallon tank swings faster than a big one, so nitrate and leftover food matter. Quarantine new galaxy rasboras as a group for a few weeks. They are peaceful nano fish and they travel better together than alone. Do not keep a bottle of broad medicine as a plan, and do not dose aquarium salt into a small tank on a guess. If the school declines, ask a veterinarian. A one-inch fish has no margin for a wrong treatment.

The foundation of Celestial Pearl Danio health management is water quality — full stop. Regular testing and diligent maintenance are your best tools.

Use a magnifying look at feeding, because a one-inch CPD does not give you a big canvas. Healthy galaxy rasboras flick through the plants and take micro food in mid-flight. A fish that sits on a leaf, breathes fast, or loses the pearl spots' brightness against the group has left its baseline. White spot and frayed fins are easy to miss on a dark, tiny body unless you look when they come forward to eat. That daily minute is the whole exam. If several fish change on the same day, think water first, then a pathogen, and do not reach for a dose that was printed for a fish ten times their size.

Quarantine celestial pearl danios as a school in a cycled nano tank with plants or moss, not as a single fish in a bare bowl. They are peaceful, an inch long, and they stop eating when they are alone, which hides disease instead of revealing it. Give them a few weeks at stable water, with micro food, before they join the display. A 10-gallon main tank is already small. Adding unobserved fish to it is how ich arrives with no spare tank to move them into. Skip speculative medications. If you see spots in quarantine, leave the display alone and get advice from a veterinarian rather than dosing both boxes.

Grooming & Maintenance

Remove uneaten food promptly. Trim live plants as needed. Monitor equipment function daily. Replace filter media according to manufacturer recommendations.

Beginner-Friendly Rating: Beginner

Celestial Pearl Danio (Galaxy Rasbora) are excellent for newcomers to fishkeeping.

Have the planted 10-gallon tank cycled and growing a little film before you buy galaxy rasboras. They are beginner nano fish only when the box is already calm, covered, and free of fish that can eat a one-inch animal. Setup on this card runs about 100 to 500 dollars or more, and the fish themselves about 5 to 100 dollars each, so the money belongs in a ready tank. Talk to keepers who run CPDs specifically. They will tell you about jumping lids and about food that has to be crushed. A forum for oscars will not. Test ammonia, nitrite, and nitrate the week before the bag arrives.

Socialization & Enrichment

Plant the tank the way a one-inch fish uses it: fine leaves, moss, and open pockets rather than a pile of sharp rock. Celestial pearl danios come from small, planted water, and a bare 10-gallon glass box leaves a peaceful school with nowhere to rest. You do not need to rebuild the scape every weekend. A hard rearrange scatters a nano group that had just started coming to the front. Change one clump if a corner has died back, and keep a clear lane where you can see them feed. Shade and cover do more for their behavior than a new ornament in the center of the sand.

Enrichment for a galaxy rasbora is a moving micro food, not a big worm it cannot swallow. Rinse baby brine into the current across the moss and let the school hunt. Frozen daphnia, broken up, does the same job when live food is unavailable. Pellets still feed them, but a fish that only ever eats a static pile loses the pecking behavior you are using as a health check. When the hunt stops, believe it. On a one-inch, 3 to 5 year fish, a missed feeding pattern is often the only early sign you are going to get.

Where to Find Celestial Pearl Danio (Galaxy Rasbora)

Choose celestial pearl danios from a shop that keeps them in a lidded, planted tank and can show you a group eating micro food. A single inflated price tag does not matter as much as pinched bellies. The card allows roughly 5 to 100 dollars per fish, which means you can overpay for a thin animal or find a fair group. Mail-order fish need a live-arrival promise and a quarantine tank that is already cycled. Open the box quickly, keep them together, and do not pour them into a community that has not been prepared. A peaceful nano fish arrives healthy or it does not arrive useful at all.

The time they live is not a single weekend.

A 10-gallon tank has almost no thermal mass. If the heater sticks on, a celestial pearl danio school can leave a comfortable range before the evening, and if it fails off in a cold room the same box chills just as fast. These are peaceful nano fish. They respond to that swing by hiding, clamping, and catching ich or a bacterial problem that a stable tank would have shrugged off. Put the thermometer where you see it without moving the lid they like to jump through. Keep a spare heater appropriate to 10 gallons, not a powerful unit borrowed from a 75-gallon tank. If the filter stops, ammonia rises quickly in this volume. An air stone during an outage is a bridge. It is not a cycled filter.

Spend on a lid, a small reliable heater, and a filter gentle enough for a one-inch fish, then stop. The card's livestock can be inexpensive, and the costly part is a planted 10-gallon system you would have to restart, along with any shrimp or other nano animals sharing it. A bargain heater without a decent thermostat is how a tiny school gets cooked. A strong filter intake without a sponge is how they get exhausted. Gear that holds a stable box for the 3 to 5 year lifespan is the purchase that matches the animal. Replace a tired pump while the galaxy rasboras are still eating, not after the tank has clouded.

What fish are compatible with Celestial Pearl Danio (Galaxy Rasbora)?

With their peaceful nano temperament, Celestial Pearl Danio (Galaxy Rasbora) are compatible with many community fish of similar size and temperament. How often should I feed Celestial Pearl Danio (Galaxy Rasbora)?

Feed Celestial Pearl Danio (Galaxy Rasbora) 1-2 times daily, offering only what they can consume within 2-3 minutes. Fasting one day per week can benefit digestive health.

Are Celestial Pearl Danio (Galaxy Rasbora) good for beginners?

Yes, Celestial Pearl Danio (Galaxy Rasbora) are excellent for beginning fishkeepers. Start with a properly cycled tank and maintain regular maintenance habits.
