Siamese Algae Eater Overview

The Siamese algae eater is the cyprinid that actually eats black beard algae, and the adult size on this card is 6 inches with a lifespan of about 10 years. A fish that reaches 6 inches needs 20 gallons or more. Temperament is listed as peaceful and the difficulty as beginner. Those words describe Crossocheilus oblongus and Crossocheilus langei, not every slim fish with a black stripe that a shop has labeled algae eater.

Flying foxes and Chinese algae eaters are the look-alikes, and they are not this fish. A Chinese algae eater grows larger, gets more aggressive, and has a different mouth. A flying fox wears a similar stripe and does not do the same job on black beard. On a true Siamese algae eater the dark stripe continues into the tail, and the mouth is not a big sucker parked under the head. A juvenile in a dealer tank looks smaller than the animal you are buying. At 6 inches it is no longer a nano fish, and 20 gallons is the floor for that length, not a suggestion that it will stay 3 inches.

They are useful against black beard when they are hungry and the algae is the soft new growth, not a foot-long forest that already took over a neglected tank. They also need a normal diet. Algae wafers, blanched greens, and ordinary omnivore foods keep them fed when the beard algae is gone. A keeper who buys one, sees the algae disappear, and then never feeds the fish will have a thin 6-inch cyprinid and a tank that grows algae again the moment the fish slows down. They do not replace water changes.

Beginner care assumes you bought the right species and gave it 20 gallons with a lid. They are active, they rest on leaves and wood at odd angles, and they can be kept singly or with a few of their own kind if the tank is long enough for a group to spread out. Peaceful does not mean they enjoy being trapped with fin-nipping barbs in a short aquarium. It means they are not a territorial cichlid. Fast, busy tankmates of a similar size are a better fit than a slow fancy goldfish or a betta.

The card's money figures are generic and wide: setup about 100 to 500 dollars or more, the fish anywhere from about 5 to 100 dollars or more, annual upkeep about 200 to 500 dollars, and food about 50 to 150 dollars a year. The expensive mistake is not the sticker price. It is paying for a look-alike that will not touch black beard and may outgrow the peaceful label. Ask for Crossocheilus and look at the stripe on the tail before you leave the shop.

Temperament & Behavior

A 10-year lifespan in 20 gallons or more only works if the adult body was part of the plan. Upgrade the swimming length as the fish passes 4 inches if the original tank was the bare minimum with other stock already in it. They are good jumpers when the lid is an afterthought. Cover the tank, feed them after the algae is under control, and do not expect a Chinese algae eater or a flying fox to honor this job description.

Housing & Environment Requirements

A minimum tank size of 20+ gallons is recommended for Siamese Algae Eaters.

Diet & Nutrition

Feed Siamese Algae Eaters a varied diet appropriate for their species.

Observing your Siamese Algae Eater's response to different food types also provides useful health information, as reduced interest in normally preferred foods is often an early indicator of illness.

Common Health Issues

Common health issues in Siamese Algae Eaters include ich (white spot disease), fin rot, bacterial infections, and stress-related conditions.

Quick Answers About Siamese Algae Eaters

What size tank do Siamese Algae Eaters need?

Don't go below 20+ gallons for a Siamese Algae Eaters.

What fish are compatible with Siamese Algae Eaters?

With their peaceful temperament, Siamese Algae Eaters are compatible with many community fish of similar size and temperament.

How often should I feed Siamese Algae Eaters?

Feed Siamese Algae Eaters 1-2 times daily, offering only what they can consume within 2-3 minutes.

Are Siamese Algae Eaters good for beginners?

Yes, Siamese Algae Eaters are excellent for beginning fishkeepers.

Long Term Commitment

Owning Siamese Algae Eaters is a commitment that spans their full lifespan of 10 years.
