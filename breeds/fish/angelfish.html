Angelfish Overview

The Freshwater Angelfish is one of the most elegant and popular cichlids in the aquarium hobby. With their distinctive triangular shape, long flowing fins, and graceful swimming style, angelfish have been captivating aquarists for over a century. They're often called the "King of the Aquarium" for their regal appearance.

Originally from the Amazon River basin, angelfish have been selectively bred into numerous color varieties including silver, black, marble, koi, gold, and platinum. While they have a reputation as peaceful community fish, they are actually cichlids with complex social behaviors and can be territorial, especially when breeding.

Freshwater angelfish are Pterophyllum scalare, a tall Amazon cichlid. The body grows to about 6 inches long. The dorsal and anal fins make the fish 8 to 10 inches tall. That height is why the minimum is a 30-gallon tall tank, not a long shallow tank with the same gallon count. A fish that cannot turn without folding its fins into the gravel is in the wrong shape of water. Lifespan is 10 to 12 years when the water stays clean and the fins stay intact.

Hold them at 76 to 84°F, pH 6.0 to 7.5, and hardness around 3 to 8 dGH. That is softer water than a livebearer tank and the wrong water for a Lake Malawi setup. Ammonia and nitrite at 0 ppm and nitrate under 20 ppm are the standing limits. They are omnivores. A cichlid pellet they can bite, plus frozen bloodworms or brine shrimp, matches the way they feed in the middle of the water column. Flakes that sink past them before they notice are a poor staple for a fish this tall.

Temperament is semi-aggressive, and a bonded pair is the moment that label becomes obvious. Angels clean a broad leaf or a piece of slate and then own that corner. Tetras that were ignored for months become food or get driven into the heater. Juveniles can be grown as a group in a tall tank. Once a pair forms, the spare angels usually have to move. Six adults in the 30-gallon minimum is not a stocking plan. The 30 is the smallest tank that fits the fin span of one or two fish.

Natural Habitat & Origin

Understanding angelfish origins helps provide optimal care.

  • Native Range: Amazon River basin, Orinoco basin, various rivers in Guiana
  • Habitat: Slow-moving, heavily vegetated waters with submerged roots and fallen trees
  • Water Type: Soft, acidic blackwater with tannins
  • Environment: Vertical hiding spots among plant stems and roots

Their tall, laterally compressed bodies evolved to navigate through dense vegetation and vertical structures in their native habitat.

Tank Requirements & Setup

Angelfish need specific tank considerations due to their body shape.

Tank Size

  • Minimum: 30 gallons tall for a single angelfish or pair
  • Recommended: 55+ gallons for a small group
  • Height: At least 18-20 inches tall to accommodate their body shape
  • Groups: Keep 5-6+ to distribute aggression, or a mated pair

Essential Equipment

  • Heater: Reliable heater for stable warm temperatures
  • Filter: Moderate flow - angelfish prefer calmer water
  • Lighting: Moderate lighting, plants provide shade
  • Thermometer: Monitor temperature closely

Decoration & Plants

  • Tall Plants: Amazon swords, vallisneria, tall crypts
  • Vertical Structures: Driftwood, tall decorations
  • Spawning Sites: Broad-leaved plants, slate, or spawning cones
  • Substrate: Sand or fine gravel
  • Open Swimming Space: Balance cover with room to swim

Temperature Sensitivity

Angelfish are sensitive to temperature fluctuations. Sudden changes can trigger ich outbreaks or stress-related illness. Use a quality heater and avoid placing the tank near windows or vents.

Diet & Feeding

Angelfish are omnivores that benefit from a varied diet: Your aquatic veterinarian and experienced Angelfish owners can offer perspective tailored to your situation.

Primary Foods

  • High-Quality Flakes/Pellets: Cichlid-specific formulas
  • Frozen Foods: Bloodworms, brine shrimp, mysis shrimp
  • Live Foods: Blackworms, brine shrimp, daphnia
  • Vegetables: Blanched spinach, zucchini
  • Specialty Foods: Color-enhancing formulas

Feeding Guidelines

  • Feed 2-3 times daily in amounts consumed within 3 minutes
  • Vary diet for optimal nutrition and coloration
  • Condition with live/frozen foods before breeding

Freshwater Angelfish consistent chemistry, controlled feeding, and deliberate quarantine sit at the centre of sustained aquatic welfare; these factors drive outcomes more than brand-name products.

Behavior & Temperament

Angelfish exhibit complex cichlid behavior: Generic guidance gets you to the starting line; the actual gains come from calibrating the plan to your specific animal.

  • Territorial: Establish and defend territories, especially when breeding
  • Hierarchical: Form pecking orders within groups
  • Pair Bonding: Mated pairs form strong bonds and work together
  • Semi-Aggressive: May bully smaller or weaker fish
  • Predatory: Will eat fish small enough to fit in their mouths
  • Intelligent: Recognize owners and feeding routines

Fin-nippers and angelfish do not share a tank. Tiger barbs will reduce a veil-fin angel to stumps, and the angel stops eating once the damage is bad. Very small fish, including newborn livebearers and tiny rasboras, fit in an adult mouth. A school of cardinal tetras is the classic dither, and even those are safer in a tank larger than the minimum. Slow fish with long fins, such as fancy guppies, get the worst of the sparring even when nobody is spawning.

Most shop fish are scalare, or a farmed line of it. Altum angels are a different species, taller still, and less forgiving of ordinary tap water. Marble, koi, and veil are color and fin forms of the common fish, and the longest fins are the first thing another cichlid shreds. Buy animals that are eating and holding the fins fully extended, not a pale fish clamped in the top corner of a wholesale vat. Ten to 12 years is a long time to keep a cichlid that will eventually refuse the tankmates you chose in year one.

Compatibility with Other

Careful tank mate selection is important with angelfish.

Compatible Tank Mates

  • Medium-sized tetras (not neons - too small when angels mature)
  • Corydoras catfish
  • Larger rasboras
  • Dwarf gouramis
  • Bristlenose plecos
  • Larger peaceful cichlids (rams, keyholes)
  • Rainbowfish

Incompatible Species

  • Small tetras (neons, cardinals - may be eaten)
  • Fin nippers (tiger barbs, serpae tetras)
  • Aggressive cichlids
  • Small shrimp (will be eaten)
  • Very active fish that may stress angels

Breeding Information

Angelfish are dedicated parents and can breed in home aquariums: Your aquatic veterinarian and experienced Angelfish owners can offer perspective tailored to your situation.

  • Pair Formation: Best to let angels choose mates from a group
  • Spawning Site: Vertical surfaces - leaves, slate, aquarium glass
  • Egg Laying: Females lay 100-1,000+ eggs in rows
  • Parental Care: Both parents guard and fan eggs
  • Hatching: 48-72 hours at 80°F
  • Fry Development: Fry become free-swimming after about 5 days
  • Fry Food: Baby brine shrimp, microworms, crushed flakes

Popular Angelfish Varieties

Common Health Issues

Angelfish can be susceptible to several conditions: Upfront effort to understand how an Angelfish actually operates usually pays dividends in fewer vet emergencies.

Ich White Spot

  • Symptoms: White spots, flashing, clamped fins
  • Treatment: Raise temperature to 86°F, ich medication

Hexamita (Hole-in-Head Disease)

  • Symptoms: Pitting around head and lateral line
  • Causes: Poor water quality, nutritional deficiencies
  • Treatment: Metronidazole, improve water quality and diet

Fin Rot

  • Symptoms: Deteriorating fins, often with redness

Angelfish Virus

  • Symptoms: Excessive slime coat, lethargy, often fatal
  • Prevention: Quarantine new fish, avoid stress

Cost of Ownership

The true cost of keeping Freshwater Angelfish extends well beyond the initial purchase price and basic equipment. While the upfront investment in a properly equipped aquarium (minimum 30 gallons tall) represents a significant portion of the total cost, ongoing expenses for water treatment chemicals, filter media, electricity for heating and lighting, and food constitute the majority of long-term spending. Many aspiring Freshwater Angelfish keepers underestimate these ongoing costs, which can lead to cut corners that ultimately compromise fish health and result in even greater expenses.
