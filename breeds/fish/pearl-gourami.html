Pearl Gourami Overview

Pearl Gouramis thrive in water temperatures of 77-82°F with a pH range of 6.0-8.0.

Trichopodus leerii is a larger gourami, not a dwarf that can be tucked into a nano tank. The adult size is 4 to 5 inches, the lifespan is 4 to 5 years, and the minimum tank is 30 gallons. A pearl gourami carries a filamentous pelvic feeler on each side, a thread the fish uses to test the space in front of it. Those threads tear on sharp plastic and get nipped off by the wrong neighbors, so the feelers are a husbandry detail, not just a field mark.

Males build bubble nests. A mature male under floating plants will stack bubbles and try to spawn if a female is ready. The tank should allow a quiet corner at the surface and a gap of still air under the lid, because this is a labyrinth fish that takes breaths from above. A tight, agitated surface with no cover makes a spawning male abandon the nest and leaves the fish gulping in a current. Temperature on the card is 77 to 82°F and pH is a wide 6.0 to 8.0. A nesting male still needs ammonia at 0 ppm and nitrite at 0 ppm. Nitrate under the nest should stay under 20 ppm.

They are more peaceful than three-spot gouramis, and the card rates them peaceful and beginner-level. That peace is conditional. Crowded pearl gouramis nip long fins. A 30-gallon tank with two males, a betta, and an angelfish is already crowded in the way that matters. One male with one or more females, or a single fish, in a planted 30 gallons is the stocking that matches the temperament line. Extra length helps more than extra height.

Behavior & Temperament

. They take flakes at the surface, frozen bloodworms, and soft vegetable matter. Feed a mix so a fish that spends its life at the top still gets more than one color of flake. Care feels easy until a male is in nest-building mode and starts shoving tankmates out of the top corner. Have a second plan for that month, even if you are not raising the fry. The fry, if you let the nest stand, need infusoria and then fine foods, and the adults will eat them if the plants are sparse.

Do not keep them in a cold room below that 77°F floor for long, and do not assume the peaceful label survives a tank of fin-nipping barbs. The threads, the bubble nest, and the 4-to-5-inch body are the three things that separate this fish from the smaller gouramis people confuse with it. A 30-gallon planted tank with a lid, gentle flow, and neighbors that leave long fins alone is the setup the card is describing.

Pearl gouramis, Trichopodus leerii, are labyrinth fish that reach about 4 to 5 inches and show their best spots in a planted tank with gentle flow. Males build a bubble nest. They eat a pellet and frozen foods and breathe at the surface, so the lid should trap moist air and the filter should not churn that surface flat.

The diet is omnivore.
