Overview

Cherry Shrimp (Neocaridina davidi) are one of the most popular freshwater invertebrates in the aquarium hobby. Originally bred from wild Neocaridina in Taiwan, these vibrant red shrimp have been selectively bred into numerous color grades, from pale pink "Sakura" to deep red "Painted Fire Red" varieties.

Their hardiness, peaceful nature, and prolific breeding make them ideal for beginners and experienced aquarists alike. Cherry Shrimp serve as excellent algae eaters and cleanup crew members while adding a splash of color to planted aquariums.

Understanding the full scope of Cherry Shrimp care requires appreciating the biological and behavioral complexity of this species. As a 1-1.5 inches (2.5-4 cm) aquatic animal with a typical lifespan of 1-2 years, the Cherry Shrimp has evolved specific physiological adaptations that directly influence how they should be kept in captivity. Their natural habitat—characterized by specific water chemistry, flow patterns, and ecological relationships—provides the blueprint for successful aquarium husbandry. Experienced aquarists consistently note that Cherry Shrimp thrive when keepers replicate these natural conditions as closely as possible, rather than simply meeting minimum survival parameters.

The Cherry Shrimp's behavioral repertoire extends well beyond what casual observers might expect. These fish exhibit complex social hierarchies, territorial behaviors, and feeding strategies that become increasingly apparent in well-maintained aquarium environments. Their well-balanced disposition means that tank mate selection requires careful consideration—not all community fish are compatible, and individual personality variation means that even within the same species, behavioral differences can be significant. Keepers who invest time in observing their Cherry Shrimp's natural behaviors are better equipped to identify stress indicators, illness onset, and social conflict before these issues escalate into serious problems.

For Cherry Shrimp, the most reliable results come from parameter consistency, species-matched diet rotation, and early correction of stress signals.

Natural Habitat

Wild Neocaridina davidi inhabit freshwater streams and ponds in Taiwan and parts of China: Understanding how this applies specifically to Cherry Shrimp helps you avoid common pitfalls.

  • Environment: Slow-moving streams with dense vegetation
  • Substrate: Rocky bottoms with leaf litter and debris
  • Water: Clean, well-oxygenated water with moderate hardness
  • Natural Diet: Biofilm, algae, decaying plant matter, and microorganisms

Tank Requirements

Setting up the ideal Cherry Shrimp habitat: Your aquatic veterinarian and experienced Cherry Shrimp owners can offer perspective tailored to your situation.

  • Tank Size: Minimum 5 gallons; 10+ gallons recommended for colonies
  • Substrate: Inert gravel, sand, or active shrimp soil
  • Filtration: Sponge filter recommended (safe for shrimplets)
  • Plants: Java moss, Java fern, Anubias, mosses, and floating plants
  • Hiding Spots: Driftwood, cholla wood, shrimp tubes, and dense plant cover
  • Lighting: Moderate lighting to support plant growth and biofilm

Copper Warning

Cherry Shrimp are extremely sensitive to copper. Avoid medications containing copper and check fertilizers for copper content. Even trace amounts can be lethal to invertebrates.

Diet & Feeding

Cherry Shrimp are omnivorous scavengers with diverse dietary needs: When in doubt, choose the guidance that names the Cherry Shrimp explicitly over the guidance that treats all pets alike.

  • Biofilm: Primary food source; grows naturally on surfaces
  • Algae: Graze on various algae types throughout the tank
  • Commercial Foods: Shrimp pellets, wafers, and specialty foods
  • Blanched Vegetables: Zucchini, spinach, cucumber, carrots
  • Protein: Occasional bloodworms, brine shrimp, or fish food
  • Calcium Sources: Cuttlebone, mineral supplements, or crushed coral

Feeding Frequency: Feed sparingly every 1-2 days. In established tanks with biofilm, supplemental feeding may be minimal.

Cherry Shrimp consistent husbandry cadence and thoughtful stocking decisions produce better outcomes than periodic equipment upgrades rather than copied from general fish templates.

Behavior & Temperament

Cherry Shrimp exhibit fascinating behaviors.

  • Peaceful: Completely non-aggressive toward tankmates
  • Social: Thrive in groups; more active in larger colonies
  • Grazing: Constantly pick at surfaces for food particles
  • Molting: Shed exoskeleton regularly as they grow
  • Swimming: Females may "swim" when releasing pheromones (breeding behavior)

The behavioral complexity of Cherry Shrimp is often underestimated by those new to the aquarium hobby. While aquarium fish are sometimes perceived as passive decorative elements, Cherry Shrimp display a rich repertoire of social behaviors, territorial strategies, and environmental interactions that become increasingly fascinating to observe over time. Their well-balanced disposition provides a general framework for predicting behavior, but individual variation is significant—experienced keepers learn to read the subtle body language cues, color changes, and swimming patterns that indicate mood, stress level, and social status within the tank hierarchy.

Compatibility

Cherry Shrimp are vulnerable prey animals - choose tankmates carefully.

Good Tankmates

  • Other Neocaridina shrimp (may interbreed)
  • Small peaceful fish: Otocinclus, Pygmy Corydoras, small Rasboras
  • Snails: Nerite, Mystery, Ramshorn
  • Amano Shrimp (won't interbreed)

Avoid

  • Most fish larger than 2 inches
  • Cichlids (even small ones)
  • Bettas (individual temperament varies)
  • Gouramis, angelfish, and other predatory species
  • Crayfish and crabs

Breeding

Cherry Shrimp breed readily in home aquariums.

  • Sexual Maturity: 3-4 months of age
  • Identification: Females larger, more colorful with curved underbelly (saddle)
  • Mating: Occurs after female molts; males actively seek receptive females
  • Eggs: Females carry 20-30 eggs under their tail for 3-4 weeks
  • Shrimplets: Hatch as miniature adults; no larval stage
  • Survival: Provide dense moss and hiding spots for babies

Color Grades

Cherry Shrimp are graded by color intensity: Your aquatic veterinarian and experienced Cherry Shrimp owners can offer perspective tailored to your situation.

  • Cherry (Regular): Light red with some transparency
  • Sakura: More solid red coverage
  • Fire Red: Deep solid red throughout
  • Painted Fire Red: Intense red with no transparency
  • Bloody Mary: Darkest red, nearly opaque

Health Issues

Common health concerns for Cherry Shrimp.

  • Failed Molts: Often caused by improper mineral balance (GH/KH)
  • Bacterial Infections: White or pink discoloration; often fatal
  • Parasites: Vorticella (white fuzzy growth), Scutariella (worms on rostrum)
  • Muscular Necrosis: White muscle tissue; stress-related and fatal
  • Planaria: Flatworms that can attack shrimp and eggs

Prevention Tips

Maintain stable water parameters, avoid sudden temperature changes, quarantine new additions, and ensure adequate calcium for successful molting. Many shrimp deaths are caused by rapid parameter swings rather than illness.

Cherry Shrimp three disciplines determine outcomes: keeping parameters stable, measuring feed portions, and quarantining new livestock thoroughly; these factors drive outcomes more than brand-name products.
