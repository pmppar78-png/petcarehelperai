Recommended for Turbo Snails

Turbo snails in the aquarium trade are marine turban snails kept for algae, commonly animals in the genus Turbo, at a size of about 2 to 3 inches. Fed, and kept in stable salt, they live 3 to 5 years. One turbo can be housed in 10 gallons of finished saltwater. Care is rated beginner, the temperament peaceful, and the diet herbivore. The water is saltwater: 72 to 82°F, pH 8.1 to 8.4, salinity 1.020 to 1.025, nitrate under 10 ppm, ammonia 0 ppm, and nitrite 0 ppm. They are not mystery snails and they will not live in a freshwater planted tank.

Turbo Snail Overview

Turbo Snails thrive in water temperatures of 72-82°F with a pH range of 8.1-8.4. As an herbivore, they primarily graze on algae and plant matter.

A beginner rating assumes the saltwater is already mixed and stable. A turbo snail dropped into a tank that is still cycling, with ammonia above 0 ppm, dies and then makes the ammonia worse. They also starve in a spotless aquarium. Film and hair algae are the food. If the glass is wiped every day and the rocks are new, feed algae wafers or dried seaweed on a clip. A 2-inch snail in a 10-gallon tank with no algae and no supplement does not last the 3-to-5-year span.

Behavior & Temperament

They are strong and clumsy. A turbo can bulldoze a loose frag plug, knock a small coral off a rock, and plow a sand bed onto a low polyp. Glue down anything fragile, and do not use them as a precision cleanup tool in a carefully set aquascape. One snail in 10 gallons is plenty. Several large turbos in a small tank will strip the film, then starve, and they add more waste than a beginner tank's skimmer may be ready for.

The shell needs calcium from the saltwater itself. Salinity that swings below the 1.020 to 1.025 band, or a pH that sags under 8.1, shows up as pitting and a slow animal. They can fall onto the sand and struggle to right an upside-down shell, especially a large one in a deep bed. Set a new snail on the rockwork and check it the next day. A snail that stays sealed and smells foul is dead and should come out. An empty-looking shell with the operculum pulled in may still be resting.

Copper, and any medication that contains it, kills marine snails. Treat fish in a separate tank if you need those products. Hermit crabs will recycle a turbo shell after the snail dies, which is not a reason to keep the two in a tiny tank and hope. Give the snail algae, stable reef-range salt, and a 10-gallon minimum that is not already full of competing grazers. The peaceful label means it will not hunt fish. It does not mean it will leave every coral plug where you glued it.
