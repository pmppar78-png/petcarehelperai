Peppermint Shrimp Overview

The Peppermint Shrimp is a small (2 in) saltwater invert invertebrate that is an excellent choice for both beginners and experienced aquarists.

Peppermint shrimp are small marine shrimp, about 2 inches, with a lifespan of 2 to 3 years. The card places them in a minimum of 10 gallons at beginner level, peaceful, and omnivorous. The water is reef water: 72 to 82°F, pH 8.1 to 8.4, salinity 1.020 to 1.025, nitrate under 10 ppm, ammonia 0 ppm, and nitrite 0 ppm. They are not a freshwater cleanup crew, and they will die quickly in a tank that is still mixing salt by guesswork.

Common Health Issues

The shrimp people want under this name are Lysmata in the peppermint complex, often kept because they pick at Aiptasia. Not every red-striped shrimp in a shop cup is that animal. Lookalikes, including camel shrimp, are sold under the same common name and may ignore anemones or pick at coral polyps instead. Ask what was imported, and do not stock a reef on the assumption that any banded shrimp will clear a pest anemone and leave the corals alone.

Behavior & Temperament

A 10-gallon tank can hold one of these shrimp if it is mature enough to offer film, detritus, and a frozen-food feeding. It is a tight home once fish are added. Peppermint shrimp are slow, nocturnal eaters and they get picked off by hawkfish, large wrasses, and anything else that hunts crustaceans. Give them rockwork with real gaps, feed mysis or a small pellet after the lights dim, and do not house them with the perching predators that treat a 2-inch shrimp as the meal.

They molt. An empty shell on the sand is not a dead shrimp until you have counted the live animal. The new shell is soft, and that is when tankmates do the most damage. Iodine and trace elements in a normal reef mix, stable salinity inside 1.020 to 1.025, and no copper in the water are what get them through a molt. Copper treatments aimed at fish parasites are lethal to this shrimp. Move it out, or do not use copper in its tank at all.

Peaceful does not mean they ignore every sessile animal. A hungry peppermint can sample polyps. Feed them so pest-eating is a bonus rather than the whole diet. Beginner care is fair only if the tank is already mixed, aged, and free of shrimp-eaters. A new 10-gallon with a swinging salinity and a damselfish is how a 2-to-3-year lifespan becomes a weekend.
