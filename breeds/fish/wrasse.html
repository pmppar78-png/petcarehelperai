Stock the wrasse you actually bought. Pseudocheilinus six-lines are small and territorial. Cirrhilabrus fairy wrasses need a lid and one male. Halichoeres often need sand to sleep in. Anampses and bird wrasses are harder and many die on frozen food. The word wrasse on a label is not a care sheet.

Wrasse Overview

The Wrasse is a varies (3-12 in) saltwater species that is well-suited for hobbyists with some experience. With a lifespan of 5-10 years, this varies species requires a minimum tank size of 50+ gal.

Wrasses thrive in water temperatures of 72-82°F with a pH range of 8.1-8.4. As a carnivore, they prefer meaty foods and live or frozen offerings.

This page groups wrasses that range about 3-12 inches and often live 5-10 years when the species is hardy and fed. That span is the problem. A 3-inch six-line and a foot-long tamarin do not share a tank size. Read the scientific name, then size the tank for the adult, not for the juvenile in the bag. A lifespan of 5-10 years is for a hardy species that eats, not for a cleaner wrasse that starves.

Temperament varies with the genus. Six-lines harass shy fish and eat pest snails, including pyramidellids. Fairy wrasses are peaceful plankton feeders. Coris and Halichoeres eat shrimp, crabs, and small fish. Do not add a wrasse to a reef whose cleanup crew is the thing you wanted to keep. Watch the fish at night. A wrasse missing at lights-out may be under the sand, not dead.

Marine wrasses need 72-82°F, pH 8.1-8.4, and salt around natural seawater. Use the overview figure of 50+ gallons for any wrasse that swims in the open or shares the tank with other fish. Sand-sleepers need a sand bed deep enough to dive into. Every wrasse tank needs a lid. A covered 50-plus-gallon tank is the plan for an open-swimming wrasse. A small six-line is the only wrasse that fits a careful 30-gallon reef, and every other wrasse on this page needs the longer tank.

Behavior & Temperament

Wrasse are characterized by a varies disposition that influences their care requirements and compatibility.

  • General Disposition: Varies nature that defines daily interactions

Wrasses on this card are saltwater fish, not a single species. Adult size is listed as varying from 3 to 12 inches, lifespan from 5 to 10 years, and the minimum tank as 50 gallons or more. Intermediate is the family rating, and the unnamed species is why. The diet is carnivore. Temperament varies. Temperature is 72 to 82°F, pH 8.1 to 8.4, salinity 1.020 to 1.025, nitrate under 10 ppm, with ammonia and nitrite at 0 ppm. Any wrasse you bring home still has to fit inside that envelope, and a 12-inch adult does not fit the same 50-gallon tank as a 3-inch fairy wrasse.

The family is the problem with a generic page. Some wrasses are small, reef-safe pickers of pests. Some bury in the sand at night and need a bed deep enough to disappear into. Some jump. Some get large enough to eat shrimp and small fish. A shop label that only says wrasse is not a care guide. Ask for the genus, then stock the 50-gallon minimum as a floor and raise it when the adult size is toward 12 inches or the fish is a known jumper. A lid belongs on the tank either way. Many wrasses leave through small gaps when the lights go out.

Carnivore feeding means mysis, brine shrimp, and marine pellets, not algae sheets and not the hope that the fish will live on pests alone. A wrasse that refuses prepared food in the shop will not invent an appetite in a new tank. Watch it eat before you buy it, especially if the species has a reputation for being picky. Intermediate care is stable saltwater in the ranges above plus the judgment to match the species to the livestock. A fish that eats ornamental shrimp cannot be added to a reef that is built around those shrimp, even if another wrasse would have been fine.

Temperament varies, so do not stock from the family name. A peaceful fairy wrasse and a territorial six-line are both wrasses and they do not behave alike. One wrasse per tank is a safer default until you know the species is gregarious. Sand-diving species need an open bed of fine sand, not a bare-bottom tank or a crushed-coral carpet that they cannot enter. Without that bed they ricochet off the glass at night and damage the mouth.

The 5-to-10-year lifespan assumes the adult size was housed from the start. A 4-inch juvenile of a species that reaches 12 inches is an upcoming tank upgrade, not a permanent nano fish. Keep nitrate under 10 ppm, salinity steady between 1.020 and 1.025, and the temperature inside 72 to 82°F. If the seller cannot name the wrasse, spend the money on one that is labeled and eating. The generic card can only give you the shared saltwater numbers and the warning that 3 inches and 12 inches are not the same commitment.

Cost of Ownership

Budget a covered display of at least 50 gallons for an open-swimming wrasse. A small six-line is the one wrasse that can live in a careful 30-gallon reef. Add a quarantine tank, frozen mysis, and a sand bed for sleepers. A wrasse that jumps, or a cleaner wrasse that refuses food, is a second purchase. The first fish's price is not the project. A six-line can be the fish that fits a covered 30-gallon reef. A bird wrasse or a large Coris does not.
