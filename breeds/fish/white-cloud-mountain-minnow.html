Overview

The White Cloud Mountain Minnow is one of the hardiest and most beginner-friendly fish in the aquarium hobby. Discovered in China in 1932, these small, peaceful fish are perfect for unheated aquariums and outdoor ponds in temperate climates. They were once called the "poor man's neon tetra" but have earned respect in their own right.

Their tolerance for cooler temperatures, ease of care, and beautiful coloration make them ideal for beginners, children's tanks, and experienced aquarists alike. They display attractive iridescent lateral lines and colorful fins.

Tanichthys albonubes comes from the White Cloud Mountain area of Guangdong in China, and the fish on this card is that species by name. Adults stay about 1.5 inches, or roughly 4 centimeters, and live 5 to 7 years. The tank is 10 gallons or more. Care is easy. The fish are peaceful, they school, and they eat an ordinary omnivore diet. The temperature line is more specific than a simple tropical range: 60 to 72°F, which is 15 to 22°C, with a note that they can tolerate a wider swing from about 40 to 80°F. Tolerance is not a target. The place they do well for years is the 60 to 72°F band, not a thermostat set near 80°F.

A long stay near 80°F, and certainly a community tank held at a tropical 82°F, sits outside both the preferred band and the stated tolerance. Do not park them in a discus or ram tank and point at the word tolerate. pH is 6.0 to 8.0 and hardness is 5 to 19 dGH, a wide mineral window that lets them live in ordinary tap water that would bother a soft-water tetra. Nitrate on this card should stay under 40 ppm. That ceiling is looser than some other small-fish pages. It is still a ceiling. Ammonia and nitrite do not get a pass because the fish is hardy. Both stay at 0 ppm.

They school. A 10-gallon tank with a planted length and a group large enough to occupy it is the easy setup. One or two fish skulk in the moss and never show the white-and-red fin flash that makes the species worth keeping. They spawn in cool water among fine leaves or a yarn mop, and a separate rearing tank is how you see the fry. The adults are not parental. Easy care includes a lid, because a 4-centimeter fish can still jump, and a filter gentle enough that the school is not pinned to one end of a small tank.

Natural Habitat

White Clouds originate from mountain streams in China.

  • Location: White Cloud Mountain (Baiyun Shan), Guangdong Province, China
  • Environment: Cool, clear mountain streams with moderate flow
  • Status: Extinct in the wild (or nearly so) due to habitat loss
  • Conservation: Thriving in aquarium populations worldwide

Varieties

Several varieties have been developed.

  • Standard: Original wild-type coloration
  • Golden: Gold/yellow body with original markings
  • Long-fin: Extended finnage; various colors
  • Meteor Minnow: Long-finned golden variety

Tank Requirements

White Clouds are adaptable but appreciate.

  • Tank Size: Minimum 10 gallons for a school of 6-8
  • Temperature: No heater needed in most homes; prefer cooler water
  • Plants: Appreciate planted tanks with some open swimming space
  • Filtration: Gentle to moderate; enjoy some current
  • Substrate: Any; natural gravel or sand
  • Outdoor: Excellent for outdoor tubs/ponds in summer or year-round in mild climates

Temperature Note

White Cloud Minnows are coldwater fish and actually do poorly in warm tropical tanks. They thrive at room temperature (65-72°F) and can tolerate temperatures down to 40°F briefly. Avoid temperatures above 80°F.

  • Frozen Foods: Bloodworms, brine shrimp

  • Variety: Appreciate varied diet

Behavior & Social Structure

White Clouds display pleasant social behaviors.

  • Schooling: Must be kept in groups of 6+; more is better
  • Peaceful: Non-aggressive with all tank mates
  • Active: Constantly swimming and exploring
  • Males Display: Males display to each other and females
  • Mid-Level Swimmers: Occupy middle to upper water column

The diet is unfussy omnivore food in small pieces. Flake, micro pellets, and frozen cyclops or baby brine all work. They feed in the open once the group feels safe. Compatibility

White Clouds work well with other peaceful coolwater fish.

Compatible Tank Mates

  • Paradise fish (juvenile or peaceful specimens)
  • Rosy barbs
  • Weather loaches
  • Corydoras (some species tolerate cool water)
  • Hillstream loaches
  • Other White Clouds
  • Goldfish (in larger setups; may be outcompeted for food)

Incompatible Species

  • Tropical fish requiring warm water (above 78°F)
  • Large or aggressive fish
  • Fish that will eat them
  • Fin nippers

Breeding

  • Ease: Very easy; often spawn spontaneously
  • Method: Egg scatterers; spawn in fine-leaved plants or spawning mops
  • Parents: May eat eggs; remove adults or provide heavy plant cover
  • Eggs: Hatch in 36-48 hours at 72°F
  • Fry Care: Feed infusoria, then baby brine shrimp
  • Temperature: Cooler temperatures slow development but often yield healthier fry
