Overview

Blennies are beloved reef fish known for their big personalities, expressive faces, and entertaining behaviors. These bottom-dwelling fish have elongated bodies, often with distinctive "eyebrow" cirri (fleshy appendages) that give them a comical appearance. They spend their time perching on rocks and darting between hiding spots.

Many blennies are excellent algae grazers, making them valuable additions to reef tanks struggling with nuisance algae. Their small size, hardiness, and peaceful nature make them perfect for nano reefs and community marine tanks.

Blenny is a family, Blenniidae, not a single marine fish with one diet. The animals sold under the name run about 2 to 6 inches depending on species and live 4 to 6 years. Tank size on the card is 20 to 55 gallons or more, again by species. They come from tropical and temperate seas, and in a home reef they need 72 to 82°F, pH 8.1 to 8.4, and a specific gravity of 1.020 to 1.025. A reef with readable ammonia, or nitrate past 20 ppm, loses blennies while the corals still look acceptable.

The diet line says herbivore to omnivore because the family really does split. A lawnmower blenny, Salarias fasciatus, grazes film algae and starves in a new tank that was scraped clean and then stocked with a hungry blenny on day one. A midas blenny will take prepared foods. A fang blenny bites fins and mucus and is not a cleanup animal at all. Read the species on the tag before you trust the word blenny. The care level easy applies to the common algae grazers in a mature tank, not to every fish in the family.

Some blennies are peaceful grazers and some are semi-aggressive. Most of the algae-grazing species want a hole they can back into, and two of them will fight over one favorite burrow. A lawnmower that has eaten the visible algae may start picking at coral mucus or at clam mantles. That is hunger, not a sudden change of personality. Feed nori and algae-based foods once the rocks are clean, or the fish declines while the rest of the reef still looks fed.

Natural Habitat

Blennies inhabit various marine environments.

  • Range: Tropical and temperate seas worldwide
  • Environment: Rocky reefs, coral rubble, tide pools
  • Behavior: Bottom-dwelling; hide in holes and crevices
  • Territory: Defend small territories around their holes

Popular Species

Numerous blenny species are kept in aquariums.

  • Lawnmower Blenny (Salarias fasciatus): Excellent algae eater; 5 inches
  • Bicolor Blenny (Ecsenius bicolor): Purple/orange bicolor; 4 inches; popular
  • Midas Blenny (Ecsenius midas): Golden yellow; 5 inches; can swim mid-water
  • Tailspot Blenny (Ecsenius stigmatura): Small at 2.5 inches; orange tail spot
  • Starry Blenny (Salarias ramosus): Dark with white spots; 5.5 inches; algae eater
  • Canary Blenny (Meiacanthus oualanensis): Yellow; 5 inches; venomous but not dangerous

Tank Requirements

Blennies need appropriate hiding spots: The closer your routine tracks the Blenny's specific traits, the easier everything downstream becomes.

Diet & Feeding

Diet varies by species: People who actually study the Blenny's natural tendencies usually build deeper trust with the animal too.

Herbivorous Blennies (Lawnmower, Starry)

  • Primary diet of algae (film algae, hair algae)
  • Supplement with nori/dried seaweed sheets
  • May accept algae-based pellets
  • Need algae to thrive; may starve in "clean" tanks

Omnivorous Blennies (Bicolor, Midas, Tailspot)

  • Varied diet including algae and meaty foods
  • Marine flakes, pellets, frozen mysis shrimp
  • More adaptable to aquarium foods

Lawnmower Blenny Diet Warning

Lawnmower Blennies are obligate herbivores that may starve in tanks without sufficient algae growth. Ensure adequate algae or supplement heavily with nori sheets. They often refuse other foods.

Feed the species printed on the bag, not the family name. A lawnmower blenny, Salarias fasciatus, scrapes film algae and starves in a bare 20-gallon reef that was wiped clean the day it arrived. A midas blenny will take prepared foods in the water column. Hold marine blennies at 72 to 82°F, pH 8.1 to 8.4, and specific gravity 1.020 to 1.025, in a tank sized inside the 20 to 55 gallon span for the adult you bought. Offer nori or an algae-based gel once the rock film thins, and keep portions small enough that leftovers do not push nitrate above 20 ppm. An omnivore-to-herbivore label is a warning that one menu does not fit every blenny.

Swap foods as the rock changes, because a blenny's grazing shifts with what is growing. Early on, a lawnmower may ignore a pellet and live on the film. Later, the same fish picks at coral mucus if you never replace that film with nori, spirulina, or an herbivore preparation. A bicolor blenny that suddenly refuses the clip of seaweed it used to attack is an early health signal, not a mood. Check salinity, temperature, and whether a tankmate has taken the perch in front of its hole. Four to 6 years of scraping rock only happens when the menu follows the species from herbivore toward omnivore, not the other way around by accident.

Behavior & Personality

Blennies have distinctive personalities.

  • Bottom Dwellers: Spend most time on rocks and substrate
  • Perching: Rest on rocks with pectoral fins as "arms"
  • Expressive: Large eyes and cirri give them character
  • Territorial: May chase similar-looking fish or other blennies
  • Curious: Often peek out from holes; interactive with owners
  • Jumping: Can jump; secure lids recommended

Blennies are not gobies. They lack the fused pelvic sucker gobies use to perch, and many species wear fleshy cirri over the eyes that look like eyebrows. They hop and perch on rock rather than hovering in open water. A lid still matters. Startled blennies leave the tank through surprisingly small gaps. A 20-gallon minimum fits a small species. A fish headed for 6 inches belongs in the upper end of the 20 to 55 gallon range, with more than one crevice.

Buy a blenny with a full belly and algae already growing in the tank it is going into. A thin lawnmower in a bare quarantine tank needs algae sheets or it wastes. Four to 6 years is a fair life for a fish that spends its day scraping rock, and it only happens when the diet matches the species. An omnivore label on a family card is not permission to feed flake to a grazer and call it done.

Blennies feed from a burrow they defend. Watch the hole, not the open water, at feeding time. A lawnmower darts, rasps, and backs in. A midas may swim up for a pellet, which is normal for that species and a poor clue if you apply it to a tailspot blenny that should stay on the rock. Two algae grazers in one 20-gallon tank will fight over a single favorite crevice and the loser stops eating. Feed so both holes get a share, and do not rely on one clip of nori placed on the winner's porch. Appetite at the burrow tells you more about this fish than color does under a blue reef light.

Compatibility

Blennies are generally peaceful community fish.

Compatible Tank Mates

  • Most reef fish (clownfish, tangs, chromis)
  • Gobies (different enough to coexist)
  • Wrasses
  • Corals and invertebrates
  • Peaceful community fish

Incompatible Species

  • Other blennies (usually territorial toward each other)
  • Fish with similar body shape (may be seen as rivals)
  • Large aggressive fish that may bully them

Breeding

Blenny breeding is possible but challenging.

  • Sexing: Males often more colorful; develop enlarged cirri
  • Spawning: Males guard eggs in holes or crevices
  • Eggs: Adhesive; attached to substrate
  • Larvae: Small and challenging to raise
  • Commercial Breeding: Some species are captive-bred

Common Health Issues

Blennies are hardy but can experience: Your aquatic veterinarian and experienced Blenny owners can offer perspective tailored to your situation.

  • Marine Ich: Common parasite; quarantine prevents introduction
  • Starvation: Herbivorous species in algae-free tanks
  • Stress: From lack of hiding spots or aggression
  • Jumping: Ensure tank has secure lid
  • Territorial Injuries: From fights with similar fish

For Blenny, prevention is always better than treatment.

A blenny that stays in the hole through feeding, breathes fast, or shows white specks against the rock is not having a shy day. Marine ich and marine velvet both move quickly in a reef at 72 to 82°F, and a fish already weakened by an empty stomach has less margin. Learn the normal rhythm of your species: lawnmowers graze all day, while some Ecsenius perch and watch. A change in that rhythm, plus frayed fins or a dusty coat, is the moment to move the fish if you can. Do not dose copper into a reef. Copper harms invertebrates, and this page has no dose to offer. A fish-only hospital tank and a veterinarian are the honest next step.

Quarantine a new blenny in marine water that already matches the display: 72 to 82°F, specific gravity 1.020 to 1.025, and a hole to sit in. A bare cup stresses a fish that lives in a crevice, so give it rock or PVC and a bit of nori if it is an algae grazer. Several weeks of watching it eat will show ich or velvet before that fish meets corals and shrimp you cannot medicate. Never run copper in the display. If you and a veterinarian use copper at all, it belongs in a fish-only hospital tank, not on the reef. A hungry lawnmower also needs film or seaweed during the wait, or quarantine itself becomes the thing that starves it.

Is This Fish Right for You?

Follow-up reading for Blenny households — the pages below answer the questions most owners hit within the first year.

Blennies Are Great For:

  • Reef aquarium enthusiasts
  • Those wanting algae control (herbivorous species)
  • Aquarists who appreciate personality
  • Nano reef setups (smaller species)

Blennies May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Tanks without algae (for herbivorous species)
  • Those wanting multiple blennies in small tanks
  • Very small tanks (under 20 gallons)
  • Open-top aquariums (jumping risk)

Aquarium keeping is a hobby of specifics, and Blenny is no exception.

A blenny that gets a mature reef, a real burrow, and the right end of the 20 to 55 gallon range becomes a fish you notice every time you walk past. Lawnmowers work the rock. Midas blennies flash yellow in the open. That engagement depends on salinity staying at 1.020 to 1.025 and on somebody feeding nori after the natural film is gone. Easy care on this card describes common grazers in a tank that already has food on the rocks, not a fang blenny and not a new box of dry live rock. Commit to the species you named, and the perch at the hole stays occupied for the 4 to 6 year lifespan.

Cost of Ownership

Planning ahead financially is one of the most practical things you can do before getting a Blenny. Blenny care costs are real and ongoing.

Marine costs follow the species you picked inside this family. A small blenny in 20 gallons still needs salt mix, a skimmer or other reef filtration, tests for salinity and nitrate, and algae-based food once the rocks are bare. A fish headed toward 6 inches belongs nearer 55 gallons, and the electricity and salt scale with that box. People who budget only the livestock later skip water changes, and nitrate above 20 ppm shows up as a blenny that hides and a reef that slimes. Plan replacement parts for the heater and return pump before a long weekend, because a reef at 72 to 82°F does not pause while a part is on order.

A heater that sticks on in a 20-gallon reef can shove a blenny out of the 72 to 82°F band in a single afternoon, while the same failure in a 55-gallon tank takes longer and is easier to shrug off. Either way the fish loses its burrow routine, stops grazing, and becomes an easy target for marine ich. A failed return pump is the other half of the problem: oxygen and flow drop, and a bottom dweller in a stale pocket declines while fish at the surface still look fine. Read the thermometer daily. Keep a spare heater sized to the tank you actually run, and a way to move water if the pump dies. Top-off with fresh water only, or a hot tank plus rising salinity hits the blenny from two sides.

Spend money on the heater controller, the lid, and the pump that keeps a blenny's hole oxygenated, not on a brighter light the fish never asked for. Most blennies are reef-safe grazers, and the livestock around them, corals and shrimp included, is what a cheap failed heater destroys. A fish living 4 to 6 years will see at least one pump wear out. Parts on the shelf cost less than a reef crash at a specific gravity you can no longer hold because the return line dumped water on the floor. Match the gear to the tank size inside the 20 to 55 gallon range, and replace it when it sounds wrong rather than when it has already stopped.
