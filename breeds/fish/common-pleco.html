Common Pleco Overview

The Common Pleco is a very large (15-24 in) freshwater species that is an excellent choice for both beginners and experienced aquarists.

Common Plecos thrive in water temperatures of 72-86°F with a pH range of 6.5-7.5.

The common pleco in the hobby is the large suckermouth catfish sold under Hypostomus and Pterygoplichthys, not the bristlenose and not the clown pleco. This page lists adult size at 15 to 24 inches and lifespan at 10 to 15 years. The tank minimum is 125 gallons. Temperature runs a wide 72 to 86°F, pH 6.5 to 7.5. Temperament is peaceful. Diet is omnivore. Care level is printed as beginner. That last line is about a juvenile that survives neglect for a month. It is not about a two-foot fish.

They are not an algae solution for a 20-gallon tank. A baby common pleco will eat some algae while it is small. Then it outgrows the tank, produces a heroic amount of waste, and needs food you actually provide: sinking wafers, vegetables, and a meaty offering, because the diet is omnivore rather than a pure algae clip. By the time the glass is too big for the fish to clean, the fish is too big for the stand. People rehome them into lakes, which is how Pterygoplichthys became a problem animal in warm waterways. Do not release one. Find a tank that fits or do not buy the fish.

A 125-gallon tank is the floor on this card, and a 20-inch fish needs the length, not a tall column of water with no floor space. Driftwood and a cave it can actually enter as an adult belong in the setup from the start. Building a cave for a 4-inch juvenile and never enlarging it is how plecos get stuck. They are nocturnal. A fish you do not see until the lights go out is normal. A fish gasping at the surface is a water problem. Ammonia and nitrite at 0 ppm and nitrate under 20 ppm take a real filter once the fish is eating like a 15-inch omnivore.

Common Pleco Tank Essentials

Peaceful means they do not hunt other fish. It does not mean a small tankmate is safe from being sucked onto and rasped during the night. Slow, flat fish and fancy goldfish can lose slime coat to a large pleco that has decided to taste them. Fast midwater fish ignore them. Keep the pleco with fish that can stay off the glass at night, in a tank that was sized for the pleco rather than for the tetras.

Behavior & Temperament

Ten to 15 years is the commitment hiding in a two-dollar juvenile. The beginner label falls apart at the first tank upgrade, which is not optional. If the shop cannot tell a common pleco from a bristlenose, look at the advice you were given about algae control in a small tank. That advice is the tell. A fish that stays 4 to 6 inches and wears bristles is the other animal. The one on this page is the fish that makes a 125-gallon tank the starting point.

Cost of Ownership

A common pleco grows past a foot and a half and produces a remarkable amount of waste. It is not an algae solution for a ten-gallon tank. Adults need a large cycled aquarium, driftwood to rasp, and a diet that includes vegetables and algae wafers, not fish waste. They live for years. The usual story is a baby bought for algae control that outgrows the tank and pollutes it.

Dial those in first.

The common pleco, Hypostomus, is the fish sold as a tiny algae eater that grows past 18 inches and often past two feet. It needs a tank of 75 gallons or more as an adult, driftwood, vegetables, and wafers. Algae on the glass will not feed it once the body is the size of a forearm.
