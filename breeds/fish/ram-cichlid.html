The ram on this page is Mikrogeophagus ramirezi unless a label says otherwise. German blue rams are a color line of the same species, not a harder fish. Bolivian rams are Mikrogeophagus altispinosus, a different animal with different needs, and they are not what this card is describing. Adult rams are small, 2 to 3 inches, and they live 3 to 4 years. A pair of rams needs 20 gallons of stable, aged water. Care is intermediate, the temperament is peaceful, and the diet is omnivore.

Ram Cichlid Overview

The Ram Cichlid is a small (2-3 in) freshwater species that is well-suited for hobbyists with some experience. With a lifespan of 3-4 years, this peaceful species requires a minimum tank size of 20 gal.

Ram Cichlids thrive in water temperatures of 78-85°F with a pH range of 5.0-7.0. As an omnivore, they accept a wide variety of foods.

Ram Cichlid the long-term baseline comes from maintenance cadence and stocking judgement calibrated to this species specifically rather than copied from general fish templates.

The water is the part that kills them. Temperature is 78 to 85°F, warm on purpose, and pH is 5.0 to 7.0. They belong in soft, acidic, stable water, not in a freshly filled tank with a swinging pH and a dusting of ammonia. Rams die in new tanks. Age the aquarium, plant it, and confirm ammonia at 0 ppm and nitrite at 0 ppm for weeks before they arrive. Hold nitrate under 20 ppm or the short lifespan gets shorter. A water change that drops the temperature out of the 78 to 85°F band is as stressful as a missed change.

Tank Requirements & Setup

This is a low-profile piece of Ram Cichlid ownership that quietly shapes year-over-year outcomes more than headline topics do.

Tank Size

  • Minimum: 20 gal
  • Recommended: Larger is always better for stability and swimming room.
  • Type: Freshwater planted or community tank

Equipment

  • Filtration: Quality canister or HOB filter rated for your tank size.
  • Heating: Reliable heater to maintain 78-85°F.
  • Lighting: Standard aquarium lighting; planted tanks benefit from full-spectrum.

Water Quality Warning

Always cycle your tank fully before adding fish. Use a quality test kit to monitor ammonia, nitrite, and nitrate levels. Never add Ram Cichlids to an uncycled or newly set up tank.

Diet & Feeding

  • Primary Diet: Omnivore.
  • Foods: High-quality flakes/pellets, frozen brine shrimp, mysis shrimp, and blanched vegetables.
  • Variety: Rotate different food types for balanced nutrition.

Behavior & Temperament

Ram Cichlid are characterized by a peaceful disposition that influences their care requirements and compatibility.

  • General Disposition: Peaceful nature that defines daily interactions
  • Social Behavior: Specific social needs that owners should understand and accommodate
  • Activity Patterns: Natural activity cycles that influence care scheduling

Wild-caught rams and badly inbred domestic lines both arrive thin. Buy fish that are eating, with intact fins and a rounded belly, and keep them in their own 20-gallon tank or with calm tankmates that can handle the heat. A group of rams in a small tank becomes a series of territories once they pair. A pair in 20 gallons with cover is a better reading of the peaceful label than six adults on top of each other. They will guard a flat stone or a leaf when they spawn, and they are not peaceful toward other fish in that moment.

Feed small omnivore foods: crushed pellets, frozen cyclops, baby brine shrimp, and a little vegetable matter. They are slow, deliberate eaters and lose food to faster tankmates. If you keep them with danios or barbs, the rams will starve in plain sight. Intermediate care is mostly this stability plus the heat. A heater that can hold 82°F in a cool room, a lid to keep that heat in, and a test kit you actually use are the equipment that matters.

Do not treat a dying ram with a shelf of medications as the first response. Check the age of the tank, the temperature, and the nitrate. A ram in a two-week-old aquarium at 74°F is outside every important line on this card. The 3-to-4-year lifespan is short even when things go well. It gets much shorter in unstable, cool, or brand-new water. If you wanted the harder Bolivian species, this is not that page and these are not those numbers.

It takes several consistent weeks to establish the rhythm, and then it becomes household background.
