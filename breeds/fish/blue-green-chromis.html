Overview

The Blue Green Chromis (also known as the Green Chromis) is one of the most popular and beginner-friendly saltwater fish in the hobby. Their peaceful nature, hardiness, stunning iridescent blue-green coloration, and active schooling behavior make them perfect for both reef aquariums and fish-only setups.

These damselfish are an exception to the typically aggressive damselfish family, remaining peaceful throughout their lives. They're often recommended as "starter" fish for cycling new marine aquariums due to their hardiness, though fishless cycling is preferred.

Natural Habitat

Blue Green Chromis are found throughout tropical waters:

  • Range: Indo-Pacific region, Red Sea, and East Africa to French Polynesia
  • Environment: Coral reefs, typically in large schools above branching corals
  • Depth: Usually 1-12 meters (3-40 feet)
  • Behavior: Form large aggregations above coral heads for protection

Tank Requirements

Blue Green Chromis are relatively easy to house:

  • Tank Size: Minimum 30 gallons for a small school (3-5 fish)
  • Reef Safe: 100% reef safe; won't harm corals or invertebrates
  • Rockwork: Provide coral branches or rock formations for shelter
  • Swimming Space: Need open water column for schooling
  • Filtration: Standard reef/marine filtration; protein skimmer recommended
  • Lighting: Any; adapt to various lighting conditions

Diet & Feeding

Blue Green Chromis are easy to feed:

  • Staple Food: High-quality marine flakes or pellets
  • Frozen Foods: Mysis shrimp, brine shrimp, chopped seafood
  • Live Foods: Copepods, brine shrimp (occasional treat)
  • Variety: Varied diet maintains health and coloration
  • Feeding Style: Mid-water feeders; catch food as it floats

Feeding Schedule: Feed 2-3 times daily; small amounts they consume in 2 minutes.

Behavior & Social Structure

Blue Green Chromis are active schooling fish:

  • Schooling: Best kept in groups of 5+ for natural behavior
  • Peaceful: Non-aggressive toward other species
  • Active: Constantly swimming in open water
  • Hierarchy: Establish pecking order within the school
  • Attrition: Numbers may dwindle over time due to aggression within the group

School Dynamics

Blue Green Chromis can be aggressive toward each other, and weaker individuals may be bullied to death over time. Start with larger groups (6+) and provide ample space and hiding places. This is normal damselfish behavior despite their generally peaceful reputation.

Compatibility

Blue Green Chromis are excellent community fish:

Compatible Tank Mates

  • Clownfish
  • Tangs (in larger tanks)
  • Wrasses
  • Gobies and blennies
  • Angelfish (dwarf and larger)
  • Most reef-safe fish
  • All corals and invertebrates

Incompatible Species

  • Highly aggressive fish (aggressive damsels, triggers)
  • Large predatory fish that may eat them
  • Fish that compete aggressively for the same space

Breeding

Breeding Blue Green Chromis is possible but challenging:

  • Spawning: Males prepare nest sites on rock or rubble
  • Courtship: Males display and attract females to nest
  • Eggs: Female deposits eggs; male guards and fans them
  • Hatching: Eggs hatch in 2-3 days
  • Fry Challenge: Larvae are tiny and difficult to raise; require rotifers

Common Health Issues

Blue Green Chromis are hardy but can experience:

  • Marine Ich: Common parasite; quarantine new fish
  • Velvet: Another parasite; appears as gold dust
  • Aggression Stress: From bullying within the school
  • Nutritional Issues: Fading color from poor diet
  • Bacterial Infections: Usually secondary to stress or injury

Is This Fish Right for You?

Blue Green Chromis Are Great For:

  • Beginner marine aquarists
  • Reef aquarium setups
  • Those wanting active schooling fish
  • Peaceful community marine tanks

Blue Green Chromis May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Very small tanks (under 30 gallons)
  • Those wanting to keep just 1-2 fish
  • Tanks with aggressive species
  • Those unprepared for some attrition in the school
