Zebra Danio Overview

The Zebra Danio is a small (2 in) freshwater species that is an excellent choice for both beginners and experienced aquarists. With a lifespan of 3-5 years, this peaceful species requires a minimum tank size of 10 gal.

Zebra Danios thrive in water temperatures of 64-77°F with a pH range of 6.0-8.0. As an omnivore, they accept a wide variety of foods.

The danio on this page is the zebra danio, Danio rerio, a 2-inch cyprinid from South Asia that lives 3 to 5 years. It is not a celestial pearl danio. A school fits in 10 gallons and not much else. Temperature is a cool 64 to 77°F, pH 6.0 to 8.0. They are peaceful omnivores on the card, and they are active enough that a small group in a short tank becomes nippy out of boredom. Beginner is a fair care level if you give them length, a school, and a lid.

They are built for moving water and cooler tanks. A heater set for a betta at 80°F is above this card. They are one of the few common tropicals that stay active in an unheated room that sits in the high 60s. That does not make them a goldfish companion. Goldfish grow into a different bioload and a different social world. It does make them a bad roommate for a fish that needs the mid-80s. Do not put them in a discus tank and call it a dither.

Tank Size

  • Minimum: 10 gal
  • Recommended: Larger is always better for stability and swimming room.
  • Type: Freshwater planted or community tank

Equipment

  • Filtration: Quality canister or HOB filter rated for your tank size.
  • Heating: Usually not needed; this is a coolwater fish that does best at 64-77°F, which most homes provide. Use a thermometer and add a heater only if the room runs colder.
  • Lighting: Standard aquarium lighting; planted tanks benefit from full-spectrum.
  • Air Pump: Optional but beneficial for oxygenation.

Water Quality Warning

Always cycle your tank fully before adding fish. Use a quality test kit to monitor ammonia, nitrite, and nitrate levels. Never add Zebra Danios to an uncycled or newly set up tank.

Diet & Feeding

  • Primary Diet: Omnivore.
  • Foods: High-quality flakes/pellets, frozen brine shrimp, mysis shrimp, and blanched vegetables.
  • Variety: Rotate different food types for balanced nutrition.

Common Health Issues

  • Ich (White Spot Disease): White spots on body and fins. Raise temperature gradually and use ich medication.
  • Parasites: Internal and external parasites; quarantine new additions.

The foundation of Danio health management is water quality — full stop. Regular testing and diligent maintenance are your best tools.

Diet and Nutrition Tips

Use vetted best practices as the starting default and iterate when your observations push you to:

  • Primary diet: Feed a high-quality staple food formulated for your Zebra Danio's dietary type (herbivore, omnivore, or carnivore). Look for foods with whole ingredients and minimal fillers.
  • Variety ensures complete nutrition and encourages natural foraging.
  • Feeding frequency: Feed small amounts 1-2 times daily.
  • Avoid overfeeding: Overfeeding is the most common nutritional mistake in fishkeeping. It leads to obesity, water pollution, and secondary health problems.

Tank Environment and Stimulation

  • Tank size: Always choose a tank that meets or exceeds the minimum recommended size for Zebra Danio. Larger tanks are more stable and forgiving of water quality fluctuations.
  • Water parameters: Maintain consistent temperature, pH, and hardness levels appropriate for Zebra Danio. Test water weekly and perform regular partial water changes.
  • Filtration: Use a quality filter rated for your tank size.

Tank Maintenance Essentials

  • Water changes: Perform 20-30% partial water changes weekly.
  • Filter maintenance: Rinse filter media in old tank water monthly.
  • Algae control: Some algae is normal, but excessive growth indicates nutrient imbalance. Adjust lighting duration and consider adding algae-eating species.
  • Water testing: Test ammonia, nitrite, nitrate, and pH at least weekly. Keep a log to track trends and catch problems early.

Living Environment

  • Compatible tankmates: Research compatibility before adding new species. Aggression, different water parameter requirements, and size mismatches are common problems.
  • Substrate choice: Select substrate appropriate for your Zebra Danio's natural behavior, whether that is sand for digging species or planted substrate for aquascaping.

Behavior & Temperament

Zebra Danio are characterized by a peaceful disposition that influences their care requirements and compatibility.

  • General Disposition: Peaceful nature that defines daily interactions
  • Social Behavior: Specific social needs that owners should understand and accommodate
  • Activity Patterns: Natural activity cycles that influence care scheduling

A school of six or more, in a tank longer than it is tall, is when zebras swim the length of the glass instead of orbiting one corner. The 10-gallon minimum holds that school and little else. They jump. Cover the tank, including the hole around the filter. Feed a flake or small pellet, plus frozen brine shrimp or daphnia. They eat at the surface and in midwater and they eat fast. Slower fish in the same tank need a second feeding lower down or the danios take it all.

Peaceful has a footnote. A zebra danio with too few companions will nip fins, especially long fins on a betta, a fancy guppy, or an angelfish. The fix is the school and the space, not a lecture. They are also poor company for any fish small enough to be bullied off the food, including chili rasboras and celestial pearl danios. Same family, wrong roommate. A 2-inch zebra is a sprinter. A 1-inch galaxy danio is not.

Three to 5 years is the life, and they often reach it because they tolerate a wide pH and a cool room. The failures are a bare bowl, a missing lid, and a pair kept alone until they shred each other's fins. Stripes run the length of the body. Long-fin and albino forms are the same species and a little less tough, because the fins are an easy target and the albino eyes like less glare. Wild-type short fins in a planted 10-gallon tank with a school are the beginner fish the card is describing.

Plan on a few weeks of intentional practice to set the habits here; the durability of the outcome is worth the upfront investment.
