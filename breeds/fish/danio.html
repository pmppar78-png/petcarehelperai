Zebra Danio Overview

Zebra Danios thrive in water temperatures of 64-77°F with a pH range of 6.0-8.0.

The danio on this page is the zebra danio, Danio rerio, a 2-inch cyprinid from South Asia that lives 3 to 5 years. It is not a celestial pearl danio. A school fits in 10 gallons and not much else. Temperature is a cool 64 to 77°F, pH 6.0 to 8.0. They are peaceful omnivores on the card, and they are active enough that a small group in a short tank becomes nippy out of boredom. Beginner is a fair care level if you give them length, a school, and a lid.

They are built for moving water and cooler tanks. A heater set for a betta at 80°F is above this card. They are one of the few common tropicals that stay active in an unheated room that sits in the high 60s. That does not make them a goldfish companion. Goldfish grow into a different bioload and a different social world. It does make them a bad roommate for a fish that needs the mid-80s. Do not put them in a discus tank and call it a dither.

Tank Size

  • Minimum: 10 gal
  • Type: Freshwater community tank. Zebra danios tolerate an unheated room in the upper 60s to mid 70s; a heater is for a room that drops colder.

Equipment

  • Heating: Usually not needed; this is a coolwater fish that does best at 64-77°F, which most homes provide. Use a thermometer and add a heater only if the room runs colder.

Behavior & Temperament

A school of six or more, in a tank longer than it is tall, is when zebras swim the length of the glass instead of orbiting one corner. The 10-gallon minimum holds that school and little else. They jump. Cover the tank, including the hole around the filter. Feed a flake or small pellet, plus frozen brine shrimp or daphnia. They eat at the surface and in midwater and they eat fast. Slower fish in the same tank need a second feeding lower down or the danios take it all.

Peaceful has a footnote. A zebra danio with too few companions will nip fins, especially long fins on a betta, a fancy guppy, or an angelfish. The fix is the school and the space, not a lecture. They are also poor company for any fish small enough to be bullied off the food, including chili rasboras and celestial pearl danios. Same family, wrong roommate. A 2-inch zebra is a sprinter. A 1-inch galaxy danio is not.

Three to 5 years is the life, and they often reach it because they tolerate a wide pH and a cool room. The failures are a bare bowl, a missing lid, and a pair kept alone until they shred each other's fins. Stripes run the length of the body. Long-fin and albino forms are the same species and a little less tough, because the fins are an easy target and the albino eyes like less glare. Wild-type short fins in a planted 10-gallon tank with a school are the beginner fish the card is describing.
