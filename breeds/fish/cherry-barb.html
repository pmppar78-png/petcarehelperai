Cherry Barb Overview

Cherry Barbs are one of the most peaceful and beginner-friendly barb species, known for the stunning cherry-red coloration of males during breeding condition. Unlike their more boisterous relatives like tiger barbs, cherry barbs are calm community fish that won't harass tank mates.

These beautiful fish are unfortunately listed as vulnerable in their native Sri Lanka due to habitat loss, making captive-bred specimens the ethical choice. Their peaceful nature, beautiful coloration, and ease of care make them excellent choices for planted community aquariums.

Cherry barbs are Puntius titteya, a small barb from the shaded streams of Sri Lanka. Plan on 20 gallons before you buy a school. Temperament is peaceful, which already sets them apart from tiger barbs. They are omnivores and easy on the care card if the tank is planted and the group is large enough for the males to color up without bullying one fish.

Male color is a husbandry result, not a strain you can buy and forget. A male kept over dark substrate, with plants and with females in the group, turns a deep red. The same male in a bare, brightly lit tank with three other males stays pale and picks fights. Females are tan with a dark lateral stripe and a rounder belly. Buy both. A tank of only males is why this peaceful barb has a reputation for nipping that the species does not earn in a mixed group.

Natural Habitat & Origin

Cherry barbs are endemic to Sri Lanka: Your aquatic veterinarian and experienced Cherry Barb owners can offer perspective tailored to your situation.

  • Native Range: Southwestern Sri Lanka only
  • Habitat: Shaded streams with slow-moving water
  • Environment: Heavily vegetated areas with leaf litter
  • Conservation: Vulnerable in the wild - buy captive-bred

Tank Requirements & Setup

Cherry barbs thrive in planted aquariums.

Tank Size

  • Minimum: 20 gallons for a school
  • School Size: Keep 6+ (ideally 8-10)
  • Ratio: 1 male to 2 females reduces aggression

Essential Equipment

  • Filter: Gentle to moderate flow
  • Lighting: Moderate, with shaded areas

Decoration & Plants

  • Live Plants: Dense planting recommended (java fern, crypts, anubias)
  • Floating Plants: Provide shade they appreciate
  • Substrate: Dark substrate enhances coloration
  • Hiding Spots: Driftwood, rocks, plant cover

Diet & Feeding

Cherry barbs are easy to feed and accept most foods.

Primary Foods

  • Micro Pellets: Sinking or floating
  • Live Foods: Enhance color and condition
  • Vegetables: Blanched vegetables occasionally

Feeding Guidelines

  • Varied diet enhances coloration
  • Color-enhancing foods help males display

They live in the middle of the tank and use plants as breaks, not as a place to hide all day. A 20-gallon tank with a school of six to eight, a soft substrate, and some cover matches the way they behave. They are a poor choice for a tank of long-finned bettas or fancy guppies only if you keep too few males and nothing else for them to do. In a real school they mostly display at each other. Feed a small pellet, flake, and frozen daphnia or brine shrimp. They are unfussy omnivores and will also pick at soft algae.

Behavior & Temperament

  • Peaceful: One of the calmest barb species
  • Schooling: Keep in groups of 6+
  • Shy: More timid than other barbs
  • Male Display: Males color up and display for females
  • Mid-Level Swimmers: Occupy middle tank areas

Sri Lankan wild stocks have been under pressure, and the fish in shops are typically captive-bred. That does not make a pale, clamped school a bargain. A cherry barb that arrives with a sunken belly in a wholesale tank of mixed barbs often carries the wear of that tank. Quarantine them where you can see them eat before they join a planted display. Hold nitrate under 20 ppm and do not introduce them into a tank that still shows ammonia or nitrite. They are hardy once settled. They are not immune to a new tank.

Four to 6 years is realistic in a planted 20-gallon tank that stays in the middle of their temperature band. They do not need the heat of a ram or the alkaline water of a molly. A heater set to 82°F and a handful of crushed coral for livebearers is a different aquarium. Keep the cherry barbs with other peaceful fish that accept pH 6.0 to 7.5: small rasboras, peaceful tetras, and corydoras. They are the barb you can keep when you have already ruled the tiger barb out.

Compatibility with Other

Compatible Tank Mates

  • Tetras (neon, cardinal, ember)
  • Rasboras
  • Corydoras catfish
  • Small peaceful gouramis
  • Other peaceful barbs
  • Shrimp and snails
  • Bettas (in larger tanks with caution)

Incompatible Species

  • Large aggressive fish
  • Aggressive barbs (tiger barbs)
  • Very large fish

Breeding Information

Cherry barbs breed readily in home aquariums: Your aquatic veterinarian and experienced Cherry Barb owners can offer perspective tailored to your situation.

  • Egg Scatterers: Scatter eggs among plants
  • Spawning: Males display intensely to females
  • Eggs: 200-300 eggs scattered in vegetation
  • Hatching: 1-2 days
  • Fry Care: Remove adults or provide dense plant cover; feed infusoria, then baby brine shrimp
