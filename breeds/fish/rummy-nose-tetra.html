Rummy-Nose Tetra Overview

Rummy-nose tetras are small schooling characins, about 2 inches as adults, with a lifespan of 5 to 8 years. The tank minimum is 20 gallons. They are peaceful and the difficulty is intermediate. The common names and the science have shifted: fish sold as true rummy-nose are tied to Hemigrammus bleheri and to Petitella, and the red on the head is only worth buying if you keep a school. A pair in a 10-gallon tank never looks like the fish on the bag.

The nose is a water-quality gauge. When conditions slip, the red recedes and the head turns pale long before the fish is belly-up. That is more useful than a vague promise that they are sensitive. Keep ammonia and the rest of the nitrogen cycle in a place where the red stays intact, and do not wait for a test kit if an entire school washes out at once. Nitrate creep, a dirty filter, and a neglected water change show up on the face. The card rates them intermediate because that signal is real, not because they are aggressive.

Stock a proper school in the 20 gallons or more. Ten fish in a planted tank with open swimming room and a dark substrate show the red head and the black-and-white tail band. Three fish in a bright, empty tank go pale and hide. They are midwater swimmers. Leave the bottom to other animals and do not add them as dither fish for a cichlid that will eventually eat a 2-inch tetra. Peaceful neighbors of a similar size are the match.

The purchase range on the card, about 5 to 100 dollars or more per fish, is wide because a true bleheri-type fish does not cost the same as a small false rummy-nose or a faded farm fish. Look at the red continuing toward the tail markings and at the body size. Setup costs on the same card run about 100 to 500 dollars or more, annual maintenance about 200 to 500 dollars, and food about 50 to 150 dollars a year. None of that spends well on a school that is too small to display or on a tank that lets the noses fade.

Temperament & Behavior

They eat fine omnivore foods: small flakes, micro pellets, and frozen cyclops or baby brine. Feed the school where it swims so every fish gets a share. A 20-gallon tank is the floor for a group of 2-inch fish, not a suggestion to start in something smaller and upgrade if they live. Intermediate care is a cycled, planted tank, a real school, and the habit of reading the color of the head as part of the daily look. A pale nose in clean-looking water is a prompt to test, not a new strain.

False rummy-nose fish and other red-headed tetras are sold beside them. They can be good fish and they are not this one. If the shop cannot tell bleheri or Petitella from the smaller lookalikes, buy on the pattern you can see and still give the school 20 gallons and clean water. The 5-to-8-year lifespan is available to a group that stays red because the water stays fit, not to a handful of pale fish in a crowded community that only gets a water change when someone remembers.
