Neon Tetra Overview

The Neon Tetra is one of the most popular freshwater aquarium fish in the hobby, beloved for its stunning iridescent blue and red coloration. These tiny gems create a breathtaking display when kept in schools, their neon stripes shimmering as they swim in unison through planted tanks.

First discovered in the Amazon basin in 1934, neon tetras quickly became aquarium staples due to their peaceful nature, hardy constitution, and affordable price. They remain one of the best choices for community tanks and are often recommended for beginners looking to add color and activity to their aquariums.

Natural Habitat & Origin

Neon tetras originate from the blackwater streams and tributaries of the Amazon basin:

  • Blackwater Streams: Tannin-stained waters with low mineral content
  • Slow-Moving Rivers: Shaded areas under dense rainforest canopy
  • Flooded Forests: Seasonal habitats with abundant hiding spots
  • Countries: Peru, Colombia, and Brazil

These waters are typically soft, acidic, and dimly lit, which explains why neon tetras often display their best colors in similar aquarium conditions.

Tank Requirements & Setup

Creating an ideal environment for neon tetras enhances their health and coloration:

Tank Size

  • Minimum: 10 gallons (38 liters) for a school of 6
  • Recommended: 20+ gallons for larger schools
  • School Size: Keep at least 6, preferably 10-12 or more

Essential Equipment

  • Heater: Adjustable heater for stable temperatures
  • Filter: Gentle filtration with moderate flow
  • Lighting: Subdued lighting enhances colors
  • Thermometer: Digital for accurate readings

Decoration & Plants

  • Live Plants: Java fern, cryptocoryne, Amazon sword
  • Driftwood: Releases tannins that mimic natural habitat
  • Dark Substrate: Makes colors pop
  • Open Swimming Space: Balance plants with swimming room
  • Floating Plants: Diffuse lighting naturally

Acclimate Carefully

Neon tetras are sensitive to sudden water parameter changes. Use drip acclimation when introducing new fish, and ensure your tank is fully cycled before adding them.

Diet & Feeding

Neon tetras are omnivores with small mouths requiring appropriately sized foods:

Primary Foods

  • Micro Pellets: High-quality small pellets
  • Flake Food: Crush to appropriate size
  • Frozen Foods: Baby brine shrimp, daphnia, cyclops
  • Freeze-Dried: Bloodworms (crushed), tubifex
  • Live Foods: Baby brine shrimp, micro worms

Feeding Guidelines

  • Feed 2-3 times daily in small amounts
  • Only what they can consume in 2-3 minutes
  • Vary diet for optimal nutrition
  • Ensure food is small enough for tiny mouths

Behavior & Temperament

Neon tetras are peaceful schooling fish with fascinating group dynamics:

  • Schooling: Feel secure and display natural behavior in groups of 6+
  • Peaceful: Non-aggressive toward other fish
  • Active: Swim throughout the middle water column
  • Timid: May hide when kept in small numbers
  • Color Change: Colors may fade at night or when stressed

Compatibility with Other Fish

Neon tetras are excellent community fish that pair well with many species:

Compatible Tank Mates

  • Other small tetras (cardinal, ember, rummy-nose)
  • Rasboras (harlequin, chili)
  • Corydoras catfish
  • Small peaceful gouramis
  • Otocinclus
  • Shrimp (cherry, amano)
  • Small peaceful livebearers

Incompatible Species

  • Large or aggressive cichlids
  • Large fish that may eat them
  • Aggressive barbs
  • Bettas (may work but risky)
  • Predatory fish

Breeding Information

Breeding neon tetras is challenging but rewarding:

  • Breeding Tank: Separate 10-gallon tank with very soft, acidic water
  • Conditions: pH 5.0-6.0, temperature 75°F, very dim lighting
  • Spawning Medium: Java moss or spawning mop
  • Spawning: Usually occurs in early morning
  • Eggs: Remove parents immediately (they eat eggs)
  • Fry Care: Eggs hatch in 24 hours; feed infusoria, then baby brine shrimp
  • Light Sensitivity: Eggs and fry are extremely light-sensitive

Common Health Issues

Neon tetras can be susceptible to several conditions:

Neon Tetra Disease (NTD)

  • Symptoms: Fading colors, curved spine, erratic swimming, white patches
  • Causes: Microsporidian parasite Pleistophora hyphessobryconis
  • Treatment: No cure; remove affected fish immediately to prevent spread

Ich (White Spot Disease)

  • Symptoms: White spots on body and fins
  • Treatment: Raise temperature gradually, use ich medication

Fin Rot

  • Symptoms: Ragged, deteriorating fins
  • Treatment: Improve water quality, antibacterial treatment

Is This Fish Right for You?

Neon Tetras Are Great For:

  • Community tanks with peaceful fish
  • Planted aquariums where they shine
  • Beginners wanting colorful, active fish
  • Aquarists who appreciate schooling behavior
  • Those with properly cycled, established tanks

Neon Tetras May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Uncycled or new tanks
  • Tanks with large predatory fish
  • Keepers wanting minimal schools (fewer than 6)
  • Tanks with fluctuating water parameters
