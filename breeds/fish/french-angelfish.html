Recommended for French Angelfishs

French angelfish are Pomacanthus paru, a marine angelfish, not the freshwater Pterophyllum that shares the word angel. Adults reach about 15 inches and live 10 to 15 years. The tank minimum is 250 gallons. Temperature is 72 to 82°F, pH 8.1 to 8.4, specific gravity 1.020 to 1.025, nitrate under 10 ppm. Care is expert. Temperament is semi-aggressive. Diet is omnivore. A juvenile in a 50-gallon reef is a temporary photograph, not a plan that satisfies this card.

French Angelfish Overview

The French Angelfish is a large (15 in) saltwater species that requires advanced knowledge and a well-established aquarium. With a lifespan of 10-15 years, this semi-aggressive species requires a minimum tank size of 250 gal.

French Angelfishs thrive in water temperatures of 72-82°F with a pH range of 8.1-8.4. As an omnivore, they accept a wide variety of foods.

Juveniles are black with curved yellow bands. Adults darken to charcoal with gold-edged scales and a yellow face. The change takes years, and the fish is eating and growing the whole time. People buy the banded juvenile because it looks like a miniature of a poster and then discover the adult needs 250 gallons and will sample corals, sponges, and ornamental invertebrates. Reef-safe as a juvenile is not a promise about the adult. Plan on a fish-only tank with solid rock, or a reef you are willing to see nibbled.

The omnivore diet in a tank means sponge-based angel foods, frozen mysis, and algae or nori, offered often. A French angel that eats only flake will be thin and will look harder at the corals and the clam mantles. Feed the angel specifically. In a mixed reef the wrasses and tangs get to the food first. Ten to 15 years is the life if the diet stays varied and the tank is actually large enough for a 15-inch fish to turn around. Expert care is the size, the diet, and the aggression toward other angels.

Match water changes, stocking, and feeding for a French Angelfish to this species. Use the schedule on this page, and change the amount when appetite or the water test shifts.

Water Quality Warning

Saltwater species are sensitive to parameter fluctuations. Use an RO/DI unit for water changes and test regularly. Never add French Angelfishs to an uncycled or newly set up tank.

Diet & Feeding

  • Primary Diet: Omnivore.
  • Foods: High-quality flakes/pellets, frozen brine shrimp, mysis shrimp, and blanched vegetables.
  • Variety: Rotate different food types for balanced nutrition.

Common Health Issues

  • Marine Ich (Cryptocaryon): White spots on body and fins. Treat with copper-based medication in quarantine tank.
  • Marine Velvet: Dusty gold appearance; highly contagious and often fatal if untreated.
  • Parasites: Internal and external parasites; quarantine new additions.

Tank Maintenance Essentials

  • Water changes: Perform 20-30% partial water changes weekly.

  • Algae control: Some algae is normal, but excessive growth indicates nutrient imbalance. Adjust lighting duration and consider adding algae-eating species.

Behavior & Temperament

French Angelfish are characterized by a semi-aggressive disposition that influences their care requirements and compatibility.

  • General Disposition: Semi-Aggressive nature that defines daily interactions
  • Social Behavior: Specific social needs that owners should understand and accommodate
  • Activity Patterns: Natural activity cycles that influence care scheduling

One French angelfish is the usual stocking. Two adults fight unless they paired as juveniles and grew up together, and even then a 250-gallon tank is the starting size rather than a guarantee. They will harass other large angels, and a smaller angel added later is a target. Semi-aggressive on the card is mild language for a fish that owns a reef stack. Give it structure and open swimming room in front of the rock. A tall tank with no length makes a 15-inch angel pace the glass.

Do not buy a juvenile you cannot house as an adult. Rehoming a 12-inch marine angel is a hard search, and the fish does not pause its growth while you shop for a 250-gallon system. The salinity and the pH are standard reef numbers. The expert label is not about mixing salt. It is about keeping a large omnivore that picks at invertebrates, lives for more than a decade, and needs a tank most people do not own when they see the yellow bands in a shop.

The high-leverage inputs are food quality, exercise, preventive veterinary care, and handling. Dial those in first.
