Overview

Red Claw Crabs (Perisesarma bidens) are striking semi-terrestrial crabs named for their vibrant red claws that contrast beautifully with their dark bodies. Native to mangrove swamps of the Indo-Pacific, these feisty crabs have become popular in the pet trade.

Despite being commonly sold as freshwater aquarium inhabitants, Red Claw Crabs actually need brackish water and access to land to thrive. They're active, personable crabs but can be aggressive, requiring careful consideration of tankmates and setup.

Perisesarma bidens is a small coastal crab, not a fully aquatic fish-tank ornament. The carapace reaches about 2 to 2.5 inches, and the lifespan is only 2 to 2.5 years. Moderate is the rating because the animal is a climber that wants land and salt. The tank on the card is 10 gallons or more set up as a paludarium, with water that is strongly preferred brackish at a specific gravity of 1.005 to 1.015. A freshwater community tank with a deep water column and no land is the wrong build, even if a crab survives there for a few weeks.

They need to leave the water. Provide rocks, wood, or a sloped bank that stays above the line, and keep the air humid. The card asks for humidity of 75 percent or higher above the water. A screen lid that dumps all the moisture into the room, with no cover over part of the land, dries the gill chamber. A tight lid matters for the opposite reason too: these crabs climb plumbing and will leave the tank. Temperature of the water is 72 to 82°F. pH is 7.5 to 8.5, general hardness 8 to 20 dGH, and carbonate hardness 6 to 16 dKH.

Ammonia and nitrite at 0 ppm are as important in a small brackish pool as in a reef, because the water volume in a paludarium is often much less than the tank's total gallons. Do not read 10 gallons of tank as 10 gallons of water if half the box is land. Size the filter, or the water-change habit, to the pool you actually poured. Breeding is listed as very difficult in captivity. Do not buy a pair expecting a colony. Males and females fight, and two males in a small paludarium will damage each other, especially around a molt when the shell is soft.

Natural Habitat

Red Claw Crabs inhabit coastal mangrove environments.

  • Range: Indo-Pacific: Taiwan, Japan, Indonesia, Southeast Asia
  • Environment: Mangrove forests, brackish estuaries
  • Behavior: Climb mangrove roots; spend much time above water
  • Water: Brackish - mixture of fresh and salt water

Tank Requirements

Red Claw Crabs need specialized paludarium setups.

  • Tank Size: 10+ gallons; more for groups
  • Land Area: Minimum 50% of tank should be dry land
  • Water Depth: Shallow areas crabs can easily exit
  • Substrate: Sand for burrowing; rocks/driftwood for climbing
  • Plants: Hardy plants like Anubias; mangrove roots ideal
  • Hiding Spots: Multiple caves, crevices, and shelters
  • Lid: ESSENTIAL - extremely skilled escape artists

Critical: Land Access Required

Red Claw Crabs are NOT fully aquatic. They MUST have dry land to climb out and rest. Crabs kept without land access will drown from exhaustion. Ensure easy ramps between water and land areas.

Diet & Feeding

Red Claw Crabs are opportunistic omnivores.

  • Protein: Bloodworms, brine shrimp, small pieces of fish/shrimp
  • Commercial Foods: Crab pellets, sinking fish food, algae wafers
  • Plant Matter: Blanched vegetables, dried seaweed
  • Calcium: Critical for molting - cuttlebone, calcium blocks
  • Fruits: Occasional pieces of fruit as treats
  • Variety: Varied diet prevents nutritional deficiencies

Feeding Tip: Red Claw Crabs may eat on land or in water. Offer food in both areas.

Proper nutrition for Red Claw Crab requires understanding not just what to feed, but how feeding strategies affect health, behavior, and water quality. A disciplined approach to feeding, where your Red Claw Crab receives the right amount of appropriate food at consistent intervals, is one of the most impactful things you can do for both the fish and the overall aquarium ecosystem.

Diet variety is essential for optimal Red Claw Crab health because no single commercial food provides the complete nutritional profile these fish require. Observing your Red Claw Crab's response to different food types also provides useful health information, as reduced interest in normally preferred foods is often an early indicator of illness.

Behavior & Temperament

Red Claw Crabs have bold, sometimes aggressive personalities: Generic guidance is a floor; it is the Red Claw Crab-specific nuance that raises the ceiling on outcomes.

  • Territorial: Especially males; will fight for space
  • Aggressive: Will attack fish, shrimp, and other invertebrates
  • Climbers: Excellent climbers; scale everything
  • Escape Artists: Will find any gap in tank lid
  • Nocturnal: More active at night
  • Molting: Vulnerable during molting; need hiding spots

They are opportunistic eaters. Pellets, blanched greens, and bits of frozen fish food are all taken, and so are slow fish and small tankmates. This is not a crab to drop into a tetra tank as a scavenger. Keep it with the understanding that anything it can catch is a possible meal, or keep it in a species setup. Molting crabs hide. An uneaten meal rotting in a 5-gallon pool of brackish water will spike ammonia past 0 ppm before you notice the crab has been under the wood for two days.

Specific gravity should stay inside 1.005 to 1.015. Pure freshwater on one end, and full marine salt at reef strength on the other, are both outside that preference. Use a refractometer, not a guess from a handful of salt. Copper medications are lethal. The short 2-to-2.5-year lifespan means a crab that arrives as an adult may not have long, so the paludarium should be ready before the animal comes home. Land, humid air, brackish water, and a lid are the moderate part. The crab itself is hardy only inside that layout.

Feeding behavior in Red Claw Crab reveals much about their ecological role and can be leveraged by keepers to promote natural behaviors and reduce aggression. Observing when, where, and how your Red Claw Crab feeds provides diagnostic information about their comfort level, health status, and social standing. Many successful Red Claw Crab keepers use varied feeding strategies including target feeding, scatter feeding, and enrichment feeders to promote natural foraging behaviors and reduce competition-related stress.

Compatibility

Red Claw Crabs have limited compatibility due to aggression: Your aquatic veterinarian and experienced Red Claw Crab owners can offer perspective tailored to your situation.

Possible Tankmates (With Caution)

  • Other Red Claw Crabs (with adequate space, one male per tank)
  • Fast brackish fish that stay in upper water column
  • Mollies (some success reported, but at risk)

Avoid

  • Small or slow fish (will be caught and eaten)
  • Bottom-dwelling fish (in crab's territory)
  • Shrimp (prey)
  • Snails (will be eaten)
  • Other crab species
  • Frogs and salamanders

Aggression Warning

Red Claw Crabs are opportunistic predators. Any fish that can be caught WILL be eaten eventually. Even "compatible" tankmates are at risk. Species-only tanks are safest for long-term success.

Breeding

  • Sexing: Males have larger, more colorful claws; females have broader abdomens
  • Mating: Occurs after female molts
  • Eggs: Female carries eggs under abdomen
  • Larvae: Require marine water conditions
  • Development: Multiple planktonic larval stages over weeks
  • Success Rate: Nearly zero in home aquaria

Health Issues

  • Failed Molts: From improper water conditions or calcium deficiency
  • Shell Rot: Bacterial infection; black spots on shell
  • Lethargy: Often indicates poor water quality or stress
  • Lost Limbs: From fights; regenerate through molting
  • Drowning: From no land access or exhaustion
  • Escape Deaths: From drying out after escaping tank

Keeping a Red Claw Crab healthy means prioritizing water quality above everything else.

Recognizing early signs of disease in Red Claw Crab requires a trained eye and consistent observation. Establishing a daily observation routine—even just a few minutes of focused attention during feeding—helps you develop a baseline understanding of what normal behavior looks like for your specific Red Claw Crab, making deviations immediately apparent.

Quarantine protocols represent one of the most impactful health management practices available to Red Claw Crab keepers. This single practice prevents the majority of disease outbreaks in established aquariums and is considered essential by virtually all experienced Red Claw Crab keepers.
