Overview

Dottybacks are small, brilliantly colored reef fish known for their bold personalities and vibrant colors. Despite their small size, they pack big attitudes and are often described as having "small dog syndrome." Their stunning colors - ranging from purple and magenta to yellow and orange - make them highly sought after for reef aquariums.

Many dottyback species are now captive-bred, making them hardier and more sustainable choices. Captive-bred specimens also tend to be less aggressive than wild-caught individuals, though they still retain their territorial nature.

Natural Habitat

Dottybacks inhabit reef environments:

  • Range: Indo-Pacific region, Red Sea, Western Pacific
  • Environment: Coral reefs, rocky areas with caves and crevices
  • Behavior: Secretive; dart between hiding spots
  • Territory: Defend small territories aggressively

Popular Species

Several dottyback species are commonly kept:

  • Orchid Dottyback (P. fridmani): Purple; one of the more peaceful species
  • Royal Dottyback (P. paccagnellae): Purple and yellow bicolor; more aggressive
  • Neon Dottyback (P. aldabraensis): Orange with blue stripe; moderately aggressive
  • Strawberry Dottyback (P. porphyreus): Magenta pink; variable aggression
  • Splendid Dottyback (Manonichthys splendens): Yellow and purple; less aggressive

Tank Requirements

Dottybacks need appropriate territory:

  • Tank Size: Minimum 30 gallons; larger for multiple fish or tankmates
  • Reef Safe: Yes; won't harm corals
  • Rockwork: Essential; provide caves and hiding spots
  • Territory: Each dottyback needs its own cave/territory
  • Filtration: Standard reef filtration
  • Lighting: Any; often hide in shadows

Diet & Feeding

Dottybacks are carnivorous with hearty appetites:

  • Staple Food: High-quality marine pellets, frozen mysis shrimp
  • Frozen Foods: Mysis shrimp, brine shrimp, chopped seafood
  • Live Foods: Readily accept live foods
  • Pest Control: Will eat bristle worms and small pests
  • Warning: May eat ornamental shrimp and small fish

Feeding Schedule: Feed 2 times daily; small amounts.

Shrimp Warning

Dottybacks may eat ornamental shrimp (especially smaller species) and very small fish. They're not safe with tiny gobies, small blennies, or decorative shrimp. Consider this before adding to your reef.

Behavior & Aggression

Understanding dottyback behavior is crucial:

  • Territorial: Highly territorial; defend their caves aggressively
  • Bold: Will challenge fish much larger than themselves
  • Secretive: Hide frequently but become bolder over time
  • Pest Controllers: Hunt bristle worms and small invertebrates
  • Species Variation: Aggression varies significantly by species

Compatibility

Tank mate selection requires careful consideration:

Potentially Compatible

  • Larger, assertive fish (tangs, larger angels)
  • Fish that occupy different tank zones
  • Clownfish (in larger tanks with separate territories)
  • Larger wrasses
  • Most corals and hardy invertebrates

Incompatible

  • Other dottybacks (unless very large tank)
  • Small gobies and blennies
  • Ornamental shrimp (species dependent)
  • Timid or passive fish
  • Fish of similar shape/color that may be seen as rivals

Breeding

Dottybacks can be bred in captivity:

  • Pairing: May form pairs; females often change sex to male
  • Spawning: Males guard eggs in caves
  • Eggs: Hatch in about 3-4 days
  • Fry: Challenging to raise; need rotifers and small foods
  • Commercial: Many species are commercially captive-bred

Common Health Issues

Dottybacks are hardy but can experience:

  • Marine Ich: Common parasite; quarantine prevents introduction
  • Velvet: Another marine parasite
  • Aggression Injuries: From fights with tank mates
  • Hiding/Not Eating: Usually stress from aggression
  • Bacterial Infections: Secondary to injury or stress

Is This Fish Right for You?

Dottybacks Are Great For:

  • Aquarists wanting bold, colorful fish
  • Reef tanks needing bristle worm control
  • Those who appreciate feisty personalities
  • Experienced marine hobbyists

Dottybacks May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Beginners to marine aquariums
  • Tanks with small, peaceful fish
  • Those with ornamental shrimp
  • Community tanks with timid species

Related Species

  • Hawkfish - Similar bold personality
  • Goby - Peaceful bottom dwellers
