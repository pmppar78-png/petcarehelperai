Honey Gourami Overview

Honey Gouramis thrive in water temperatures of 72-82°F with a pH range of 6.0-7.5.

Honey gouramis are Trichogaster chuna, a small labyrinth fish that stays about 2 inches and lives 4 to 8 years. House them from 10 gallons up. They want 72 to 82°F and pH 6.0 to 7.5. Diet is omnivore. Temperament is peaceful. Care is listed as beginner, and for once the label is closer to fair, with one condition: they are not dwarf gouramis. Dwarf gouramis commonly carry an iridovirus with no treatment. Honeys are a different species and a hardier one. Buying a honey because the dwarfs in the shop look sick is a reasonable decision.

Males in breeding color are amber-gold with a dark throat and a dark belly stripe. Females are plainer and a little larger. The sunset and red morphs are color forms of the same fish. Wild-type honeys are duller and calmer in a planted tank. Two males in a 10-gallon tank will bicker. One male and one female, or a single male with peaceful roommates, fits the minimum. They are shy. A bare tank with fast danios makes a honey gourami hide under the filter and slowly thin out. Plants, a dimmer corner, and tankmates that do not win every race to the food are the setup.

Tank Size

  • Minimum: 10 gal
  • Type: A warm, planted freshwater community. Honey gouramis do best with a gentle filter and a lid, around 10 gallons for a pair.

Behavior & Temperament

They breathe at the surface. Leave an opening in any floating cover and keep a lid on the tank. They are poor jumpers compared with a hatchetfish and they still find a gap. Feed a small pellet, flake, and frozen daphnia. They pick food out of the plants and from the surface. They lose to barbs and to zebra danios. A 10-gallon tank of honey gouramis and chili rasboras or ember tetras is a better match than a honey in a barb school. The peaceful label assumes the neighbors are peaceful too.

Bubble nests happen. A male under a leaf, spitting bubbles, is ready to spawn and will chase the female for a while. In a planted 10-gallon tank she can get away. In an empty tank she cannot. Fry are tiny and most community tanks eat them. Four to 8 years is a long life for a 2-inch fish and it is realistic when the water stays in range and nobody is nipping the feelers. Those pelvic filaments are easy to damage. A honey with shortened, white-tipped feelers is being chewed on, even if you have not seen it happen.

Beginner care here means a planted 10-gallon tank, a filter with a gentle outflow, and no medication habits borrowed from a fish tank that uses copper or a harsh dye every time a fin looks ragged. They are sensitive to dirty water and to sudden cold more than they are sensitive to a pH that sits calmly at 7. Keep ammonia and nitrite at zero and do not park the heater at the top of the range for no reason. A honey gourami is the small peaceful gourami to choose when a dwarf gourami is the fish the shop is pushing and the shop cannot explain the virus.

The strongest outcomes come from combining reliable guidelines with responsive, attentive care.
