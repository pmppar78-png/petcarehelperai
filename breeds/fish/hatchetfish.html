Overview

Hatchetfish are fascinating freshwater fish named for their distinctive hatchet-shaped bodies with deep, keel-like bellies. These unique surface dwellers are the only freshwater fish capable of true powered flight - they can literally fly above the water surface using their large pectoral muscles to escape predators.

Their unusual appearance and interesting behavior make them popular among experienced aquarists, though their specialized care requirements mean they're not ideal for beginners. They spend virtually all their time at the water's surface, making them perfect for filling the top level of a community tank.

Natural Habitat

Hatchetfish originate from South American waters:

  • Range: Amazon and Orinoco river basins
  • Environment: Slow-moving streams, flooded forests, and blackwater habitats
  • Water: Soft, acidic, tannin-stained water
  • Behavior: Patrol surface feeding on fallen insects

Species Varieties

Several Hatchetfish species are available:

  • Common Hatchetfish (Gasteropelecus sternicla): 2.5 inches; silver with black lateral line
  • Silver Hatchetfish (Gasteropelecus levis): Smaller; plain silver coloration
  • Marbled Hatchetfish (Carnegiella strigata): 1.5 inches; beautiful marbled pattern
  • Black-winged Hatchetfish (Carnegiella marthae): Smaller; dark pectoral fins
  • Giant Hatchetfish (Thoracocharax securis): Up to 3 inches; largest species

Tank Requirements

Hatchetfish have specific tank needs:

  • Tank Size: Minimum 20 gallons for a school of 6
  • Tank Shape: Long tanks preferred; surface area more important than depth
  • Lid: ESSENTIAL - tight-fitting lid with no gaps; notorious jumpers
  • Water Level: Leave some space between water and lid
  • Filtration: Gentle filtration; avoid strong surface agitation
  • Plants: Floating plants provide security and reduce jumping
  • Lighting: Subdued; use floating plants to diffuse light

Jumping Warning

Hatchetfish are extreme jumpers and will find any gap in aquarium lids. Cover all openings, including around filter intakes and heater cords. Many hatchetfish deaths are from jumping out of tanks.

Diet & Feeding

Hatchetfish are surface-feeding insectivores:

  • Natural Diet: Insects that fall on the water surface
  • Floating Foods: Floating flakes or pellets (may need to train them)
  • Live Foods: Fruit flies, small crickets, mosquito larvae
  • Frozen Foods: Bloodworms, daphnia, brine shrimp (floating)
  • Freeze-dried: Bloodworms, tubifex (floating)

Feeding Schedule: Feed 2-3 times daily in small amounts. Food must float; they rarely eat from mid-water or bottom.

Behavior & Social Structure

Hatchetfish display distinctive behaviors:

  • Surface Dwellers: Spend virtually all time at water surface
  • Schooling: Must be kept in groups of 6+; reduces stress and jumping
  • Peaceful: Non-aggressive toward all tank mates
  • Skittish: Easily startled; may jump when frightened
  • Flying: Can leap and glide above water surface

Compatibility

Hatchetfish work well in community setups:

Compatible Tank Mates

  • Small tetras (Cardinal, Neon, Ember)
  • Corydoras catfish (bottom dwellers)
  • Small peaceful rasboras
  • Pencilfish
  • Dwarf cichlids (Apistogramma, Rams)
  • Small plecos and Otocinclus

Incompatible Species

  • Large or aggressive fish
  • Fin-nippers (Tiger Barbs, Serpae Tetras)
  • Fish that compete for surface food
  • Boisterous tank mates that cause stress

Breeding

Breeding Hatchetfish is challenging:

  • Difficulty: Rarely bred in home aquariums
  • Conditions: Very soft, acidic water; dim lighting
  • Spawning: Scatter eggs among floating plants
  • Fry: Extremely small; require infusoria as first food
  • Success Rate: Low; most specimens are wild-caught

Common Health Issues

Hatchetfish can be sensitive to water conditions:

  • Ich: Common parasite; treat carefully as they're sensitive to medications
  • Stress: Often from poor water quality or insufficient school size
  • Velvet: Another parasite; appears as gold dust on body
  • Internal Parasites: Common in wild-caught specimens; treat prophylactically
  • Jump Injuries: Often fatal; prevention is key

Is This Fish Right for You?

Hatchetfish Are Great For:

  • Experienced aquarists wanting unique fish
  • Blackwater or biotope aquarium setups
  • Filling the top level of community tanks
  • Those who appreciate unusual fish behavior

Hatchetfish May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Beginners to fishkeeping
  • Tanks with open-top setups
  • Hard, alkaline water conditions
  • Those unable to provide floating foods
