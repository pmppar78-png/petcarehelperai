White Cloud Mountain Minnow Overview

The White Cloud Mountain Minnow is a very small (1.5 in) freshwater species that is an excellent choice for both beginners and experienced aquarists.

White Cloud Mountain Minnows thrive in water temperatures of 60-72°F with a pH range of 6.0-8.0.

pH can sit anywhere from 6.0 to 8.0, so they are not a blackwater specialist. Cool water is not a license for nitrate at or above 20 ppm. Ammonia and nitrite at 0 ppm matter just as much in an unheated tank as in a warm one, and a cold tank does not mean a dirty tank is acceptable. Beginner care is a cycled 10 gallons, a school, and a room that actually stays inside 60 to 72°F. A tank in a sunny window that climbs into the high 70s every afternoon is a warm tank with a cool label.

They are active for their size and they belong in a group. A 10-gallon tank with a school of these 1.5-inch fish, fine plants, and an open lane is a complete setup. Adding them as a dither school to a warm angelfish tank fails the temperature line even if the temperament line says peaceful. Choose other cool-water neighbors if you want company, and check that those neighbors share 60 to 72°F. A single white cloud in a bowl is not the beginner project this card describes.

Feed small omnivore foods. Crushed flake, micro pellets, and the occasional frozen baby brine shrimp are enough. They feed in the upper and middle water and they are not shy once the school is large enough. Overfeeding a 10-gallon tank is the usual way to push nitrate over 20 ppm. Small meals they finish are a better match for a fish this size than a pinch aimed at a goldfish.

Tank Size

  • Type: Cool freshwater community tank. White clouds school in the upper 60s to low 70s and do not need a tropical heater in a normal room.

Behavior & Temperament

The 5-to-7-year lifespan shows up in cool, clean water and disappears in a heated community. If your other fish need the upper 70s, keep the white clouds in their own tank. An air-powered sponge filter is often enough at this bioload and does not add heat. Cap the tank. A 1.5-inch fish still fits through a cable notch. They are small, and a gap at the rim is enough for one to land on the stand. Beginner does not mean a fishbowl on a windowsill. It means an easy fish if you respect 60 to 72°F, 10 gallons, and a real school.

Color is stronger when the fish are cool, mature, and not stressed by a tiny group. Males show more red on the fins. You can breed them in a separate cool tank with fine plants or a mop if you want young, and the adults will eat eggs if they share the space. Breeding is optional. Temperature is not. A white cloud that lives for years in an unheated, stable 10-gallon tank is the ordinary success. A white cloud in a tropical 82°F display is a fish being kept above this card.
