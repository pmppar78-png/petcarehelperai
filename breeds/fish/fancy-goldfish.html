Recommended for Fancy Goldfish

Fancy goldfish are the round-bodied, coldwater fish: oranda, ryukin, ranchu, and the other double-tail forms. They are not single-tail comets or commons. Adults reach about 6 to 8 inches and live 10 to 15 years. The tank minimum is 20 gallons per fish. Temperature sits at 65 to 75°F, pH 6.0 to 8.0. They are peaceful omnivores. The beginner label is only true if the tank is filtered, cool, and bigger than a bowl. An unfiltered bowl is not a home for this fish.

Fancy Goldfish Overview

Freshwater is the type on the Fancy Goldfish page, and 6 point 0-8 point 0 is the ph range, and 20 gal per fish is the min tank size, and Medium 6-8 in is the size.

The round body is the husbandry problem. The same stout shape that makes a ranchu or an oranda look the way it does crowds the swim bladder and the gut. They are prone to buoyancy problems. A fish floating sideways or stuck at the surface may be reacting to dry floating food gulped with air, to a temperature swing, or to a deformity that was bred in. Soak pellets before you feed them. Skip the cheap dry flake that they snap at the surface. Sinking foods, gel foods, and a blanched vegetable reduce the air they swallow. Buoyancy trouble that persists is a reason to talk to a vet who sees fish, not a reason to add a tonic.

They need more filtration than a single-tail comet of the same length. The body is compact, the waste is not, and a 20-gallon tank for one fish still needs a filter rated well beyond 20 gallons plus regular water changes. Ammonia and nitrite at 0 ppm and nitrate under 20 ppm are the limits, and a fancy goldfish can blow past them after a heavy feeding. Twenty gallons per fish is the floor. Two fancies in a 20-gallon tank are already over the card. A comet can live a different, faster life in a pond. A ryukin cannot keep up with it and should not be asked to::

Tank Size

  • Minimum: 20 gal per fish
  • Type: Cool freshwater tank with a strong filter. Fancy goldfish dig and waste heavily, so plants are optional and a heater is only for a cold room.

Equipment

  • Heating: Usually not needed; this is a coolwater fish that does best at 65-75°F, which most homes provide. Use a thermometer and add a heater only if the room runs colder.

Behavior & Temperament

Keep them cold. A tropical community at 78°F is above this card and shortens the life of a fish that can live 10 to 15 years in cool, clean water. Room temperature in a house that stays under 75°F often works without a heater. A heater set for a gourami does not. They are peaceful with each other and with other cool, slow fish of similar size. They will eat small tankmates and they will mouth plants. A planted aquascape and a fancy goldfish is a short story. Smooth ornaments and sand they cannot choke on are safer than sharp gravel.

The wen on an oranda grows over the head and can cover the eyes. A fish that startles when you approach from the side, or that misses food, may not be able to see. Keep the wen clean of debris and do not pick at it. Ranchu lack a dorsal fin and are even less athletic. Do not house the most extreme body shapes with faster fancies that take all the food. Ten to 15 years only happens in a filtered tank that stays cool and gives each fish its 20 gallons. The bowl kills them through ammonia and heat, not through a lack of affection. Beginner care starts with the filter.

Cost of Ownership

Fancy goldfish are round-bodied Carassius that need cool water, a large filter, and sinking food. A single fish belongs in about 20 to 30 gallons with more volume for each additional fish, and the temperature should stay below the low 70s Fahrenheit. Floating flake gulped at the surface contributes to buoyancy trouble.
