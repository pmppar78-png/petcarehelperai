Swordtail Overview

Swordtails are elegant livebearing fish named for the distinctive sword-like extension on the lower tail fin of males. Related to platies (and capable of interbreeding), swordtails are slightly larger and make impressive additions to community aquariums. They combine hardiness with striking appearance, available in numerous colors including red, orange, pineapple, black, and combinations.

Their active swimming behavior and interesting social dynamics make them engaging to watch. Swordtails are excellent for beginners while still being appreciated by experienced aquarists for their beauty and ease of breeding.

Natural Habitat & Origin

Swordtails originate from Central American waterways:

  • Native Range: Mexico, Guatemala, Honduras, Belize
  • Habitat: Streams, rivers, springs, and ponds
  • Environment: Vegetated areas with hard, alkaline water
  • Wild Coloration: Olive-green with red/yellow lateral stripe

Tank Requirements & Setup

Swordtails need adequate space for their active swimming:

Tank Size

  • Minimum: 20 gallons for a small group
  • Recommended: 30+ gallons for multiple males
  • Ratio: 1 male to 2-3 females minimum
  • Lid: Essential - swordtails are jumpers

Essential Equipment

  • Filter: Moderate flow filtration
  • Heater: Maintain stable temperatures
  • Lighting: Standard aquarium lighting
  • Secure Lid: Prevent jumping escapes

Decoration & Plants

  • Live Plants: Vallisneria, Amazon sword, hornwort
  • Open Swimming: Plenty of room to swim
  • Hiding Spots: For females and fry
  • Substrate: Gravel or sand

Diet & Feeding

Swordtails are omnivores with a preference for plant matter:

Primary Foods

  • Flake Food: High-quality tropical/omnivore flakes
  • Algae/Spirulina: Important vegetable component
  • Frozen Foods: Brine shrimp, bloodworms
  • Vegetables: Blanched spinach, zucchini, peas
  • Live Foods: Daphnia, brine shrimp (occasional)

Feeding Guidelines

  • Feed 2-3 times daily
  • Include vegetable matter regularly
  • Only what they consume in 2-3 minutes

Popular Swordtail Varieties

  • Red Swordtail: Classic solid red
  • Red Wag: Red body with black fins
  • Pineapple: Yellow-orange with red accents
  • Marigold: Golden-yellow coloration
  • Black: Solid black or near-black
  • Neon: Bright, iridescent colors
  • Tuxedo: Dark rear body half
  • Lyretail: Extended, lyre-shaped tail

Behavior & Temperament

  • Active Swimmers: Need open swimming space
  • Generally Peaceful: Good community fish
  • Male Competition: Males may spar for dominance
  • Jumpers: Require secure lids
  • Hierarchical: Establish pecking orders

Compatibility with Other Fish

Compatible Tank Mates

  • Other livebearers (platies, mollies, guppies)
  • Corydoras catfish
  • Larger tetras
  • Rainbowfish
  • Peaceful barbs
  • Gouramis

Incompatible Species

  • Aggressive cichlids
  • Fin nippers
  • Very small fish that may be bullied
  • Slow-moving long-finned fish

Breeding Information

Swordtails breed readily in home aquariums:

  • Livebearers: Give birth to live fry
  • Gestation: 28-40 days
  • Fry Per Batch: 20-100+ fry
  • Frequency: Every 4-6 weeks
  • Fry Care: Dense plants for hiding, or breeding box
  • Sex Change: Females can develop swords and become male-like

Common Health Issues

Ich

  • Symptoms: White spots on body and fins
  • Treatment: Raise temperature, ich medication

Fin Rot

  • Symptoms: Deteriorating sword and fins
  • Treatment: Clean water, antibacterial treatment

Swim Bladder Issues

  • Symptoms: Swimming difficulties
  • Treatment: Fast, feed peas, improve diet

Is This Fish Right for You?

Swordtails Are Great For:

  • Beginners wanting elegant fish
  • Community tank enthusiasts
  • Those interested in livebearer breeding
  • Aquarists with hard water

Swordtails May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Very small tanks (under 20 gallons)
  • Soft, acidic water setups
  • Tanks without secure lids
  • Those not wanting population growth

Related Species

  • Platy - Close relative, can interbreed
  • Molly - Larger livebearer
  • Guppy - Smaller, colorful livebearer
  • Rainbowfish - Similar size and activity level
