Swordtail Overview

Swordtails are elegant livebearing fish named for the distinctive sword-like extension on the lower tail fin of males. Related to platies (and capable of interbreeding), swordtails are slightly larger and make impressive additions to community aquariums. They combine hardiness with striking appearance, available in numerous colors including red, orange, pineapple, black, and combinations.

Their active swimming behavior and interesting social dynamics make them engaging to watch. Swordtails are excellent for beginners while still being appreciated by experienced aquarists for their beauty and ease of breeding.

Swordtails are Xiphophorus hellerii, livebearers from Mexico to Honduras. Adults are listed at 4 to 5 inches, and the male is the one that grows the sword, a lower tail extension that makes him look longer than a female of the same body. Adults typically live 3 to 5 years while producing brood after brood. The adults need 20 gallons before the fry are even counted. They are easy omnivores, peaceful overall, with the note that males can be competitive. That competition is real in a short tank with two mature males and no break in the line of sight.

Water is hard and alkaline: 72 to 79°F, pH 7.0 to 8.4, hardness 12 to 30 dGH. Nitrate is tolerated under 40 ppm on this card. The nitrogen cycle still has to show ammonia at 0 ppm and nitrite at 0 ppm. Soft rainwater tanks that suit wild tetras are a poor match. If your tap water is hard, you are already closer to this fish than to a blackwater species. If it is not, raise the mineral content and test it rather than pouring salt in at random. The swordtail's preference is hardness and a moderate temperature, not a brine tank.

Natural Habitat & Origin

Swordtails originate from Central American waterways.

  • Native Range: Mexico, Guatemala, Honduras, Belize
  • Habitat: Streams, rivers, springs, and ponds
  • Environment: Vegetated areas with hard, alkaline water
  • Wild Coloration: Olive-green with red/yellow lateral stripe

Tank Requirements & Setup

Swordtails need adequate space for their active swimming.

Tank Size

  • Minimum: 20 gallons for a small group
  • Recommended: 30+ gallons for multiple males
  • Ratio: 1 male to 2-3 females minimum
  • Lid: Essential - swordtails are jumpers

Essential Equipment

  • Filter: Moderate flow filtration
  • Heater: Maintain stable temperatures
  • Secure Lid: Prevent jumping escapes

Decoration & Plants

  • Live Plants: Vallisneria, Amazon sword, hornwort
  • Open Swimming: Plenty of room to swim
  • Hiding Spots: For females and fry
  • Substrate: Gravel or sand

Diet & Feeding

Swordtails are omnivores with a preference for plant matter: Health and behavior metrics for a Swordtail. Swordtails are omnivores with a preference for plant matter. They also need a lid, because they jump, and a tank long enough for a male to display the sword without being nipped to pieces. Livebearing means a female can drop fry you did not plan to raise. Keep males from harassing one female into exhaustion. A swordtail in a ten-gallon with three males is a fight, not a display.

Primary Foods

  • Flake Food: High-quality tropical/omnivore flakes
  • Algae/Spirulina: Important vegetable component
  • Frozen Foods: Brine shrimp, bloodworms
  • Vegetables: Blanched spinach, zucchini, peas
  • Live Foods: Daphnia, brine shrimp (occasional)

Feeding Guidelines

  • Feed 2-3 times daily
  • Include vegetable matter regularly
  • Only what they consume in 2-3 minutes

Behavior & Temperament

  • Active Swimmers: Need open swimming space
  • Generally Peaceful: Good community fish
  • Male Competition: Males may spar for dominance
  • Jumpers: Require secure lids
  • Hierarchical: Establish pecking orders

They are livebearers, and they produce more fry than a small tank can hold. A female drops large broods and can continue after she is separated from males. A 20-gallon aquarium with a pair will not stay a pair. Fry hide in plants for a while, then they grow, and the nitrate number climbs toward that 40 ppm ceiling. Harvest young to another tank if you want them, or stock fewer adults than the gallon rule of thumb suggests and let the plants and the adults account for some of the surplus.

Planning with these specific traits in focus produces a care programme calibrated to the animal in your home, not to a breed average that may not describe it well.

Compatibility with Other

Compatible Tank Mates

  • Other livebearers (platies, mollies, guppies)
  • Corydoras catfish
  • Larger tetras
  • Rainbowfish
  • Peaceful barbs
  • Gouramis

Incompatible Species

  • Aggressive cichlids
  • Fin nippers
  • Very small fish that may be bullied
  • Slow-moving long-finned fish

Breeding Information

Swordtails breed readily in home aquariums.

  • Livebearers: Give birth to live fry
  • Gestation: 28-40 days
  • Fry Per Batch: 20-100+ fry
  • Frequency: Every 4-6 weeks
  • Fry Care: Dense plants for hiding, or breeding box
  • Sex Change: Females can develop swords and become male-like

Common Health Issues

Ich

Fin Rot

  • Symptoms: Deteriorating sword and fins
  • Treatment: Clean water, antibacterial treatment

Swim Bladder Issues

  • Symptoms: Swimming difficulties
  • Treatment: Fast, feed peas, improve diet

Feed a swordtail a flake or pellet that is not pure protein, plus blanched greens. They tolerate a range of hard water and they do not tolerate a filthy one. Ich, fungus on a nipped fin, and a female that is bullied until she hides are the common problems. Quarantine new livebearers. A shop swordtail often arrives with parasites and a pregnancy.

Swordtails Are Great For:

  • Beginners wanting elegant fish
  • Community tank enthusiasts
  • Those interested in livebearer breeding
  • Aquarists with hard water

Swordtails May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Soft, acidic water setups
  • Tanks without secure lids
  • Those not wanting population growth

The best way to decide if a Swordtail Fish is right for your tank is to talk to people who already keep them.

A well-maintained Swordtail Fish is one of the more satisfying species to keep, and most owners who take the time to get it right have no regrets.

Tank Maintenance Essentials

They hybridize with platys. Both are Xiphophorus, and a mixed community produces fry that do not belong cleanly to either species. Keep them apart if you care what the young are. Inside the swordtails themselves, choose males with intact swords and females that are not being run ragged. A 20-gallon tank can hold a small group if it is longer than it is tall. Several adult males in that footprint will tear swords and stress the females even though the species is called peaceful.

Feed flakes, spirulina, and frozen foods. They eat at the surface and in the middle and they are not picky, which helps the easy rating and hurts the water if you feed until the sword is wagging over a pile of leftovers. The 3-to-5-year lifespan is typical, not a failure, for a fish that breeds this hard. Give the adults 20 gallons, hard water inside the ranges above, and a plan for more babies than the tank can raise. A male with no sword yet is often just a young male. Give him the length to grow the extension before you decide the fish is a female.

Living Environment

Expect a short installation period followed by a long stretch of near-autopilot maintenance.

Is Swordtail Fish

Swordtails, Xiphophorus hellerii, are livebearers whose males grow a sword on the tail. They do better in hard water, eat flake and algae, and produce fry if plants give cover. A long fin and a sword are targets for barbs and angelfish. Give them a tank of 20 gallons or more and a lid.
