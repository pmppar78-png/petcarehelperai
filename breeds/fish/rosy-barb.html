Overview

The Rosy Barb is one of the most popular and hardy barbs in the aquarium hobby. Males display stunning rosy-pink coloration, especially during breeding, while females show a more subtle golden-orange hue. Their active nature, ease of care, and tolerance for cooler temperatures make them excellent fish for both beginners and experienced aquarists.

Unlike some barb species, Rosy Barbs are generally peaceful and rarely nip fins when kept in proper schools. They're one of the larger commonly available barbs, reaching up to 6 inches in ideal conditions.

Pethia conchonius is a South Asian barb that gets bigger than the tiny rose-colored fish many people picture. Adults are 4 to 6 inches and live 5 to 7 years. They need 30 gallons so the chasing has somewhere to go. They are active omnivores, rated easy, and the card calls them peaceful. They still nip fins. Long-finned tankmates in a short tank pay for that gap between the label and the mouth. A 30-gallon footprint with room to swim is what keeps the nipping from becoming the whole day.

The temperature range is the other surprise: 64 to 77°F. This is a cool-water fish compared with a typical tropical community held in the low 80s. A tank at a steady 82°F sits above the listed ceiling. pH can run from 6.0 to 8.0 and hardness from 5 to 19 dGH, so they are less fussy about minerals than about heat and space. Keep nitrate under 30 ppm once those 4-to-6-inch bodies are grown. Ammonia still has to read 0 ppm, and nitrite does too. An unheated tank in a temperate room often lands inside 64 to 77°F more naturally than a heater set for discus.

Natural Habitat

Rosy Barbs originate from South Asian waters.

  • Range: Northern India, Nepal, Bangladesh, and surrounding regions
  • Environment: Lakes, ponds, and slow-moving streams
  • Climate: Subtropical; experience seasonal temperature variations
  • Habitat: Vegetated areas with sandy or muddy substrates

Tank Requirements

Rosy Barbs need space for their active swimming.

  • Swimming Space: Long tanks preferred for horizontal swimming
  • Plants: Appreciate planted tanks but will eat soft plants
  • Substrate: Sand or gravel; enjoy digging
  • Filtration: Moderate to strong; enjoy some current
  • Lid: Recommended; may jump when startled

Behavior & Social Structure

Rosy Barbs are active, social fish: Your aquatic veterinarian and experienced Rosy Barb owners can offer perspective tailored to your situation.

  • Schooling: Must be kept in groups of 6+ to reduce aggression
  • Active: Constantly swimming throughout the tank
  • Peaceful: Generally non-aggressive when properly schooled
  • Male Rivalry: Males display and spar but rarely cause injury
  • Plant Interactions: May nibble on soft-leaved plants

Males color into a deep rosy red when they are in good condition and have females to display to. Females stay plainer and fuller in the belly. A group, rather than a single male, is how that color shows up, and it also spreads the chasing. They are fast. Slow fish with trailing fins, especially in a crowded 30 gallons, get tattered. Choose tankmates that are similarly quick, or give the barbs enough length that they spend the energy on each other.

Feed them as omnivores: flakes, small pellets, frozen foods, and greens. They will eat almost anything that hits the water, which makes them easy and makes overfeeding easy too. A 6-inch adult is a real bioload in the minimum tank. Change water often enough to hold nitrate under 30 ppm once the fish are grown, not only while they are 2-inch juveniles. Care feels easy because they eat and they do not hide. The work is the adult size and the cooler temperature.

They spawn readily in cooler water with fine plants or a spawning mop, and a healthy female can produce a lot of eggs. You do not need to breed them to keep the color. You do need to avoid cooking them in a lidded tank aimed at 82°F year round. If the rest of the stock must live that warm, the rosy barb is the fish that should move, not the heater. The 5-to-7-year lifespan lines up with 30 gallons or more, 64 to 77°F, and neighbors that are not delicate fin-bearers.

Compatibility

Rosy Barbs work well in community setups: Upfront effort to understand how a Rosy Barb actually operates usually pays dividends in fewer vet emergencies.

Compatible Tank Mates

  • Other barbs (Tiger Barbs, Gold Barbs, Cherry Barbs)
  • Danios and larger rasboras
  • Rainbowfish
  • Corydoras catfish
  • Loaches
  • Medium-sized peaceful cichlids

Incompatible Species

  • Long-finned slow fish (Bettas, fancy goldfish)
  • Very small fish that may be bullied
  • Aggressive territorial fish
  • Delicate shrimp (may be eaten)

Breeding

Rosy Barbs are relatively easy to breed.

  • Sexing: Males more colorful with black-tipped fins; females plumper
  • Conditioning: Feed high-protein foods; slightly warmer water
  • Spawning: Scatter eggs among fine-leaved plants
  • Eggs: Up to 200 eggs per spawn; hatch in 1-2 days
  • Parents: Will eat eggs; remove after spawning
  • Fry Care: Feed infusoria, then baby brine shrimp

Common Health Issues

Rosy Barbs are hardy but can experience.

  • Ich: Common parasite; easily treated
  • Bloat: From overfeeding; avoid excessive food
  • Stress: From inadequate school size or aggression
  • Temperature Shock: Avoid rapid temperature changes

Is Rosy Barb

Rosy barbs, Pethia conchonius, are cool-water fish from South Asia. A school of six or more in a long 20-gallon tank, often in the mid-60s to low 70s Fahrenheit, is the peaceful version. One rosy barb in a warm tetra tank nips fins. They eat flake and frozen foods and will use the whole length of the tank.
