Overview

Pencilfish are slender, elegant characins named for their elongated, pencil-like body shape. These peaceful nano fish are prized for their subtle beauty, interesting swimming posture, and calm demeanor. Many species swim at an oblique angle, giving them a distinctive appearance.

A unique feature of Pencilfish is their dramatic color change between day and night. Many species display horizontal stripes during the day that transform into vertical bars at night - a camouflage adaptation that makes them fascinating to observe at different times.

Natural Habitat

Pencilfish originate from South American waters:

  • Range: Amazon basin, Orinoco, and Guiana Shield drainages
  • Environment: Slow-moving streams, flooded forests, and blackwater habitats
  • Water: Soft, acidic, often tannin-stained water
  • Habitat: Among dense vegetation and submerged leaf litter

Species Varieties

Several Pencilfish species are popular in aquariums:

  • Coral Red Pencilfish (N. mortenthaleri): Stunning red coloration; most colorful species
  • Golden Pencilfish (N. beckfordi): Golden body with red accents; most common
  • Dwarf Pencilfish (N. marginatus): Tiny at 1.2 inches; great for nano tanks
  • Three-lined Pencilfish (N. trifasciatus): Three horizontal stripes
  • One-lined Pencilfish (N. unifasciatus): Single bold horizontal stripe
  • Purple Pencilfish (N. rubrocaudatus): Purple/red tail coloration

Tank Requirements

Pencilfish thrive in well-planted aquariums:

  • Tank Size: Minimum 10 gallons for a school of 6-8
  • Plants: Heavily planted with fine-leaved plants
  • Floating Plants: Provide shade and security
  • Substrate: Dark substrate enhances coloration
  • Filtration: Gentle filtration; avoid strong currents
  • Lighting: Subdued to moderate; diffused through floating plants
  • Decor: Driftwood and leaf litter for blackwater effect

Diet & Feeding

Pencilfish are micro-predators with small mouths:

  • Staple Food: High-quality micro pellets or crushed flakes
  • Live Foods: Baby brine shrimp, daphnia, micro worms
  • Frozen Foods: Cyclops, daphnia, baby brine shrimp
  • Important: Food must be small enough for their tiny mouths
  • Variety: Rotate foods for optimal nutrition

Feeding Schedule: Feed 2-3 small meals daily; they pick at food throughout the day.

Behavior & Social Structure

Pencilfish display interesting behaviors:

  • Schooling: Should be kept in groups of 6+ for security
  • Swimming Angle: Many species swim at an oblique (head-up) angle
  • Peaceful: Non-aggressive toward all tank mates
  • Day/Night Colors: Dramatic pattern changes between day and night
  • Territorial Males: Males may display to each other but rarely fight

Compatibility

Pencilfish make excellent community fish:

Compatible Tank Mates

  • Small tetras (Cardinal, Ember, Green Neon)
  • Corydoras catfish
  • Otocinclus
  • Small rasboras
  • Dwarf cichlids (Apistogramma)
  • Other Pencilfish species
  • Peaceful dwarf shrimp

Incompatible Species

  • Large or aggressive fish
  • Fast-moving fin nippers
  • Fish that outcompete for food
  • Boisterous species that cause stress

Breeding

Pencilfish can be bred with effort:

  • Sexing: Males more colorful; females rounder when gravid
  • Setup: Heavily planted breeding tank with fine-leaved plants
  • Spawning: Scatter eggs among plants in early morning
  • Eggs: Adhesive eggs stick to plants; remove parents after spawning
  • Fry: Hatch in 24-48 hours; feed infusoria then baby brine shrimp

Common Health Issues

Pencilfish are generally hardy but can experience:

  • Ich: Common parasite; treat with temperature increase and medication
  • Stress: From poor water quality or inadequate school size
  • Fin Rot: Usually from poor conditions
  • Internal Parasites: Possible in wild-caught specimens
  • Nutritional Issues: If not fed appropriately sized foods

Is This Fish Right for You?

Pencilfish Are Great For:

  • Planted nano aquarium setups
  • Peaceful community tanks
  • Aquarists who appreciate subtle beauty
  • Blackwater or biotope aquariums

Pencilfish May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Tanks with aggressive or boisterous fish
  • High-flow or bare-bottom setups
  • Those wanting very active, flashy fish
  • Hard, alkaline water conditions
