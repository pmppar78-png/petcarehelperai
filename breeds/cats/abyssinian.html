A ticked coat and no off switch

Abyssinians weigh about 6 to 10 pounds and live roughly 12 to 15 years. Each hair is banded, which is the ticking, and the usual color is ruddy. Under the coat is a climber that opens cupboards and loses interest in a toy in four minutes. Activity on the stats table is very high. Vocalization is moderate: comments, not a Siamese aria. They are poor pets for a house that is empty ten hours and excellent ones for someone who will play on purpose.

A tall cat tree is equipment. Puzzle feeders are how you feed a cat this smart without turning the bowl into the only event of the day. They are usually fine with children who understand that a cat on a shelf is finished being touched, and with other pets that can match the pace.

Eyes, red cells, kidneys

Progressive retinal atrophy from the rdAc variant can be DNA-tested, and a breeder without that paper is hoping you like the color enough to skip it. Pyruvate kinase deficiency causes an anemia you can also test for before you buy. Renal amyloidosis, protein laid down in the kidneys, shows up in this family and in Somalis and does not come with a clean cheek-swab clearance. Weight loss in a busy cat gets blamed on "being active." Weigh them. Dental disease and a slipping kneecap are the lesser, still real, problems. Hyperesthesia, rippling skin and sudden frantic grooming, needs a vet so it is not written off as a quirk.

Food, routine care, and gear often total about $1,350 to $2,450 a year. Kittens from careful breeders are often $1,000 to $2,000. VCA's library explains retinal atrophy and kidney disease without a cattery slogan. A cat that stops eating is the not-eating page, and the checker is the urgency sort.

Abyssinian questions

How is an Abyssinian different from a Somali?

The Somali is the longhaired version of this cat. The ticked coat, the busy brain, and several of the same diseases are shared. If you wanted a quiet lap cat, neither one is that cat.

What DNA tests should an Abyssinian breeder show?

Progressive retinal atrophy, the rdAc variant, and pyruvate kinase deficiency. Renal amyloidosis does not have a simple swab that clears a line. Ask what the parents' relatives died of, especially kidneys.

Do Abyssinians like to be held?

Usually they want to be in the room and on the shelf, not pinned for a cuddle. A second athletic cat often works better than expecting this one to become a pillow.
