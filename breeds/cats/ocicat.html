Ocicat Overview

The Ocicat is a medium to large cat breed known for being active, social, devoted. Weighing 6-15 lbs with a lifespan of 12-18 years, Their high activity level and very high intelligence make them engaging and entertaining pets that keep their owners on their toes.

Ocicats are good with respectful family members and generally get along well with other pets when properly introduced.

The Ocicat's active, social, devoted character is not simply a breed description but reflects deep-seated behavioral tendencies shaped by genetics, early socialization, and the breed's historical development.

The active, social, devoted traits associated with Ocicat manifest in daily life through specific play preferences, social interaction patterns, vocalization tendencies, and activity rhythms.

Personality & Temperament

Ocicats have distinctive personalities that set them apart.

  • Active: This core trait defines the Ocicat experience and makes them beloved companions.
  • Social: Their social nature means they form strong bonds with their families.
  • Devoted: Ocicats show remarkable devoted in daily life.
  • Intelligence: With very high intelligence, they are quick learners who can master tricks and enjoy puzzle toys.
  • Social Nature: Ocicats appreciate companionship while also valuing their independence.

The active, social, devoted temperament of the Ocicat manifests in daily life through patterns of behavior that experienced owners learn to anticipate, appreciate, and manage.

Owners who proactively manage their Ocicat's environmental stability typically report fewer stress-related health issues and behavioral problems over the cat's lifetime.

Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy Screening for Ocicat

HCM data specific to Ocicats is limited, but the breed is considered at moderate risk given its Siamese and Abyssinian ancestry, which both carry cardiac susceptibility. The Ocicat's mixed heritage (Siamese, Abyssinian, American Shorthair) means HCM may arise through multiple genetic pathways.

Screening Protocol: Echocardiographic screening from 2 years of age is recommended, repeated every 1-2 years. Their active temperament may make sedation necessary for accurate imaging in some individuals.

Ocicats are generally healthy with good genetic diversity, but their foundation breed contributions each carry independent cardiac risk factors that warrant monitoring.

Key Risk Factors: Inbreeding in less diverse breeding programs can increase HCM risk. Seek breeders who perform cardiac screening on all breeding stock.
