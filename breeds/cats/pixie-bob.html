Pixie-bob Cat Overview

The Pixie-bob is a domestic cat breed developed in Washington State in the 1980s to resemble the North American Bobcat. Despite legends suggesting wild bobcat heritage, DNA testing has confirmed they are fully domestic cats. The breed was founded by Carol Ann Brewer with a cat named Pixie.

Pixie-bobs are known for their wild appearance, featuring spotted or rosetted coats, bobbed tails (though tail length varies), and a distinctive wild facial appearance. They are the only breed standard that allows polydactylism (extra toes), with up to seven toes per paw being acceptable.

Personality & Temperament

Pixie-bobs have exceptional, dog-like personalities:

  • Extremely Loyal: They form strong bonds and are devoted to their families.
  • Dog-Like Behavior: They often follow owners, come when called, and enjoy walks on leash.
  • Intelligent: Quick learners who can be taught tricks and commands.
  • Gentle Giants: Despite their wild appearance, they are gentle and patient.
  • Quiet Communicators: They chirp, chatter, and growl rather than meow.
  • Family-Oriented: They do well with children and other pets.

Common Health Issues

Pixie-bobs are generally healthy due to careful breeding:

Potential Concerns

  • Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM): Heart disease that can affect any cat; screening recommended.
  • Cryptorchidism: Undescended testicles in males; more common in this breed.
  • Cystic Endometrial Hyperplasia: Uterine condition in unspayed females.
  • Dystocia: Difficult births in females; breeders should be prepared.

General Health Concerns

  • Obesity: Their stocky build can mask weight gain.
  • Dental Disease: Regular dental care is important.
  • Polydactyl Nail Care: Extra toes mean extra nails to maintain.

Health Screening Recommendation

Ask breeders about HCM screening and breeding history. Responsible breeders test for common health issues. Consider Basepaws DNA testing for comprehensive health screening.

Activity Level & Exercise

Pixie-bobs have moderate activity needs:

  • Interactive Play: 15-20 minutes of active play daily
  • Leash Walks: Many enjoy outdoor walks on leash
  • Fetch: They often play fetch naturally
  • Puzzle Toys: Mental stimulation is appreciated
  • Climbing Space: Cat trees and perches for observation

Nutrition & Feeding

Proper nutrition supports Pixie-bob health:

  • High-Quality Protein: Animal protein should be the first ingredient
  • Weight Management: Monitor portions to prevent obesity
  • Joint Support: Consider foods with glucosamine for larger cats
  • Fresh Water: Always available; many enjoy fountains
  • Slow Maturation: They take up to 4 years to mature fully

Grooming Requirements

Pixie-bobs come in shorthair and longhair varieties:

  • Shorthair: Weekly brushing to remove loose hair
  • Longhair: 2-3 times weekly brushing to prevent mats
  • Bathing: Occasionally as needed
  • Nail Trimming: Every 2-3 weeks; check all toes on polydactyl cats
  • Ear Cleaning: Weekly checks and cleaning as needed
  • Dental Care: Regular brushing recommended

Is a Pixie-bob Right for You?

Pixie-bobs Are Great For:

  • Families wanting a loyal, dog-like companion
  • Homes with children and other pets
  • Those who appreciate a wild appearance in a domestic cat
  • First-time cat owners (they're very adaptable)
  • People who want an interactive, trainable cat

Pixie-bobs May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Those wanting a truly independent cat
  • People away from home for long hours
  • Anyone expecting actual wild cat behavior
  • Those on a tight budget (higher initial cost)

Related Cat Breeds to Consider

If you're interested in Pixie-bobs, you might also consider:

  • American Bobtail - Similar wild appearance and personality
  • Manx - Tailless or short-tailed, loyal personality
  • Highlander - Wild appearance with curled ears
  • Bengal - Wild spotted appearance, more active
