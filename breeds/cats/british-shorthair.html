The British Shorthair is the stocky grey cat from the postcards, though the breed comes in many colors. Calm, easy-going, and dignified is the right temperament line, and it means something specific: this cat often watches the room, accepts petting on its own schedule, and does not demand a conversation. Activity is low to moderate. Vocalization is low. They are usually good with children and other pets without being velcro.

Adults commonly weigh 9 to 18 pounds. Lifespan on the card runs 12 to 20 years, a wide band that depends on weight and heart more than on coat color.

The plush coat hides the waist

The coat is dense and needs a weekly brush, more when it sheds in season. Grooming needs are low to moderate. The health problem owners create is fat. A broad chest already looks square, so a British Shorthair can be obese before anyone says the word. Feed measured meals. Use a scale or at least a monthly rib check. Diabetes and arthritis follow the extra pounds, and a heavy cat is harder to medicate later.

Play still matters. A short wand session and a puzzle feeder keep a low-activity cat from becoming a completely sedentary one. They are fine in apartments. They are not a breed that needs a leash-walk to stay sane, though some enjoy one.

Hearts and the yearly bill

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy is the inherited concern. When you are choosing a kitten, ask what cardiac testing the parents had. For a pet already at home, mention any murmur, any sudden hind-leg weakness, or any open-mouth breathing immediately. Those hind-leg events can be a clot, and they are an emergency.

Routine yearly cost is in the neighborhood of $1,200 to $2,500 once you add food, litter, and mid-range insurance. The VCA library has a readable HCM page. This breed suits someone who wants a quiet, sturdy companion and will keep the food scoop honest. It bores people who wanted a Bengal. A cat that does not eat, or that seems painful when walking, should land on the not-eating guide or the checker the same day.

British Shorthair weight and temperament

Are British Shorthairs cuddly?

They are calm and loyal in a reserved way. Many like to be near you and not on you. Expect a dignified cat, not a Siamese running commentary.

How much do they weigh?

Often 9 to 18 pounds, with males heavier. The dense body hides weight gain. You should still feel ribs under the plush coat.

Do they shed?

Moderately, with a seasonal blow of the dense coat. A weekly brush handles most of it. They are not a hairless breed and not a Persian.

What should breeders test?

Hearts. Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy occurs in the breed. A DNA panel is not a substitute for knowing how the parents' hearts looked on ultrasound.
