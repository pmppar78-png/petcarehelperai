Cheetoh Overview

The Cheetoh cat is a striking hybrid breed created by crossing Bengal cats with Ocicat bloodlines, resulting in a large, muscular cat with a wild, cheetah-like appearance but a thoroughly domestic temperament. Developed by Carol Drymon in 2001, the Cheetoh was specifically bred to combine the exotic spotted coat pattern of wild-looking breeds with an exceptionally gentle and sociable personality. These cats are remarkably larger than most domestic breeds, with males sometimes reaching 25 pounds of lean muscle. Their coat features bold spots or rosettes on a warm golden, silver, or brown background, creating an unmistakable wild appearance. Despite their impressive size and exotic looks, Cheetohs are known for being extraordinarily gentle, social, and affectionate - they love being around people and are often described as having dog-like personalities.

The Cheetoh's social, gentle, playful character is not simply a breed description but reflects deep-seated behavioral tendencies shaped by genetics, early socialization, and the breed's historical development.

The social, gentle, playful traits associated with Cheetoh manifest in daily life through specific play preferences, social interaction patterns, vocalization tendencies, and activity rhythms.

Diet & Nutrition

High-protein, grain-free diet formulated for large, active cats. Feed 1/2-3/4 cup of quality dry food or equivalent wet food daily. Their large size requires adequate caloric intake.

Some Cheetohs do great on standard kibble; others need a different approach due to allergies, sensitivities, or individual metabolism. Work with your vet to find what works, and be willing to adjust as your Cheetoh's needs change with age.

Housing & Environment

Needs spacious living environment with climbing structures. Tall cat trees and wall shelves essential. They are active and need room to play and explore.

Grooming Requirements

Minimal grooming - weekly brushing to remove loose fur. Short coat is nearly self-maintaining. Regular nail trims every 2-3 weeks.

Activity & Exercise

30+ minutes of active interactive play daily. They enjoy fetch, puzzle toys, and chasing games. Consider harness training for outdoor adventures.

Where to Find a Cheetoh

When searching for a Cheetoh, seek out reputable breeders who health-test their breeding cats, raise kittens in a home environment, provide health guarantees, and are active in the cat fancy community.

Many wonderful Cheetohs and Cheetoh mixes are available for adoption.

Daily Life with

Living with a Cheetoh is a uniquely rewarding experience. Morning and evening feeding times create structure, while scheduled play sessions provide the physical and mental stimulation that Cheetohs need.

Given their high energy levels, Cheetohs do best in homes that provide plenty of interactive play opportunities, climbing structures, and enrichment activities throughout the day.

Are Cheetohs good with children?

Yes, Cheetohs are excellent with children. Their social, gentle, playful nature makes them patient and gentle companions for families with kids. They enjoy interactive play and are typically tolerant of the bustle of family life. Always supervise young children with any cat and teach respectful handling.
