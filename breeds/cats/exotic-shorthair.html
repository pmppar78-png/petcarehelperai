Persian problems, plush coat

Exotic Shorthairs weigh about 8 to 15 pounds and often live 12 to 15 years. They were built to look like a Persian that you could brush in five minutes. The five minutes is roughly true. The brachycephalic skull is not a discount. Nostrils are small, eyes are prominent, and the nose fold collects moisture. Lifespan and vet bills follow that face more than they follow the coat length.

Temperament is the pleasant part: calm, affectionate, low voice, fine with gentle children. Play happens, then a long nap. Grooming supplies are still on the budget because the plush coat tangles behind the ears and the face needs a daily damp cloth and a full dry.

Kidneys and corneas

Polycystic kidney disease is the DNA test to see before you pay, same as in Persians and Himalayans. A young cat that drinks like a dog needs bloodwork, not a bigger bowl as the whole plan. Corneal ulcers announce themselves with squinting and a sudden excess of tears. Cherry eye and entropion show up. Crowded teeth mean dentals under anesthesia, which is a bigger deal in a short-faced cat whose airway is already tight. Tell the clinic about the skull before they sedate.

Routine yearly costs often total about $1,250 to $2,450. Kittens are commonly $1,500 to $3,000. VCA covers corneal ulcers and polycystic kidney disease. Squinting or labored breathing uses the checker the same day. A cat off its food uses the not-eating page.

Exotic Shorthair questions

Are Exotic Shorthairs low maintenance Persians?

The coat is easier. The skull is not. You still wipe the eyes, watch the breathing, and want a PKD test. Calling them low maintenance skips the part that sends people to the emergency clinic.

How calm are they?

Activity is low to moderate. They are affectionate, usually patient with children, and happier on a cushion than on a cat wheel. A dog that chases is a bad roommate. A quiet dog can be fine.

What does the face put at risk?

Airway noise that can become distress, corneal ulcers, tear-staining skin infections, and crowded teeth. Heat makes all of it worse.
