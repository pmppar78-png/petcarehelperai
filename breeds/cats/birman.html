Points, gloves, and a coat that felts

Birmans weigh about 7 to 14 pounds and often live 12 to 16 years. The pattern is a pale body, darker points, and white feet. People buy the legend and keep the cat, which is gentle, social, and less frantic than the Abyssinian side of the catalog. Grooming is moderate: the fur is silky and still mats behind the ears and in the hind furnishings if you only brush before a photo. A comb twice a week is the real schedule.

They talk a little. They follow a bit. A household that is gone all weekend with nobody stopping in is a poor fit. One that has kids, a dog that is polite, and a windowsill is a good one.

Hearts and older kidneys

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy is the disease to ask about by name. A clear yearly listen does not replace an echocardiogram if a murmur shows up or if you are choosing a breeding line. Kidneys fail in older cats of many breeds; in a Birman, increased thirst and a lighter frame are the clues to take to bloodwork, not to a new food you picked alone. Teeth need checking under the sweet face.

Routine yearly costs often fall around $1,225 to $2,250. Kittens are commonly $800 to $1,500. VCA covers cardiomyopathy and chronic kidney disease in plain language. Not eating for more than a day is the not-eating page. Open-mouth breathing uses the checker as you leave for the clinic.

Birman questions

What are Birman gloves?

White feet on a colorpoint cat, ideally even, which is the breed's mark. Uneven gloves do not change the care. The silky coat and the calm temperament are what you live with.

Are Birmans lap cats?

Often they want to be in the same room and will sit with you, without the constant narration of a Siamese. Activity is moderate. They are usually patient with children and other pets.

What health screening matters?

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. Ask for heart ultrasounds on the parents, not only a stethoscope note. Older Birmans also need kidney checks when thirst or weight changes.
