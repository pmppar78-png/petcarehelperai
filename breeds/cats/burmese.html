Burmese Cat Overview

The Burmese is a medium-sized cat breed known for its compact, muscular body and incredibly social personality. Often described as "velcro cats" or "dog-like," Burmese cats form deep bonds with their families and thrive on human interaction. The breed originated from a single cat named Wong Mau, brought from Burma (now Myanmar) to the United States in 1930.

Burmese cats are famous for their satin-like coat, expressive golden eyes, and charming personalities. They are heavier than they appear due to their solid, muscular build - often called "bricks wrapped in silk." Their need for companionship makes them unsuitable for owners who are away frequently.

Temperament & Personality

Burmese cats have distinctive personality traits that make them unforgettable:

  • People-Oriented: They crave human companionship and will follow you everywhere, often called "shadow cats."
  • Playful Throughout Life: Burmese maintain kitten-like playfulness well into their senior years.
  • Vocal Communicators: They have softer voices than Siamese but will engage in conversations with their owners.
  • Lap Cats: True lap cats that love to cuddle and sleep with their humans.
  • Intelligent: Quick learners who can be taught tricks and enjoy puzzle toys.
  • Social Butterflies: Usually friendly with visitors and adapt well to new people.

Common Health Issues

Burmese cats have some breed-specific health concerns:

Genetic Conditions

  • Diabetes Mellitus: Burmese have a higher predisposition to diabetes than many other breeds.
  • Hypokalemia: A genetic condition causing low potassium levels leading to muscle weakness.
  • Burmese Head Defect: A fatal developmental abnormality in some kittens (responsible breeders test for this).

Other Health Concerns

  • Dental Disease: Prone to gingivitis and periodontal disease; regular dental care is essential.
  • Eye Conditions: Cherry eye and corneal sequestrum can occur.
  • Obesity: Their love of food combined with their compact build makes weight management important.

Health Screening Recommendation

Ask breeders about testing for hypokalemia and head defect genes. Consider Basepaws DNA testing and regular glucose monitoring given their diabetes risk.

Care Requirements

Burmese cats need attention and engagement:

  • Companionship: Not suited for homes where they'll be alone for long periods - consider getting two.
  • Interactive Play: 30+ minutes of active play daily to burn energy.
  • Mental Stimulation: Puzzle feeders, training sessions, and interactive toys.
  • Vertical Space: Cat trees and perches for climbing and surveying.
  • Indoor Living: Best kept indoors due to their trusting nature.

Grooming Needs

Burmese cats have minimal grooming requirements:

  • Brushing: Weekly brushing is sufficient to remove loose hair.
  • Coat Type: Short, satin-like coat that sheds minimally.
  • Bathing: Rarely needed; they are excellent self-groomers.
  • Nail Trimming: Every 2-3 weeks.
  • Dental Care: Daily brushing recommended due to dental disease predisposition.
  • Ear Cleaning: Monthly checks and cleaning as needed.

Nutrition & Feeding

Proper nutrition supports Burmese health:

  • High-Protein Diet: Quality animal protein should be the main ingredient.
  • Low Carbohydrate: Important given their diabetes predisposition.
  • Portion Control: They love food and can become overweight easily.
  • Measured Meals: Two to three measured meals daily rather than free-feeding.
  • Fresh Water: Multiple water sources; many enjoy fountains.

Is a Burmese Cat Right for You?

Burmese Cats Are Great For:

  • People who work from home or have flexible schedules
  • Families with children who want an interactive, playful cat
  • Those wanting a dog-like, loyal feline companion
  • Multi-cat households (they love feline companionship)
  • People who enjoy an engaged, communicative pet

Burmese Cats May Not Be Ideal For:

  • People away from home frequently or for long periods
  • Those seeking an independent, low-maintenance cat
  • People who prefer quiet cats
  • Those unable to provide regular interactive play time

Related Breeds to Consider

If you're interested in Burmese cats, you might also consider:

  • Tonkinese - Burmese-Siamese cross with similar personality
  • Bombay - Burmese-American Shorthair cross, black coated
  • European Burmese - More color varieties, slightly different build
  • Siamese - Similar vocalization and social nature
