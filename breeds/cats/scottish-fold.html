The ear is the warning label

Scottish Folds are medium cats, about 6 to 13 pounds, often sweet, quiet, and easy with children and other pets. Lifespan is listed around 11 to 14 years. The folded ear is not a harmless costume. It is osteochondrodysplasia, a cartilage defect that also hits the legs, the spine, and the tail. Cats with one copy vary from mildly sore to clearly lame. Two copies, from breeding fold to fold, are the severe end. A breeder who does that is producing pain on purpose.

Watch the tail and the jump. A cat that stops going to a favorite shelf, or that stands oddly at the litter box, may be arthritic years before a "senior" label would suggest it. The cost table includes joint supplements at $100 to $300 because this is expected care, not an extra. Ear canals that are pinched shut also need cleaning so they do not infect.

Heart and kidneys still count

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and polycystic kidney disease occur in some lines and are separate from the ear gene. A murmur deserves an ultrasound. A DNA test for PKD is a fair question. None of that cancels the cartilage problem, and a clear heart does not make the fold ethical.

Activity is moderate. They play, then they sit. Grooming is ordinary unless arthritis makes them stop reaching the back, which is itself a sign. A routine year often runs about $1,500 to $3,100 before radiographs and pain drugs. Merck describes osteochondrodysplasia in clinical language. A cat that will not eat or cannot jump uses the not-eating guide and the checker.

Scottish Fold questions

Do all Scottish Folds have joint disease?

Every cat with folded ears has the cartilage mutation, called osteochondrodysplasia. Severity varies. Many develop painful arthritis while still young. Breeding fold to fold makes the worst form. A straight-eared kitten did not inherit the fold.

What does a bad tail mean?

A short, thick, or stiff tail is a clue that the cartilage problem is not limited to the ears. It is not a cute variation. Pain, a reluctance to jump, and a potty posture that looks wrong belong in a clinic.

Should I get a Scottish Fold or a Scottish Straight?

If you want the calm, sweet temperament without the folded-ear gene, choose a straight-eared cat. The fold is the disease. Insurance and joint supplements do not remove the mutation.
