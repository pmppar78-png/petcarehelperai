Korat Cat Overview

The Korat (pronounced ko-RAHT) is an ancient natural breed from Thailand, where they are called "Si-Sawat" (meaning "color of the sawat seed" - a grayish-blue hue). Considered good luck cats in their homeland, Korats have been treasured for centuries and are mentioned in the ancient Thai "Cat-Book Poems" dating back to the 14th century.

Korats have a distinctive silver-blue coat with silver-tipped hairs that create a halo effect, heart-shaped faces, and large luminous green eyes. They are often confused with Russian Blues and Chartreux but are genetically distinct. Korats come in only one color - silver-tipped blue - and breed true to type.

Temperament & Personality

Korats are known for their devoted personalities:

  • Intensely Loyal: Form strong bonds with their chosen people.
  • Intelligent: Excellent memory; they remember people and routines.
  • Gentle: Soft-natured cats that prefer calm environments.
  • Observant: Always watching and aware of their surroundings.
  • Sensitive: Dislike loud noises and sudden movements.
  • Playful: Enjoy interactive games but aren't hyperactive.

Common Health Issues

Korats have some breed-specific health concerns:

Genetic Conditions

  • GM1 and GM2 Gangliosidosis: Fatal neurological diseases; DNA testing is essential and available. Responsible breeders test all breeding cats.

Other Health Concerns

  • Low Body Fat: Korats have low body fat; may be sensitive to anesthesia.
  • Respiratory Sensitivity: May be sensitive to respiratory infections.
  • Generally Healthy: Otherwise, they are a robust, long-lived breed.

Critical Health Screening

ALWAYS ask breeders for GM1/GM2 gangliosidosis test results - this is essential. Kittens from tested parents are safe. Consider Basepaws DNA testing for comprehensive genetic screening.

Care Requirements

Korats need a calm, stable environment:

  • Quiet Home: They're sensitive to loud noises and chaos.
  • Routine: Appreciate predictable schedules.
  • Companionship: Form strong bonds and dislike being alone.
  • Interactive Play: Daily play sessions to keep them engaged.
  • Indoor Living: Must be kept indoors due to rarity and trusting nature.
  • Slow Introductions: Take time introducing new pets or people.

Grooming Needs

Korats have minimal grooming requirements:

  • Brushing: Weekly brushing to remove loose hair.
  • Coat Type: Single coat, short to medium length, lies close to body.
  • Shedding: Minimal shedding year-round.
  • Bathing: Rarely needed; they're excellent self-groomers.
  • Nail Trimming: Every 2-3 weeks.
  • Dental Care: Regular brushing recommended.

Nutrition & Feeding

Proper nutrition supports Korat health:

  • High-Quality Protein: Animal protein should be the primary ingredient.
  • Low Body Fat: May need slightly higher calorie content to maintain weight.
  • Balanced Diet: No special dietary requirements.
  • Portion Control: Despite lean build, monitor intake.
  • Fresh Water: Multiple sources; many enjoy water fountains.

Is a Korat Right for You?

Korats Are Great For:

  • Quiet households without young children
  • People who want a devoted, loyal companion
  • Those seeking a healthy, long-lived breed
  • People who appreciate rare, ancient breeds
  • Those willing to wait for a kitten (limited availability)

Korats May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Noisy, chaotic households
  • Families with very young, loud children
  • Those who want an immediately outgoing cat with strangers
  • People frequently away from home

Related Breeds to Consider

If you're interested in Korats, you might also consider:

  • Russian Blue - Similar appearance, different personality
  • Chartreux - Another blue cat with quiet nature
  • British Shorthair Blue - Blue variety with calm demeanor
  • Burmese - Similar loyalty, more social
