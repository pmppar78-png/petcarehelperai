The tail is a spine story

Manx cats weigh about 8 to 12 pounds. The lifespan line, 8 to 14 years, is wider and shorter than a typical shorthair because the famous trait is a vertebral mutation. A cat with a sound back can be playful, dog-like, and good with children. A cat with Manx syndrome cannot hold urine or stool, hops, and may have an open spinal defect. That is the fact to put beside the round rear in the photo.

Reputable breeding avoids pairing two tailless cats. Kittens should be watched for a wet hind end, a bunny hop, and a spine you can feel ending in a dimple. If you are adopting an adult that is already continent and athletic, you are past the worst of the congenital risk and still own a cat that may develop arthritis where the spine is odd.

Everyday care if the back is sound

Grooming is low to moderate. Activity is moderate, with a notable jump. They often like to fetch and to follow. The coat is not the project. Weight, teeth, and a yearly neuro look at the hind end are. Incontinence, if it shows up later, is a medical problem and a nursing problem: urine scald is painful and it will not be solved with a different litter brand alone.

A routine year often runs about $1,250 to $3,050, a wide band because spinal disease is expensive and a healthy longy is not. Merck describes spinal malformations in clinical terms. A cat that stops using the box, or stops eating, uses the not-eating guide and the checker.

Manx questions

Is a tailless Manx healthy?

Some are. The gene that shortens the tail can also disrupt the spine. Rumpies, with no tail, are the higher-risk end. A breeder who always breeds rumpy to rumpy is stacking that risk. Ask how kittens' bowels and gaits were checked.

What is Manx syndrome?

Spina bifida and related defects: a hopping walk, fecal or urinary incontinence, and sometimes a spine that did not close. It is not a personality. Affected cats may need help emptying the bladder and can develop sores. Some are euthanized young because the nursing care is constant.

Do all Manx lack tails?

No. Rumpies have none, rumpy risers have a nub, stumpies have a short tail, and longies have a full one. Longies can still carry the gene. The litter, not the postcard, tells you the risk.
