Bombay Cat Overview

The Bombay was created in the 1950s by Kentucky breeder Nikki Horner, who wanted to develop a cat that looked like a miniature black panther. By crossing sable Burmese with black American Shorthairs, she achieved her goal - a sleek, jet-black cat with a muscular build and striking copper or gold eyes.

Named after Bombay, India (the land of the black leopard), this breed is 100% domestic cat despite its wild appearance. The Bombay's coat is often described as "patent leather" for its high-gloss sheen. These cats combine the Burmese's social, people-loving nature with the American Shorthair's easy-going temperament.

Temperament & Personality

Bombay cats are known for their charming personalities:

  • Velcro Cats: Extremely attached to their people; want to be involved in everything.
  • Heat Seekers: Love warm laps, sunny spots, and cozy blankets.
  • Playful: Enjoy interactive games and maintain playfulness into adulthood.
  • Intelligent: Can learn tricks and enjoy puzzle toys.
  • Social: Generally good with visitors and adapt well to busy households.
  • Moderate Vocalization: Will "talk" to you with distinctive raspy meows.

Common Health Issues

Bombays inherit health considerations from both parent breeds:

From Burmese Lines

  • Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM): Heart disease that should be monitored.
  • Craniofacial Defect: Burmese head defect can appear in some lines.
  • Excessive Tearing: Some cats may have watery eyes.

General Health Concerns

  • Respiratory Issues: Some may have breathing difficulties due to shortened muzzle.
  • Obesity: Love of food and warmth can lead to weight issues.
  • Dental Issues: May be prone to gum disease.

Health Screening Recommendation

Ask breeders about HCM screening and Burmese head defect testing. Consider Basepaws DNA testing for comprehensive genetic health screening.

Care Requirements

Bombay cats are relatively easy to care for:

  • Companionship: They crave attention and shouldn't be left alone for long periods.
  • Interactive Play: Daily play sessions to keep them mentally and physically engaged.
  • Warm Environment: They love warmth - heated beds are appreciated.
  • Indoor Living: Best kept indoors for safety.
  • Mental Stimulation: Puzzle toys and training keep them happy.

Grooming Needs

Bombays have minimal grooming requirements:

  • Brushing: Weekly brushing with a rubber brush brings out coat shine.
  • Coat Type: Short, close-lying coat that requires minimal maintenance.
  • Bathing: Rarely needed; they're excellent self-groomers.
  • Eye Care: Wipe away any tear stains as needed.
  • Nail Trimming: Every 2-3 weeks.
  • Dental Care: Regular brushing recommended.

Nutrition & Feeding

Proper nutrition maintains the Bombay's sleek physique:

  • High-Quality Protein: Animal protein should be the primary ingredient.
  • Portion Control: Important as they tend toward weight gain.
  • Measured Meals: Two measured meals daily rather than free-feeding.
  • Coat Health: Omega fatty acids maintain their glossy coat.
  • Fresh Water: Always available; many enjoy water fountains.

Is a Bombay Right for You?

Bombays Are Great For:

  • People who want an affectionate, attached companion
  • Families with children seeking a patient, playful cat
  • Those who love the look of black cats
  • People who work from home or have flexible schedules
  • First-time cat owners wanting a friendly breed

Bombays May Not Be Ideal For:

  • People away from home frequently
  • Those seeking an independent, aloof cat
  • People who don't want a "shadow" following them
  • Homes where pets must be left alone all day

Related Breeds to Consider

If you're interested in Bombay cats, you might also consider:

  • Burmese - Parent breed with similar personality
  • American Shorthair - Parent breed, more colors
  • Tonkinese - Similar social personality
  • Havana Brown - Similar build in chocolate color
