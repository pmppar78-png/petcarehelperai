Serval ancestry, domestic rules

A Savannah is a hybrid of an African serval and a domestic cat. Size on the stats table, 12 to 25 pounds, is the whole argument: generation decides whether you brought home a tall athlete or something closer to a large house cat. Lifespan is listed at 12 to 20 years. Activity is very high. They chirp, hiss, and investigate faucets. Many enjoy water. Not every one will join you in the shower, and a photo of one in a sink is not a behavior guarantee.

They jump high enough to treat the top of a door as a shelf. A studio apartment and an F1 is a mismatch. A secure outdoor enclosure helps the later generations too, if it is actually escape-proof. Tell your veterinarian the generation before any anesthetic. Do not accept a breeder's vaccine recipe in place of the clinic's protocol.

Price, heart, eyes

Food alone is often $800 to $1,500 because the bodies are large and active. A routine year runs about $2,450 to $4,300 before the purchase price. Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy needs ultrasound, not a slogan. Progressive retinal atrophy and pyruvate kinase deficiency come in from the domestic side and can be DNA-tested. Stomachs can be sensitive. A cat that drops meals for a day is the not-eating guide, sooner rather than later in a lean, high-output animal.

TICA has a breed standard. It is not a permit. Local law is. Breathing trouble or collapse uses the checker while you travel.

Savannah questions

Are Savannahs legal everywhere?

No. Some states and cities restrict early-generation hybrids or exotic-cat crosses. Check the rule where you live before you wire a deposit. A breeder who says it is fine everywhere is wrong.

What does the generation number mean?

An F1 is half serval and is the large, costly cat, often $12,000 to $20,000. Later generations are more domestic in size and price and still not a Bengal or a tabby. Early-generation males are often sterile, which matters to breeders more than to pet homes.

Can a Savannah live with other pets?

Sometimes. Prey drive and size make small animals a bad idea, and a timid cat may be bullied. Older children do better than toddlers. This is a high-energy animal that can open doors.
