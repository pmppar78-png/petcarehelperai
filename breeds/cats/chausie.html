Chausie Cat Overview

The Chausie (pronounced "chow-see") is a hybrid breed developed by crossing the wild Jungle Cat (Felis chaus) with domestic Abyssinians. The name comes from the Latin name for the Jungle Cat. This breed was developed to provide a domestic cat with the exotic look of a wild cat and has been recognized by TICA since 1995.

Chausies are one of the largest domestic cat breeds, with males sometimes reaching 25 pounds of lean, athletic muscle. They have a wild appearance with long legs, large ears, and a cougar-like face. Despite their wild ancestry, properly bred Chausies (F4 generation and beyond) make loyal, interactive companions for the right owners.

The Chausie Cat's athletic, loyal, intelligent character is not simply a breed description but reflects deep-seated behavioral tendencies shaped by genetics, early socialization, and the breed's historical development.

Personality & Temperament

Chausies are not for first-time or casual cat owners.

  • Extremely Athletic: They can jump up to 6 feet from a standing position and need space to run.
  • Highly Intelligent: They require mental stimulation and can figure out how to open doors and cabinets.
  • Loyal Companions: They bond deeply with their chosen humans and can be devoted.
  • Active and Playful: They need extensive daily play and exercise.
  • Dog-Like Behaviors: Many enjoy fetch, walks on leashes, and following their owners.
  • Sensitive to Neglect: They do not tolerate being left alone for long periods.

The athletic, loyal, intelligent temperament of the Chausie Cat manifests in daily life through patterns of behavior that experienced owners learn to anticipate, appreciate, and manage.

Common Health Issues

Chausies have specific health considerations related to their hybrid nature.

Digestive Issues

  • Food Sensitivities: Many Chausies have shortened intestinal tracts and cannot properly digest grains, vegetables, or plant-based ingredients. This is a significant breed characteristic.
  • Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD): Can develop if fed inappropriate diets containing plant matter.

Inherited from Lines

  • Pyruvate Kinase Deficiency (PKD): An inherited enzyme deficiency causing anemia. DNA testing available.
  • Renal Amyloidosis: Protein deposits in kidneys, more common in Abyssinian-related breeds.

General Health Concerns

  • Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM): Heart disease screening recommended.
  • Dental Disease: Regular dental care important.

Critical Diet Information

Chausies often cannot digest plant-based foods. They may require a meat-only diet. Consult with your veterinarian about appropriate grain-free, high-protein diets. to understand your cat's genetic health profile.

Activity Level & Exercise

Chausies have extremely high activity needs.

  • Space Requirements: They need large living spaces or outdoor access (secure catio)
  • Daily Play: Multiple vigorous play sessions totaling 30-60 minutes daily
  • Vertical Space: Tall cat trees, shelving, and climbing opportunities essential
  • Running: Cat wheels are excellent for indoor exercise
  • harness training: Many Chausies excel at outdoor walks on leash
  • Water Play: Many enjoy playing with water

Nutrition & Feeding

Diet is critically important for Chausies.

  • Meat-Based Diet: Many require diets with minimal or no plant ingredients
  • High-Quality Protein: Animal protein must be the primary (ideally only) ingredient
  • Raw or Wet Food: May be better tolerated than kibble
  • Avoid Grains: Most Chausies cannot digest grains, corn, or plant fillers
  • Fresh Water: Always available; many prefer running water

Chausies Are Great For:

  • Experienced cat owners who understand hybrid breeds
  • Active households that can provide extensive exercise
  • Those with large homes or secure outdoor access

Chausies May Not Be Ideal For:

  • First-time cat owners
  • Small apartments without vertical space
  • Homes with very young children
  • Those unable to afford specialized diets
  • Areas with legal restrictions on hybrid cats
