The fox cat

Somalis are Abyssinians in a longer coat. Longhaired kittens showed up in Abyssinian litters in the 1960s, and breeders kept the look: a ticked coat with bands of color on each hair, a bushy tail, and ears that make people say fox. Ruddy, also called sorrel, is the classic color. The cat underneath is still the Abyssinian: light, busy, and interested in whatever you just opened.

Adults are about 6 to 10 pounds. The quick facts list 12 to 16 years, very high activity, low to moderate voice, and excellent tolerance for children. That last line assumes the children can live with a cat that follows them and also vanishes up a bookshelf. Grooming is moderate. The coat does not mat the way a Persian does, but a weekly comb keeps the ticking from looking dusty, and seasonal shed is real.

A house that is a course

Plan vertical space. A Somali will use the top of the fridge, the curtain rod if you let them, and any shelf you thought was decorative. Two play sessions a day is a minimum, not a bonus. Puzzle feeders help because the cat is smart enough to get bored with a bowl. They are usually fine with other pets if the introduction is slow. A dog that chases is a bad match. A second athletic cat is often a good one.

They talk less than a Siamese and more than a statue. Soft chirps when they want the wand toy are normal. Silence plus hiding, in a cat that usually narrates the hallway, is not.

Kidneys, eyes, and the Abyssinian file

Somalis share the Abyssinian's medical worries. Progressive retinal atrophy can be DNA-tested, and a breeder who shrugs at PRA is a reason to leave. Polycystic kidney disease testing belongs in the same conversation. Renal amyloidosis, protein deposits that damage the kidneys, shows up in this family and does not have a simple cheek-swab clearance. Ask what died of kidney disease in the line, and budget a yearly exam that actually checks urine and blood, not only a vaccine sticker. Weight loss in a cat this active is easy to blame on "being busy." Weigh them.

Food on the cost table runs about $350 to $550 a year, routine vet care about $200 to $400, before a specialist workup. VCA's pet library is a plain-language place to read about kidney disease and retinal atrophy. A Somali that stops eating is the situation on the cat not-eating page, and the checker is the urgency sort. This is a beginner cat only for someone who wanted a roommate that never sits still.

Somali questions

Is a Somali just a longhaired Abyssinian?

Yes. The breed began with longhaired kittens born to Abyssinians in the 1960s. The coat is the obvious difference. The ticked pattern, the energy, and several health tests are shared.

How long do Somali cats live?

Often 12 to 16 years. Kidney disease, including renal amyloidosis in this family, is the reason the long end of that range is not automatic.

Do Somalis need a lot of exercise?

Yes. Activity is very high. A Somali that cannot climb, chase, and inspect the whole house will invent a worse hobby. This is a poor cat for someone who wants a quiet lap ornament.

What health tests matter?

Ask for progressive retinal atrophy and polycystic kidney disease DNA results, and ask the breeder what they know about renal amyloidosis in the line. A yearly exam should include a look at kidneys and weight, not only vaccines.
