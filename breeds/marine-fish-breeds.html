Marine fishkeeping is one of the most rewarding aquarium hobbies, offering access to some of the most spectacularly colored creatures on Earth. From the iconic Clownfish to the majestic Angelfish, saltwater aquariums bring a piece of the ocean into your home. Our marine fish directory provides detailed profiles for each species, covering compatibility, diet, tank requirements, and health considerations.

Getting Started with Marine Fish

Marine tanks are unforgiving of the same mistakes that a freshwater tank would shrug off. Salinity drift, an overcrowded stocking plan, or a skipped cycle can wipe out months of work in a single weekend. The five checkpoints below are what separates marine builds that thrive from marine builds that crash:

  • Tank size: Start with at least 30 gallons for a fish-only system; 50+ gallons for a reef tank. Larger volumes are more stable and forgiving of mistakes
  • Water chemistry: Maintain salinity (1.023-1.025 SG), pH (8.1-8.4), and temperature (76-82°F). Invest in a reliable refractometer and test kit
  • Filtration: Live rock provides biological filtration, while protein skimmers remove organic waste before it breaks down. Both are essential for marine systems
  • Cycling: Cycle your tank for 4-6 weeks before adding fish. Use live rock and a bacterial starter to establish the nitrogen cycle
  • Compatibility: Research species compatibility carefully — many marine fish are territorial or aggressive toward similar-looking species
  • Quarantine: Always quarantine new fish for 2-4 weeks before adding them to your display tank to prevent disease introduction

Recommended Marine Fish for Beginners

Hardy species for new marine fishkeepers include Clownfish (captive-bred are ideal), Royal Gramma, Firefish Goby, and Pajama Cardinals. Clownfish & Damsels

Using the marine list

Saltwater animals on this page are not a harder version of a goldfish. Salinity, alkalinity, and a skimmer or refugium are part of the hobby before the fish color is. Beginner-friendly marine species are the ones that forgive a slowly matured tank: certain clownfish, some blennies, and similar hardy animals. A mandarin or a delicate wrasse is a way to kill an animal in a tank that is not growing pods yet.

Size matters twice. The fish has an adult length, and the tank has a gallon number that the shop will lowball. Use the size control where the quick facts include a size, and read the profile when the quick facts only show a habitat tag. Lifespan for marine fish is often longer than people budget for, which is why the lifespan filter exists next to search.

Quarantine is the unglamorous half of this hobby. A new fish who flashes or gasps can bring ich or flukes into a display you cannot easily medicate, especially if invertebrates share the water. Copper and many retail "treatments" kill shrimp, crabs, and corals. The surface gasping page covers the water tests that come before any bottle. If you cannot name your salinity and ammonia today, you are not ready to add another animal tomorrow.

Marine Husbandry Primer

A marine tank is a patience test before it is a stocking exercise. The nitrogen cycle takes four to six weeks to establish on live rock and a bacterial starter, and stocking one or two small fish into an uncycled system to "speed it up" is the most common avoidable early mistake. Ammonia and nitrite should both read zero for at least a week before the first animal goes in. Nitrate should stabilize at or below 20 ppm and stay there before stocking expands.

Quarantine is not optional. Every new fish should spend two to four weeks in a bare-bottom hospital tank with PVC hides before it ever sees the display. Tank transfer for marine ich, prazipro for flukes, and copper (carefully, at therapeutic concentration) for persistent parasites catch the three most common introductions. Skipping quarantine is how otherwise careful aquarists lose an entire system to velvet or ich in a single week, because copper cannot be used safely in a display with invertebrates or live rock.

Aggression and stocking order drive long-term compatibility more than species lists suggest. Add the most peaceful species first and the most aggressive last; rearrange live rock when introducing a potentially territorial addition to scramble existing territories. Tangs need swimming volume measured in feet, not gallons, and surgeonfish of overlapping body shape should not be mixed in tanks under 180 gallons. Angelfish and butterflyfish need to be evaluated on a species-by-species basis for reef safety — generalizations fail often enough that the only safe approach is to check the specific species before purchase.

Feeding quality and quantity drive water quality and fish health equally. Rotate frozen mysis, brine, cyclops, and marine pellets rather than feeding a single staple; use a turkey baster for target-feeding shy species and soak frozen food in a vitamin supplement once or twice a week. Overfeeding is the single largest preventable source of nuisance algae and elevated nitrate in an established system, so prefer multiple small feedings to one large one and keep the skimmer pulling consistent dark skimmate.
