Asleep on a leaf, on purpose

Red-eyed tree frogs, Agalychnis callidryas, are the leaf-sleepers of Central American rainforests, from Costa Rica through Panama. Adults are about 2 to 3 inches, green above, blue and yellow on the flanks, with red eyes they keep shut all day. People buy them for that postcard and then worry that the frog "never moves." It is nocturnal. The daytime statue is the healthy behavior. Waking it up to show the eyes stresses the animal you paid for.

The quick facts list 5 to 10 years, 72 to 82°F, humidity 60 to 80 percent, a tall 18 by 18 by 24 inch vivarium, and intermediate care. Treat the lifespan as often closer to the low end. These are not White's tree frogs in brighter paint.

Branches over water, insects after dark

They climb, drink droplets, and lay eggs over water in the wild. In a tank that means sturdy branches above a bowl, plants with broad leaves, and misting that raises humidity at night without leaving them soggy at noon. Crickets, fruit flies, and small roaches, dusted, are the diet. Offer them when the eyes are actually open.

Handling rubs off the protective slime and is how tiny toe injuries start. Move them with a cup if you must. Children can watch the tank. They should not carry the frog to school.

The expensive postcard

Animals often cost $40 to $75, and a proper tall setup $200 to $400. A frog that stays brown, refuses food for a string of nights, or develops raw skin is past the "maybe it's just sleeping" stage. ARAV clinics are the right call. The not-eating page and the checker help you decide if the morning is soon enough. Buy captive-bred. The wild version of this frog is not a souvenir.

Red-eyed tree frog questions

Are red-eyed tree frogs good for beginners?

The quick facts say intermediate. The photo sells a beginner fantasy. They are nocturnal, humidity-sensitive, and a poor handling animal.

How long do red-eyed tree frogs live?

The table says 5 to 10 years. Five to seven is the common captive span. Ten is a well-kept outlier, not the number to plan a child's childhood around.

Why are the eyes red?

Startle color. A sleeping frog tucks them away. When disturbed it flashes red eyes and bright flanks. It is a defense, not a sign you should wake it for guests.

What temperatures do they need?

About 72 to 82°F, with humidity around 60 to 80 percent and a climb over a water bowl. They are not a dry lizard and not a flooded dart-frog box.
