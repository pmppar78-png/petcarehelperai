A frog that pretends to be fruit

Tomato frogs are the round red frogs of Madagascar, genus Dyscophus. Females of the true tomato frog, Dyscophus antongilii, become the postcard: several inches of red. Males stay smaller and duskier. A lot of pet "tomato frogs" are actually Dyscophus guineti, the false tomato frog, which is a bit less intensely red and is the one more often exported. Care overlaps. The name on the invoice matters because antongilii is the species with the heavier trade limits. Ask before you pay.

The quick facts say 2 to 4 inches, 6 to 8 years, 65 to 80°F, humidity 70 to 80 percent, a 10-gallon terrestrial tank, beginner, and a sedentary temperament. They sit in a hole. That is the personality. People who need interaction should pick a different amphibian.

The glue is the defense

Grab a tomato frog and it may inflate and leak a white secretion that sticks to skin and is genuinely unpleasant. It is not a party trick. It stresses the frog and ruins the afternoon. Housing is a moist hide, a few inches of substrate they can back into, a shallow water dish, and insects. Crickets, earthworms, and dubia roaches, dusted, match the diet line. They are not strong swimmers, so the dish stays shallow.

One frog per tank is simpler. A large female and a small male are not automatically a happy pair, and two animals in a 10-gallon will argue over the one good burrow.

Six to eight quiet years

Cost is often $25 to $50 for the frog and $100 to $200 for the tank. A frog that stays on the surface, looks dry, or skips several meals needs a clinic rather than a brighter lamp. ARAV lists amphibian vets. The checker and the not-eating page are the tools while you wait for a call back. Beginner here means humidity and a feeding tong, not hands-on.

Tomato frog questions

Why is one tomato frog red and the other dull?

Adult females are larger and brighter. Males stay smaller and more orange or brown. A pair that looks like two species may be two sexes, or a true tomato frog beside a false tomato frog.

Which species are sold as tomato frogs?

Dyscophus antongilii is the true tomato frog and is the more regulated animal. Dyscophus guineti, sometimes called the false tomato frog, is common in the trade. Care is similar. The receipt should name which one you bought.

Can you hold a tomato frog?

No. Stress makes them ooze a white, glue-like secretion that is miserable to wash off and worse for the frog. Watch them in the tank.

How long do tomato frogs live?

They are a beginner frog for someone who wants a sit-and-wait animal, not a climber or a caller you interact with.
