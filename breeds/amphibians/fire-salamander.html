Yellow spots, cool dirt, no pool

Fire salamanders, Salamandra salamandra, are the black-and-yellow salamanders of European forests. Adults are about 6 to 10 inches, nocturnal, and slow. The pattern is a warning. Skin toxins are real, and a curious dog or a child who picks the animal up can get a painful introduction. They are a watching pet.

The quick facts say terrestrial, 55 to 68°F, 70 to 80 percent humidity, a 20-gallon tank, intermediate care, and 10 to 20 years. That temperature is the whole hobby. They overheat in ordinary summer rooms. Adults do not want a swimming aquarium. They want deep damp substrate, hides, and a water dish shallow enough that a salamander cannot become trapped underwater. Larvae are aquatic. An adult found swimming desperately is in trouble, not "going for a soak."

Worms, and a fungus worth knowing

Earthworms are the best staple, with crickets, slugs, and the occasional waxworm. Feed at dusk. They often learn the tongs. Do not offer a communal tank with newts from another continent. Bsal, Batrachochytrium salamandrivorans, has wiped out fire salamander populations in parts of Europe and is linked to Asian salamander trade. Quarantine anything new. Do not rinse tanks into the garden.

Some subspecies give birth to larvae, others to fully formed young. Breeding them is a specialist project with legal and disease consequences, not a fun extra.

Cost of keeping them cool

Animals often run $40 to $100, and a terrestrial setup $150 to $300, before you price a cool room. A salamander that stops eating, develops sores, or sprawls in the water dish needs a vet who understands amphibians, found through ARAV. The not-eating page and the checker are the first sort. Lifespan of 10 to 20 years is realistic when the summer problem is solved and a fantasy when the tank sits under a lamp.

Fire salamander questions

Do adult fire salamanders live in water?

No. Salamandra salamandra adults are terrestrial. A shallow water dish is right. A deep pool drowns them. Larvae are the aquatic stage.

Why is temperature such a big deal?

They want about 55 to 68°F. A reptile heat lamp is the wrong tool. A cool basement beats a tropical rack.

Are fire salamanders poisonous?

Yes. The black-and-yellow skin secretes toxins, including samandarin. Do not handle them for display, and do not let a dog investigate the tank.

What is Bsal and why mention it?

Batrachochytrium salamandrivorans is a fungus that has killed European fire salamanders in the wild. Quarantine new animals and do not mix them with imported Asian newts.
