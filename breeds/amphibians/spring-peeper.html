The peep is the point

Spring peepers are the thumbnail frogs with a dark X across the back. The scientific name is Pseudacris crucifer, and the range is eastern North American woodland, not a tropical frog room. Adults are about 0.75 to 1.5 inches. The quick-facts table lists a lifespan of 3 to 4 years, and that range is the honest one. Wild peepers usually live about three years. A cool terrarium might add a season. It does not turn them into a ten-year pet, and it does not make ownership a multi-decade project.

Cool air and tiny food

They want 55 to 72°F. A living room that sits at 75°F is already too warm. Humidity around 60 to 80 percent, a tall 10-gallon enclosure, thin branches or cork, and a water dish they can climb out of are the setup. Fruit flies, springtails, and pinhead crickets are the diet. Dusting calcium onto prey that small is awkward, so many keepers culture flies instead of buying one cup and hoping it lasts.

Care level is advanced for a reason. The frog is easy to lose in moss, easy to overheat, and easy to starve if the only food in the house is a full-sized cricket. This is a poor first amphibian.

They do call

Males peep. That rising note is the breeding call, and one male in a quiet apartment can still produce it when the tank is cool and wet. An earlier version of this page claimed amphibians communicate with posture rather than sound. That claim is wrong for this species. The call is as much the field mark as the X. Between calls they hide. Anyone who wanted a frog on the glass every evening will find a peeper disappointing.

Buying and warning signs

Look for captive-bred animals. Taking a chorus out of a local pond moves disease around and is illegal in many places. Fuzzy skin, a frog parked on the warm glass, or a male who stops calling after weeks of nightly noise are reasons to phone a clinic that sees amphibians. ARAV's clinic list is the practical directory. A peeper that vanishes from the food dish is easy to miss; the not-eating page and the urgency checker are the on-site tools. The table puts the animal near $5 to $15 and a basic setup near $100 to $200. The short life is the fact people skip when they only read the price.

Spring peeper questions

How long do spring peepers live?

About 3 to 4 years. That is a normal peeper lifespan, not a sign that care failed, and it is not a 10-year tree-frog figure copied onto the wrong animal.

Do spring peepers make noise?

Yes. Males give the high peeping call this frog is named for, especially on cool, damp evenings. They are not a silent species.

Are spring peepers beginner frogs?

No. They are an advanced frog because the tank must stay cool, the prey is tiny, and a brown frog the size of a fingernail disappears into the plants.

What temperature does a spring peeper need?

Keep the vivarium between 55 and 72°F. Ordinary apartment heat is what kills peepers that were sold as easy tree frogs.
