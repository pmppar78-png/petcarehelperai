The maroon clownfish is Amphiprion biaculeatus, still widely called Premnas, and it is the largest of the common clownfishes. Adults reach about 6 inches and live 7 years or more. A 6-inch maroon needs 30 gallons at the very least. Intermediate is the rating, and the bite is why. The diet is omnivore: pellets, frozen mysis, brine shrimp, and flakes. Maroon Clownfish Overview

The Maroon Clownfish is a medium (6 in) saltwater species originating from the Indo-Pacific. Known for being aggressive, territorial, this species is rated intermediate care level, making it well-suited for hobbyists with some marine aquarium experience. With a lifespan of 7+ years, this is a rewarding species that can be enjoyed for many years with proper care.

Largest and most aggressive clownfish species; females can reach 6+ inches and dominate entire tanks; gold stripe variety highly sought after; bonds fiercely with host anemone

They can host in an anemone and they do not require one. A bubble-tip is a project of its own, with light, flow, and a habit of wandering. A maroon will claim a rock or a corner just as readily and will defend that spot against fish that would have been acceptable tankmates for an ocellaris. Thirty gallons is the minimum for one fish or a known pair. It is small once the female is 6 inches and owns the aquascape. Gold-stripe maroons cost more inside the listed 20 to 80 dollar range. The stripe does not change the aggression or the adult size. Setup costs on the card run about 200 to 500 dollars.

Add them with a plan for the hierarchy. A large female and a smaller male can form a pair. Two similar adults often fight until one is dead. They will also charge new fish, shrimp, and the person who cleans the glass. Intermediate care means you accept a territorial 6-inch clown and you do not put your fingers in its cave. Feed pellets and mysis on a schedule so the aggression is not fueled only by hunger, and do not expect feeding to cancel it.

Omnivore: pellets, frozen mysis, brine shrimp, flakes is the feeding and behavior note for Maroon Clownfish. Temperament on the same card is Aggressive, Territorial.

Temperament & Compatibility

The Maroon Clownfish is described as aggressive, territorial and is considered reef safe, making it compatible with coral-dominated aquariums.

  • Compatible tankmates: Choose tankmates carefully. This species can be territorial and may harass more peaceful fish.

Temperature is 72 to 82°F, salinity 1.020 to 1.025, and pH 8.1 to 8.4. Nitrate under 10 ppm in a reef and under 20 ppm in a fish-only tank. Alkalinity 8 to 12 dKH, calcium 380 to 450 ppm, and magnesium 1250 to 1350 ppm are the reef targets beside a fish the card calls reef safe. Even a hardy maroon needs ammonia at 0 ppm and nitrite at 0 ppm. Hardiness is not the same trait as a mild temper. Maroons survive conditions that kill delicate fish, and they use that stamina to hold a territory you cannot argue with.

A lid and a cave they have chosen are the furniture. Do not keep them with an ocellaris and call it a clownfish pair. Different species under one host is a fight. The 7-year-or-more lifespan is a long time to keep a fish that bites, so choose it on purpose, in 30 gallons or more, with tankmates that can stay out of its hole. If you wanted a peaceful beginner clown, that is the ocellaris page and a different adult size.

Is Maroon Clownfish

Evaluating a Maroon Clownfish for your marine aquarium is less about enthusiasm and more about practical constraints.

May Be Great

  • An experienced clownfish keeper who wants one pair of Premnas biaculeatus and a host anemone only if the anemone is already thriving
  • Someone who accepts maroons bite hands and bully tankmates
  • An aquarist with a tank large enough that other fish can stay out of the territory

May Not Be

  • A first clownfish, a reef of shy gobies, or a keeper who wants to put a hand in the tank
  • Two pairs, or a maroon added to a tank that already has a clownfish pair
  • Anyone who buys the anemone and the fish on the same day

Premnas biaculeatus grows larger than an ocellaris clown and defends a territory. One fish or a matched pair needs about 30 gallons or more. They eat a marine pellet and mysis and do not need an anemone to feed. A second clown added later is often killed. Torn fins mean a fight. A dark fish breathing fast needs the water tested the same day.
