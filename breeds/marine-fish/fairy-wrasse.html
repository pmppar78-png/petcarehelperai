Fairy Wrasse Overview

The Fairy Wrasse is a small (3-5 in) saltwater species originating from the Indo-Pacific. Known for being peaceful, active swimmer, this species is rated intermediate care level, making it well-suited for hobbyists with some marine aquarium experience. With a lifespan of 5-8 years, this is a rewarding species that can be enjoyed for many years with proper care.

Males display incredibly vibrant coloring during courtship displays (nuptial flashing); numerous species available in the hobby; peaceful reef inhabitants

Fairy wrasses are the small, colorful Cirrhilabrus and their close allies, listed here as a group rather than one species. Adults run about 3 to 5 inches and live 5 to 8 years. Fairy wrasses need 55 gallons of open water above the rock. Intermediate care comes from the lid and the repeated small feedings. They are peaceful, active swimmers, and reef safe. The diet is carnivore: mysis, brine shrimp, copepods, and pellets. Cost depends on the species, roughly 30 to 100 dollars, with a setup around 300 to 500. A rare male is not a different care category. He is a more expensive fish with the same need to eat and the same need for a lid.

Fairy wrasses, genus Cirrhilabrus, are peaceful zooplanktivores. Keep one male. A second male of the same or a similar species is often harassed until it hides and starves. Females can share the tank if it is large and broken up by rock. They flash their fins at dusk and sleep in a mucus cocoon, sometimes buried in sand or tucked in rock. That cocoon is why a rough netting can injure them. They are a poor mix with hungry dottybacks, large wrasses, and hawkfish that treat a small colorful fish as food.

Hold a fairy wrasse at 72-78°F, specific gravity about 1.024 to 1.026, and pH 8.1-8.4, in a tank that has finished cycling. The overview on this page asks for 55 gallons because these fish need open swimming room above the rock, not a 20-gallon nano with a powerhead blasting the water. They eat from the water column. A bare new reef with no copepods and one feeding a day is how a thin fairy wrasse dies in the first month. Quarantine in a covered tank. Marine ich and flukes arrive with the fish, and a jump during a freshwater dip is a known way to lose one. Older template lines on this page say a minimum 30 gallons. The tank answer here is 55 gallons, and that is the number to use.

Temperament & Compatibility

The Fairy Wrasse is described as peaceful, active swimmer and is considered reef safe, making it compatible with coral-dominated aquariums.

  • Compatible tankmates: Other peaceful community fish of similar size. Avoid keeping with aggressive or highly territorial species.
  • Stocking density: Marine fish require more space per individual than freshwater fish. The 55 gallon minimum is for this species alone increase tank size for community setups.

Males flash at each other and at females, shooting color across the body when they display. That display needs open water above the rock, which is why 55 gallons of length matters more than a tall cube of the same volume. One male with females, or a single fish, is the peaceful version. Two adult males of the same species in 55 gallons will display until it becomes harassment. Know the sex and the species before you add a second.

They jump. A fairy wrasse can leave through a small feeding hole when the lights switch or when a male is chased. Cover the tank completely, including gaps around plumbing. They also need to eat small foods several times a day in a busy reef, because they are not the fish that wins a single feeding against a tang. Mysis, enriched brine, and pellets they recognize are the staple. Copepods on the rock are a supplement, not a plan, unless the tank is mature and the wrasse is one of the smaller species.

Temperature on this card is 72 to 78°F, slightly cooler at the top end than a general 82°F reef recipe. Hold the salt between 1.020 and 1.025 for these swimmers. The pH window for a fairy wrasse tank is 8.1 to 8.4. A reef should keep nitrate under 10 ppm, while a fish-only system can allow under 20 ppm. Ammonia and nitrite at 0 ppm, alkalinity at 8 to 12 dKH, calcium at 380 to 450 ppm, and magnesium at 1250 to 1350 ppm round out a tank that may also hold corals. Intermediate care is the lid, the feeding frequency, and the choice of one male.

Reef safe here means they do not graze coral the way an angel does. They may still eat tiny mobile invertebrates. A reef of SPS and LPS with a fairy wrasse is a standard combination. A tank built around a rare copepod-dependent fish may not want the extra hunter. The 5-to-8-year lifespan fits a covered 55-gallon tank and a fish that was eating before you bought it. A male that fades and hides is usually a second male, a missing lid, or a feeding plan that never reaches him.

Accounting for these specifics from day one saves the corrective rework that shows up when they are discovered later

Tank Setup & Requirements

Tank Specifications

  • Minimum size: 55 gallon. Larger is strongly recommended for parameter stability.
  • Sump systems recommended for tanks over 50 gallons.
  • Water flow: Moderate to strong flow using powerheads or wavemakers. Active swimmers appreciate strong linear flow.
  • Lighting: Full-spectrum reef lighting if keeping with corals. LED systems offer energy efficiency and controllable spectrum.
  • Aquascaping: Live rock providing caves, overhangs, and swim-through passages. Ensure open swimming space for active species.

Critical: Cycle Your Tank First

Quarantine a fairy wrasse in a lidded tank before it meets corals or other fish. Observe appetite. A fish that will not eat frozen mysis after a few days is not ready for the display. Treat flukes and external parasites in quarantine, not in the reef, and keep the quarantine tank covered the entire time.

Diet & Nutrition

The Fairy Wrasse is classified as carnivore. Proper nutrition is essential for maintaining health, coloration, and immune function.

  • Primary diet: Carnivore: mysis, brine shrimp, copepods, pellets.
  • Feeding frequency: 2-3 small feedings per day. Small frequent meals are better than one large feeding.
  • Variety: Rotate between different food types to ensure complete nutrition. Offer frozen mysis shrimp, brine shrimp, and high-quality pellets.
  • Avoid: Overfeeding is the most common feeding mistake.

Proper nutrition for Fairy Wrasse requires understanding not just what to feed, but how feeding strategies affect health, behavior, and water quality. A disciplined approach to feeding, where your Fairy Wrasse receives the right amount of appropriate food at consistent intervals, is one of the most impactful things you can do for both the fish and the overall aquarium ecosystem.

Diet variety is essential for optimal Fairy Wrasse health because no single commercial food provides the complete nutritional profile these marine fish require. Observing your Fairy Wrasse's response to different food types also provides useful health information, as reduced interest in normally preferred foods is often an early indicator of illness.

Grooming & Maintenance

  • Equipment maintenance: Clean protein skimmer cup 2-3 times weekly. Replace filter media monthly. Calibrate refractometer regularly.
  • Algae management: Magnetic algae scrapers for glass cleaning. Clean-up crew (snails, hermit crabs) helps control nuisance algae.

Is Fairy Wrasse Right for Your Aquarium?

A fairy wrasse fits an established reef with a lid, rockwork, and a keeper who already quarantines new fish. It does not fit a brand-new cube, an uncovered lagoon, or a tank whose only plan for ich is a bottle poured into the display. Plan the adult size, the vet, and the daily work for the fairy wrasse before it comes home.

Fairy Wrasse May Be Great For:

  • A keeper with a lidded, established reef and a quarantine tank, adding one male Cirrhilabrus and no second male
  • Someone who will feed small meaty foods several times a day and keep copepods in the rockwork
  • An aquarist who acclimates in a covered bucket, because fairy wrasses jump when startled

Fairy Wrasse May Not Be Ideal For:

  • A new tank still cycling, or a tank with no lid and open overflow teeth
  • A reef that already has a male fairy wrasse of a similar species
  • Anyone who nets fish in the open, because a fairy wrasse can jump out during capture and the body is soft

Buy captive-raised when you can find them, acclimate under a cover, and feed the day you see the fish hunting the water column. A fairy wrasse that spends the first week at the surface against the lid is telling you the cover, the flow, or the tankmates are wrong.
