Coral Beauty Angelfish Overview

The Coral Beauty Angelfish is a small (4 in) saltwater species originating from the Indo-Pacific. Known for being semi-aggressive, hardy dwarf angel, this species is rated intermediate care level, making it well-suited for hobbyists with some marine aquarium experience.

One of the hardiest dwarf angelfish; beautiful blue and orange coloration; occasional coral nipping makes reef compatibility unpredictable

Centropyge bispinosa, the coral beauty, is a dwarf angelfish of about 4 inches with a lifespan of 5 to 7 years. A coral beauty needs 70 gallons of mature rock to graze. Intermediate care is one angel and a reef you can stand to see sampled. It is a hardy dwarf angel by angel standards, semi-aggressive, and an omnivore that should get spirulina, mysis, and foods made for angelfish. The reef-safe line is with caution because it may nip corals. That caution is the correct confidence level. This is not a fish to call reef safe and then be surprised by a pecked LPS.

They graze film on the rocks. A mature 70-gallon reef with algae on the back surfaces suits that habit better than a sterile new tank full of dry rock. Grazing is also how they find coral polyps and clam mantles to sample. Some individuals never touch a coral. Some do it every night. You cannot see which one you bought until it is in your tank, so stock it only where a nipped coral is an acceptable loss. One coral beauty per tank. A second Centropyge in 70 gallons is a territorial fight more often than a pair.

Semi-aggressive shows up toward other angels, toward new fish, and sometimes toward shrimp. Add the angel after quieter fish have settled, or accept that a coral beauty already in residence will meet the next introduction with a charge. They are not maroons and they are not damsels, but the cute juvenile in the shop is still a dwarf angel with a territory. Seventy gallons gives that territory somewhere to be besides the entire aquascape.

From a water chemistry perspective, maintaining a saltwater aquarium for Coral Beauty Angelfish demands consistent attention to parameters including temperature (72-82°F), pH (8.1-8.4), and tank capacity (minimum 30 gallons). These parameters are not merely guidelines—they represent the range within which Coral Beauty Angelfish's metabolic processes, immune function, and reproductive behaviors operate optimally. Successful Coral Beauty Angelfish keeping therefore requires not just the right equipment, but a disciplined approach to monitoring and maintaining water quality over the long term.

Temperament & Compatibility

The Coral Beauty Angelfish is described as semi-aggressive, hardy dwarf angel and is considered reef safe with caution monitor closely in reef tanks as individual specimens may develop coral-nipping behavior.

  • Compatible tankmates: Semi-aggressive community fish of similar size. Introduce simultaneously when possible to reduce territorial aggression.
  • Stocking density: Marine fish require more space per individual than freshwater fish. The 70 gallon minimum is for this species alone increase tank size for community setups.

Feed angelfish preparations and spirulina so the film on the rocks is not the only meal. A hungry dwarf angel nips more. A fed one still might. Intermediate care is a 70-gallon tank, a single specimen, and a reef you are willing to police each evening for fresh white pecks on coral tissue. If the display cannot lose a polyp, choose a fish that does not carry this caution. The 5-to-7-year lifespan is plenty of time for a casual nipper to reshape a garden.

Color varies from deep blue and orange to paler fish, and color is not a behavior test. Watch the mouth, not the poster. A coral beauty that spends the day picking at rock film and prepared food is doing the job you can actually expect. One that settles onto a particular coral every afternoon should be moved to a fish-only system with live rock rather than debated with the reef.

Tank Setup & Requirements

The Coral Beauty care item most frequently postponed is the same one whose effects compound most steadily — it deserves a place on the current list, not the later list.

Tank Specifications

  • Minimum size: 70 gallon. Larger is strongly recommended for parameter stability.
  • Sump systems recommended for tanks over 50 gallons.
  • Water flow: Moderate to strong flow using powerheads or wavemakers. Provide areas of both higher and lower flow within the tank.
  • Lighting: Standard marine lighting adequate for fish-only systems.
  • Aquascaping: Live rock providing caves, overhangs, and swim-through passages. Ensure open swimming space for active species.

Diet & Nutrition

The Coral Beauty Angelfish is classified as omnivore. Proper nutrition is essential for maintaining health, coloration, and immune function.

  • Primary diet: Omnivore: spirulina, mysis, angelfish preparations.
  • Feeding frequency: 2-3 small feedings per day. Small frequent meals are better than one large feeding.
  • Variety: Rotate between different food types to ensure complete nutrition. Offer a mix of frozen foods, pellets, and algae-based foods.
  • Avoid: Overfeeding is the most common feeding mistake.

Proper nutrition for Coral Beauty Angelfish requires understanding not just what to feed, but how feeding strategies affect health, behavior, and water quality. A disciplined approach to feeding, where your Coral Beauty Angelfish receives the right amount of appropriate food at consistent intervals, is one of the most impactful things you can do for both the fish and the overall aquarium ecosystem.

Diet variety is essential for optimal Coral Beauty Angelfish health because no single commercial food provides the complete nutritional profile these marine fish require. Observing your Coral Beauty Angelfish's response to different food types also provides useful health information, as reduced interest in normally preferred foods is often an early indicator of illness.

Grooming & Maintenance

  • Equipment maintenance: Clean protein skimmer cup 2-3 times weekly. Replace filter media monthly. Calibrate refractometer regularly.
  • Algae management: Magnetic algae scrapers for glass cleaning. Clean-up crew (snails, hermit crabs) helps control nuisance algae.
  • Water testing: Test salinity, pH, ammonia, nitrite, and nitrate weekly. Test alkalinity, calcium, and magnesium for reef systems.
