Flame Hawkfish Overview

The Flame Hawkfish is a small (3-4 in) saltwater species originating from the Western Pacific. Known for being semi-aggressive, perching predator, this species is rated beginner care level, making it an excellent choice for aquarists new to saltwater keeping.

Perches on rocks and coral watching for prey; brilliant flame-red coloration; lacks swim bladder so sits on surfaces rather than hovering; charismatic personality

Temperament & Compatibility

The Flame Hawkfish is described as semi-aggressive, perching predator and is considered reef safe with caution monitor closely in reef tanks as individual specimens may develop coral-nipping behavior.

Hawkfish do not have a swim bladder that lets them hover the way a wrasse does. They perch, then launch. Give this one a stable rock pile with ledges at several heights, not a bare sand field. It will choose a favorite spot and defend the view. A second hawkfish in 30 gallons, flame or otherwise, usually means one fish gets the perch and the other gets harassed. Beginner care is one fish, meaty foods, and no ornamental shrimp you intend to keep.

Is Flame Hawkfish Right for Your Aquarium?

Evaluating a Flame Hawkfish for your marine aquarium is less about enthusiasm and more about practical constraints.

Flame Hawkfish May Be Great For:

  • A reef keeper who wants one Neocirrhites armatus and no decorative shrimp
  • Someone with rock perches, a lid, and fish too big to fit in its mouth
  • An aquarist who accepts it may nip clam mantles and small snails

Flame Hawkfish May Not Be Ideal For:

  • A shrimp reef or a tank of small gobies and firefish
  • A second flame hawkfish in anything but a very large tank
  • An uncovered aquarium

Whether a Flame Hawkfish is right for you depends on your specific setup, schedule, and commitment level.

Flame hawkfish, Neocirrhites armatus, perch on the rock and lack a swim bladder, so they hop along the rock and rest on their fins. They eat meaty foods and will swallow small shrimp and tiny fish. Give them a cave and a secure lid. They are poor neighbors for a cleanup crew made of ornamental shrimp.
