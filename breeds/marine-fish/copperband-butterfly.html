Copperband Butterflyfish Overview

The Copperband Butterflyfish is a medium (8 in) saltwater species originating from the Indo-Pacific. Known for being peaceful, finicky eater, this species is rated expert care level, making it appropriate only for experienced marine aquarists with well-established systems. With a lifespan of 5-10 years, this is a rewarding species that can be enjoyed for many years with proper care.

Prized for eating pest Aiptasia anemones; distinctive copper-orange vertical bands on silver body with false eyespot near tail; extremely difficult to get feeding in captivity

Chelmon rostratus, the copperband butterflyfish, is a specialized Indo-Pacific feeder with a long snout, an adult size around 8 inches, and a lifespan of 5 to 10 years. An 8-inch copperband needs 75 gallons of rock to forage across. Expert is the rating because so many of them never eat. Temperament is peaceful, and the feeding line is the risk: they are finicky. Many never take a normal aquarium diet. The card lists Aiptasia, mysis, frozen foods, and clams, and it notes that the fish requires an established tank with live food sources. A copperband that is not eating is not a fish you train with optimism. It is a fish that starves.

The only honest recommendation is a captive-bred animal or a fish that is already eating in front of you. Wild copperbands often refuse pellets, refuse mysis, and pick at rockwork until the body thins behind the head. If the shop cannot show this individual taking frozen food, walk away. Buying one to clear Aiptasia, then discovering it will not eat at all, solves neither the anemones nor the hunger. Aiptasia on the diet list is a bonus for a fish that is already feeding, not a job description for a new arrival.

From a water chemistry perspective, maintaining a saltwater aquarium for Copperband Butterflyfish demands consistent attention to parameters including temperature (72-82°F), pH (8.1-8.4), and tank capacity (minimum 30 gallons). These parameters are not merely guidelines—they represent the range within which Copperband Butterflyfish's metabolic processes, immune function, and reproductive behaviors operate optimally. Successful Copperband Butterflyfish keeping therefore requires not just the right equipment, but a disciplined approach to monitoring and maintaining water quality over the long term.

Temperament & Compatibility

The Copperband Butterflyfish is described as peaceful, finicky eater and is considered reef safe with caution monitor closely in reef tanks as individual specimens may develop coral-nipping behavior.

  • Compatible tankmates: Other peaceful community fish of similar size. Avoid keeping with aggressive or highly territorial species.
  • Stocking density: Marine fish require more space per individual than freshwater fish. The 75 gallon minimum is for this species alone increase tank size for community setups.

They nip coral and anemones. The card's reef-safe line is caution, specifically clam mantles, and the behavior extends to coral tissue and to ornamental anemones. A reef built around expensive LPS and a tridacna clam is the wrong place to experiment. A large fish-only tank with live rock, or a reef you are willing to see sampled, is the honest layout. Peaceful refers to other fish. It does not refer to sessile invertebrates.

Seventy-five gallons is the minimum for an 8-inch fish that forages all day across the rock. For a copperband that is eating, hold 72 to 82°F, salinity 1.020 to 1.025, and pH 8.1 to 8.4. Nitrate under 10 ppm on a reef and under 20 ppm in a fish-only system, with alkalinity 8 to 12 dKH, calcium 380 to 450 ppm, and magnesium 1250 to 1350 ppm when corals are present. Ammonia and nitrite at 0 ppm are part of expert care because a stressed, new copperband stops eating at the first swing. The listed cost is about 30 to 60 dollars, which is not enough money to justify a fish that may never feed.

Give a confirmed eater several small offerings of mysis, clam, and other frozen foods through the day. A single sprinkle at lights-on is easy to miss for a slow, snout-feeding animal. House it with calm fish. Damsels and aggressive tangs will keep it off the rocks and the weight will fall off. Expert means the tank was mature before the fish arrived, the fish was already eating, and you are prepared to rehome it or return it at the first week of refusal rather than waiting for a rescue feeding that does not come.

Tank Specifications

  • Minimum size: 75 gallon. Larger is strongly recommended for parameter stability.
  • Sump systems recommended for tanks over 50 gallons.
  • Water flow: Moderate to strong flow using powerheads or wavemakers. Provide areas of both higher and lower flow within the tank.
  • Lighting: Standard marine lighting adequate for fish-only systems.
  • Aquascaping: Live rock providing caves, overhangs, and swim-through passages. Ensure open swimming space for active species.

Diet & Nutrition

The Copperband Butterflyfish is classified as carnivore. Proper nutrition is essential for maintaining health, coloration, and immune function.

  • Primary diet: Carnivore: Aiptasia, mysis, frozen foods, clams.
  • Feeding frequency: 2-3 small feedings per day. Small frequent meals are better than one large feeding.
  • Variety: Rotate between different food types to ensure complete nutrition. Offer frozen mysis shrimp, brine shrimp, and high-quality pellets.
  • Avoid: Overfeeding is the most common feeding mistake.

Proper nutrition for Copperband Butterflyfish requires understanding not just what to feed, but how feeding strategies affect health, behavior, and water quality. A disciplined approach to feeding, where your Copperband Butterflyfish receives the right amount of appropriate food at consistent intervals, is one of the most impactful things you can do for both the fish and the overall aquarium ecosystem.

Diet variety is essential for optimal Copperband Butterflyfish health because no single commercial food provides the complete nutritional profile these marine fish require. Observing your Copperband Butterflyfish's response to different food types also provides useful health information, as reduced interest in normally preferred foods is often an early indicator of illness.

Copperband Butterflyfish May Not Be Ideal For:
