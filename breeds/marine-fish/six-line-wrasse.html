Six-Line Wrasse Overview

The Six-Line Wrasse is a small (3 in) saltwater species originating from the Indo-Pacific. Known for being semi-aggressive, active, this species is rated beginner care level, making it an excellent choice for aquarists new to saltwater keeping.

Natural pest controller eating flatworms, pyramidellid snails, and bristleworms; six distinctive horizontal lines; can become territorial in smaller tanks

Pseudocheilinus hexataenia is a small Indo-Pacific wrasse, about 3 inches, with a lifespan of 4 to 6 years. Thirty gallons is as small as this territorial wrasse should go. The card rates them beginner, which underplays how they treat a small tank. The diet is carnivore: mysis, brine shrimp, pellets, and a useful habit of hunting flatworms. They are active all day and semi-aggressive about the whole footprint. A six-line eats flatworms and other pests, and it also eats ornamental shrimp. In a small tank the territorial side is the one you will see every day.

Carnivore: mysis, brine shrimp, pellets, hunts flatworms is the feeding and behavior note for Six-Line Wrasse.

Temperament & Compatibility

The Six-Line Wrasse is described as semi-aggressive, active and is considered reef safe, making it compatible with coral-dominated aquariums.

Thirty gallons is enough for one 3-inch wrasse and small enough that the fish treats the whole box as a territory. It will chase new additions, harass a peaceful goby, and pick at a cleaner shrimp until the shrimp is gone. Beginner care works when the livestock plan includes that behavior: pests are the target, ornamental shrimp are not present, and the other fish are not timid cave dwellers. A six-line in a 30-gallon reef with a firefish and a sexy shrimp is three projects that cancel each other.

They are active through the day and they jump. Fit a lid, because a six-line will use a feeding slot as an exit. A six-line does not need a deep sand bed the way a sand-diving Halichoeres does, but it does need rockwork to hunt through. Feed mysis and pellets so the flatworm work is not the entire diet. A tank with no pests and no feedings will push the wrasse onto whatever small invertebrate remains. Reef safe on the card means corals are generally left alone. It does not mean the reef's shrimp are safe, and it does not mean a second six-line will be tolerated.

Temperature is 72 to 78°F, salinity 1.020 to 1.025, pH 8.1 to 8.4. Nitrate under 10 ppm is the reef target that matches how people usually keep them, and under 20 ppm is the fish-only allowance. Alkalinity of 8 to 12 dKH, calcium of 380 to 450 ppm, and magnesium of 1250 to 1350 ppm sit with the corals. Ammonia and nitrite at 0 ppm are assumed by the beginner label and still have to be true. Expect to pay about 15 to 30 dollars for the wrasse and about 200 to 400 for the setup. Cheap and useful against flatworms is how the fish gets added to tanks that are too small for its temper.

One wrasse, a lid, meaty food, and no prized shrimp is the 30-gallon version that matches the card. The 4-to-6-year lifespan is reasonable for that layout. A six-line that spends the day pinning a goby in a corner is not being active and charming. It is being semi-aggressive in a tank with nowhere else to put the aggression. Move the wrasse, or do not combine it with the fish it is cornering. Pest control is a side effect of a predator, not a personality that stays gentle because the flatworms are gone.
