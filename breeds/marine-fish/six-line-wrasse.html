Six-Line Wrasse Overview

The Six-Line Wrasse is a small (3 in) saltwater species originating from the Indo-Pacific. Known for being semi-aggressive, active, this species is rated beginner care level, making it an excellent choice for aquarists new to saltwater keeping. With a lifespan of 4-6 years, this is a rewarding species that can be enjoyed for many years with proper care.

Natural pest controller eating flatworms, pyramidellid snails, and bristleworms; six distinctive horizontal lines; can become territorial in smaller tanks

Pseudocheilinus hexataenia is a small Indo-Pacific wrasse, about 3 inches, with a lifespan of 4 to 6 years. Thirty gallons is as small as this territorial wrasse should go. Temperament & Compatibility

The Six-Line Wrasse is described as semi-aggressive, active and is considered reef safe, making it compatible with coral-dominated aquariums.

  • Compatible tankmates: Semi-aggressive community fish of similar size. Introduce simultaneously when possible to reduce territorial aggression.
  • Stocking density: Marine fish require more space per individual than freshwater fish.

Thirty gallons is enough for one 3-inch wrasse and small enough that the fish treats the whole box as a territory. It will chase new additions, harass a peaceful goby, and pick at a cleaner shrimp until the shrimp is gone. Beginner care works when the livestock plan includes that behavior: pests are the target, ornamental shrimp are not present, and the other fish are not timid cave dwellers. A six-line in a 30-gallon reef with a firefish and a sexy shrimp is three projects that cancel each other.

Diet & Nutrition

The Six-Line Wrasse is classified as carnivore. Proper nutrition is essential for maintaining health, coloration, and immune function.

  • Primary diet: Carnivore: mysis, brine shrimp, pellets, hunts flatworms.
  • Feeding frequency: 2-3 small feedings per day. Small frequent meals are better than one large feeding.
  • Variety: Rotate between different food types to ensure complete nutrition. Offer frozen mysis shrimp, brine shrimp, and high-quality pellets.
  • Avoid: Overfeeding is the most common feeding mistake.

Proper nutrition for Six-Line Wrasse requires understanding not just what to feed, but how feeding strategies affect health, behavior, and water quality. A disciplined approach to feeding, where your Six-Line Wrasse receives the right amount of appropriate food at consistent intervals, is one of the most impactful things you can do for both the fish and the overall aquarium ecosystem.

Diet variety is essential for optimal Six-Line Wrasse health because no single commercial food provides the complete nutritional profile these marine fish require. Observing your Six-Line Wrasse's response to different food types also provides useful health information, as reduced interest in normally preferred foods is often an early indicator of illness.

The foundation of Six Line Wrasse health management is water quality — full stop. Regular testing and diligent maintenance are your best tools.

Grooming & Maintenance

  • Equipment maintenance: Clean protein skimmer cup 2-3 times weekly. Replace filter media monthly. Calibrate refractometer regularly.
  • Algae management: Magnetic algae scrapers for glass cleaning. Clean-up crew (snails, hermit crabs) helps control nuisance algae.
  • Water testing: Test salinity, pH, ammonia, nitrite, and nitrate weekly. Test alkalinity, calcium, and magnesium for reef systems.
