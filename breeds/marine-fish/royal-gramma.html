Royal Gramma Overview

The Royal Gramma is a small (3 in) saltwater species originating from the Western Atlantic, Caribbean. Known for being peaceful to semi-aggressive, cave dweller, this species is rated beginner care level, making it an excellent choice for aquarists new to saltwater keeping. With a lifespan of 5-8 years, this is a rewarding species that can be enjoyed for many years with proper care.

Stunning bicolor purple/yellow coloration; often swims upside-down under ledges; excellent beginner saltwater fish; peaceful community member

Gramma loreto, the royal gramma, is a small western Atlantic and Caribbean fish of about 3 inches, with a lifespan of 5 to 8 years. One cave-dwelling gramma starts in 30 gallons. Beginner fits a single fish that already has a hole to guard. The diet is carnivore: mysis, brine shrimp, pellets, and copepods. Temperament runs from peaceful to semi-aggressive, and the habit is a cave dweller. They do not pick at coral tissue. The price is about 20 to 40 dollars and the setup about 200 to 400. It is not a royal dottyback. A dottyback is a Pseudochromis with a different shape, a different attitude, and a different family. The gramma is the purple-and-yellow fish that backs into a hole and watches the room.

From a water chemistry perspective, maintaining a saltwater aquarium for Royal Gramma demands consistent attention to parameters including temperature (72-82°F), pH (8.1-8.4), and tank capacity (minimum 30 gallons). These parameters are not merely guidelines—they represent the range within which Royal Gramma's metabolic processes, immune function, and reproductive behaviors operate optimally. Successful Royal Gramma keeping therefore requires not just the right equipment, but a disciplined approach to monitoring and maintaining water quality over the long term.

Temperament & Compatibility

The Royal Gramma is described as peaceful to semi-aggressive, cave dweller and is considered reef safe, making it compatible with coral-dominated aquariums.

  • Compatible tankmates: Other peaceful community fish of similar size. Avoid keeping with aggressive or highly territorial species.
  • Stocking density: Marine fish require more space per individual than freshwater fish.

Caves are the requirement, not a decoration. A royal gramma that has a tight recess in the rock will hover at the entrance and come out to feed. A royal gramma in a tank of smooth, hole-free rock will hide behind equipment and fade. Build at least one cave it can defend before you buy the fish. Two grammas in 30 gallons will fight. They can be kept as a single fish, or as a known pair in a larger system, and two strangers will damage each other even though the species looks mild in a shop photo. Peaceful to semi-aggressive is that split: mild toward other kinds of fish, intolerant of a second gramma in the same small territory.

They are reef safe and they are a poor match for a tank that depends on a dense copepod population, because copepods are on the menu. In an ordinary reef they take mysis and pellets and the pods are extra. Feed those prepared foods to the cave mouth. A gramma will not cross the tank to compete with a tang, and it will slowly thin if every meal is a scramble in the open water. Beginner care is a 30-gallon tank that already has the cave, stable salt, and one fish.

Gramma loreto does well at 72 to 82°F with salinity held at 1.020 to 1.025. Test pH and keep the cave's water between 8.1 and 8.4. Reef nitrate should stay under 10 ppm. Fish-only water can sit under 20 ppm. Alkalinity at 8 to 12 dKH, calcium at 380 to 450 ppm, and magnesium at 1250 to 1350 ppm are the usual reef minerals beside a fish that leaves corals alone. Ammonia and nitrite at 0 ppm should be old news before a cave dweller is asked to settle. They can jump during an argument or a sloppy water change, so a lid is cheap insurance.

The 5-to-8-year lifespan fits one gramma in a cave it trusts. It does not fit two fish forced to share a 30-gallon hole. If the seller calls the fish a royal dottyback, stop and look at the body. A gramma is purple in front and yellow behind, and it is the fish this card describes. Stock it once, feed the cave, and do not add a second to make the color symmetrical.

Tank Setup & Requirements

People often underestimate how much this piece of a Royal Gramma's routine influences later health outcomes.

Tank Specifications

  • Minimum size: 30 gallon. Larger is strongly recommended for parameter stability.
  • Sump systems recommended for tanks over 50 gallons.
  • Water flow: Moderate to strong flow using powerheads or wavemakers. Provide areas of both higher and lower flow within the tank.
  • Lighting: Full-spectrum reef lighting if keeping with corals. LED systems offer energy efficiency and controllable spectrum.
  • Aquascaping: Live rock providing caves, overhangs, and swim-through passages. Ensure open swimming space for active species.

Diet & Nutrition

The Royal Gramma is classified as carnivore. Proper nutrition is essential for maintaining health, coloration, and immune function.

  • Primary diet: Carnivore: mysis, brine shrimp, pellets, copepods.
  • Feeding frequency: 2-3 small feedings per day. Small frequent meals are better than one large feeding.
  • Variety: Rotate between different food types to ensure complete nutrition. Offer frozen mysis shrimp, brine shrimp, and high-quality pellets.
  • Avoid: Overfeeding is the most common feeding mistake.

Proper nutrition for Royal Gramma requires understanding not just what to feed, but how feeding strategies affect health, behavior, and water quality. A disciplined approach to feeding, where your Royal Gramma receives the right amount of appropriate food at consistent intervals, is one of the most impactful things you can do for both the fish and the overall aquarium ecosystem.

Diet variety is essential for optimal Royal Gramma health because no single commercial food provides the complete nutritional profile these marine fish require. Observing your Royal Gramma's response to different food types also provides useful health information, as reduced interest in normally preferred foods is often an early indicator of illness.

Grooming & Maintenance

  • Equipment maintenance: Clean protein skimmer cup 2-3 times weekly. Replace filter media monthly. Calibrate refractometer regularly.
