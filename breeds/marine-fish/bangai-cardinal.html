Bangai Cardinalfish Overview

The Bangai Cardinalfish is a small (3 in) saltwater species originating from the Banggai Islands, Indonesia (endemic). Known for being peaceful, slow-moving, schooling, this species is rated beginner care level, making it an excellent choice for aquarists new to saltwater keeping.

Critically endangered in the wild; males mouthbrood eggs for 3 weeks without eating; distinctive silver body with black vertical bars and elongated fins; captive breeding has helped reduce wild collection pressure

Banggai cardinalfish, Pterapogon kauderni, are small marine cardinalfish from a tiny home range in the Banggai Islands of Indonesia. A slow, hovering school can use 20 gallons if nobody bullies the food. They are peaceful, slow-moving, and schooling, rated beginner, and they are reef safe. The honest buying decision sits next to those easy numbers: the wild population is endangered, and the responsible fish is captive-bred. The card prices captive-bred animals around 15 to 30 dollars, against a setup of about 200 to 400 dollars.

They are mouthbrooders. The male holds the eggs and then the young in his mouth, and a brooding male looks as if he has given up on food. He has not become ill by default. He is carrying a clutch, and he should not be netted, mixed into a crowded dealer tank, or starved of cover while he does it. Captive-bred pairs in a quiet 20-gallon tank with rockwork will sometimes raise young. Wild-caught adults from a declining endemic range are the fish this page is asking you not to treat as a disposable school.

Carnivore: mysis, brine shrimp, small pellets is the feeding and behavior note for Bangai Cardinalfish. Temperament on the same card is Peaceful, Slow-moving, Schooling. .

Temperament & Compatibility

The Bangai Cardinalfish is described as peaceful, slow-moving, schooling and is considered reef safe, making it compatible with coral-dominated aquariums.

Slow eaters get bullied. Faster tankmates, including many damsels and wrasses, take mysis and pellets before a Banggai has left its hovering spot among the rocks. Feed carnivore foods they can actually catch: mysis, brine shrimp, and small pellets, placed near the group after pushier fish have been fed or in a tank where the cardinals are the main event. A school in 20 gallons works better than one shy fish. They are not active swimmers that need a long sprint lane. They need places to hover without being chased off the food.

Saltwater on this card is 72 to 82°F and a specific gravity of 1.020 to 1.025. pH sits at 8.1 to 8.4. Nitrate under 10 ppm is the reef target, with a looser under-20 ppm figure for a fish-only system. Alkalinity of 8 to 12 dKH, calcium of 380 to 450 ppm, and magnesium of 1250 to 1350 ppm are the mineral background if corals share the tank. Ammonia and nitrite have nowhere to hide at anything above 0 ppm. A new 20-gallon aquarium that has not finished cycling will kill a mouthbrooding cardinal faster than it kills a damsel.

Beginner care is fair for a captive-bred group that is already eating and is not housed with bullies. It is a poor description of a wild Banggai added to a brand-new reef to fill an empty corner. Give them 20 gallons, caves that let a male brood without being harassed, and food that reaches a slow fish. The 5-to-7-year lifespan is realistic for that setup. A pale, thin cardinal in a tank of fast eaters is not a fish that needs a new supplement. It needs a quieter feeding plan, or a different tank.
