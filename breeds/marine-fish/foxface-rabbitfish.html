Foxface Rabbitfish Overview

The Foxface Rabbitfish is a medium (8-10 in) saltwater species originating from the Western Pacific. Known for being peaceful, algae grazer, this species is rated intermediate care level, making it well-suited for hobbyists with some marine aquarium experience.

Excellent algae control; venomous dorsal spines cause intense pain (handle with extreme caution); can rapidly change color when stressed from bright yellow to mottled brown

Foxface Rabbitfish baseline welfare rests on three habits: stable chemistry, measured feeding, and disciplined quarantine of new arrivals; these factors drive outcomes more than brand-name products.

Handle a foxface with a container, not a loose net and a squeeze. Do not put your hand into a cave it has entered. The venom is a defense, and a calm fish in the open is not a tame fish when it is cornered. Tell anyone who maintains the tank. Intermediate care includes that respect, plus a 75-gallon swimming tank. They are active grazers and they need length. A tall tank that is short from front to back turns an 8-inch rabbitfish into a pacing animal.

They eat hair algae, and they also eat some coral. Nori on a clip, spirulina, algae pellets, and blanched vegetables should be in the tank every day so the soft corals are not the salad. The caution is real. A foxface that ignores LPS for months can still strip a leather or a zoanthid patch when it is hungry or when the nori runs out. Peaceful applies to fish. It does not apply to every sessile invertebrate, and it does not apply to your hand.

From a water chemistry perspective, maintaining a saltwater aquarium for Foxface Rabbitfish demands consistent attention to parameters including temperature (72-82°F), pH (8.1-8.4), and tank capacity (minimum 75 gallons). These parameters describe the working range for this fish, and they bound the conditions within which Foxface Rabbitfish's metabolic processes, immune function, and reproductive behaviors operate optimally. Successful Foxface Rabbitfish keeping therefore requires not just the right equipment, but a disciplined approach to monitoring and maintaining water quality over the long term.

Temperament & Compatibility

The Foxface Rabbitfish is described as peaceful, algae grazer and is considered reef safe with caution monitor closely in reef tanks as individual specimens may develop coral-nipping behavior.

  • Compatible tankmates: Other peaceful community fish of similar size. Avoid keeping with aggressive or highly territorial species.
  • Stocking density: Marine fish require more space per individual than freshwater fish. The 75 gallon minimum is for this species alone increase tank size for community setups.

Keep the swimming tank at 72 to 82°F, salinity 1.020 to 1.025, and pH 8.1 to 8.4. Nitrate under 10 ppm on a reef and under 20 ppm in a fish-only system. Alkalinity 8 to 12 dKH, calcium 380 to 450 ppm, and magnesium 1250 to 1350 ppm are the reef numbers you are spending effort on if corals are present to be nipped. Ammonia and nitrite at 0 ppm matter because a rabbitfish eats a large volume of greens and produces the waste of a herbivore that size. One fish per tank is the usual limit unless the system is huge. They can squabble with their own kind in 75 gallons.

Feed the vegetables even after the hair algae is gone. A foxface that cleaned the rocks and then wasted away was never given a replacement diet. The 5-to-7-year lifespan fits a 75-gallon tank, daily herbivore food, and a keeper who does not learn about the spines during a panicked netting. If the reef cannot lose a soft coral, this is a grazer for a different system. The venom does not fade because the fish is useful.

Tank Specifications

  • Minimum size: 75 gallon. Larger is strongly recommended for parameter stability.
  • Sump systems recommended for tanks over 50 gallons.
  • Water flow: Moderate to strong flow using powerheads or wavemakers. Provide areas of both higher and lower flow within the tank.
  • Lighting: Standard marine lighting adequate for fish-only systems.
  • Aquascaping: Live rock providing caves, overhangs, and swim-through passages. Ensure open swimming space for active species.

Diet & Nutrition

The Foxface Rabbitfish is classified as herbivore. Proper nutrition is essential for maintaining health, coloration, and immune function.

  • Primary diet: Herbivore: nori, algae, spirulina, blanched vegetables.
  • Feeding frequency: 2-3 small feedings per day. Small frequent meals are better than one large feeding.
  • Variety: Rotate between different food types to ensure complete nutrition. Offer nori sheets, spirulina-enriched foods, and blanched vegetables.
  • Avoid: Overfeeding is the most common feeding mistake.

Proper nutrition for Foxface Rabbitfish requires understanding not just what to feed, but how feeding strategies affect health, behavior, and water quality. A disciplined approach to feeding, where your Foxface Rabbitfish receives the right amount of appropriate food at consistent intervals, is one of the most impactful things you can do for both the fish and the overall aquarium ecosystem.

Diet variety is essential for optimal Foxface Rabbitfish health because no single commercial food provides the complete nutritional profile these marine fish require. Observing your Foxface Rabbitfish's response to different food types also provides useful health information, as reduced interest in normally preferred foods is often an early indicator of illness.

Grooming & Maintenance

  • Equipment maintenance: Clean protein skimmer cup 2-3 times weekly. Replace filter media monthly. Calibrate refractometer regularly.
