Kole Tang (Yellow-Eye Tang) Overview

The Kole Tang (Yellow-Eye Tang) is a medium (7 in) saltwater species originating from the Hawaii. Known for being peaceful, active algae grazer, this species is rated intermediate care level, making it well-suited for hobbyists with some marine aquarium experience.

One of the best algae-eating tangs; unique bristle-like teeth scrape film algae from glass and rock; more peaceful than many tang species; distinctive yellow eye ring

The kole tang, Ctenochaetus strigosus, is a Hawaiian bristletooth tang of about 7 inches with a lifespan of 5 to 7 years. A single kole needs 75 gallons of swimming length. Intermediate is a fair rating for one bristletooth with a daily nori clip. It is a peaceful, active algae grazer, reef safe on this card, and a herbivore that should get nori, spirulina, algae, and the film it is built to comb. The price runs about 50 to 120 dollars, with a setup around 300 to 500. The mouth is the detail that separates it from a yellow tang. A kole has bristle-like teeth for brushing film off rock. It does not carry a scalpel at the base of the tail in the same way a surgeonfish such as a yellow tang does. It still has a caudal spine. Treat the tail with the same caution you would give any tang when you net it.

One tang per tank unless the tank is huge. A kole in 75 gallons owns the grazing circuit. A second tang, kole or otherwise, in that footprint is a chase that ends with torn fins and a fish that will not come out to eat. Seventy-five gallons is the minimum for a single 7-inch grazer, not a starter size for a tang collection. They swim more than they perch. Length matters. A tall tank without a lap to swim will show as a fish that only circles the heater.

Temperament on the same card is Peaceful, Active algae grazer. Reef safe is listed as Yes. Ammonia stays at 0 ppm and nitrite stays at 0 ppm in that 75 gallon system.
