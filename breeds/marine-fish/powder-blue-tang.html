Powder Blue Tang Overview

The Powder Blue Tang is a medium (9 in) saltwater species originating from the Indian Ocean. Known for being semi-aggressive, active, this species is rated expert care level, making it appropriate only for experienced marine aquarists with well-established systems. With a lifespan of 10+ years, this is a rewarding species that can be enjoyed for many years with proper care.

One of the most beautiful tangs with powder blue body, black face mask, and yellow dorsal fin; notoriously sensitive to water quality and disease

Ich finds this species easily. A powder blue that skipped quarantine, came from a crowded system, or went into a tank that was still maturing is a common way to watch white spots spread across a dark blue body. Expert care starts before the fish is in the display: a quarantine period, stable salinity, and no shortcuts because the fish was expensive and looked fine in the bag. This tang is listed cooler than many reef fish, at 72 to 78°F. A hot, swinging reef at the edge of that band adds stress to a fish that already carries this risk. Salinity should stay inside 1.020 to 1.025 without the large fresh-water top-off mistakes that drop it overnight.

Aggression is the other half. One powder blue in 125 gallons can work with dissimilar fish. A second tang in that tank usually cannot. They are semi-aggressive toward other surgeons and they will also harass fish that graze the same rocks. Add the tang last only if the other fish are robust, or give it the tank and choose tankmates that can take a chase. The peaceful yellow tang you might be picturing is not this species. Use the 125-gallon floor and assume the temper is part of the purchase.

Herbivore: nori, spirulina, algae sheets, mysis shrimp is the feeding and behavior note for Powder Blue Tang.

Temperament & Compatibility

The Powder Blue Tang is described as semi-aggressive, active and is considered reef safe, making it compatible with coral-dominated aquariums.

Feed herbivore foods all day. Nori on a clip, spirulina, and algae sheets, with mysis as the meaty extra the card includes, keep a 9-inch tang from living only on the film it can strip off the glass. They are active. A 125-gallon tank that is mostly a tall column without a swimming lap will show as constant pacing. pH at 8.1 to 8.4, ammonia at 0 ppm, nitrite at 0 ppm, and nitrate under 10 ppm on a reef or under 20 ppm if the system is fish-only are the water targets. Alkalinity 8 to 12 dKH, calcium 380 to 450 ppm, and magnesium 1250 to 1350 ppm support corals beside a fish the card rates reef safe. Reef safe does not mean tang-safe. The caudal spine is a weapon and a netting hazard.

The 10-year-or-more lifespan is why the tank has to be right at the start. A powder blue does not stay a 4-inch juvenile, and it does not become a hepatus if you wished for the other blue fish. Expert means quarantine, 125 gallons, one surgeonfish, constant algae-based food, and salinity that does not swing. Anything less is how a striking Indian Ocean tang becomes an ich outbreak with a short life.

Buy the fish for the adult. A thin powder blue in a shop with clamped fins is already showing you the sensitivity. A fat one in a clean system, eating nori, is the individual worth a 125-gallon commitment. Do not add it to prove a new tank is ready. This is one of the last fish a large reef receives, not the first.
