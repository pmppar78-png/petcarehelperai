Blue Damselfish Overview

The Blue Damselfish is a small (3 in) saltwater species originating from the Indo-Pacific. Known for being semi-aggressive to aggressive, this species is rated beginner care level, making it an excellent choice for aquarists new to saltwater keeping. With a lifespan of 5-6 years, this is a rewarding species that can be enjoyed for many years with proper care.

Often recommended as first saltwater fish due to extreme hardiness; brilliant blue coloration; can become very territorial and aggressive as they mature, especially in smaller tanks

Chrysiptera cyanea, the blue damselfish, is the cheap bright fish that convinces people a marine tank is easy. Adults are about 3 inches and live 5 to 6 years. House a blue damsel in 30 gallons or larger. The printed care level is beginner, which describes hardiness rather than manners. The diet is omnivore, flakes and pellets and frozen foods, and the fish is reef safe. Temperament is where the card stops being cute: semi-aggressive to aggressive. A juvenile that dashes around a dealer tank becomes a bully that owns the rockwork and meets every new fish at the glass.

They are hardy, which is why they are stocked first and why that is often a mistake. A blue damsel in a cycling 30-gallon tank may live through ammonia that would kill a cardinal or a wrasse, and it will then spend years attacking the fish you actually wanted. Add it last if you add it at all, into a tank that already has its quieter residents established, and give it enough rock that it can hold a territory without patrolling the entire footprint. One damsel is plenty. A group of Chrysiptera in 30 gallons is a series of fights.

Is Blue Damselfish Right for Your Aquarium?

Fit between a Blue Damsel and a household is usually decided by three questions: Can the environment be sustained at the required stability? Can the daily care cadence be maintained year-round, including during travel and busy periods? Is the household prepared for the typical lifespan and commitment the species requires? Two out of three rarely works; all three needs to be a clear yes.

Blue Damselfish May Be Great For:

  • A keeper who wants Chrysiptera cyanea as a hardy, aggressive fish and will stock it with fish that can take a nip
  • Someone who adds the damsel last or keeps it in a species tank
  • An aquarist who will not believe the juvenile's color means a peaceful temperament

Blue Damselfish May Not Be Ideal For:

  • A peaceful community of firefish, grammas, and small wrasses
  • Anyone told a blue damsel is a beginner fish that stays nice
  • A reef where the damsel already owns the rock and new fish are being added
