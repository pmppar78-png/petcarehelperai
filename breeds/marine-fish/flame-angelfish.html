Flame Angelfish Overview

The Flame Angelfish is a small (4 in) saltwater species originating from the Pacific (Marshall Islands, Hawaii). Known for being semi-aggressive, this species is rated intermediate care level, making it well-suited for hobbyists with some marine aquarium experience.

Stunning bright red-orange with vertical black bars and blue-tipped fins; one of the most popular dwarf angels in the hobby

Centropyge loricula, the flame angelfish, is a dwarf angel from the Pacific, including Hawaii and the Marshall Islands. A flame angel needs 70 gallons before you trust it near corals. The care level is intermediate because the nipping is unpredictable. Semi-aggressive shows up toward other dwarf angels first. The reef-safe line is caution, and the specific problem is nipping at LPS and SPS corals. They also nip clam mantles. One per tank. A second flame angel in 70 gallons is a territorial dispute you will not win with more rock.

They are beautiful enough that the caution gets ignored. A juvenile in a dealer tank is busy, red, and small. The adult holds a patch of reef and tests corals with its mouth, especially large-polyp stones and the edge of a clam. Some individuals are mild. The card does not let you assume you bought one of those. Stock a flame angel where coral damage is survivable, or keep it in a fish-only tank with live rock and heavy feeding. Calling it reef safe with confidence is how a clam mantle disappears.

Omnivore: spirulina, mysis, angelfish formula, algae is the feeding and behavior note for Flame Angelfish. Reef safe is listed as Caution (nips at LPS/SPS corals).

Temperament & Compatibility

The Flame Angelfish is described as semi-aggressive and is considered reef safe with caution monitor closely in reef tanks as individual specimens may develop coral-nipping behavior.

The diet is omnivore: spirulina, mysis, angelfish formula, and algae. Feed those on purpose so grazing is not the whole intake. A fed angel still nips. An underfed one nips more and loses color. Semi-aggressive also applies to other dwarf angels and to new fish that cross the territory. Add the flame angel after the shy fish, not before, and do not add it to a tank that already has a coral beauty unless the system is far larger than 70 gallons.

Keep a flame angel at 72 to 82°F and salinity from 1.020 to 1.025. pH should read 8.1 to 8.4. Nitrate under 10 ppm is the reef number and under 20 ppm is the fish-only number. Alkalinity of 8 to 12 dKH, calcium of 380 to 450 ppm, and magnesium of 1250 to 1350 ppm are the coral-side targets you are protecting if you insist on keeping this fish among LPS. Ammonia and nitrite at 0 ppm are the fish-side targets. The purchase price runs about 60 to 120 dollars, with a setup around 300 to 500. The fish costs more than a coral beauty and nips for the same family reasons.

Seventy gallons is the minimum, not a suggestion to try a 40 and upgrade if the corals suffer. One fish, a lid, and a feeding habit that includes spirulina and mysis are the intermediate work. The 5-to-7-year lifespan will outlast a reef that was not supposed to be on the menu. If the display is a clam and a garden of LPS, pick a different fish. The flame angel is a dwarf angel first and an ornament second.
