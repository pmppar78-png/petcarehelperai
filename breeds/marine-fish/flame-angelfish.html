Flame Angelfish Overview

The Flame Angelfish is a small (4 in) saltwater species originating from the Pacific (Marshall Islands, Hawaii). Known for being semi-aggressive, this species is rated intermediate care level, making it well-suited for hobbyists with some marine aquarium experience.

Stunning bright red-orange with vertical black bars and blue-tipped fins; one of the most popular dwarf angels in the hobby

Centropyge loricula, the flame angelfish, is a dwarf angel from the Pacific, including Hawaii and the Marshall Islands. Adults are about 4 inches and live 5 to 7 years. A flame angel needs 70 gallons before you trust it near corals. The care level is intermediate because the nipping is unpredictable. Semi-aggressive shows up toward other dwarf angels first. The reef-safe line is caution, and the specific problem is nipping at LPS and SPS corals. They also nip clam mantles. One per tank. A second flame angel in 70 gallons is a territorial dispute you will not win with more rock.

They are beautiful enough that the caution gets ignored. A juvenile in a dealer tank is busy, red, and small. The adult holds a patch of reef and tests corals with its mouth, especially large-polyp stones and the edge of a clam. Some individuals are mild. The card does not let you assume you bought one of those. Stock a flame angel where coral damage is survivable, or keep it in a fish-only tank with live rock and heavy feeding. Tank Setup & Requirements

Tank Specifications

  • Minimum size: 70 gallon. Larger is strongly recommended for parameter stability.
  • Sump systems recommended for tanks over 50 gallons.
  • Water flow: Moderate to strong flow using powerheads or wavemakers. Provide areas of both higher and lower flow within the tank.
  • Lighting: Standard marine lighting adequate for fish-only systems.
  • Aquascaping: Live rock providing caves, overhangs, and swim-through passages. Ensure open swimming space for active species.

Diet & Nutrition

The Flame Angelfish is classified as omnivore. Proper nutrition is essential for maintaining health, coloration, and immune function.

  • Primary diet: Omnivore: spirulina, mysis, angelfish formula, algae.
  • Feeding frequency: 2-3 small feedings per day. Small frequent meals are better than one large feeding.
  • Variety: Rotate between different food types to ensure complete nutrition. Offer a mix of frozen foods, pellets, and algae-based foods.
  • Avoid: Overfeeding is the most common feeding mistake.

Proper nutrition for Flame Angelfish requires understanding not just what to feed, but how feeding strategies affect health, behavior, and water quality. A disciplined approach to feeding, where your Flame Angelfish receives the right amount of appropriate food at consistent intervals, is one of the most impactful things you can do for both the fish and the overall aquarium ecosystem.

Diet variety is essential for optimal Flame Angelfish health because no single commercial food provides the complete nutritional profile these marine fish require. Observing your Flame Angelfish's response to different food types also provides useful health information, as reduced interest in normally preferred foods is often an early indicator of illness.

Grooming & Maintenance

  • Equipment maintenance: Clean protein skimmer cup 2-3 times weekly. Replace filter media monthly. Calibrate refractometer regularly.
  • Algae management: Magnetic algae scrapers for glass cleaning. Clean-up crew (snails, hermit crabs) helps control nuisance algae.

Is the Flame Angelfish reef safe?

The Flame Angelfish is rated: Caution (nips at LPS/SPS corals). Reef compatibility can vary between individual specimens. Providing adequate feeding can reduce the likelihood of fish picking at corals.

How big of a tank does a Flame Angelfish need?

The Flame Angelfish requires a minimum tank size of 70 gallon. Larger tanks provide more stable water parameters and reduce territorial aggression. Consider future fish additions when sizing your tank.

What do Flame Angelfishs eat?

Flame Angelfishs are classified as omnivore. Feed spirulina, mysis, angelfish formula, algae. Variety is essential for complete nutrition and maintaining vibrant coloration. Consider using an automatic feeder for consistency.

Are Flame Angelfishs good for beginners?

Flame Angelfishs are rated intermediate care level. They require some experience with saltwater aquariums and stable, established systems. Understanding water chemistry, proper acclimation, and disease prevention is important.

How long do Flame Angelfishs live?

Flame Angelfishs live 5-7 years in captivity with proper care.
