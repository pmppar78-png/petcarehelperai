Green Chromis Overview

The Green Chromis is a small (3 in) saltwater species originating from the Indo-Pacific. Known for being peaceful, schooling, this species is rated beginner care level, making it an excellent choice for aquarists new to saltwater keeping.

One of the best beginner saltwater fish; iridescent green coloration shimmers under reef lighting; creates beautiful schooling displays in groups of 5+; extremely hardy and disease-resistant

Chromis viridis is a small Indo-Pacific schooling damsel, about 3 inches, and the lifespan on this card is a long 8 to 15 years. The school needs 30 gallons before you add anyone else. Beginner care here means a cycled tank and a full group. They are peaceful, reef safe, and omnivores that take flakes, pellets, mysis, and brine shrimp. The price is low, about 5 to 15 dollars each, with a setup around 200 to 400. They are a school. A pair of green chromis in a new 30-gallon tank is the stocking that fades, gets marine ich, and makes people decide the species is fragile.

Buy a real group, and add them together to a tank that has already finished cycling. A school that is too small spends its time hiding and fighting instead of hovering in the open the way the fish does on a reef. A brand-new aquarium does the same damage from the other direction. Chromis show stress as washed-out color and as ich when the group is undersized or the tank is new. That is a husbandry result, not a reason to treat the species as disposable because each fish was cheap.

Omnivore: flakes, pellets, mysis, brine shrimp is the feeding and behavior note for Green Chromis. Temperament on the same card is Peaceful, Schooling.

Temperament & Compatibility

The Green Chromis is described as peaceful, schooling and is considered reef safe, making it compatible with coral-dominated aquariums.

Thirty gallons can hold a modest school of 3-inch fish if the tank is long and the filtration is ahead of the bioload. It cannot hold a school plus a pile of other fish and still look like beginner care. They are peaceful toward other species and less peaceful toward a stray chromis added months later. Buy the group once. Replacing them one at a time is how you get a veteran that harasses every newcomer until the school never reforms.

Chromis viridis is kept at 72 to 82°F and a specific gravity of 1.020 to 1.025. pH should sit between 8.1 and 8.4 above the rock. Keep nitrate under 10 ppm if corals share the water and under 20 ppm if the tank is fish-only. Ammonia and nitrite at 0 ppm are the cycling lesson this fish is often used to skip and should not be. Alkalinity at 8 to 12 dKH, calcium at 380 to 450 ppm, and magnesium at 1250 to 1350 ppm matter when the school is in a reef, which is a fair place for them because they are reef safe and they swim above the corals rather than eating them.

The 8-to-15-year lifespan is the surprise in a fish that costs less than a meal. It only shows up if the school stays intact and the tank stays mature. Feed them in the open water so every fish eats, not only the bold one at the front. A green chromis that is dark, thin, and scratching on the sand is a prompt to look at group size and at how new the aquarium is. This page is the marine Chromis viridis school, not a freshwater blue-green chromis with a similar common name and a different salt requirement.
