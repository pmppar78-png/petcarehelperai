Melanurus Wrasse Overview

The Melanurus Wrasse is a small (5 in) saltwater species originating from the Western Pacific. Known for being semi-aggressive, active, this species is rated beginner care level, making it an excellent choice for aquarists new to saltwater keeping.

Outstanding reef pest controller: eats flatworms, pyramidellid snails, bristleworms, and small snails; stunning multicolored appearance; sleeps in sand cocoon at night

They jump, and they bury in the sand to sleep. A lid is required, including the gaps around pipes, because a melanurus will leave through a hole that looks too small when the lights go off. A sand bed is required too. Bare-bottom tanks and crushed coral give them nowhere to dive, and they ricochet off the glass at night and damage the mouth. Fine sand, open enough to enter, is part of the 50-gallon layout. A bed that is only a decorative dusting in one corner is not a bed.

Carnivore: mysis, brine shrimp, pellets, hunts pests is the feeding and behavior note for Melanurus Wrasse. Reef safe is listed as Yes (excellent pest controller).

Temperament & Compatibility

The Melanurus Wrasse is described as semi-aggressive, active and is considered reef safe, making it compatible with coral-dominated aquariums.

This wrasse is listed at 72 to 78°F, cooler at the top than a general 82°F reef. Keep its salinity between 1.020 and 1.025. pH should remain 8.1 to 8.4 while it hunts the rocks. Nitrate under 10 ppm suits the reef they are usually bought for, and under 20 ppm is the fish-only allowance. Alkalinity of 8 to 12 dKH, calcium of 380 to 450 ppm, and magnesium of 1250 to 1350 ppm are the reef side of a tank you hope will keep its corals while this fish works the rocks for pests. Ammonia and nitrite at 0 ppm should already be stable. Beginner refers to a fish that eats and lives in ordinary marine water. It does not refer to a fish you can keep without a cover or a sand bed.

Feed mysis and pellets so pest hunting is extra, not the only calories. A melanurus that is expected to live on pyramidellid snails and flatworms in a sparse tank will get thin and then start sampling whatever small invertebrate is left. Semi-aggressive shows up toward smaller wrasses and toward fish that hover in its path. One per tank in 50 gallons is simpler than a pair. Males and females differ in color, and a male will boss a tank that seemed big enough at the dealer.

Spend the setup on the lid and the sand, not only on the fish. The 5-to-8-year lifespan fits a covered 50-gallon tank with a real bed and a carnivore diet. A melanurus on the floor, or a melanurus with a shredded mouth from a bare bottom, is a missing piece of furniture. Reef safe on the label still means you chose it knowing the ornamental shrimp might be part of the pest control.

Is Melanurus Wrasse Right for Your Aquarium?

Melanurus Wrasse May Be Great For:

  • A reef keeper who wants Halichoeres melanurus for pest snails and will provide sand and a lid
  • Someone who adds it after corals and before very shy fish have nowhere to hide
  • An aquarist who feeds meaty frozen food and checks the sand at night

Melanurus Wrasse May Not Be Ideal For:

  • A decorative-shrimp tank or a bare-bottom reef
  • An open top. Melanurus wrasses jump
  • A second Halichoeres in a small system
