Melanurus Wrasse Overview

The Melanurus Wrasse is a small (5 in) saltwater species originating from the Western Pacific. Known for being semi-aggressive, active, this species is rated beginner care level, making it an excellent choice for aquarists new to saltwater keeping. With a lifespan of 5-8 years, this is a rewarding species that can be enjoyed for many years with proper care.

Outstanding reef pest controller: eats flatworms, pyramidellid snails, bristleworms, and small snails; stunning multicolored appearance; sleeps in sand cocoon at night

They jump, and they bury in the sand to sleep. A lid is required, including the gaps around pipes, because a melanurus will leave through a hole that looks too small when the lights go off. A sand bed is required too. Bare-bottom tanks and crushed coral give them nowhere to dive, and they ricochet off the glass at night and damage the mouth. Fine sand, open enough to enter, is part of the 50-gallon layout. A bed that is only a decorative dusting in one corner is not a bed.

Diet & Nutrition

The Melanurus Wrasse is classified as carnivore. Proper nutrition is essential for maintaining health, coloration, and immune function.

  • Primary diet: Carnivore: mysis, brine shrimp, pellets, hunts pests.
  • Feeding frequency: 2-3 small feedings per day. Small frequent meals are better than one large feeding.
  • Variety: Rotate between different food types to ensure complete nutrition. Offer frozen mysis shrimp, brine shrimp, and high-quality pellets.
