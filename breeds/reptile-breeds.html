Reptile & Amphibian Species Guide

Explore reptile and amphibian profiles, from geckos and pythons to turtles and frogs, with habitat, diet, temperature, and care fundamentals.

Using the reptile list

Filter by type first: lizard, snake, or turtle. Those groups do not share a heating plan. A leopard gecko's warm hide is a different project from a basking turtle who needs strong UVB and a water quality routine. Beginner-friendly on this page means the species is commonly captive-bred and the enclosure can be done correctly with a thermostat and a thermometer you can read, not that the animal thrives in a tank from a fair.

Lifespan will surprise people who think of reptiles as short school projects. Many of the animals in this directory are a ten-to-twenty-year commitment, and some tortoises outlive the lease, the job, and the original owner. If the lifespan filter says long, believe it and name a backup caretaker before you buy.

Search is literal. If you type "python," you will not also get every boa unless the blurb says python. Open the profile for temperatures, humidity, and whether UVB is optional or mandatory. Metabolic bone disease is what happens when that line is skipped. A reptile who stops eating and loses weight needs a clinician who sees reptiles. The not-eating guide explains how that differs from a normal seasonal pause.

Large constrictors and some turtles carry legal restrictions. The profile is where those cautions live. Wild-caught animals are a welfare and disease problem even when a shop will sell one.

Lizards

Lizards are diverse reptiles ranging from small geckos to large monitors. They require proper heating, lighting, and humidity to thrive, with needs varying greatly by species.

Geckos

Small, generally docile lizards that are among the most beginner-friendly reptiles — if you get the heating, lighting, and humidity right from day one. Most are insectivores; a few larger species take fruit or nectar. Handling tolerance varies widely by species.

Leopard Gecko

LizardBeginner

Perfect beginner reptile. Docile, handleable, and don't require UVB lighting. Lifespan 15-20 years.

Crested Gecko

LizardBeginner

Easy to care for with room temperature needs. Can eat commercial diet. Lifespan 15-20 years.

Gargoyle Gecko

LizardHardy

Similar care to crested geckos. Bumpier texture, regenerate tails. Lifespan 15-20 years.

African Fat-Tailed Gecko

LizardGentle

Similar to leopard geckos but need higher humidity. Very docile. Lifespan 15-20 years.

Tokay Gecko

LizardAdvanced

Beautiful but notorious for biting. Loud calls, impressive colors. Lifespan 10-20 years.

Day Gecko (Phelsuma)

LizardDisplay

Brilliant green coloring, active during day. Not for handling, display animals. Lifespan 10-15 years.

Leachianus Gecko

LizardLarge

Largest gecko species. Impressive size, can be handleable. Lifespan 20+ years.

Long-Tailed Grass Lizard

Chameleons

Approach this with patience and a willingness to adapt as you learn what works best in your specific situation.

Snakes

Snakes are low-maintenance pets that don't require daily feeding. They range from small species perfect for beginners to large constrictors requiring experienced care.

Perfect first snake. Docile, easy to care for, many color morphs. Lifespan 15-20 years.

Garter Snake

Active, diurnal snakes. Can eat varied diet including fish. Lifespan 10-15 years.

Turtles & Tortoises

Turtles and tortoises are long-lived reptiles requiring significant space and specialized care. Aquatic species need large tanks with filtration, while tortoises need outdoor or large indoor enclosures.

Aquatic Turtles

There is always more to learn, and the best pet owners stay curious and open to refining their approach over time.

Red-Eared Slider

TurtleCommon

Most common pet turtle. Need large tanks (75+ gallons) and strong filtration. Lifespan 20-40 years.

Painted Turtle

TurtleNative

Beautiful native species with colorful markings. Similar care to sliders. Lifespan 25-30 years.

Map Turtle

TurtleUnique

Map-like patterns on shell. Need very clean water. Lifespan 15-25 years.

Musk Turtle (Stinkpot)

TurtleSmall

Small aquatic turtles staying under 5 inches. Need smaller tanks. Lifespan 30-50 years.

Mud Turtle

TurtleSmall

Small, hardy turtles. Can live in smaller setups than sliders. Lifespan 40-50 years.

Softshell Turtle

TurtleAdvanced

Unique leathery shell. Fast, can be aggressive. Need pristine water. Lifespan 25-50 years.

Tortoises

Greek Tortoise

Similar to Hermann's, many subspecies. Hardy and personable. Lifespan 50-100 years.

Sulcata Tortoise

Third largest tortoise (100+ lbs). Need outdoor enclosures. Lifespan 70-100+ years.

Red-Footed Tortoise

TortoiseTropical

Colorful tropical tortoise. Need higher humidity than Mediterranean species. Lifespan 50+ years.

Leopard Tortoise

TortoiseLarge

Beautiful spotted shell pattern. Get large (40-50 lbs). Lifespan 80-100 years.
