Diamond Dove Overview

The Diamond Dove is a small (7-8 in) dove that has captivated bird enthusiasts worldwide. Their low (gentle coo) noise level makes them suitable for apartments and noise-sensitive environments.

Their diet of seeds, grit, greens requires consistent quality and variety.

A conversation with your avian veterinarian ensures these general guidelines get adapted to your Diamond Dove's unique needs, age, and overall condition.

Ask Our AI About Diamond Doves

Ask the AI Now

A Diamond Dove is a tiny Australian dove, smaller than a ringneck dove, and it frightens easily. They do better as a pair you watch than as a bird you clutch. They need a long flight cage, a fine-seed and pellet mix, and grit or a cuttlebone for calcium. Cold drafts and a cat in the same room are the usual disasters. A puffed, still dove is a sick bird, not a calm one.

Diet and Nutrition Tips

A balanced diet is critical for your Diamond Dove's longevity and quality of life.

  • Pellet base: A high-quality pelleted diet should form 60-70% of your Diamond Dove's food intake.

Exercise Requirements

Adequate flight time and physical activity are essential for your Diamond Dove's physical and mental health.

  • Include foraging toys, puzzle feeders, and chew toys appropriate for your Diamond Dove's size.

Cost of Ownership

The first twelve months absorb the bulk of startup costs — a suitable cage or aviary, initial avian vet exam, perches, foraging toys, and appropriate dietary staples. Beyond that, you are primarily covering food and supplements, cage maintenance, annual avian vet checkups, and toy rotation, which are far more predictable and manageable.
