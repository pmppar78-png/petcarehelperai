Adult males often show a black neck ring and a pink collar. That plumage marks sex in a mature male. It is not a health score, and a young bird in a shop may not have grown it yet. Hens and immature males can look plain around the neck without being a different species. Both sexes need the same roomy cage, the same diet, and time out of the cage every day. Buying a juvenile only for the collar misses the adult voice, which arrives on its own schedule.

A 23-inch parakeet needs a cage that lets the tail clear the bars while the bird climbs and turns. Budgie housing is the wrong object, and a decorative dome is worse. Daily time outside the cage, in a room with the door closed and the ceiling fan off, is part of basic care. Nonstick fumes, open windows, and other pets change that free time from exercise into an accident. The beak will test latches, picture frames, and any perch thin enough to splinter.

Talking practice is short repetition of ordinary words, not a shouting match with the contact call. Answering every scream teaches the bird that volume works. Thin apartment walls and a neighbor on the other side of them are part of the decision. A person who wants a quiet evening should listen to an adult Alexandrine before treating the talking score as the only fact on the card. The loud rating does not expire after the first phrases are learned.

Temperament & Behavior

Pellets and vegetables should be the ordinary meal, with seed and nuts measured rather than left in a hopper. Sunflower as the whole diet is a common path to vitamin A shortage, dull feathers, and respiratory irritation. Weigh an 8 to 10 ounce bird on a gram scale when the look of the chest changes. A sudden drop, or a bird that sits heavy and stops climbing, belongs with an avian veterinarian. New arrivals also raise the usual questions about hygiene between cages and about psittacine beak and feather disease.

Initial setup on this card runs from a few hundred dollars into the thousands, mostly because a cage that survives this beak is not a budgie starter kit. Annual food, veterinary care, and toys continue for the full 25 to 30 years. Spring hormones can make a previously easy bird territorial toward one person in the house. That shift is seasonal behavior in a large Psittacula, handled with a calmer routine and less shoulder time, not with punishment. Captive-bred birds from a source that will discuss diet and parentage are the birds to seek.

Housing & Environment Requirements

Provide a spacious cage appropriate for Alexandrine Parakeets - minimum dimensions should allow full wingspan extension plus room to climb and play.

Diet & Nutrition

A balanced diet for Alexandrine Parakeets should include high-quality pellets as the foundation (60-70% of diet), supplemented with fresh fruits and vegetables (20-30%), and limited seeds/nuts as treats (5-10%).

Common Health Issues

Common health concerns for Alexandrine Parakeets include respiratory infections, psittacine beak and feather disease, feather plucking (often stress-related), and nutritional deficiencies.

By the time an Alexandrine Parakeet displays obvious symptoms such as fluffed feathers, tail bobbing, or sitting on the cage bottom, the underlying condition may already be advanced.

Grooming & Maintenance

Regular grooming for Alexandrine Parakeets includes providing bathing opportunities (mist spray or shallow dish), nail trimming as needed, and wing clipping (optional and debated - consult your avian vet).

Beginner-Friendly Rating: Intermediate

Alexandrine Parakeets are suitable for keepers with some experience.

Quick Answers About Alexandrine Parakeets

How long do Alexandrine Parakeets live?

Alexandrine Parakeets typically live 25-30 years in captivity with proper care.

Are Alexandrine Parakeets noisy?

Alexandrine Parakeets have a loud noise level. They can be quite vocal, especially during dawn and dusk. This breed is best suited for homes where noise tolerance is high and neighbors are not in close proximity.

Can Alexandrine Parakeets talk?

Alexandrine Parakeets have very good talking ability. With patience and consistent training, many individuals learn to speak words and phrases clearly. Start with simple, frequently repeated words.

Do Alexandrine Parakeets need a companion?

While Alexandrine Parakeets are social creatures, a single bird can thrive with sufficient human interaction and enrichment.

Long Term Commitment

Bringing an Alexandrine Parakeet into your home is a commitment that can last 25-30 years.
