Size, noise, and lifespan

Ring-necked doves, Streptopelia risoria, are about a foot long and often live 12 to 20 years. Care is beginner if you mean diet and cage cleaning, and intermediate if you mean you understand they are birds with dusty down and a hormone season. A cage of 24 inches cube is a minimum for a pair that also gets out, and it is small if they never do.

They are gentle and they startle. Children who chase them will have a dove that lives on the cage floor in fear. The dove is the pet, for a decade or two. Macaw keeping is a different skill.

Eggs and hidden illness

Females lay even without a male. Chronic laying drains calcium. A dove straining on the cage floor may be egg bound, which is an emergency. AAV clinics see doves. Fluffed and quiet is the quiet bird page, same as any other bird.

Domestic ringnecks and wild doves

Streptopelia risoria, the ring-necked dove or Barbary dove, is a domestic bird kept for centuries and generally treated as descended from the African collared dove. The bird on a suburban wire is often a mourning dove or a Eurasian collared-dove. A mourning dove and a Eurasian collared-dove are different species from the domestic ringneck. A ringneck is about a foot long, pale, with a narrow black half-collar, and the table's 12 to 20 years is a real commitment for a bird people buy because it looked quiet in a booth. They walk more than they climb. They startle. They coo.

Cage size

The 24 by 24 by 24 inch minimum on the table works for a pair that gets out. It is small as a life. Bar spacing around three quarters of an inch keeps a startled bird from putting its head through and panicking. Perches should be flat or wide and varied, because dove feet walk. A narrow dowel is a poor perch for the whole day. Put the cage in a room with a steady schedule. A doorway where people burst through startles them off the perch at night and snaps blood feathers. No mirror sold as a friend. A mirror is a rival that never leaves, and it encourages a single bird to court a piece of glass. Give a shallow bath dish. Skip the nest bowl unless you intend eggs, because a hen will use it.

Diet

The questions already name a dove or pigeon mix, grit, and greens, and they already reject parrot pellets as the whole plan and seed with no mineral. In amounts, that is about one to two tablespoons of a dove mix per bird each day, a separate small dish of insoluble granite grit they can take or leave, a cuttlebone or mineral block, and chopped greens several times a week. Pull wilted greens. Keep the grit dish small. A heaped bowl lets them fill the crop with grit and skip the seed. They swallow seed whole and use the grit in the gizzard. Fresh water daily, in a dish they will stand in, so you will be washing it. A dove that suddenly eats less is sick. They do not dramatize.

Eggs, canker, and pigeon contact

Females lay without a male. Chronic laying spends calcium. A dove straining, fluffed on the cage floor, or unable to perch may be egg bound. That is an emergency. Do not grease the vent with an essential oil and wait. AAV clinics see doves, and the quiet bird page is the same picture as for any bird that has gone still. Trichomonas, pigeon canker, shows up as wet eyes, gaping, or plaques in the mouth. It spreads in shared water. Do not buy a pigeon medicine online and dose the water. Paramyxovirus and pox ride in with contact from outdoor pigeons. A pet dove does not belong on a balcony where feral pigeons drink from the same rail. Respiratory clicking, a wing droop, or weight you can feel dropping along the breast need a veterinarian. Tell the clinic it is a dove so they do not prep a parrot cage and a seed lecture.

Baths, nails, and handling

Offer the bath. Change the water when they have stood in it, which will be immediately. Nails that curl get trimmed by someone who can see the vein. Grabbing the tail feathers pulls them out. Towel a dove gently around the body if you must move it, wings folded, feet supported. A dove that is caught nightly for a cuddle learns to live on the cage floor. Dust from the feathers is milder than a cockatoo's powder and it is still real. People with severe bird allergies find out in the second month. An air purifier helps the person. It does not replace cleaning the cage paper.

Exercise and breeding

Time on a safe floor, a scatter of seed in a tray of dry leaves, and a perch rearrangement are the exercise. They do not learn party tricks. A pair will court, drive, and ignore you, which the questions already admit. One dove can bond to a person and still needs a flock's worth of attention. Breeding because the eggs are pretty produces more doves than you have homes for, and a hen that lays repeatedly is the calcium problem above. Remove eggs if a veterinarian or an experienced dove keeper has shown you how, or leave them and accept chicks you have already planned to house. Do not shake an egg.

Costs in October 2026

A fair cage and stand in October 2026 often run $80 to $250. Seed, grit, and greens for a pair are about $12 to $25 a month. An avian exam is commonly $70 to $160 before tests. The doves themselves may be $30 to $80 each, which is why they are impulse birds at fairs. The impulse does not cover a 15-year supply of seed or a night visit when a hen is egg bound. Budget the years of seed and veterinary care. The price at the booth is the small part.

Migratory Bird Treaty Act

Native migratory birds, including the mourning dove, are protected by the Migratory Bird Treaty Act. You cannot keep one you found, raised from a nest, or bought from someone who picked it up. The domestic ringneck is the bird this page is about, and it is widely kept without a federal migratory-bird permit. Cities, landlords, and homeowners' associations still ban outdoor aviaries and loose birds. Releasing a ringneck because you are bored seeds a feral flock and is a cruel way to dispose of a domestic animal. If you are not sure which dove is in the carrier, do not buy it. Take a wild bird to a rehabilitator. Do not put it in a pet cage.

Suitability

The table says beginner. That fits a person who will keep the cage clean, accept cooing, and not need a bird that talks. It does not fit a small child who wants to carry a bird around. Doves startle and then live afraid. Dogs and cats are predators even when they "only look." A pair is the more natural picture. One dove with a person who is home can work. Two species of birds in one room need a quarantine of about 30 days for any newcomer, in a separate air space if you can manage it, because canker and respiratory disease travel.

Before you buy

Ask for closed bands and what they mean, or accept that an unbanded adult is a guess on age. Watch both birds eat. Nasal discharge, a wet eye, a lump, or a bird sitting fluffed while the other feeds are reasons to leave the room. Ask whether the hen is laying and whether you are prepared for eggs. Ask the seller to name the species. You want a domestic ringneck. A netted wild dove is a different animal. Have the cage paper, the mix, and the avian clinic picked before the box comes home. A dove that rides around in a pocket for a day of errands is a dove that may not eat again.

Ring-necked dove questions

Do doves talk?

No. They coo. The sound is softer than a parrot scream and it is still constant in a breeding pair. Know that before you put the cage by a desk.

Can I keep one dove?

A single dove can bond to a person and still needs a flock-sized amount of attention. A pair is the more natural picture, and the pair may ignore you.

What do they eat?

A dove or pigeon mix, grit, and greens. Parrot pellets as the whole diet are the wrong shape of plan. Seed alone, with no mineral, is also wrong.

Is a ring-necked dove the same as a mourning dove?

No. Streptopelia risoria is a domestic bird. A mourning dove is a native migratory species protected by the Migratory Bird Treaty Act. Keeping one is illegal.

How much does a ring-necked dove eat?

About one to two tablespoons of a dove or pigeon mix per bird each day, plus a separate dish of insoluble grit, a mineral block, and greens several times a week. Parrot pellets as the whole diet are the wrong plan.

What does it cost to keep a pair?

As of October 2026, a proper cage is often $80 to $250, seed and greens about $12 to $25 a month, and an avian exam commonly $70 to $160 before tests. The purchase price at a fair is the small number.
