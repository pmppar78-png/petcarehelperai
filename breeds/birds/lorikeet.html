Daily care

Rainbow lorikeets, Trichoglossus moluccanus, are about 10 to 12 inches and often live 20 to 30 years. Care is advanced because the food is wet, the mess is constant, and the bird is loud and busy. A cage of 24 by 24 by 36 inches is only a start. They need flight or a long time out, and a room you can wash.

A quiet seed-eating conure is the wrong picture. They scream. They play hard. A bored lorikeet plucks or bites. Captive-bred birds are the ones to buy. Wild-caught lorikeets are a conservation and a disease problem.

Coastal eastern Australia

Trichoglossus moluccanus is the rainbow lorikeet of coastal eastern Australia, from Queensland down through New South Wales and Victoria, with feral flocks in places people planted the wrong trees. The brush tongue is built for nectar and pollen. Fruit is a side, insects an occasional extra. That anatomy is why a cockatiel seed mix fails them, which the questions already say, and why the mess is liquid. A 10- to 12-inch bird that can live 20 to 30 years needs a washable room and a household that can live with the noise.

Cage size and flight space

The table's 24 by 24 by 36 inch cage is a holding size. A bird with this wingspan paces in it if the door stays shut. Give a flight closer to 48 inches of width, or treat the smaller cage as a bedroom and keep the bird out for most of the day in a room without ceiling fans, nonstick cookware fumes, or an open window. Bar spacing around three quarters of an inch is the usual fit. They chew soft wood and they flick nectar onto everything, so perches you can scrub beat a decorative branch that stays damp. No nest box for a pet. A dark hut, plus petting under the wings, brings on a territorial bite and a hen that lays until she is calcium-poor.

Feeding schedule

Use a commercial lorikeet nectar and follow that brand's scoop. Mix what the bird will finish in a few hours, morning and again late in the day. Wash the cup before the next batch. Nectar left overnight grows bacteria and yeast, and a sour crop is the result. Fruit and a few vegetables go in at the same time and come out before they ferment. Offer seed only as a small training reward. Do not fill a bowl with it. Do not pile a generic parrot vitamin or an iron-heavy pellet on top of a complete nectar unless an avian veterinarian has asked for that exact product. Iron-storage disease is a known lorikeet problem, and extra supplements push the diet toward too much iron. Fresh water still sits in its own clean dish. Wet droppings are normal for this bird. A vent that stays pasted, or droppings that suddenly change color and smell, needs a veterinarian.

When to call a veterinarian

Sitting fluffed, leaving the nectar, vomiting, or bleeding from a broken feather is a same-day call. applies, and the Association of Avian Veterinarians directory lists clinics that have seen a brush tongue. Egg binding and a hen that lays clutch after clutch need a veterinarian, calcium decisions included. Do not crush a calcium tablet into the nectar from a recipe you found. Psittacosis can move from bird to person. Say "lorikeet" to the physician if people in the house are sick alongside the bird. Baths, a shower perch, or a dish of water are hygiene. Air freshener, scented candles, and self-cleaning oven cycles are not. Fumes kill parrots without much warning.

Beak, nails, and bathing

A lorikeet that bathes will paint the wall and then look after itself. Wipe dried nectar off the face with plain water. Do not use soap. Nails and a beak that meet normally wear on the perch and the cuttlebone. An overgrown beak is a medical question. Wing clipping is a fall risk in a bird this heavy and this clumsy on the floor. A safe room beats a clipped bird that still reaches the pot of soup.

Toys

Foraging cups you can wash, paper, and a swing of rope that does not fray into a toe-trap are the useful toys. Wood that soaks up nectar and stays wet grows mold. Rotate a few toys. A bored lorikeet screams, plucks, or bites, which the opening already notes. Flight, a shredding project, and a person who can tolerate the volume are the exercise plan. Another lorikeet is company only when you have the space to separate them. Two birds in the minimum cage is a fight with better lighting.

Costs in October

In October 2026 a US keeper should budget a flight cage from about $250 to $700, nectar and fruit around $30 to $55 a month, and an avian visit commonly $80 to $200 before lab work. The bird itself, captive-bred, is often several hundred dollars and sometimes more than a thousand. Budget cleanup supplies with the cage. People who put a cheap cage in a carpeted rental often rehome a healthy lorikeet by year two. Price the hose and the washable walls before you price the bird.

Import rules and Hawaii

Australia does not treat native lorikeets as a casual export. In the United States the Wild Bird Conservation Act limits wild-caught parrot imports, and parrots sit on CITES Appendix II unless a species is on Appendix I. Buy a captive-bred bird and ask for the band or the breeder. Hawaii's Department of Agriculture lists lories as prohibited for private possession, and a rainbow lorikeet is a lory. Read the animal guidelines before any move. An unbanded "wild type" at a flea market is a wild-caught bird. Paying for it funds that trade.

Suitability

The table says advanced, high noise, nectar. That is accurate for an adult with a washable room and no illusion of a quiet apartment. Children get bitten when the bird is hormonal, and a bite from this beak needs a wash, and sometimes a doctor. Dogs and cats are predators in the same room. A beginner who has kept only a budgie is not prepared for the nectar routine. A beginner who likes the photograph should stop at the photograph.

Before you buy

Look at the chest. Bare patches from plucking, a sharp keel, pasted vents, or nostrils with discharge are reasons to leave. Ask what nectar brand the bird already eats. Watch it fly as well as step up. A bird that cannot fly may be injured or clipped into helplessness. Ask about the last avian exam. Confirm you can keep the bird if your housing is legal where you live, including a written check of Hawaii if that is the destination. Have the flight cage built and the first jar of nectar in the cupboard before the deposit clears.

Rainbow lorikeet questions

Can lorikeets eat seed?

Seed is not their diet. They eat nectar, pollen, and fruit. A lorikeet on a cockatiel mix fails slowly. Use a commercial nectar made for them and clean the cups the same day, because nectar grows bacteria.

Why is the cage so messy?

Their droppings are squirted and wet. Walls, cages, and your shirt get painted. People who hate that mess should not keep a lorikeet.

Are they cuddly?

They can be, and they bite when they are hormonal or overstimulated. The noise is high. An apartment is a poor match.

How often do you change lorikeet nectar?

Mix a fresh batch morning and late day, only what the bird will finish in a few hours, and wash the cup each time. Nectar left overnight grows bacteria and yeast.

Are rainbow lorikeets legal in Hawaii?

Hawaii's Department of Agriculture lists lories as prohibited for private possession. A rainbow lorikeet is a lory. Mainland captive-bred birds are a different legal picture, and wild-caught imports are restricted by the Wild Bird Conservation Act.

Why not feed a regular parrot pellet?

The tongue and the gut are built for nectar and pollen. A seed mix or a random pellet fails them, and an extra iron-heavy supplement on top of a complete nectar invites iron-storage disease. Change the diet only with an avian vet.
