Hawk-Headed Parrot (Red-Fan Parrot) Overview

The hawk-headed parrot is Deroptyus accipitrinus, also called the red-fan parrot, from South America. The card lists 12 to 14 inches and 6 to 10 ounces, a lifespan of 30 to 40 years, noise that is moderate to loud, and moderate talking ability. Temperament is printed as unique, bold, and affectionate. Unique is the honest word. This is not a cuddly Amazon. The genus is its own, the display is its own, and the bite is not an Amazon pinch with a different paint job.

When the bird is alarmed it raises a feather ruff, the fan of elongated nape feathers that gives the red-fan name. The crown lifts, the neck widens, and the bird looks larger and more hawk-like than it did on the perch a moment earlier. That display is a stress and threat signal. It is not a party trick and not a cue to clap, film, or push a hand closer so the fan opens again. A keeper who provokes the ruff to show guests is manufacturing the mood that leads to a bite.

Hawk-heads can be nippy and unpredictable. Bold on the card is real. A bird that greets one person with interest may lunge at another, or at the same person on a different day, without a long warning that an Amazon owner would recognize. Affectionate applies to a bond built slowly with one careful handler. It does not describe a parrot that wants every visitor to scratch its neck. Unique means you cannot copy a caique or Amazon routine and expect the same result from Deroptyus.

A patient handler is the minimum skill, not a decorative phrase. Step-up, a neutral perch, and an end to the session before the fan comes up are the tools. Punishment, surprise grabs, and crowded rooms make a nippy bird worse. Thirty to 40 years is the lifespan attached to that patience. A hawk-head bought as a first parrot often changes households after the first serious bite. The second household inherits a bird that has learned hands are a threat, which confirms the unpredictable label and was preventable.

Moderate to loud is the noise line. The call is a harsh parrot voice, enough for a house and too much for a thin-walled apartment that wanted something picturesque. Moderate talking means some birds learn words and many use sound effects and contact calls instead. Do not buy Deroptyus as a talking project. Buy it, if at all, as a display species you are willing to read carefully for decades. South America is the origin. Captive-bred birds with a known history are the ones to consider. Wild-caught hawk-heads are a poor fit for the affectionate half of the temperament line.

Temperament & Behavior

Housing is a strong parrot cage sized for a 12 to 14 inch bird that fans its neck and needs personal space, not a conure starter kit. Give a retreat so the bird can leave the front bars when the room is busy. Toys should occupy the beak without requiring the keeper to reach in past a bird that is already displaying. Foraging helps. So does a predictable schedule. Affection grows from that predictability. It does not grow from forcing a cuddle on a bold animal that has already lifted the ruff.

Weight spans 6 to 10 ounces on this card, so two healthy adults can look quite different. Learn the individual bird's weight and appetite instead of guessing from chest shape alone. Diet is a standard parrot mix of pellets, vegetables, and a restrained nut portion. Seed as the whole bowl is as wrong here as it is for any large psittacine, and a hawk-head that feels ill becomes harder to handle, not easier. A quiet, fluffed bird that has stopped threatening is more worrying than one that still displays.

Find an avian veterinarian before you need a bite wound managed on either side of the relationship. The handler's skin and the bird's stress are both part of this species. Thirty to 40 years of improvising with a dog clinic is a bad match for a parrot this unusual. Take the paperwork, the species name, and a recent weight to the first visit so the clinic is not guessing which Amazon it has.

People meet the fan in a photograph and expect a showy pet that likes attention. The bird behind the photograph is 12 to 14 inches, 6 to 10 ounces, moderate to loud, and only moderately talented at speech. It raises a feather ruff when alarmed. It can be nippy. It can also be affectionate with the person who earned that trust and did not spend it on performances. A patient handler with decades to give, and no need for an Amazon-shaped relationship, is the household that fits Deroptyus accipitrinus.

Diet & Nutrition

Pellets should be about 60 to 70 percent of a hawk-headed parrot's diet, fresh fruits and vegetables about 20 to 30 percent, and seeds or nuts about 5 to 10 percent. Clean water is constant. Avocado, chocolate, caffeine, alcohol, and salty foods stay out of reach. A 6 to 10 ounce South American parrot will eat the fatty portion first if you allow it. An avian veterinarian can adjust the plan as a young bird becomes a 30 to 40 year adult. A unique temperament is not permission to share kitchen scraps. Moderate talking ability is unrelated to the toxic list.

Individual hawk-headed parrots do not all eat alike. Grooming & Maintenance

Mist or a dish bath, nail trims when needed, and a cautious conversation with an avian veterinarian about whether wings should ever be clipped: that is grooming for a red-fan parrot. Clipping a bird that displays with raised feathers and still needs exercise is a decision, not a default. Clear dropped food each day, wash the cage more fully each week, and change food and water every morning. Rotate toys so boredom does not become plucking. Annual toy spending is listed around 100 to 250 dollars, food 150 to 400, vet care 100 to 300. The nape fan should be watched for stuck debris, not decorated.
