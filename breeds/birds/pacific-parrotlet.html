Five inches and a full parrot

Pacific parrotlets, Forpus coelestis, are about 4.5 to 5 inches and an ounce, with a life often 15 to 20 years. They are bold, loud for the size, and affectionate on their terms. A cage that would satisfy a pair of finches is a jail. Give length to fly or at least to sprint, and hours on a play stand. Pellets, vegetables, and a little seed are the diet. Millet as the whole bowl is how a parrotlet gets fat and short of vitamins.

They nest in pockets and drawers if you let them. Hormonal biting follows a dark hut and a lot of petting on the back. Keep hands off the back. Keep the cage in the room where life is, not in a spare bedroom.

A bite the size of the bird

The beak bruises. Children should watch, not handle. A fluffed parrotlet crashes faster than a macaw because there is no body reserve. Weigh the bird. AAV is the vet search. The quiet bird page is what a puffed-up, silent parrotlet looks like.

Pacific parrotlet questions

Are parrotlets good for children?

No. They bite hard for their size and they bond to one person. A child who wants a pet to hold will be bitten, and the bird will be scared.

Can two parrotlets share a cage?

Often they fight. One bird with a person is the safer plan unless you have a true bonded pair and a cage large enough to separate them the day it goes wrong.

Do they talk?

A few words, sometimes. Buy one because you want a tiny parrot, not because you want a lecture.
