Plum-Headed Parakeet Overview

The plum-headed parakeet is Psittacula cyanocephala, a parrot from the Indian subcontinent listed at 13 inches and 2.5 to 3 ounces. Adult males have the plum-colored head. Birds without that head may be hens or young males, so a grey-headed juvenile is not automatically a different species and not automatically female. The voice is rated low, and talking ability on the card is moderate. Temperament is gentle, quiet, and social. Low and quiet are real next to an Alexandrine, and they do not turn this bird into a decoration that can live in a budgie cage.

Quieter than an Alexandrine is the useful comparison. Thirteen inches and a few ounces is a slimmer Psittacula than P. eupatria, and the call is easier to live with. It is still a parrot call. Apartments that cannot bear any flock noise will hear this one too, just not at Alexandrine volume. Moderate talking means some males learn words and phrases. Speech is a pleasant extra, not the reason to skip foraging and time out of the cage. Social birds want a person or a well-matched partner. A covered cage in a spare room is not social.

The usable version of this plan is the one your avian veterinarian writes after examining your Plum Headed Parakeet in person.

Lifespan is 15 to 20 years. That is shorter than a large macaw and long enough to outgrow a starter cage bought in a hurry. Out-of-cage time is a daily need, in a room you have checked for fans, open doors, and metals the beak should not find. The long tail needs perch placement that does not force the feathers into a corner. Gentle birds bite when they are hormonal or grabbed. The bite is smaller than an Alexandrine's and still a poor fit for a child's unsupervised hand.

Temperament & Behavior

Diet is pellets and vegetables, with seed and a little fruit as the smaller share. At 2.5 to 3 ounces, a sunflower habit puts fat on a bird that looks fine until it is not. Offer greens the bird will actually try, cut small, and rotate them. Bathing keeps the plum head and the body feathers in order. A shallow dish or a light mist, not a blast of cold water, is enough. Weight on a gram scale tells you whether the quiet bird is quiet because it is content or because it is ill.

Indian subcontinent origin sits on the card for the wild range. Pets should be captive-bred. A male in full plum head, a low voice, and a moderate gift for words are the attractions. The obligations are a Psittacula-sized cage, daily freedom in a safe room, and 15 to 20 years of a diet that is not a seed stick. Quiet on the temperament line means you can hold a conversation in the same room. It does not mean the bird can be left without company, without flight, or without a veterinarian who sees parrots.

Housing & Environment Requirements

Provide a spacious cage appropriate for Plum-Headed Parakeets - minimum dimensions should allow full wingspan extension plus room to climb and play. Maintain temperatures between 65-80°F with appropriate humidity levels.

Diet & Nutrition

A balanced diet for Plum-Headed Parakeets should include high-quality pellets as the foundation (60-70% of diet), supplemented with fresh fruits and vegetables (20-30%), and limited seeds/nuts as treats (5-10%). Fresh, clean water should always be available. Avoid avocado, chocolate, caffeine, alcohol, and high-salt foods. Consult an avian veterinarian for species-specific dietary recommendations.

Common Health Issues

Common health concerns for Plum-Headed Parakeets include respiratory infections, psittacine beak and feather disease, feather plucking (often stress-related), and nutritional deficiencies. Birds hide illness well, so annual avian vet checkups are essential.

Grooming & Maintenance

Regular grooming for Plum-Headed Parakeets includes providing bathing opportunities (mist spray or shallow dish), nail trimming as needed, and wing clipping (optional and debated - consult your avian vet). Keep the cage clean with daily spot cleaning and weekly deep cleaning. Replace food and water daily. Rotate toys regularly to prevent boredom.
