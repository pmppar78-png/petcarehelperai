A genus of loud, clear talkers

Amazon parrots are Amazona, more than 30 species. Shops flatten more than 30 species into one range: 10 to 18 inches, 40 to 70 years, high noise, excellent talking, and care that runs from intermediate to advanced. A yellow-naped Amazon and a smaller species do not share a cage size or a bite force. Get the species name before you trust one cage plan.

They talk well when they are going to talk, and they scream when the flock should answer. Spring hormones make many males, and some females, bluff, bite, and sing at volumes that surprise a household that loved the whisper-talk in February. Give them at least 36 by 24 by 36 inches. Large species need more. Seed-only Amazons become obese and short of vitamin A.

Forty years of spring

Buy captive-bred. Several Amazons are protected in the wild. Have an avian vet from AAV who has seen reproductive disease, because chronic egg laying and hormonal aggression are the cases these birds bring in. A quiet Amazon that used to shout is a medical change. See the quiet bird page.

Amazon parrot questions

Which Amazon is the pet?

There is not one. Yellow-naped, double yellow-headed, blue-fronted, and orange-winged Amazons are all common, and size runs from about 10 to 18 inches. Ask for the species.

Why is my Amazon screaming in the spring?

Many Amazons get louder and more aggressive in breeding season. Extra sleep, less petting on the back, and a predictable routine help. A sudden change still deserves a vet if the bird stops eating.

How long do Amazons live?

40 to 70 years. That is a working lifetime. Write down who takes the bird.

What the choana shows

Amazona covers more than 30 species from Central America, South America, and the Caribbean, and the cage plan waits on the species name. In a seed-fed Amazon, an avian veterinarian looks at the choanal papillae on the roof of the mouth. Blunted papillae are a classic sign of vitamin A shortage, described in the Merck Veterinary Manual chapter on pet-bird nutrition, alongside the obesity that same seed bowl produces. Captive-bred birds, a clinic found through the Association of Avian Veterinarians, and a diet led by pellets and vegetables are the practical setup. Yellow-naped, double yellow-headed, blue-fronted, and orange-winged birds are different animals inside the 10 to 18 inch range.
