Wirehaired Pointing Griffon Overview

The Wirehaired Pointing Griffon, often called the "Supreme Gundog" or simply "Griff," was developed in the late 19th century by Dutch sportsman Eduard Korthals. He wanted to create the ideal versatile hunting dog that could point and retrieve in all terrains and conditions. The result is a rugged, adaptable sporting dog with excellent hunting instincts.

Known for their distinctive rough, unkempt coat and friendly expression, Griffons are beloved for their trainability and eagerness to please. They're versatile hunters equally skilled at pointing upland birds and retrieving waterfowl. Beyond hunting, they make wonderful family companions who are devoted to their people and gentle with children.

The Wirehaired Pointing Griffon is a breed that commands attention not just for its physical appearance but for the depth of personality and capability it brings to a household. With a lifespan averaging 12-15 years, the decision to welcome a Wirehaired Pointing Griffon into your family is one that will shape your daily routine, activity levels, and emotional life for well over a decade. This breed's friendly, devoted, trainable temperament is the product of generations of selective breeding for specific traits—understanding this heritage provides valuable insight into why your Wirehaired Pointing Griffon behaves the way it does and what it needs from you as an owner to truly thrive.

The difference between a good Wirehaired Pointing Griffon owner and a great one comes down to understanding what this particular animal actually needs, rather than projecting assumptions based on appearance or general expectations. Every Wirehaired Pointing Griffon has traits rooted in its background that influence behavior, health, and daily care requirements. Working with those traits — instead of against them — is the foundation of a successful experience.

A Wirehaired Pointing Griffon will change your household in ways both expected and surprising. Some of those changes are practical — new equipment, a feeding schedule, a cleaning routine. Others are subtler: a heightened awareness of temperature, a new attentiveness to behavior, a different rhythm to your evenings. Owners who welcome these shifts rather than resisting them tend to build a more harmonious relationship with their Wirehaired Pointing Griffon.

Temperament & Personality

Wirehaired Pointing Griffons have an endearing, people-oriented personality:

  • Friendly & Affectionate: Love being with their families and are known for their devotion.
  • Eager to Please: Highly trainable with a strong desire to work with their handler.
  • Gentle: Patient and gentle, especially with children.
  • Versatile: Adapt well to various activities and living situations.
  • Social: Generally friendly with other dogs and people.

The friendly, devoted, trainable nature of the Wirehaired Pointing Griffon is not a simple personality label—it is a complex behavioral profile shaped by breed history, individual genetics, early socialization experiences, and ongoing environmental factors. What this means in practice is that two Wirehaired Pointing Griffon from different lines, raised in different environments, can display meaningfully different behavioral tendencies while still sharing core breed characteristics. Understanding this distinction helps owners set realistic expectations and develop training strategies tailored to their individual dog rather than relying solely on breed generalizations.

Common Health Issues

Wirehaired Pointing Griffons are generally healthy with few major concerns:

Orthopedic Conditions

  • Hip Dysplasia: Joint condition that can affect mobility; screening recommended.
  • Elbow Dysplasia: Developmental joint abnormality.

Other Conditions

  • Progressive Retinal Atrophy (PRA): Genetic eye condition; DNA testing available.
  • Hypothyroidism: Underactive thyroid; manageable with medication.
  • Ear Infections: Floppy ears can trap moisture; regular cleaning important.

Health Screening Recommendation

Before getting a Wirehaired Pointing Griffon, ask breeders for OFA hip/elbow scores and eye certifications. Consider Embark DNA testing for comprehensive health screening. The breed is relatively healthy compared to many others.

For a Wirehaired Pointing Griffon, the most effective health strategy is a consistent one. That means not just scheduling annual exams, but also staying alert at home to shifts in behavior, appetite, or energy that might otherwise go unnoticed. Owners who approach their Wirehaired Pointing Griffon's health with this level of everyday awareness tend to catch problems earlier and spend less on emergency interventions down the road.

Modern genetic panels offer Wirehaired Pointing Griffon owners a window into breed-specific health risks that were previously invisible until symptoms developed. Armed with this information, you can discuss proactive screening protocols with your vet and adjust care routines before problems take root. The value of genetic testing lies not in predicting exactly what will happen, but in narrowing down what to watch for most closely.

Aging in a Wirehaired Pointing Griffon does not happen overnight, and neither should the adjustments to their care. Gradually introducing senior-appropriate nutrition, moderating exercise intensity, and increasing the frequency of wellness checks creates a smoother transition than waiting for obvious decline. Owners who start these conversations with their vet during middle age tend to see better outcomes in the senior years.

Exercise & Activity Requirements

Wirehaired Pointing Griffons are active sporting dogs:

  • Daily Exercise: 1-2 hours of activity including walks, runs, and play
  • Hunting: Excel as hunting companions for upland birds and waterfowl
  • Swimming: Love water and are excellent swimmers
  • Mental Stimulation: Enjoy puzzle toys and training challenges
  • Dog Sports: Excel at field trials, hunt tests, and agility

Training Tips for Wirehaired Pointing Griffons

Griffons are highly trainable and eager to please:

  • Positive Methods: Respond excellently to positive reinforcement
  • Early Socialization: Expose to various people, places, and situations
  • Consistent Training: Regular training sessions build a strong bond
  • Field Training: If hunting, begin field training early
  • Mental Challenges: Keep their intelligent minds engaged
  • Patience: Some may be slower to mature; be patient

Nutrition & Feeding

Proper nutrition supports the Griffon's active lifestyle:

  • High-Quality Protein: Active dogs need quality protein sources
  • Active Dog Formula: Support for their energy needs
  • Portion Control: Maintain athletic build; typically 2-3 cups daily
  • Joint Support: Foods with glucosamine beneficial for active dogs
  • Omega Fatty Acids: Support coat health

What you feed your Wirehaired Pointing Griffon matters more than most owners realize. Diet affects energy, coat condition, digestive health, immune function, and even behavior. There is no single "best food" — the right choice depends on your Wirehaired Pointing Griffon's age, activity level, any health conditions, and how they respond to specific ingredients. Expect some trial and adjustment, especially in the first year.

Selecting food for your Wirehaired Pointing Griffon does not need to be complicated. Look past the packaging claims and focus on whether the formula supports your Wirehaired Pointing Griffon's actual needs. A quality food that keeps them at a healthy weight with a good coat and reliable digestion is more valuable than the most expensive option on the shelf.

Grooming Requirements

The Griffon's coat requires regular maintenance:

  • Brushing: 2-3 times weekly with a slicker brush
  • Hand Stripping: Occasional stripping maintains proper coat texture
  • Bathing: Every 6-8 weeks or as needed
  • Ear Cleaning: Weekly - important for floppy-eared breeds
  • Beard Care: Clean after meals and drinking
  • Nail Trimming: Every 2-4 weeks

Is a Wirehaired Pointing Griffon Right for You?

Griffons Are Great For:

  • Active families who enjoy outdoor activities
  • Hunters seeking a versatile gundog
  • Those wanting a trainable, devoted companion
  • Families with children
  • People who appreciate low-shedding breeds

Griffons May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Sedentary lifestyles
  • Those without time for daily exercise
  • People wanting a calm, quiet dog
  • Small apartments without outdoor access
  • Those who won't provide mental stimulation

Owning a Wirehaired Pointing Griffon is a commitment measured in years, not months. The enthusiasm of the first few weeks fades, and what remains is a daily routine of feeding, exercise, grooming, and vet visits. If that sounds like a satisfying rhythm rather than a burden, you are probably in a good position to move forward. If it sounds exhausting, it is worth reconsidering.

People who live with a Wirehaired Pointing Griffon tend to develop a deep appreciation for the breed's personality — the friendly, devoted, trainable nature becomes part of the household's rhythm. That bond does not happen overnight, but it builds steadily when care is consistent and expectations are grounded.
