Wirehaired Pointing Griffon Overview

The Wirehaired Pointing Griffon, often called the "Supreme Gundog" or simply "Griff," was developed in the late 19th century by Dutch sportsman Eduard Korthals. He wanted to create the ideal versatile hunting dog that could point and retrieve in all terrains and conditions. The result is a rugged, adaptable sporting dog with excellent hunting instincts.

Known for their distinctive rough, unkempt coat and friendly expression, Griffons are beloved for their trainability and eagerness to please. They're versatile hunters equally skilled at pointing upland birds and retrieving waterfowl. Beyond hunting, they make wonderful family companions who are devoted to their people and gentle with children.

Temperament & Personality

Wirehaired Pointing Griffons have an endearing, people-oriented personality:

  • Friendly & Affectionate: Love being with their families and are known for their devotion.
  • Eager to Please: Highly trainable with a strong desire to work with their handler.
  • Gentle: Patient and gentle, especially with children.
  • Versatile: Adapt well to various activities and living situations.
  • Social: Generally friendly with other dogs and people.

Common Health Issues

Wirehaired Pointing Griffons are generally healthy with few major concerns:

Orthopedic Conditions

  • Hip Dysplasia: Joint condition that can affect mobility; screening recommended.
  • Elbow Dysplasia: Developmental joint abnormality.

Other Conditions

  • Progressive Retinal Atrophy (PRA): Genetic eye condition; DNA testing available.
  • Hypothyroidism: Underactive thyroid; manageable with medication.
  • Ear Infections: Floppy ears can trap moisture; regular cleaning important.

Health Screening Recommendation

Before getting a Wirehaired Pointing Griffon, ask breeders for OFA hip/elbow scores and eye certifications. Consider Embark DNA testing for comprehensive health screening. The breed is relatively healthy compared to many others.

Exercise & Activity Requirements

Wirehaired Pointing Griffons are active sporting dogs:

  • Daily Exercise: 1-2 hours of activity including walks, runs, and play
  • Hunting: Excel as hunting companions for upland birds and waterfowl
  • Swimming: Love water and are excellent swimmers
  • Mental Stimulation: Enjoy puzzle toys and training challenges
  • Dog Sports: Excel at field trials, hunt tests, and agility

Training Tips for Wirehaired Pointing Griffons

Griffons are highly trainable and eager to please:

  • Positive Methods: Respond excellently to positive reinforcement
  • Early Socialization: Expose to various people, places, and situations
  • Consistent Training: Regular training sessions build a strong bond
  • Field Training: If hunting, begin field training early
  • Mental Challenges: Keep their intelligent minds engaged
  • Patience: Some may be slower to mature; be patient

Nutrition & Feeding

Proper nutrition supports the Griffon's active lifestyle:

  • High-Quality Protein: Active dogs need quality protein sources
  • Active Dog Formula: Support for their energy needs
  • Portion Control: Maintain athletic build; typically 2-3 cups daily
  • Joint Support: Foods with glucosamine beneficial for active dogs
  • Omega Fatty Acids: Support coat health

Grooming Requirements

The Griffon's coat requires regular maintenance:

  • Brushing: 2-3 times weekly with a slicker brush
  • Hand Stripping: Occasional stripping maintains proper coat texture
  • Bathing: Every 6-8 weeks or as needed
  • Ear Cleaning: Weekly - important for floppy-eared breeds
  • Beard Care: Clean after meals and drinking
  • Nail Trimming: Every 2-4 weeks

Is a Wirehaired Pointing Griffon Right for You?

Griffons Are Great For:

  • Active families who enjoy outdoor activities
  • Hunters seeking a versatile gundog
  • Those wanting a trainable, devoted companion
  • Families with children
  • People who appreciate low-shedding breeds

Griffons May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Sedentary lifestyles
  • Those without time for daily exercise
  • People wanting a calm, quiet dog
  • Small apartments without outdoor access
  • Those who won't provide mental stimulation
