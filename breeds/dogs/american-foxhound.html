American Foxhound Overview

The American Foxhound is one of America's oldest native breeds, with roots tracing back to 1650 when Robert Brooke brought English hounds to the colonies. George Washington was an early breeder who helped develop the American Foxhound, crossing English hounds with French hounds given to him by the Marquis de Lafayette.

Lighter and faster than their English cousins, American Foxhounds were bred for the rougher terrain of America. They are the state dog of Virginia and remain popular with hunters, though they are rare as pets. Their musical bay, sweet disposition, and athletic ability make them unique companions for the right owners.

Temperament & Personality

American Foxhounds are friendly, easygoing dogs:

  • Sweet & Gentle: They have a kind, loving nature and are excellent with families.
  • Easygoing: Generally laid-back and good-natured when their exercise needs are met.
  • Pack-Oriented: They thrive with other dogs and do not do well as only pets.
  • Independent: Bred to work at a distance from handlers, they think for themselves.
  • Musical Voice: They have a distinctive, melodious bay that carries for miles.
  • Strong Prey Drive: Will pursue scent trails with single-minded determination.

Common Health Issues

American Foxhounds are generally very healthy:

Potential Health Concerns

  • Thrombocytopathy: A rare platelet disorder that can cause bleeding problems; testing available.
  • Hip Dysplasia: Can occur; responsible breeders test for this.
  • Ear Infections: Long ears require regular cleaning to prevent infections.
  • Bloat (GDV): Deep-chested breeds have some risk of gastric torsion.

Less Common Issues

  • Deafness: Occasionally reported.
  • Eye Problems: Some eye conditions may occur.
  • Hypothyroidism: Can develop in middle age.

Health Screening Recommendation

Request hip evaluations and thrombocytopathy testing from breeders when available. Consider Embark DNA testing for comprehensive health screening.

Exercise & Activity Requirements

American Foxhounds need extensive daily exercise:

  • Daily Exercise: 1-2+ hours of vigorous activity is essential
  • Running: Excellent running partners with exceptional endurance
  • Secure Fencing: Essential - they will follow scent trails and can climb or dig
  • Canine Sports: Tracking, rally, and field events provide excellent outlets
  • Mental Stimulation: Scent games and puzzle toys help tire their minds
  • Never Off-Leash: In unfenced areas, scent drive overrides training

Training Tips for American Foxhounds

American Foxhounds require patient, consistent training:

  • Food Motivation: Most respond well to treats as training rewards
  • Patience Required: They can be stubborn and easily distracted by scents
  • Positive Methods: They respond better to rewards than corrections
  • Scent Training: Channel their natural abilities into productive activities
  • Early Socialization: Expose to various situations while young
  • Recall Challenges: Accept that reliable recall may be impossible when scent is present

Nutrition & Feeding

American Foxhounds need quality nutrition for their active lifestyle:

  • High-Quality Protein: Supports their active metabolism and lean muscle
  • Monitor Calories: Adjust based on activity level - they can become overweight if under-exercised
  • Two Meals Daily: Split feedings help maintain energy
  • Fresh Water: Always available, especially during and after exercise
  • Avoid Free Feeding: They can overeat if food is always available

Grooming Requirements

American Foxhounds have easy grooming needs:

  • Brushing: Weekly brushing with a hound glove removes loose hair
  • Bathing: As needed - they have a natural "hound" odor
  • Ear Cleaning: Weekly - essential for their long, floppy ears
  • Nail Trimming: Every 2-3 weeks
  • Dental Care: Daily brushing recommended

Is an American Foxhound Right for You?

American Foxhounds Are Great For:

  • Active families in rural or suburban settings
  • Homes with other dogs - they need canine companionship
  • Runners and outdoor enthusiasts
  • Those with large, securely fenced yards
  • Hunting enthusiasts

American Foxhounds May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Apartment dwellers
  • Sedentary owners
  • Homes where the dog will be the only pet
  • Those bothered by barking (they bay loudly and often)
  • Neighbors close by who may be disturbed by vocalization
  • Those wanting reliable off-leash obedience

Related Breeds to Consider

If you're interested in American Foxhounds, you might also consider:

  • English Foxhound - Sturdier build, similar temperament
  • Treeing Walker Coonhound - Related breed, developed for different quarry
  • Harrier - Smaller, similar to both foxhound breeds
  • Beagle - Smaller scent hound, more adaptable
