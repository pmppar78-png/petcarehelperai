Black and Tan Coonhound Overview

The Black and Tan Coonhound is one of America's earliest native breeds, developed from Bloodhounds and Foxhounds brought to the colonies. They were the first coonhound breed to be recognized by the AKC (in 1945) and are known for their exceptional tracking ability and distinctive appearance.

With their striking black coat with rich tan markings, long ears, and soulful expression, Black and Tan Coonhounds are both beautiful and functional. Bred to track raccoons and larger game like bear and mountain lion, they possess incredible stamina and one of the most powerful noses in the dog world.

The Black and Tan Coonhound is a breed that commands attention not just for its physical appearance but for the depth of personality and capability it brings to a household. With a lifespan averaging 10-12 years, the decision to welcome a Black and Tan Coonhound into your family is one that will shape your daily routine, activity levels, and emotional life for well over a decade. This breed's easygoing, friendly, bright temperament is the product of generations of selective breeding for specific traits—understanding this heritage provides valuable insight into why your Black and Tan Coonhound behaves the way it does and what it needs from you as an owner to truly thrive.

A Black and Tan Coonhound is a pack scent hound of 65 to 110 pounds and 23 to 27 inches, easygoing once the hunt is spent, friendly with children and other dogs, and independent because the old job happened far from the handler. The voice is a deep bay that carries. The nose does not take the day off. One to two hours is the exercise floor. Anyone judging this dog by a calm couch photo is meeting the recovered hound, not the one that still has a line to run. Lifespan is about 10 to 12 years. Food runs $600 to $1,000.

Welcoming a Black And Tan Coonhound into your home is less about adding a pet and more about adopting a new set of daily responsibilities.

Temperament & Personality

Black and Tan Coonhounds are gentle, friendly dogs.

  • Easygoing & Mellow: When their exercise needs are met, they are calm, relaxed companions.
  • Friendly & Social: They love people and are excellent with children.
  • Pack-Oriented: They get along wonderfully with other dogs.
  • Independent: Bred to work at a distance, they think for themselves.
  • Vocal: They have a deep, melodious bay that carries for miles.
  • Strong Prey Drive: Their tracking instincts are powerful and persistent.

The easygoing, friendly, bright nature of the Black and Tan Coonhound is not a simple personality label—it is a complex behavioral profile shaped by breed history, individual genetics, early socialization experiences, and ongoing environmental factors. What this means in practice is that two Black and Tan Coonhound from different lines, raised in different environments, can display meaningfully different behavioral tendencies while still sharing core breed characteristics.

Coonhound manners are built in layers and they fall apart in layers. A Black and Tan that met people and dogs as a puppy, then spent a year alone in a kennel, often comes back noisy, leash-rude, or overly intense with unfamiliar dogs. A dog with a thin start can still improve if the new exposures stay positive and regular. Pack-oriented is the breed truth: they usually like other dogs, and that is practice, not a free pass at the dog park. Children do well with a friendly hound that has been taught not to bay in their faces. Keep the prey drive on a long line until the recall exists, which in a scent hound is a project, not a weekend.

Tired legs and an idle nose will still send a Black and Tan Coonhound to bay, chew, or pace a fence for the rest of the evening. One to two hours has to include scent, not only miles. Drag a line, hide food in brush, or let the dog trail a known path, because this is a 65 to 110 pound hunter bred to stick on a raccoon track until the handler catches up. Puzzle toys help on a rain day and do not replace outdoor nose work. The melodious bay is the breed's report that it is hunting, and it will be used on the mailman if that is the only quarry available. Mental work is also how you keep a dog-friendly pack hound from inventing friction out of boredom. Long ears need a clean after the wet trails. A deep chest should not get a huge meal and then a hard run. Hips, ectropion, and hypothyroidism are reasons the plan gets shorter in the later part of a 10 to 12 year life. Coonhound paralysis, a temporary neurologic reaction linked to raccoon saliva, is the odd emergency this breed's job creates. Do not treat a sudden weakness after a woods walk as ordinary soreness.

Common Health Issues

Black and Tan Coonhounds have some breed-specific health concerns.

Potential Health Concerns

  • Hip Dysplasia: Common in larger breeds; breeders should test.
  • Ear Infections: Their extremely long ears require diligent cleaning.
  • Bloat (GDV): Deep-chested breeds are at risk for gastric torsion.
  • Coonhound Paralysis: A neurological reaction to raccoon saliva causing temporary paralysis.

Less Common Issues

  • Ectropion: Droopy lower eyelids that can cause eye issues.
  • Hypothyroidism: Underactive thyroid can occur.
  • Cataracts: Eye problems may develop.

Health Screening Recommendation

Request hip and elbow evaluations from breeders. Be aware of Coonhound Paralysis if hunting raccoons. Consider Embark DNA testing for comprehensive health screening.

Prevention for a Black and Tan Coonhound is ears, eyes, hips, and a stomach you do not gamble with. The extremely long ears infect if they stay dirty. Ectropion leaves a droopy lid that collects debris. Hip dysplasia is common enough in a 65 to 110 pound hound that breeding dogs should be tested. Bloat in a deep chest is the after-hours emergency. Hypothyroidism explains some middle-aged weight gain and dull coats. Routine care at $350 to $600 is the scheduled version of all of that. A 10 to 12 year life is short enough that skipping the middle years is skipping most of the dog.

What you can learn before a Black and Tan puppy comes home is hip status in the parents and a frank talk about ectropion, thyroid, and bloat history. Coonhound paralysis is not a simple DNA checkbox, so the practical genetic-and-family question is whether relatives stayed sound after real hunting. Those answers should change how hard you work a young hound and how carefully you clean ears after every wet trail. Supplements do not replace films. A 23 to 27 inch scent hound will run 1 to 2 hours if you allow it. The paperwork tells you whether that is wise.

Senior Black and Tan care starts while the bay is still strong. Inside 10 to 12 years, hips and a hypothyroid metabolism often change the dog before the face looks old. Shorten the trailing work, keep the nose busy at a walk, and get the long ears dried every time. Ectropion can make the eyes look 'houndy' and still be a problem that needs ointment or surgery rather than a compliment. Bloat risk does not decline with age. A pack hound that loses its canine friends often loses its off switch. Plan the company as carefully as the bed.

Exercise & Activity Requirements

Black and Tan Coonhounds need significant daily exercise.

  • Daily Exercise: 1-2 hours of vigorous activity
  • Tracking & Scent Work: Their nose needs regular work
  • Secure Fencing: Essential - they will follow scent trails relentlessly
  • Hunting: Many still work as hunting dogs
  • Long Walks: They have excellent endurance for hiking
  • Never Off-Leash: Their tracking instinct overrides recall

Training Tips for Black and Tan Coonhounds

Black and Tan Coonhounds need patient, consistent training.

  • Food Motivation: They typically respond well to treats
  • Positive Reinforcement: They respond poorly to harsh corrections
  • Patience: Training requires time due to their independent nature
  • Scent Training: Channel their abilities productively
  • Early Socialization: Expose to many situations while young
  • Accept Limitations: Off-leash reliability may not be achievable

Nutrition & Feeding

Black and Tan Coonhounds need quality nutrition for their size.

  • High-Quality Protein: Supports their large, muscular frame
  • Multiple Meals: 2-3 meals daily to reduce bloat risk
  • Monitor Weight: They can become overweight if under-exercised
  • Slow Feeders: May help prevent gulping and bloat
  • Fresh Water: Always available

Black and Tan feeding is individual inside a wide 65 to 110 pound band. Premium food is $600 to $1,000. A lean trail dog and a backyard hound with the same pedigree do not share a cup. Some do well on a standard large-breed kibble. Others need a change when hypothyroidism, a quieter season, or a touchy stomach shows up. Adjust with the vet when the body changes, and judge the result by ribs, recovery after 1 to 2 hours, coat, and stool. Split meals in a deep chest. Do not free-feed a scent hound that will hoover the bowl and then ask to hunt.

Protein and fat percentages on the bag are useful for a large hound, and calories per cup matter so you are not guessing across a 65 to 110 pound Black and Tan range. Five minutes with the guaranteed analysis beats a front-of-bag hunting scene. The analysis will not mention ectropion, ears, or bloat. Those are why the dog stays lean and why meals are split. If the coat dulls and the weight climbs on an unchanged cup, ask about the thyroid before you buy a 'skin and coat' formula.

Grooming Requirements

Black and Tan Coonhounds have moderate grooming needs.

  • Brushing: Weekly brushing removes loose hair
  • Bathing: As needed - they have a strong hound odor
  • Ear Cleaning: 2-3 times weekly - their long ears need constant attention
  • Nail Trimming: Every 2-3 weeks
  • Dental Care: Daily brushing recommended
  • Skin Folds: Check and clean facial wrinkles if present

Black and Tan Coonhounds Are Great For:

  • Active families with large, fenced yards
  • Hunters wanting a versatile tracking dog
  • Homes with other dogs
  • Those who appreciate the classic hound personality
  • People with space and time for a large, active dog

Black and Tan Coonhounds May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Apartment dwellers
  • Close neighborhoods (very vocal)
  • Sedentary owners
  • Those sensitive to "hound odor"
  • People wanting off-leash reliability
  • Homes with small pets

Quiet apartments, single-dog homes, and anyone who needs silence are a poor fit for a Black and Tan Coonhound. The bay carries. The nose will pull a 65 to 110 pound body through a bad fence. They are excellent with children and other dogs when the 1 to 2 hours are real, and miserable company when that work is a theory. Meet hunting hounds in person, hear the voice, and look at the ear-cleaning chore before you decide a 10 to 12 year pack dog belongs in your house.

People who live with a Black and Tan Coonhound tend to develop a deep appreciation for the breed's personality — the easygoing, friendly, bright nature becomes part of the household's rhythm.
