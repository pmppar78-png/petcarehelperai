A working dog with a short clock

Rottweilers stand about 22 to 27 inches and weigh 80 to 135 pounds. Plan on 9 to 10 years. They are loyal and confident when someone has actually trained them, and dangerous when the training was a weekend video. Exercise needs are high. A bored young male will invent a job at the fence.

Same-sex friction is ordinary. Do not assume two adults will share a yard because they shared a puppy class. Children in the home need the dog to have a place to leave, and the dog needs a handler who can interrupt rude behavior without a fight. Food guarding should be taken seriously the first time it happens.

Bone, heart, and a DNA panel

Osteosarcoma is common enough that a sudden limp in a middle-aged Rottweiler is not "he slept wrong." Subaortic stenosis is a heart defect to listen for in puppies, then confirm with a cardiologist if a murmur is present. JLPP is a DNA test, not a guess. Hip dysplasia still matters. VCA has a readable osteosarcoma page. A limp that worsens over a week belongs on the limping guide.

Rottweiler questions

Are Rottweilers safe with children?

A well-bred, trained adult can be steady with kids the dog knows. Size and a guarding instinct mean this is not a dog you leave to supervise a party. Same-sex dog fights are common.

What is JLPP?

Juvenile laryngeal paralysis and polyneuropathy, a genetic disease that shows up in young Rottweilers as noisy breathing and weak legs. Breeding dogs should be DNA-tested.

How long do they live?

Often 9 to 10 years. Cancer, especially bone cancer, is a common reason the life is shorter than a Labrador's.
