Irish Wolfhound Overview

The Irish Wolfhound is the tallest of all dog breeds, with a history dating back over 2,000 years in Ireland. Originally bred to hunt wolves, elk, and wild boar, these magnificent dogs were so valued by Irish chieftains that they were given as royal gifts and their export was once restricted by law.

Despite their imposing size and fierce hunting heritage, Irish Wolfhounds are famously described as "gentle giants" - calm, patient, and remarkably sweet-natured. Their commanding presence combined with their gentle temperament makes them beloved family companions.

Temperament & Personality

Irish Wolfhounds have a wonderfully gentle nature:

  • Gentle & Patient: Despite their enormous size, they are remarkably gentle with children and small animals.
  • Dignified & Calm: They carry themselves with quiet nobility and are not hyperactive or demanding.
  • Courageous: When needed, they will protect their family with fierce determination.
  • Sensitive: They are emotionally intuitive and responsive to their family's moods.
  • Friendly: They are generally welcoming to strangers - not ideal guard dogs.
  • Prey Drive: They retain hunting instincts and may chase small animals.

Common Health Issues

Irish Wolfhounds have significant health concerns, contributing to their shorter lifespan:

Major Health Concerns

  • Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM): Heart disease is the leading cause of death. Annual cardiac screening essential.
  • Osteosarcoma: Bone cancer is common in giant breeds. Early detection is crucial.
  • Bloat (GDV): Deep-chested breeds are at very high risk for life-threatening gastric torsion.
  • Liver Shunt: Portosystemic shunt can occur; testing recommended.

Other Health Concerns

  • Hip & Elbow Dysplasia: Joint problems common in giant breeds.
  • Pneumonia: Aspiration pneumonia can occur, especially after anesthesia.
  • Von Willebrand's Disease: A bleeding disorder; DNA testing available.
  • Anesthesia Sensitivity: Sighthound-appropriate protocols required.

Health Screening Recommendation

Request cardiac evaluations, hip/elbow scores, and liver function tests from breeders. Annual cardiac screening throughout life is essential. Consider Embark DNA testing for comprehensive genetic screening. Discuss bloat prevention surgery with your veterinarian.

Exercise & Activity Requirements

Irish Wolfhounds need moderate exercise with special considerations:

  • Daily Exercise: 30-60 minutes of moderate activity, more as adults
  • Puppy Caution: Limit exercise until growth plates close (18-24 months) to protect developing joints
  • Secure Fencing: 5-6 foot fence recommended - they can clear shorter barriers
  • Soft Surfaces: Running on grass is preferred to reduce joint impact
  • Lure Coursing: Good activity for adult wolfhounds when properly conditioned
  • Couch Time: They love lounging and need large, comfortable resting areas

Training Tips for Irish Wolfhounds

Irish Wolfhounds are more trainable than most sighthounds:

  • Early Training Essential: Start young before they reach full size - a well-trained giant is a joy
  • Positive Reinforcement: They respond well to gentle, reward-based training
  • Leash Manners: Critical - an adult pulling can be dangerous
  • Socialization: Expose to many experiences while young to build confidence
  • Gentle Corrections: They are sensitive and harsh methods are unnecessary and harmful
  • Recall Training: Important, though prey drive may override training at times

Nutrition & Feeding

Irish Wolfhounds require careful nutrition management:

  • Giant Breed Puppy Food: Essential for controlled growth to protect developing bones and joints
  • Multiple Small Meals: 2-3 meals daily to reduce bloat risk
  • Slow Feeders: Prevent gulping which increases bloat risk
  • Rest After Eating: No exercise for 1-2 hours after meals
  • Quality Protein: Essential for maintaining muscle mass
  • Controlled Growth: Avoid overfeeding puppies - steady growth is healthier than rapid growth

Grooming Requirements

Irish Wolfhound grooming is moderate:

  • Brushing: 2-3 times weekly to remove dead hair and prevent mats
  • Hand Stripping: Optional for maintaining proper coat texture
  • Bathing: Every 6-8 weeks or as needed
  • Beard Cleaning: Wipe after eating and drinking
  • Nail Trimming: Every 2-3 weeks - large nails can be challenging
  • Ear Cleaning: Weekly checks and cleaning
  • Dental Care: Daily brushing recommended

Is an Irish Wolfhound Right for You?

Irish Wolfhounds Are Great For:

  • Those wanting a gentle, affectionate giant companion
  • Families with space for a very large dog
  • People prepared for a shorter lifespan and potential health issues
  • Those who can provide appropriate nutrition and veterinary care
  • Calm households that appreciate a dignified companion

Irish Wolfhounds May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Apartment dwellers or those with limited space
  • Those on a tight budget (high care costs)
  • Families wanting a long-lived companion
  • People unable to provide giant-breed appropriate care
  • Those with very small children (accidental knockdowns possible)
  • Homes with small pets that could trigger prey drive

  • Borzoi - Elegant sighthound, somewhat smaller
  • Great Dane - Similar size, different appearance
