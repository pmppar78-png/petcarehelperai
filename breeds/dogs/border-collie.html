The work drive is the whole dog

Border Collies weigh about 30 to 55 pounds and stand 18 to 22 inches. Lifespan is often 12 to 15 years. They were built to move sheep by eye contact and small body cues. In a suburb they will move bicycles, cats, and toddlers the same way unless someone gives them a legal job. The stats table's "very high, two hours and up" is a floor, and half of that time should be thinking, not only running.

They are sensitive. Harsh corrections shut them down or make them frantic. They can be cool with strangers. Early, boring exposure to people and dogs matters more than a busy dog park. A mostly white dog should have a hearing test.

Diseases you can actually test

Trapped neutrophil syndrome is an immune failure that kills affected puppies; DNA testing exists and carriers should not be paired. Neuronal ceroid lipofuscinosis is a storage disease that destroys the nervous system, also testable. Collie eye anomaly, progressive retinal atrophy, and primary lens luxation threaten sight. MDR1 changes how certain drugs are tolerated, so the result should be in the clinic file before a new prescription. Epilepsy, hip dysplasia, and osteochondritis dissecans are the reasons a first seizure or a persistent limp is not "too much fetch."

Yearly costs often run about $2,000 to $4,600 once classes and gear are included. Merck describes collie eye anomaly without the trial-dog romance. Seizures or a sudden limp use the checker and the limping page.

Border Collie questions

Are Border Collies the smartest breed?

They top lists that measure how fast a dog learns an obedience cue. That is a narrow kind of intelligence. They also learn, in one repetition, that herding the children works. A smart dog without a job is a difficult dog.

What DNA tests are specific to Border Collies?

Trapped neutrophil syndrome and neuronal ceroid lipofuscinosis are fatal and testable. Collie eye anomaly, progressive retinal atrophy, and MDR1 drug sensitivity belong on the same sheet. A breeder who only talks about hips is half done.

Can a Border Collie be happy as a pet?

Yes, in a home that trains every day and exercises hard: herding, agility, scent work, or a real running schedule. A condo and a tennis ball on Saturday is a common way to get a dog that stares, paces, and nips.
