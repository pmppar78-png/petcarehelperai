A water dog the size of a person

Newfoundlands stand about 26 to 28 inches and weigh 100 to 150 pounds. Plan on about 9 to 10 years. They are sweet, heavy shedders, and moderate exercisers: a walk and a swim, not a marathon. The coat mats if it is ignored, and it holds water for hours after a pond.

They are usually good with other dogs and fond of children. A lean Newf lives more comfortably than a fluffy one you cannot feel ribs on.

A heart murmur and a urine crystal

Subaortic stenosis is a heart defect. A puppy murmur needs a cardiologist, not a promise that large dogs "grow out of it." Cystinuria makes stones that can block a male dog. Straining with no urine is an emergency. Hip and elbow dysplasia are common. VCA explains urinary blockage. That page, and the vomiting and straining notes, matter more than another brush.

Newfoundland questions

Are Newfoundlands nanny dogs?

They are often patient. A hundred-pound dog can still knock a toddler over and can still guard a bone. Supervision is the safety plan, not the nickname.

How much do they drool?

A lot, especially after water or exercise. Keep a towel by the door. If you dislike slime on walls, pick a different giant breed or accept the laundry.

Can they live in a hot climate?

Only with air conditioning and sense. The coat and the size make heatstroke realistic on a warm hike. Swim in clean water, then dry the ears.
