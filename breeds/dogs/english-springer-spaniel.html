Merry, and still a hunting spaniel

English Springer Spaniels are about 40 to 50 pounds and 19 to 20 inches, with feathered ears and a lifespan around 12 to 14 years. They are friendly to the point of being useless as guards, and they are busy. Families who hunt or hike fit them. Families who want a quiet spaniel on the rug will meet a dog that dismantles the rug. Separation anxiety shows up when they are left alone for long stretches, because the original job was staying with a person in a field.

Grooming is real money, often $400 to $800 a year, mostly so the ears can be managed. Weekly ear checks are the health habit that prevents the rest of the skin from being blamed. They swim and retrieve. Use that.

An enzyme, then the eyes

PFK deficiency is the Springer-specific test. Dogs that lack the enzyme can cramp, pass dark urine, and become anemic after exertion or even a long bout of barking. It is genetic and it is avoidable in a breeding program that tests. Progressive retinal atrophy, retinal dysplasia, cataracts, and glaucoma threaten sight. Hips and elbows should be evaluated. Autoimmune hemolytic anemia and immune-mediated low platelets are the emergencies with pale gums or unexplained bruising. Epilepsy occurs in some lines.

A routine year often costs about $2,150 to $4,150. VCA explains ear disease and anemia without a field-trial anecdote. Weakness, dark urine, or collapse uses the checker immediately. Chronic scratching uses the itching guide so the ears are actually examined.

Springer Spaniel questions

What is PFK deficiency?

Phosphofructokinase deficiency is an inherited enzyme problem in English Springers. Affected dogs can develop muscle disease and a hemolytic anemia, sometimes after hard exercise, with dark urine and weakness. DNA testing exists. Breeding dogs should have results.

How much exercise does a Springer need?

The stats table says high, 1 to 2 hours. They were built to quarter fields all day. A stroll around the block leaves a dog that is still merry and now destructive.

Do Springers suddenly become aggressive?

A rare idiopathic aggression has been described in the breed. It is not the ordinary Springer, which is friendly. A dog that bites without a trigger needs a veterinarian and a behavior specialist, not a nickname.
