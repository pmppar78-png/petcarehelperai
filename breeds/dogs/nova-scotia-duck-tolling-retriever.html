Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever Overview

The Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever, or "Toller," is the smallest of the retriever breeds and one of the most unique hunting dogs ever developed. Bred in Nova Scotia in the early 19th century, Tollers were designed to lure (or "toll") ducks within shooting range by frolicking playfully along the shoreline, piquing the curiosity of waterfowl. Once the birds were close enough, the dog would then retrieve them after the shot. With their striking red-gold coat, white markings, and fox-like appearance, Tollers are both beautiful and highly capable working dogs. They are intensely focused, energetic, and possess a natural love of water that makes them exceptional companions for active outdoor enthusiasts.

Temperament & Personality

The Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever is known for being intelligent, alert, outgoing.

Their very high exercise needs mean they require owners who can commit to regular physical activity and mental stimulation.

Managing the Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever's energy and drive within a household context requires strategic thinking rather than just exercise.

Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retrievers are generally healthy dogs, but like all breeds, they can be prone to certain conditions.

Housing & Environment

Needs an active home with outdoor access. Not ideal for apartment living. A fenced yard is important. Proximity to water for swimming is a bonus.

Exercise & Activity Requirements

90+ minutes of vigorous exercise daily. Needs both physical and mental stimulation. Excels in dock diving, agility, flyball, and obedience competitions.

Grooming Requirements

Brush thoroughly 2-3 times weekly. Heavy seasonal shedding requires daily brushing. Bathe monthly. Keep ears clean and trim nails regularly.
