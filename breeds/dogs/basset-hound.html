Nose down, back at risk

Basset Hounds are scent hounds of up to 15 inches and 40 to 65 pounds. Lifespan is often 12 to 13 years. The loose skin and the sad eyes are the postcard. The spine is a long lever, the ears are incubators, and the chest can still twist a stomach. They are patient with children and other dogs, independent when a smell is better than your cue, and louder than a short dog is supposed to be.

Walks should be slow and smelly. Jumping off the bed is how discs go. Keep them lean; the skin hides fat until the ribs are a guess. Ear cleaning is a weekly chore, listed as its own cost because infections are the default if you skip it.

Eyes, discs, stomach

Glaucoma in this breed can destroy vision quickly. Redness, cloudiness, or a dog that hides from light needs a clinic that can measure eye pressure. Intervertebral disc disease looks like a yelp and a weak hind end. Entropion and ectropion roll the lids the wrong way and scratch the eye. Hip and elbow dysplasia occur. Bloat does not care that the dog is close to the ground.

A routine year often totals about $1,550 to $2,900. Merck describes glaucoma and disc disease without the cartoon. A yelp when lifted uses the limping guide the same day. Unproductive retching uses the checker while you drive.

Basset Hound questions

Do Bassets only need a short walk?

The stats table says 30 to 45 minutes, and that is fair if it includes sniffing. They are not jog partners. They are also not furniture. Extra weight blows discs and makes the ears worse.

Why are Basset eyes an emergency sometimes?

Primary glaucoma is common and painful and can take the eye. A suddenly red, cloudy, or squinting eye is same-day, not Monday. A DNA test and an eye exam are reasonable questions for a breeder.

Can a short dog bloat?

Yes. The chest is deep even though the legs are not. A hard swollen abdomen and retching that produces nothing is an emergency hospital trip, the same as in a Great Dane.
