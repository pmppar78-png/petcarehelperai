Maltipoo Overview

The Maltipoo is a beloved designer breed created by crossing a Maltese with a Toy or Miniature Poodle. This adorable hybrid combines the gentle, affectionate nature of the Maltese with the intelligence and hypoallergenic qualities of the Poodle, resulting in an ideal companion dog.

Maltipoos have become extremely popular due to their small size, low-shedding coats, and sweet temperaments. They're often described as "forever puppies" due to their youthful appearance and playful nature that persists throughout their lives.

Parent Breed Characteristics

  • Maltese: An ancient breed known for their silky white coat, gentle disposition, and devotion to their owners. Bred specifically as companion dogs for thousands of years.
  • Toy/Miniature Poodle: Highly intelligent, hypoallergenic, and elegant. Among the most trainable of all breeds, with a playful yet dignified personality.

A Maltipoo is a Maltese crossed with a toy or miniature poodle. Lifespan averaging 12-16 years is a long time to comb a coat that mats to the skin. Adult weight is not a breed standard. Ask what each parent weighed. The Maltese side brings tear staining and a white coat that shows dirt and sunburn. The poodle side brings a curly or wavy coat and the same crowded teeth. Hypoallergenic is a sales word. Dander is still there. Lifespan averaging 12-16 years is the comb, the dental, and the knees, not a low-work decade.

The mix does not cancel the parents' diseases. Luxating patellas, Legg-Calve-Perthes disease, progressive retinal atrophy, and crowded teeth are already on this page's list because both breeds contribute them. A puppy from untested parents is not a blank slate. Ask for the poodle parent's patella grade and eye exam, and for the size of both dogs, before you pay for a color.

Daily life is the coat, the teeth, and a short walk, not a hike. A missed week of combing becomes a shavedown and a skin infection. A missed year of dental work becomes extractions under anesthesia in a toy dog. Plan the groomer and the dental before the puppy is cute enough to postpone both.

Temperament & Personality

Maltipoos are known for their charming personalities.

  • Affectionate: Love to cuddle and be close to their owners; excellent lap dogs.
  • Gentle: Sweet-natured and typically non-aggressive.
  • Playful: Enjoy games and play sessions throughout their lives.
  • Alert: Good watchdogs who will alert to visitors (may be prone to barking).
  • Social: Enjoy meeting new people and generally friendly with everyone.
  • Sensitive: Respond best to gentle handling and positive reinforcement.

Affectionate and playful does not mean a Maltipoo is sturdy. Puppies and very small adults can develop hypoglycemia if they skip a meal: wobbling, weakness, or a puppy that will not eat needs sugar on the gums and a vet. White shaker syndrome, tremors in small white dogs, is a vet visit, not a video. Portosystemic shunts, a liver bypass, show up in both parent breeds. A puppy that stays small, heads-presses, or seems drunk after eating needs imaging, not a smaller bowl.

Common Health Issues

Maltipoos can inherit health conditions from either parent breed.

hip and joint issues

  • Luxating Patella: Common in small breeds; kneecap slips out of place.
  • Legg-Calve-Perthes Disease: Hip joint deterioration seen in toy breeds.

Eye & Dental Issues

  • Progressive Retinal Atrophy (PRA): Can cause gradual blindness.
  • Dental Problems: Small mouths prone to crowding and tooth decay.
  • Tear Staining: Common cosmetic issue, especially in lighter-colored dogs.

Other Concerns

  • White Shaker Syndrome: Tremors that can affect small white dogs.
  • Hypoglycemia: Low blood sugar, especially in puppies and very small dogs.
  • Epilepsy: Can be inherited from Poodle lines.
  • Portosystemic Shunt: Liver condition seen in both parent breeds.

Health Screening Recommendation

Embark DNA testing When getting a Maltipoo, request eye certifications, patella evaluations, and cardiac exams from both parents.

Watch the kneecaps, the eyes, the gums, and the appetite. A skip in the hind leg is the patella until a vet says otherwise. A mouth that smells is dental disease. A puppy that fades at lunchtime may be low on blood sugar. Those are the checks, not a general promise to pay attention.

A DNA panel is useful after you know the parents. It does not replace an eye exam or a patella score on the poodle parent. Progressive retinal atrophy and other named risks can be in the mix. Use the test to ask the vet what to recheck, and still brush the teeth and comb the coat. A panel does not groom the dog.

A senior Maltipoo often needs the dental that was postponed and a slower walk if a kneecap or the heart is the limit. Weigh the dog monthly. A coat that hides the ribs also hides weight gain, and extra weight makes a loose patella hurt sooner. Ask the vet to listen to the heart at each visit. Small dogs develop valve disease as they age.

The table on this page already prices grooming at several hundred dollars a year and dental as its own line. Those are not optional add-ons for this mix. A matted coat shaved at the vet costs more than a standing groom. An anesthesia dental on a toy dog costs more than the puppy's first bowl. Insurance is worth pricing before the patella or the mouth becomes a claim.

Exercise & Activity Requirements

Maltipoos have relatively modest exercise needs.

  • Short Walks: Several brief walks are better than one long walk
  • Indoor Play: Can meet much of their exercise needs indoors
  • Avoid Overexertion: Small size means they tire more quickly

Training Tips for Maltipoos

Maltipoos are intelligent and respond well to training.

  • Positive Reinforcement: Use treats, praise, and gentle methods only
  • Potty Training: May require extra patience due to small bladder size
  • Barking Control: Address excessive barking early with training
  • Socialization: Expose to various people, places, and experiences
  • Consistency: Small dogs need the same consistent rules as big dogs

Nutrition & Feeding

Comb to the skin on a schedule you can keep, feed a small dog often enough that a tiny puppy does not crash, and book the dental before the gums are angry. That is the routine. The rest is the individual dog's knees, eyes, and teeth.

  • Small Breed Formula: Smaller kibble size and appropriate nutrients
  • Frequent Meals: 3-4 small meals daily for puppies, 2-3 for adults
  • Quality Protein: Essential for maintaining lean muscle
  • Prevent Hypoglycemia: Don't let small Maltipoos go too long without food
  • Dental Health: Consider dental-specific foods or treats

Grooming Requirements

Maltipoos require regular grooming maintenance.

  • Coat Types: Silky, wavy, or curly depending on genetics
  • White Shaker Syndrome, Luxating Patella, and Legg-Calve-Perthes Disease and the coat on this profile set brushing for the Maltipoo, done on dry hair.
  • Face Cleaning: Daily cleaning around eyes to prevent tear staining
  • Luxating Patella, Legg-Calve-Perthes Disease, Epilepsy, and PRA get harder to manage when the mouth is skipped, so tooth brushing for the Maltipoo aims at the molars several times a week.
  • Legg-Calve-Perthes Disease, Epilepsy, PRA, and Progressive Retinal Atrophy PRA set the nail plan for the Maltipoo: trim when the nails click, and part any feathering so the quick stays visible.

Maltipoos Are Great For:

  • Apartment dwellers
  • Seniors looking for a companion
  • Allergy sufferers (low-shedding)
  • First-time dog owners
  • Those wanting an affectionate lap dog
  • People who work from home or can be with their dog often

Maltipoos May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Families with very young children (too fragile)
  • Those away from home frequently
  • People who dislike grooming maintenance
  • Those wanting a jogging or hiking companion
  • Homes with large, rambunctious dogs
