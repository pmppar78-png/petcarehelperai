Old English Sheepdog Overview

The Old English Sheepdog, affectionately known as the "OES" or "Bobtail," is a large, athletic herding breed from England. Despite their name, they're not actually that old as breeds go - they were developed in the early 19th century to drive cattle and sheep to market. Their distinctive shaggy coat protected them from harsh weather while working.

With their bear-like shuffle, peek-a-boo hairdo, and clownish personality, Old English Sheepdogs have charmed families for generations. They became popular in American advertising, most notably as the Dulux paint dog. Their gentle, playful nature and love of children make them wonderful family companions, though their grooming needs are substantial.

Temperament & Personality

Old English Sheepdogs have a delightful, fun-loving personality.

  • Playful & Clownish: Known for their goofy antics and entertaining behavior.
  • Gentle & Patient: Excellent with children; often become beloved nannies.
  • Adaptable: Adjust well to various family situations and living arrangements.
  • Social: Love being part of family activities; don't do well left alone.
  • Alert: Good watchdogs with a distinctive deep bark.

Common Health Issues

Old English Sheepdogs have several health concerns to be aware of:

joint and skeletal conditions

  • Hip Dysplasia: Common in large breeds; screening recommended.
  • Elbow Dysplasia: Joint developmental abnormality.

Eye Conditions

  • Progressive Retinal Atrophy (PRA): Genetic eye disease; DNA testing available.
  • Primary Ciliary Dyskinesia: Can affect eyes and respiratory system.

Other Conditions

  • Deafness: Some individuals may be affected.
  • Autoimmune Conditions: Including immune-mediated hemolytic anemia (IMHA).
  • Gastric Dilatation-Volvulus (Bloat): Deep-chested breeds at risk.

Health Screening Recommendation

Exercise & Activity Requirements

Old English Sheepdogs need regular exercise to stay healthy.

  • Daily Exercise: 1-2 hours of activity including walks and play
  • Mental Stimulation: Puzzle toys and games engage their intelligence
  • Herding Activities: Enjoy herding games and activities
  • Cool Weather: Prefer cooler temperatures; prone to overheating
  • Dog Sports: Can excel at agility and herding trials

Training Tips for Old English Sheepdogs

OES are intelligent but can have a stubborn streak.

  • Start Early: Begin training and socialization as puppies while they're manageable
  • Positive Methods: Respond well to praise, treats, and play rewards
  • Consistency: Clear, consistent rules help them understand expectations
  • Patience: May need extra patience; don't take their stubbornness personally
  • Grooming Training: Train to accept extensive grooming from puppyhood
  • Herding Management: Redirect herding instincts appropriately

Nutrition & Feeding

Proper nutrition supports OES health.

  • Large Breed Formula: Choose food appropriate for large breeds
  • Portion Control: Monitor weight; typically 3-4 cups daily
  • Joint Support: Foods with glucosamine and omega-3s beneficial
  • Quality Protein: Support for their active lifestyle

Grooming Requirements

Old English Sheepdogs have INTENSIVE grooming needs - possibly the highest of any breed.

  • Professional Grooming: Every 4-6 weeks; consider keeping in a "puppy cut" for easier maintenance
  • Mat Prevention: Mats form quickly and can cause skin problems
  • Face Cleaning: Clean around eyes and beard regularly

OES Are Great For:

  • Families with children who want a gentle, playful companion
  • Those TRULY committed to extensive grooming (or professional grooming budget)
  • People who enjoy having their dog involved in family activities
  • Homes with space for a large, active dog
  • Those who appreciate a dog with personality and humor

OES May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Those unwilling or unable to commit to extensive grooming
  • Hot climate residents without air conditioning
  • People who work long hours away from home
  • Those wanting a low-maintenance dog
  • Apartment dwellers without adequate space

An Old English Sheepdog disappears under a heavy coat that has to be brushed to the skin or it mats into a pelt. They are a large herding dog, not a living stuffed animal, and hip dysplasia is the structural risk. Budget grooming time every week or pay a groomer who will not shave a neglected coat down to damaged skin.

Life with an Old English Sheepdog settles into a rhythm that many owners find surprisingly grounding.

Hip and Joint

The Orthopedic Foundation for Animals (OFA) reports a hip dysplasia prevalence of approximately 15.8% in evaluated Old English Sheepdogs (large breed, typical weight 60-100 lbs).

Old English Sheepdogs' heavy coat and body mass place significant demands on the hip joints. Their thick coat can make visual assessment of body condition difficult — hands-on body condition scoring at every vet visit is important.

Exercise Guidelines: Moderate exercise on varied terrain. Their herding heritage means they enjoy structured activities. Regular grooming sessions provide an opportunity to assess gait and joint mobility.
