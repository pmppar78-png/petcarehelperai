A circus dog that wants your lap

Havanese are the Cuban companion breed: 7 to 13 pounds, 8.5 to 11.5 inches, a silky coat, and a lifespan often 14 to 16 years. They were circus performers for a reason. Tricks stick, and the stats table rates trainability as excellent. They also follow you into the bathroom. That attachment is charming until you leave for work and the dog has never practiced being alone.

Children and other dogs usually go well. They are a weak guard and a decent apartment dog if someone walks them 30 to 60 minutes and plays. A yard is optional. A comb is not.

Knees, eyes, and a shunt

Luxating patellas are the common limp: a skip, then they are fine, until they are not. Legg-Calvé-Perthes can destroy a puppy's hip around 6 to 12 months. Cataracts, progressive retinal atrophy, cherry eye, and extra eyelashes that scratch the cornea are why an eye exam belongs in the purchase paperwork. Some lines carry portosystemic shunts. A small puppy that stares or stumbles after meals needs bile acids, not a richer diet. Congenital deafness is tested with BAER, not by clapping once in a noisy room.

Ordinary yearly costs often sit near $1,650 to $3,250. The AKC breed directory is a short introduction; the eye and patella results are the part that matters. A skipping hind leg is the limping guide. A puppy that fades after dinner belongs in the urgency checker the same day.

Havanese questions

Are Havanese good first dogs?

Often yes. They are small, trainable, and usually friendly with children and other dogs. The work is the coat, the kneecaps, and not letting a velcro dog practice panic every time you leave.

What genetic problems should a Havanese breeder test for?

Eyes, including cataracts and progressive retinal atrophy, plus hips and patellas. Liver shunts and congenital deafness also occur. Chondrodysplasia, a dwarfism trait, is something to ask about if the legs look unusually crooked.

How much grooming does a Havanese need?

The professional grooming line is often $500 to $1,000 a year, with brushing several times a week between visits. A short pet clip is reasonable if daily combing will not happen.
