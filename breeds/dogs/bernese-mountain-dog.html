Beautiful, and often gone by eight

Bernese Mountain Dogs are the tricolor draft dogs of about 70 to 115 pounds and 23 to 27.5 inches. People buy them for the calm, the kids, the postcard coat. The lifespan is 6 to 8 years. Say that out loud before the deposit. Histiocytic sarcoma, a cancer of immune cells, is disproportionately a Berner disease. Lymphoma, bone cancer, and mast cell tumors add to it. A breeder who quotes 12 or 14 years with no ages of death in the pedigree is hoping you will not ask.

They are gentle, usually good with children and other dogs, and happier with about an hour of walking than with a marathon. Heat knocks them down. The coat sheds constantly and then sheds worse. A rake and a vacuum are equipment. Hip and elbow dysplasia are common. Bloat happens. Degenerative myelopathy and von Willebrand disease show up in the family often enough to ask about DNA and clotting.

What a sudden collapse can mean

A Berner that pales, weakens, or collapses may be bleeding from a splenic or cardiac tumor. That is an emergency hospital, not a morning appointment. Weigh them, watch the appetite, and feel the belly and limbs on a schedule so a change is obvious. Insurance only helps if the policy starts before the diagnosis.

Food runs about $800 to $1,500. The yearly total often sits near $2,700 to $5,200. The Merck Veterinary Manual is where histiocytic sarcoma is described without the breeder brochure. Collapse or unproductive retching uses the checker while you drive. A persistent limp uses the limping page and then radiographs, because bone cancer hides in "he's stiff."

Bernese questions

Why do Bernese Mountain Dogs die so young?

Cancer. Histiocytic sarcoma is unusually common, and lymphoma, osteosarcoma, and mast cell tumors show up too. The 6 to 8 year lifespan on the stats table is the breed, not a pessimistic edit.

Do Berners overheat?

Yes. The coat is a cold-weather tool. Summer hiking at noon, a hot car, and a yard without shade are dangerous. An hour of morning exercise fits them better than a jogging program.

What should I budget besides food?

Insurance in the $600 to $1,200 range, bought while the dog is young, because cancer claims start early. A routine year on this profile is about $2,700 to $5,200 before oncology.
