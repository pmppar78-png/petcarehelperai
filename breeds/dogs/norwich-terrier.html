Norwich Terrier Overview

The Norwich Terrier is one of the smallest working terriers, distinguished by its erect, pointed ears (unlike its drop-eared cousin, the Norfolk Terrier). Developed in East Anglia, England, these compact hunters were bred to hunt vermin and bolt foxes from their dens.

Often described as "a big dog in a small package," Norwich Terriers combine terrier spirit with an affectionate, loyal nature. They were once called "Jones Terriers" after a Cambridge student who sold them, and later became popular mascots for Cambridge University students.

Temperament & Personality

Norwich Terriers are charming companions with classic terrier traits:

  • Affectionate & Loving: They thrive on human companionship and form strong bonds.
  • Curious & Alert: Always interested in their surroundings and quick to investigate.
  • Confident & Fearless: Don't realize their small size when it comes to courage.
  • Good-Natured: Generally get along well with other dogs and people.
  • Active & Energetic: Despite their size, they need regular activity and stimulation.

Common Health Issues

Norwich Terriers are generally healthy but can be prone to certain conditions:

Respiratory Issues

  • Upper Airway Syndrome: Norwich can have elongated soft palates and narrow tracheas causing breathing difficulties.
  • Tracheal Collapse: Weakening of tracheal rings can occur in some individuals.

Orthopedic Issues

  • Patellar Luxation: Kneecap displacement common in small breeds.
  • Hip Dysplasia: Can occur; screening recommended for breeding dogs.

Other Concerns

  • Epilepsy: Seizure disorders can occur in the breed.
  • Dental Disease: Small mouths lead to crowded teeth requiring dental care.
  • Eye Problems: Cataracts and other eye conditions can develop.

Health Screening Recommendation

Before getting a Norwich Terrier, ask breeders for patella evaluations, hip certifications, and eye exams. Consider Embark DNA testing to screen for genetic health conditions.

Exercise & Activity Requirements

Norwich Terriers need regular activity despite their small size:

  • Daily Exercise: 30-45 minutes of walks and active play
  • Secure Yard: Important due to prey drive and digging tendencies
  • Mental Stimulation: Puzzle toys, training games, and nose work
  • Dog Sports: Excel at earthdog, agility, and barn hunt
  • Moderate Heat Tolerance: Be cautious in hot weather due to potential airway issues

Training Tips for Norwich Terriers

Norwich Terriers are intelligent and eager to please:

  • Positive Reinforcement: Food rewards and praise work exceptionally well
  • Early Socialization: Expose to various people, dogs, and situations
  • Consistent Boundaries: Be firm but kind with rules
  • Recall Training: Essential due to their prey drive
  • Prevent Small Dog Syndrome: Train them like you would a larger dog

Nutrition & Feeding

Proper nutrition supports the Norwich's health:

  • Small Breed Formula: Kibble sized for small mouths
  • Portion Control: 1/2 to 3/4 cup daily, divided into two meals
  • Quality Ingredients: High-quality protein supports their energy
  • Dental Health: Consider dental-specific foods or treats
  • Weight Monitoring: Prevent obesity which can worsen breathing issues

Grooming Requirements

Norwich Terriers require regular coat maintenance:

  • Hand Stripping: Every 6-8 weeks for proper coat texture
  • Brushing: 2-3 times weekly to prevent matting
  • Ear Cleaning: Weekly cleaning of upright ears
  • Dental Care: Daily brushing recommended for small mouths
  • Nail Trimming: Every 2-3 weeks

Is a Norwich Terrier Right for You?

Norwich Terriers Are Great For:

  • Apartment dwellers seeking an active small dog
  • Families with children of all ages
  • Those wanting a loyal, affectionate companion
  • People who enjoy training and activities with their dogs
  • Owners seeking a portable travel companion

Norwich Terriers May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Homes with small pets like hamsters or guinea pigs
  • Those wanting a very quiet dog
  • Owners who prefer minimal grooming
  • People seeking a completely obedient dog
  • Those who want to leave their dog alone for long periods

Related Breeds to Consider

If you're interested in Norwich Terriers, you might also consider:

  • Norfolk Terrier - Nearly identical except for drop ears
  • Cairn Terrier - Similar size and personality
  • Scottish Terrier - Similar size, more independent
  • Border Terrier - Similar temperament, slightly larger
