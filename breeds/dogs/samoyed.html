A sled dog that wants to be in the conversation

Samoyeds stand about 19 to 23 inches and weigh 35 to 65 pounds. Many live 12 to 14 years. They are friendly, high-energy, and social. Leaving one alone all day with a dirty coat and no exercise produces a howl and a chewed door. Children usually do well. Other dogs usually do well. The grooming is the part people underestimate when they buy the smile.

Exercise is high: walks, pulling, or play that lasts. They overheat in summer. Shaving a Samoyed does not make it cooler in a useful way and it ruins the coat that was insulating the skin. Trim sanitary areas. Do not clip the dog naked.

Hips, eyes, sugar, and a genetic kidney disease

Hip dysplasia, progressive retinal atrophy, and diabetes show up. Hereditary glomerulopathy is the kidney disease to ask about by name. A Samoyed that drinks more and loses weight needs blood and urine, not a new food bag as the only step. VCA describes diabetes mellitus. Increased thirst is also where the symptom guide starts. A Samoyed that is panting in the shade is already too hot to keep walking.

Two immune diseases in this breed look like ordinary skin trouble at the start. Sebaceous adenitis destroys the oil glands. The coat thins, the skin scales, and a musty smell survives a bath. Diagnosis is a biopsy, not a different shampoo. Uveodermatologic syndrome attacks pigment and the inside of the eye. A dog that squints, a nose or lip that loses color, or a sudden haze in one eye needs a veterinary ophthalmologist within days, because the inflammation can blind the dog. Ask for OFA or PennHIP hip results on both parents before the puppy is chosen for the smile.

Samoyed questions

How often do Samoyeds blow coat?

Heavily, about twice a year, with steady shed in between. Line brushing to the skin is the chore. A bath without brushing the undercoat out first makes felt.

Do they bark?

They talk. Samoyeds are a vocal northern breed. An apartment can work if you train quiet and you exercise the dog. It fails if you hoped the fluff would be silent.

What kidney problem do they inherit?

Samoyed hereditary glomerulopathy, an X-linked disease that affects males severely. Ask whether the line has been tested. A young male that is losing protein in the urine needs a workup.

A Samoyed's smile sits on a double coat that mats and overheats if you only skim the top. Brush to the skin, walk in the cool hours, and expect digging and barking. Hereditary glomerulopathy is the kidney disease to ask a breeder about before a puppy comes home. A Samoyed in a hot car or a noon run is a heatstroke risk.

Daily Samoyed work is the coat, a real walk, and a check of the skin under the ruff. They blow the undercoat and the house fills with hair. That is normal. Mats against the skin, a dog that pants in the shade, or a change in drinking and urine are not normal and need a person, not another day of waiting.
