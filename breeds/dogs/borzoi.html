Borzoi Overview

The Borzoi, formerly known as the Russian Wolfhound, was developed by Russian aristocracy to hunt wolves, foxes, and hares across the vast open terrain of Russia. These elegant sighthounds were treasured by the Russian nobility for centuries, with grand hunting parties sometimes featuring hundreds of Borzois.

With their distinctive long, narrow head, graceful silhouette, and flowing silky coat, Borzois combine stunning beauty with remarkable athletic ability. They can reach speeds of 35-40 mph and possess the courage and strength to tackle wolves, yet are gentle and affectionate companions at home.

Temperament & Personality

Borzois have a gentle, refined personality:

  • Gentle & Affectionate: Despite their size and hunting heritage, they are remarkably sweet and loving with family.
  • Quiet & Calm: They rarely bark and are peaceful, dignified house companions.
  • Independent: Like most sighthounds, they think for themselves and may seem aloof.
  • Sensitive: They are emotionally attuned and dislike harsh treatment or raised voices.
  • Cat-like: They often curl up in small spaces, groom themselves, and can be selective about affection.
  • Strong Prey Drive: Their hunting heritage means they will chase small animals - secure fencing essential.

Common Health Issues

Borzois have some important health considerations:

Major Health Concerns

  • Bloat (GDV): Deep-chested breeds are at high risk for gastric torsion, a life-threatening emergency.
  • Cardiac Issues: Heart problems including cardiomyopathy can occur. Regular cardiac screening recommended.
  • Osteosarcoma: Bone cancer is more common in large breeds like Borzois.
  • Progressive Retinal Atrophy (PRA): Can cause vision loss; DNA testing available.

Other Health Considerations

  • Anesthesia Sensitivity: Critical - sighthound-appropriate protocols required.
  • Hypothyroidism: Can occur; regular thyroid screening recommended.
  • Degenerative Myelopathy: Progressive spinal cord disease; DNA testing available.
  • Drug Sensitivity: May be sensitive to certain medications and flea/tick preventatives.

Health Screening Recommendation

Request cardiac evaluations, eye certifications, and thyroid testing from breeders. Discuss bloat prevention with your veterinarian. Consider Embark DNA testing for comprehensive genetic screening.

Exercise & Activity Requirements

Borzois need opportunities to run but are otherwise relatively calm:

  • Daily Exercise: 1-2 hours of activity including long walks and running opportunities
  • Secure Fencing: Minimum 6 feet - they can jump and have intense prey drive
  • Lure Coursing: Excellent outlet for their natural hunting drive
  • Sprinting Opportunities: They need safe areas to run at full speed periodically
  • Couch Potatoes: Despite their athleticism, they love lounging at home between activities
  • Cold Tolerant: Their Russian heritage means they handle cold weather well

Training Tips for Borzois

Borzois are intelligent but require patience and understanding:

  • Gentle Approach: They are sensitive and respond poorly to harsh methods
  • Positive Reinforcement: Use treats, praise, and play as rewards
  • Accept Independence: They may understand commands but choose when to comply
  • Keep Sessions Short: They bore easily - brief, varied training works best
  • Early Socialization: Important for building confidence with various situations
  • Never Off-Leash: Their prey drive makes reliable recall virtually impossible

Nutrition & Feeding

Borzois require careful feeding management due to bloat risk:

  • Multiple Small Meals: 2-3 meals daily to reduce bloat risk
  • Slow Feeding: Use slow feeder bowls to prevent gulping
  • Rest After Meals: No vigorous exercise for 1-2 hours after eating
  • Quality Protein: Essential for maintaining their lean muscle mass
  • Maintain Lean Weight: Extra weight stresses their frame and increases bloat risk

Grooming Requirements

Borzoi grooming is moderate compared to their luxurious appearance:

  • Brushing: 2-3 times weekly to prevent mats; daily during shedding seasons
  • Bathing: Every 4-6 weeks or as needed
  • Mat Prevention: Pay attention to behind ears, legs, and hindquarters
  • Nail Trimming: Every 2-3 weeks
  • Ear Cleaning: Weekly checks and cleaning
  • Dental Care: Daily brushing recommended

Is a Borzoi Right for You?

Borzois Are Great For:

  • Those wanting a gentle, elegant companion
  • Quiet households that appreciate a calm indoor dog
  • Homes with secure, high fencing and running space
  • People who understand sighthound independence
  • Lure coursing enthusiasts

Borzois May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Homes with small pets (cats, rabbits, small dogs)
  • Those wanting an easily trainable, responsive dog
  • People without secure fencing
  • Small living spaces - they need room to stretch out
  • Those wanting off-leash hiking companions
  • Families not prepared for their size and exercise needs

Related Breeds to Consider

If you're interested in Borzois, you might also consider:

  • Irish Wolfhound - Larger but similarly gentle
  • Scottish Deerhound - Similar temperament, wire coat
  • Saluki - Smaller sighthound with similar elegance
  • Greyhound - Lower maintenance sighthound
