Name the corgi before you buy the ramp

People say corgi and almost always mean the Pembroke: 10 to 12 inches, 25 to 30 pounds, often living 12 to 15 years, with little or no tail. The Cardigan is the other corgi, larger, with a tail, and it should not be shoved into a Pembroke care plan or a Pembroke breeding. If a seller cannot say which breed the papers name, walk away.

Pembrokes are friendly and busy. They comment on the mail carrier. They will herd a toddler. Training is usually easy if you are more interesting than the squirrel, and it falls apart if the dog is bored. A puzzle toy and a short walk do not replace a job, but they cover a Tuesday.

Weight is a back problem

Every extra pound sits on a long spine. Keep the waist visible from above. Disc trouble announces itself as a yelp, a hunched back, or a dog that will not jump into the car it jumped into yesterday. Degenerative myelopathy is the slower look, dragging nails and crossing the back legs, and it needs a neurologist to separate it from a disc and from hip pain. AKC breed profiles separate Pembroke and Cardigan. Back pain that stops a dog from walking is a same-day clinic trip.

Welsh corgi questions

Pembroke or Cardigan?

If the dog is naturally bobtailed or docked, smaller, and fox-faced, you are usually looking at a Pembroke. Cardigans are heavier, long-tailed, and a separate breed with their own health list.

How much do they shed?

A lot. The undercoat releases in seasonal blows. Weekly brushing fails during those weeks. Daily raking is the actual chore.

Are they apartment dogs?

Only if the barking is trained and the hour of exercise happens outside. They are not a quiet lap substitute for a cavalier.

A Pembroke Welsh Corgi is a dwarf herding dog, and the long back is why sofas and extra weight matter. Intervertebral disc disease can paralyze a dog that was jumping off the couch yesterday. Keep them lean, use a ramp, and do not encourage the jump. They herd children by nipping heels. That is instinct, and it needs training, not a laugh. Feel the ribs every week. A round Pembroke is a disc injury waiting for a stair.

Daily Pembroke care is a couple of brisk walks, nail trims, and a body-condition check you can do with your hands. You should feel ribs. A corgi that is round is a disc injury waiting for a stair. Brush the double coat when it blows. They bark at the door because herding dogs comment on movement.
