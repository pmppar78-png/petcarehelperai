Dachshunds were earth dogs, built to go into a badger sett. The long spine and short legs that made them useful underground are the reason a modern pet needs ramps. Clever, stubborn, and devoted is exact. They bond tightly, argue about recall, and will tell the street about every passing dog.

Two sizes, three coats, one spine

Standards run about 16 to 32 pounds and 8 to 9 inches tall. Miniatures stay under about 11 pounds and 5 to 6 inches. Smooth coats are the low-grooming default. Longhaired dogs need brushing. Wirehairs need a hand strip or a tidy clip a couple of times a year. Shedding is low to moderate. Lifespan is often 12 to 16 years, which is a long time to keep a back healthy.

Exercise is moderate: two or three walks and some nose work. They do not need marathon runs, and pounding down stairs is a bad hobby. Teach a ramp early, while it is a game, not a rehabilitation tool.

Disc disease is the budget item

Intervertebral disc disease can look like a dog that hesitates before jumping, cries when lifted, or suddenly cannot use the hind legs. Weakness, a hunched back, or loss of bladder control is an emergency. Surgery, when it is offered, often runs several thousand dollars. Weight control is the prevention you actually own. A dachshund does not get to be "cute chubby."

Other issues include dental disease in small mouths, patella luxation in some minis, and progressive retinal atrophy in certain lines. Ask breeders about those, and still assume the spine is the main plot. The VCA explanation of disc disease is worth reading before you need it at 11 p.m.

The household

Other dogs are a maybe. Dachshunds can be selective and noisy about it. Kids should sit on the floor to play. Apartments are fine if barking is managed with exercise and you accept that a hound will still announce the hallway. Food, routine vet care, and insurance often total about $1,300 to $2,650 a year, before ramps. Insurance in the $400 to $800 band is one of the more sensible lines in that table because of the back.

Trainability is moderate. They are not unintelligent. They are unconvinced. Food, short sessions, and a harness get further than repeating yourself. A sudden limp or a dog that will not climb the curb belongs on the limping guide and, if the legs are going out, at an emergency clinic rather than the checker alone.

Dachshund backs, bark, and fit

How do you protect a Dachshund's back?

Keep the dog lean, use ramps or steps onto furniture, pick the dog up with the chest and hips supported, and use a harness instead of yanking a collar. Jumping off the couch is the everyday injury.

Are they good with young children?

Better with older kids. A toddler who grabs a long back, or drops a dog off a lap, can cause a disc injury. The breed is devoted and also sharp when startled.

That long life is the reason the back-care habits have to start in puppyhood and stay boring.

Do miniature and standard Dachshunds have the same risks?

Both get intervertebral disc disease. Miniatures are under about 11 pounds; standards are roughly 16 to 32 pounds. The smaller dog is easier to lift and just as able to hurt a disc on a bad landing.
