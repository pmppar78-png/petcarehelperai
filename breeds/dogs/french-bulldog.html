The skull is the care sheet

A French Bulldog stands about 11 to 13 inches and stays under 28 pounds, which makes the breed look like an easy apartment dog. The body that fits a couch is the same body that struggles to breathe, cool itself, and give birth. Most of the daily work is not training. It is keeping the airway open, the skin folds dry, and the spine from taking stupid impacts.

Reputable breeders screen hips and spines and refuse to breed dogs that cannot breathe through a short walk. A pale blue, merle, or "exotic" longhair puppy advertised on a payment plan is a different product: color mutations stacked onto an already compressed airway. Spend the money on health records, not on a coat color.

What a normal Tuesday looks like

Morning is a short walk in cool air, then breakfast from a measured cup. Frenchies gain weight easily, and extra fat makes the airway worse. Midday they sleep. If you work away from home, a crate in a cool room is safer than a balcony. Evening is another short outing, a food puzzle, and wrinkle care: wipe the facial folds and tail pocket with a damp cloth and dry them. Left wet, those folds grow yeast and the dog smells sour.

Exercise stays in the "low to moderate" band on the quick-facts card. Ten minutes of play on a rug beats a noon jog. Swimming is a bad idea unless you are in the water with them; the front-heavy body sinks. Stairs are for carrying, not for a puppy to tumble down while the joints and the hemivertebrae are still forming.

Health problems that actually show up

Brachycephalic obstructive airway syndrome is the headline. Noisy breathing at rest, gagging, exercise intolerance, and sleep that keeps breaking off are not "just how they sound." A surgeon can shorten the soft palate and widen the nostrils. That operation often costs a few thousand dollars and does not turn the dog into a retriever.

Spinal malformations, including hemivertebrae, are common enough that a sudden yelp, a wobbly hind end, or pain when picked up needs a same-day exam. Allergies drive ear infections and paw licking. Many bitches cannot deliver puppies safely, so a pet that is bred "once" can become an emergency cesarean. The VCA pet library has plain-language pages on airway disease and heatstroke if you want the clinical version of this list.

Money

Annual routine costs on this site's breed card run about $1,900 to $4,400, with insurance alone often $600 to $1,500 because claims are frequent. A well-bred pet puppy commonly sells for several thousand dollars. Budget a separate emergency number, not a hope that insurance will cover every congenital problem. Read the policy for airway surgery and hereditary exclusions before you rely on it.

Who they fit

Frenchies fit adults and calm families in climate-controlled homes who want a clownish, people-focused dog and will skip midday heat. They are a weak choice for runners, households that travel in hot cars, or anyone who needs the dog to be cheap for a decade. Children can live with them if the kids are taught not to sit on the dog or drag it by the face. Trainability is moderate and they can be stubborn; short sessions and food work better than repeating a command.

Lifespan is often 10 to 12 years, shorter when the airway and spine are unmanaged. If breathing changes, the gums look blue, or the dog collapses, skip the forum post and use the when-to-see-a-vet checker or go straight to an emergency clinic. Vomiting that will not stop is covered on the dog vomiting guide.

Before you bring a Frenchie home

Can a French Bulldog live in an apartment?

Yes, if the apartment stays cool and you can carry the dog down the stairs. They do not need miles of running, and they do poorly with heat, humidity, and homes that cannot manage snoring, allergies, or a possible surgery bill.

Why do French Bulldogs overheat so easily?

Their short muzzle, narrow nostrils, and often thick soft palate make it hard to pant effectively. A warm car, a sunny patio, or a game of fetch in July can turn into heatstroke faster than it would in a longer-nosed dog.

How much does a French Bulldog cost in a year?

The breed card on this page puts routine annual costs around $1,900 to $4,400 before a puppy price. Airway surgery, spinal imaging, or a cesarean litter bill sits on top of that range.

Are French Bulldogs good for first-time owners?

They suit a first-time owner who has a quiet, air-conditioned home and a vet fund. They are a poor match for someone who wants a cheap, sturdy, hike-all-day dog.
