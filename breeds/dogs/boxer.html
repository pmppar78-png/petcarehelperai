Cancer first, then the clown

Boxers are funny, people-obsessed dogs of about 50 to 80 pounds and 21.5 to 25 inches. The stats table gives them 10 to 12 years. Cancer is why a lot of families get fewer. Mast cell tumors and lymphoma lead the list; hemangiosarcoma appears too. Run your hands over the dog every week. A new lump gets a needle at the clinic even when it looks boring. Boxers that seem bouncy at seven can already be medical seniors.

The other disease with a Boxer name is ARVC, a rhythm disorder. Listening with a stethoscope does not clear it. Holter monitoring is how clinics catch the dangerous beats. Subaortic stenosis is a separate heart defect and a reason to buy from someone who cardiac-tested the parents. Degenerative myelopathy has a DNA marker; it causes a slow hind-end fade and is worth knowing so you do not call it "just arthritis."

Kids, heat, and a white coat

They are often wonderful with children and terrible at judging their own weight. A joyful leap flattens a toddler. Same-sex dog tension shows up. Training works when it is food, play, and short sessions. Stacked corrections produce a sulk or a worse idea.

Heat is not a small print item. Noisy breathing at rest, gagging, or a dog that quits a short walk needs a vet, not a joke about snoring. Mostly white puppies need a hearing test. Insurance in the $500 to $1,000 range is the line item that matters, inside a yearly total around $2,000 to $4,100, because oncology bills do not wait for enrollment periods. VCA's library covers mast cell tumors and heatstroke. Collapse or vomiting that will not stop is the checker, and a new limp is the limping guide.

Boxer questions

What heart problem do Boxers get?

Arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy, still called Boxer cardiomyopathy. The heart's rhythm breaks. A dog can collapse or die with a normal-sounding exam. A Holter monitor, a 24-hour ECG, is the screening tool, starting around age two in a dog you plan to keep.

Are Boxer lumps usually harmless?

You cannot tell by looking. Mast cell tumors are common and can resemble a wart. Any new bump should be aspirated, not watched for a month.

Do Boxers overheat?

Yes. The muzzle is short enough that panting is inefficient. Midday runs, parked cars, and humid hikes are how these dogs get into heatstroke. Morning and evening walks are the summer plan.

Is a white Boxer deaf?

Not always, but extreme white is linked with deafness. Ask for a BAER hearing test. Fawn and brindle, with or without white flash, are the ordinary colors.
