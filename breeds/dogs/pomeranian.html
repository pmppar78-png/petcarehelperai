A spitz the size of a cat

Pomeranians stand about 6 to 7 inches and weigh 3 to 7 pounds. A sturdy one lives 12 to 16 years. They descend from larger sled-type dogs and still act like it: bold, noisy, and unconvinced that a German shepherd outranks them. That mismatch is how small dogs get shaken.

They do well with older children and with dogs that will not swat them. Exercise is a half hour and a puzzle toy. The coat needs a comb that reaches the skin. Alopecia X, the coat disease that leaves a fuzzy tail and bald flanks, is a Pomeranian problem. It is cosmetic more often than it is sick, and a vet should say which.

Knees, throat, and a dental plan

Slipping kneecaps, dental disease, and tracheal collapse are the trio. A puppy that reverse-sneezes now and then is usually fine. A dog that coughs every time it is picked up is not. Open fontanelles occur. VCA describes collapsing trachea. A new honk after a pull on a collar is a reason to use the checker and book the clinic.

Pomeranian questions

Do Pomeranians shed?

Yes. The double coat blows seasonally. Brushing several times a week, and daily during the blow, keeps the undercoat off the furniture. Shaving the coat ruins the insulation.

What does a collapsing trachea sound like?

A honking cough, worse with excitement or a collar. Switch to a harness and have a vet listen. It is common in this breed and in other toys.

Are they good apartment dogs?

The exercise fits an apartment. The bark does not, unless you train a quiet. They alarm-bark at hallway noise.

A harness keeps the load off the throat

The American Kennel Club places the Pomeranian in the toy group, a miniature of larger German spitz dogs, at about 6 to 7 inches and 3 to 7 pounds. The double coat and the frill are the outline in the standard, and the coat blows seasonally. Alopecia X can leave a fuzzy tail and bald flanks, and a veterinarian is who sorts cosmetic coat loss from illness. Collapsing trachea is the cough that follows pressure on the neck. Veterinary notes on that condition, including the write-ups VCA publishes, describe a harness as the walking tool that keeps the load off the throat. Slipping patellas, dental disease, and open fontanelles are the other questions for the breeder. The attitude still picks fights with dogs that outweigh this one several times over.
