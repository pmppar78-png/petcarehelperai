A gray dog that wants a job and a person

Weimaraners stand about 23 to 27 inches and weigh 55 to 90 pounds. Lifespan is often 10 to 13 years. They were built to hunt all day beside someone. The friendly temperament is real, and so is the destruction when that someone is at an office and the walk was ten minutes.

They can live with older children. Other dogs are a mixed report, especially pushy adults. The coat is short and the shed is moderate. They are not yard ornaments. A lean Weimaraner is the healthy one. The magazine gray dog that is ribby and frantic is a husbandry problem.

Bloat, hips, and a puppy that should not be jogged

Deep chest means gastric dilatation-volvulus: unproductive retching and a tight belly, and the treatment is surgery now. Hip dysplasia shows up. So does hypertrophic osteodystrophy in growing pups. Do not take a five-month-old on a five-mile run to "burn energy." VCA describes bloat. The vomiting page is the one to have read before a holiday weekend.

Weimaraner questions

Can a Weimaraner stay home alone?

Poorly, unless you train it in short steps. Separation distress is common. A dog that shreds a door is not spiteful. It is panicked, and a crate plus a real exercise plan comes before another chew toy.

How much running do they need?

Plan on two hours of real work most days: running, field work, or a sport. A yard without a person does not count.

What is HOD?

Hypertrophic osteodystrophy, a painful bone disease of fast-growing puppies. A Weimaraner puppy with a fever and swollen legs needs a vet, not more food to 'build muscle.'
