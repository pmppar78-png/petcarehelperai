Collie Overview

The Collie, immortalized by Lassie in books, movies, and television, is one of the most recognized and beloved dog breeds in the world. Originally bred as herding dogs in Scotland and Northern England, Collies became popular companions during the Victorian era when Queen Victoria fell in love with the breed.

Collies come in two varieties: Rough (long-haired) and Smooth (short-haired). Both share the same gentle, intelligent temperament. They're known for their elegant appearance, expressive faces, and exceptional devotion to their families. Collies are often described as the perfect family dog due to their patient, gentle nature with children.

Temperament & Personality

Collies are renowned for their wonderful temperaments:

  • Devoted & Loyal: Form deep bonds with their families and are extremely devoted companions.
  • Gentle & Patient: Exceptional patience with children, often becoming protective babysitters.
  • Intelligent: Highly intelligent and eager to please, making them easy to train.
  • Sensitive: Emotionally attuned to their owners and responsive to the household atmosphere.
  • Alert but Not Aggressive: Will bark to alert but are generally friendly with strangers once introduced.
  • Playful: Maintain a playful spirit throughout their lives while being calm indoors.

Common Health Issues

Collies have some breed-specific health concerns:

Genetic Conditions

  • MDR1 Gene Mutation: Drug sensitivity affecting approximately 70% of Collies. DNA testing essential before medications.
  • Collie Eye Anomaly (CEA): Inherited eye condition present at birth, ranging from mild to severe. DNA test available.
  • Progressive Retinal Atrophy (PRA): Degenerative eye disease causing vision loss.

Other Eye Conditions

  • Collie Nose: Solar dermatitis affecting the nose in sun-exposed dogs.
  • Dermatomyositis: Skin and muscle disease that can affect Collies.

Other Health Concerns

  • Bloat (GDV): Deep-chested breeds like Collies can be at risk.
  • Hip Dysplasia: Can occur but less common than in many large breeds.
  • Epilepsy: Seizure disorder that occurs in some lines.

MDR1 Testing is Essential

Before any medication or surgery, ensure your Collie has been tested for the MDR1 gene mutation. Common drugs like ivermectin, loperamide, and certain anesthetics can be dangerous or fatal to affected dogs. Get tested with Embark DNA or UC Davis VGL.

Exercise & Activity Requirements

Collies have moderate exercise needs:

  • Daily Exercise: About 1 hour of activity including walks and play
  • Mental Stimulation: Enjoy training, puzzle toys, and interactive games
  • Herding Activities: May enjoy herding activities or herding ball games
  • Adaptable: Can adjust to more or less activity based on owner's lifestyle
  • Good Running Partners: Can accompany joggers once fully mature
  • Dog Sports: Excel in agility, obedience, rally, and herding trials

Training Tips for Collies

Collies are among the easiest breeds to train:

  • Eager to Please: Respond excellently to positive reinforcement
  • Sensitive Souls: Never use harsh methods - they'll shut down
  • Early Socialization: Important to prevent shyness, though generally friendly
  • Channel Intelligence: Keep them mentally engaged with training and activities
  • Address Barking: Can become excessive barkers - train "quiet" commands
  • Natural Herders: May try to herd children - redirect this behavior appropriately

Nutrition & Feeding

Proper nutrition supports Collie health:

  • High-Quality Protein: Support their active lifestyle with quality ingredients
  • Skin & Coat Support: Omega fatty acids help maintain their beautiful coats
  • Multiple Meals: Feed 2 meals daily rather than one large meal (bloat prevention)
  • Weight Management: Keep lean to support their active nature
  • Avoid Overfeeding: They're not as food-motivated as some breeds

Grooming Requirements

Grooming needs vary by coat type:

  • Rough Collie: Brush thoroughly 2-3 times weekly; daily during shedding season
  • Smooth Collie: Weekly brushing usually sufficient
  • Heavy Shedding: Both varieties shed, with Roughs shedding significantly more
  • Bathing: Every 4-8 weeks or as needed
  • Never Shave: Their double coat provides insulation and protection
  • Nail Trimming: Every 2-3 weeks
  • Dental Care: Regular brushing recommended

Is a Collie Right for You?

Collies Are Great For:

  • Families with children (often considered the ideal family dog)
  • First-time dog owners seeking an easy-to-train breed
  • Those wanting a devoted, loyal companion
  • Moderately active households
  • People interested in dog sports and activities

Collies May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Those bothered by shedding (especially Rough variety)
  • People who want a quiet dog (they can be vocal)
  • Hot climates without adequate cooling (their coats are warm)
  • Those who can't commit to regular grooming (Rough Collies)

Related Breeds to Consider

If you're interested in Collies, you might also consider:

  • Shetland Sheepdog - Similar temperament in a smaller package
  • Australian Shepherd - More energetic herding breed
  • Border Collie - More intense working drive
  • Golden Retriever - Similar family-friendly temperament
