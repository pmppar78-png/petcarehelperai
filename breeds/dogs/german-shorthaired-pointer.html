A job, or a ruined baseboard

The German Shorthaired Pointer was built to hunt all day: range, point, retrieve, and come back willing. In a house that offers a lunchtime stroll, that brain becomes a demolition crew. Adults are about 45 to 70 pounds and 21 to 25 inches, with a short liver or liver-and-white coat that sheds a moderate amount and dries fast. Lifespan sits around 10 to 12 years if the hips and the stomach cooperate.

They are friendly, biddable, and usually excellent with children who can handle a dog that greets at full speed. Trainability is high. So is the need to be tired. Plan a real outing, not a lap of the block: sniffing a field, swimming, or structured retrieves with a release word. Crate the young dog when you leave until settling is a skill, not a hope.

Hips, stomach, ears

Hip dysplasia is the orthopedic headline. Ask for evaluations on the parents. The chest is deep enough that bloat, stomach gas that can twist, belongs on your emergency list: a hard swollen abdomen, retching that produces nothing, pacing. That is a drive to a clinic, not a search result. Rest after meals and skip the one enormous dinner.

Floppy ears after a swim collect yeast. Some GSP puppies develop puffy legs or a puffy chest from a lymphatic problem; swelling in a puppy is a vet visit. Entropion, a lid that rolls in, shows up often enough to watch the eyes for squinting. The Merck Veterinary Manual is the reference version of bloat and hip dysplasia if you want more than a breed page.

Yearly food, routine care, insurance, and the rest commonly fall around $1,800 to $4,050 before an emergency gastropexy. A pointer that vomits repeatedly is the case on the vomiting page. The checker sorts urgency. This is a poor first dog for someone who travels every weekend and a very good one for someone who is already outside.

Before a pointer comes home

Can a German Shorthaired Pointer live in an apartment?

Only if you treat the apartment as a kennel and the rest of the day as a hunting schedule. Two short walks and a puzzle toy do not meet a dog bred to quarter fields.

How much running is enough?

The stats table says very high, two hours and up. A safe off-leash field, a long retrieve session, or actual hunt work counts. A fenced yard the dog patrols alone does not.

What health problems show up in GSPs?

Hips, bloat in a deep chest, ear infections, eyelid rolls, and a lymphatic swelling seen in some puppies. Ask for hip scores and learn what unproductive retching looks like before you need the emergency clinic.
