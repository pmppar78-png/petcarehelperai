The back is the limiting factor

Pembroke Welsh Corgis are herding dogs stuffed into a long, low frame: about 10 to 12 inches tall and 25 to 30 pounds, living 12 to 15 years when the spine cooperates. They are bold, bright, and often funny. They also nip heels, because moving livestock was the original job and children look like livestock if nobody teaches a cutoff.

Intervertebral disc disease is the acute disaster. A disc blows, the dog screams, the hind end goes weak or useless. That is a same-day clinic, sometimes a surgeon. Keep them lean. Use a ramp to the bed. Skip repeated stair sprints while they are still growing. Degenerative myelopathy is the other back-end disease and it feels different: gradual, usually not painful, a slow knuckling that DNA testing for the SOD1 variant can warn you about. One is a rupture. The other is a cord disease. They are not the same limp.

Coat, hips, eyes

The double coat sheds heavily twice a year and steadily the rest of the time. Bathing does not stop it. Hips can be dysplastic even in a small dog, and progressive retinal atrophy plus von Willebrand disease show up in some lines, so ask what was tested. Exercise on the stats table is moderate to high, about an hour, of trotting and training rather than agility jumps off furniture.

Yearly costs often land around $1,550 to $3,200 before spinal imaging. VCA's library covers disc disease in plain language. Hind-end weakness or a yelp when lifted is the limping page, and the checker is there if you are unsure whether it can wait until morning. It usually cannot.

Corgi questions

What is the difference between a Pembroke and a Cardigan?

They are separate breeds. Pembrokes are the smaller, fox-faced dogs people mean by corgi, about 25 to 30 pounds. Cardigans are heavier, longer in the body, and the tail is part of that breed's standard. A Pembroke with a natural tail is still a Pembroke.

Why do corgis hurt their backs?

The long spine and short legs make intervertebral disc disease common, and extra weight makes it worse. Jumping off the couch is the usual trigger. A sudden yelp or weak hind legs is an emergency, not a pulled muscle.

Do Pembroke corgis shed?

Yes. The undercoat blows hard enough that owners call the hair glitter. A brush several times a week is normal life, and a slicker in shedding season is a tool, not a luxury.
