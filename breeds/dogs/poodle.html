Poodles were water retrievers before they were salon dogs. The AKC files the breed as non-sporting; the origin is a gundog, and the brain stayed. Intelligent, active, and alert is the right temperament line. "Second smartest" rankings are magazine math. What you will actually see is a dog that learns a routine quickly and gets bored if the routine is only a lap around the block.

Three dogs under one name

Standards stand over 15 inches and weigh roughly 45 to 70 pounds. Miniatures fall around 10 to 15 inches and 15 to 17 pounds. Toys are under 10 inches and about 6 to 9 pounds. Lifespan is often 12 to 15 years, and the smaller sizes frequently reach the long end. Exercise is moderate to high for all of them, scaled to the legs: a standard wants a real hike, a toy wants brisk play and training, not a five-mile run on pavement.

Kids do well with standards when the dog is taught not to bowl people over. Toys are better with older, careful children. Other dogs are usually fine. Trainability is excellent. Use it, because an under-worked poodle barks and invents games with the trash.

The coat is a contract

Hair grows continuously. Plan a groom every four to six weeks, often $70 to $150 a visit, which is why the quick facts list professional grooming at $600 to $1,500 a year. Brushing between visits matters at the ears, armpits, and base of the tail. You can keep a short pet clip at home if you actually do it. Shaving a pelt off once a year is how skin gets cut.

Low shedding is real. Allergy relief is a maybe. Saliva on skin still carries Can f 1.

Health splits by size

Standards are the bloat breed in this trio: unproductive retching and a swelling abdomen means an emergency clinic, and a prophylactic gastropexy is a conversation at the spay or neuter. Addison's disease, sebaceous adenitis, hip dysplasia, and progressive retinal atrophy show up in standards. Toys and minis add luxating patellas, dental disease, and, in toys, collapsing trachea. Ask for the eye, hip, and (in toys) patella clearances that match the size you are buying. The VCA library explains Addison's and bloat without a sales pitch.

A year of food, grooming, and routine care often spans about $1,950 to $4,900 because the three sizes eat and clip so differently. A standard in an apartment needs a handler more than a bigger floor plan. If breathing or vomiting looks wrong, the checker and the vomiting page are the on-site next step.

Poodle questions by size

Which Poodle size is the right one?

Standards are 45 to 70 pounds and need the most exercise and the bloat watch. Miniatures are about 15 to 17 pounds. Toys are 6 to 9 pounds and bring dental and kneecap issues. All three need haircuts.

Are Poodles hypoallergenic?

They shed very little, which helps some allergic people. The proteins that trigger allergies are still in saliva and dander, so spend an afternoon with an adult poodle before you plan a life around the coat.

How often is a haircut?

Every four to six weeks at a salon, or a strict home schedule. A neglected corded coat mats to the skin and becomes a medical shave.

Are they good for beginners?

Yes, if the beginner accepts grooming as a bill and likes training. A standard is a poor match for someone who wants a low-exercise dog. A toy is a poor match for a toddler-heavy house.
