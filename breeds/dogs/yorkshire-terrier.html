Four pounds is a medical fact

A Yorkshire Terrier is a toy terrier of about 4 to 7 pounds and 7 to 8 inches, with a long silky coat that keeps growing. Lifespan is often 11 to 15 years. The personality is larger than the skeleton: affectionate, sharp, and easy to sour if everyone picks them up to stop a behavior. They do better with older children who sit on the floor than with toddlers who squeeze.

Grooming is the bill people notice, often $400 to $1,200 a year if a salon keeps the coat, or a serious home routine if you clip it into a pet trim. Mats pull on skin. The "hypoallergenic" line means low shedding. It does not mean a person with asthma will be fine.

Shunts, throats, knees

Portosystemic shunts are a Yorkie problem. Blood bypasses the liver, toxins linger, and a puppy that is tiny and weird after dinner needs bile acids and imaging, not more treats. Tracheal collapse produces a honking cough; use a harness. Luxating patellas make them skip. Legg-Calvé-Perthes hits the hip of young toy dogs and looks like a sudden hind limp. Baby teeth stick around and crowd the mouth, so a dental plan starts early. Tiny puppies can also crash from low blood sugar if they skip a meal.

Routine yearly spending often sits around $1,500 to $3,450, and the groomer is a bigger slice than the kibble. VCA explains liver shunts and collapsing trachea without the breeder gloss. A limp in a dog this small is the limping page. Breathing that worsens, or vomiting with lethargy, goes through the checker the same day.

Small-dog questions

Are Yorkshire Terriers hypoallergenic?

The coat sheds very little, which is what that word means on the stats table. Saliva and dander still bother many allergic people. Spend an afternoon with an adult Yorkie before you trust the label.

Why does my Yorkie cough like a goose?

Collapsing trachea is common. Excitement, heat, and a collar yank make it worse. Switch to a harness and have a vet listen; a cough is not a cute breed sound.

What is a liver shunt in a Yorkie puppy?

Portosystemic shunt lets blood skip the liver. The puppy stays small, and after a meal it may stare, stumble, or act drunk. A bile-acid test finds it. It is not something the puppy grows out of on its own.
