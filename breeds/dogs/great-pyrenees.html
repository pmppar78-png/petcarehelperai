Great Pyrenees Overview

The Great Pyrenees is a majestic giant breed with a history dating back thousands of years as a livestock guardian dog in the Pyrenees Mountains between France and Spain. These powerful, patient dogs were bred to live with sheep flocks, protecting them from predators like wolves and bears with minimal human direction.

Today, Great Pyrenees are valued as both working livestock guardians and beloved family companions. Their calm demeanor, patience with children, and natural protective instincts make them excellent family dogs, though their independent nature and guardian instincts require understanding owners. Their stunning white coat and noble bearing add to their appeal.

Temperament & Personality

Great Pyrenees have a distinctive guardian temperament:

  • Calm & Patient: Remarkably gentle and patient, especially with children and smaller animals in their "flock."
  • Independent & Confident: Bred to make decisions without human guidance; can seem stubborn.
  • Protective: Natural guardian instincts; will alert to and deter perceived threats.
  • Nocturnal Tendencies: Most active at night; natural behavior for predator deterrence.
  • Barking: Communicate through barking, especially at night; this is an instinctive behavior.

Common Health Issues

Great Pyrenees are generally healthy but have some breed-specific concerns:

Orthopedic Conditions

  • Hip Dysplasia: Common in large breeds; screening recommended.
  • Elbow Dysplasia: Joint developmental abnormality.
  • Patellar Luxation: Kneecap displacement; can affect mobility.

Other Conditions

  • Gastric Dilatation-Volvulus (Bloat): Life-threatening stomach condition; deep-chested breeds at high risk.
  • Osteosarcoma: Bone cancer more common in giant breeds.
  • Addison's Disease: Adrenal gland insufficiency; manageable with medication.
  • Neurological Issues: Some lines may have hereditary neurological conditions.
  • Eye Conditions: Cataracts and progressive retinal atrophy can occur.

Health Screening Recommendation

Before getting a Great Pyrenees, ask breeders for OFA hip/elbow scores, patellar evaluations, and eye certifications. Consider Embark DNA testing for comprehensive health screening. Discuss bloat prevention with your veterinarian.

Exercise & Activity Requirements

Great Pyrenees have moderate exercise needs:

  • Daily Exercise: About 1 hour of moderate activity including walks and yard patrol
  • Secure Fencing: Essential - they will roam to patrol their territory if given the chance
  • Not for Running: Not built for jogging; prefer leisurely walks and patrol
  • Cool Weather: Most active in cooler temperatures; prone to overheating
  • Mental Stimulation: Enjoy having a "job" such as guarding or training activities

Training Tips for Great Pyrenees

Great Pyrenees require patient, understanding training:

  • Understand Independence: They were bred to think independently; this isn't disobedience
  • Positive Methods: Respond to patience and rewards; harsh methods damage trust
  • Start Early: Begin training and socialization while they're still small enough to manage
  • Leash Training: Critical before they reach full size and strength
  • Manage Barking: Teaching a "quiet" command helps but won't eliminate natural barking
  • Escape Prevention: Train reliable recall; ensure fencing is adequate

Nutrition & Feeding

Proper nutrition supports the Great Pyrenees' health:

  • Giant Breed Formula: Choose food specifically designed for giant breeds
  • Controlled Puppy Growth: Slow, steady growth protects developing bones and joints
  • Bloat Prevention: Feed 2-3 smaller meals; avoid exercise around mealtimes
  • Moderate Calories: Not highly active; avoid overfeeding to prevent obesity
  • Joint Support: Foods with glucosamine and omega-3s benefit joint health

Grooming Requirements

Great Pyrenees have a beautiful coat that requires regular maintenance:

  • Brushing: 2-3 times weekly; daily during heavy shedding seasons
  • Heavy Shedding: "Blow" their undercoat seasonally; prepare for massive shedding
  • Bathing: Every 4-8 weeks or as needed; coat is somewhat self-cleaning
  • Double Dewclaws: Unique to the breed; trim regularly to prevent overgrowth
  • Nail Trimming: Every 2-4 weeks
  • Ear Cleaning: Weekly to prevent infections

Is a Great Pyrenees Right for You?

Great Pyrenees Are Great For:

  • Rural homes with acreage and livestock to protect
  • Families wanting a gentle, patient guardian
  • Those who appreciate independent, thinking dogs
  • People with secure fencing and tolerant neighbors
  • Experienced owners who understand livestock guardian breeds

Great Pyrenees May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Apartment or suburban living with close neighbors (barking)
  • Those wanting an eager-to-please, obedient dog
  • Hot climate residents without air conditioning
  • People who can't provide secure fencing
  • First-time owners unprepared for their independence

Related Breeds to Consider

  • St. Bernard - Similar gentle giant temperament
  • Newfoundland - Gentle giant, excellent with families
  • Kuvasz - Similar livestock guardian breed
  • Bernese Mountain Dog - Large, gentle, family-friendly
