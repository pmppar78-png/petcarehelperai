Treeing Walker Coonhound Overview

The Treeing Walker Coonhound is an American original, developed in the 18th century from Walker Foxhounds. Bred to track and tree raccoons, they are known for their speed, excellent scenting ability, and distinctive "bugle" voice. The breed was named after Thomas Walker, who helped develop them in Virginia.

Often described as "the people's choice" among coonhounds, Treeing Walkers are one of the most popular coonhound breeds due to their versatility as both hunting dogs and family companions. Their tricolor coat (white, black, and tan) and athletic build make them a handsome, distinctive breed.

Temperament & Personality

Treeing Walker Coonhounds are affectionate, intelligent dogs:

  • Loving & Affectionate: They are deeply devoted to their families and love being with people.
  • Confident & Intelligent: They have a keen mind and problem-solving abilities.
  • Energetic: Built for stamina, they have seemingly endless energy for outdoor activities.
  • Sociable: They get along well with other dogs and are friendly with strangers.
  • Vocal: They have a melodious bay and will "talk" about their day.
  • Strong Prey Drive: Their hunting heritage means they will chase small animals.

Common Health Issues

Treeing Walker Coonhounds are generally healthy dogs:

Potential Health Concerns

  • Hip Dysplasia: Can occur in active breeds; breeders should test.
  • Ear Infections: Long, floppy ears require regular cleaning and monitoring.
  • Polyradiculoneuritis (Coonhound Paralysis): A reaction to raccoon saliva causing temporary paralysis; can occur after raccoon bites.
  • Bloat (GDV): Deep-chested breeds are at some risk.

Less Common Issues

  • Eye Problems: Some eye conditions reported.
  • Thyroid Issues: Hypothyroidism can occur.

Health Screening Recommendation

Request hip evaluations from breeders. Be aware of Coonhound Paralysis if your dog hunts raccoons. Consider Embark DNA testing for comprehensive health screening.

Exercise & Activity Requirements

Treeing Walker Coonhounds need significant daily exercise:

  • Daily Exercise: 1-2 hours of vigorous activity
  • Running: Excellent jogging or running partners
  • Hunting: Many still work as hunting dogs and excel at treeing game
  • Secure Fencing: Essential - they will follow scent trails enthusiastically
  • Scent Work: Nose work and tracking provide excellent mental stimulation
  • Never Off-Leash: Their scent drive makes recall unreliable in unfenced areas

Training Tips for Treeing Walker Coonhounds

Treeing Walkers are smart but require consistent training:

  • Food Motivation: They respond well to treat-based training
  • Positive Reinforcement: Harsh methods are counterproductive
  • Patience: Scent distractions will derail focus
  • Consistency: Clear, consistent rules help them understand expectations
  • Channel Their Instincts: Scent work and tracking are productive outlets
  • Early Socialization: Expose to various people, animals, and situations

Nutrition & Feeding

Treeing Walker Coonhounds need quality nutrition:

  • High-Quality Protein: Supports their active lifestyle
  • Monitor Weight: Adjust portions based on activity level
  • Two Meals Daily: Split feedings maintain energy
  • Fresh Water: Always available, especially during activity
  • Working Dog Formulas: May be appropriate for hunting dogs

Grooming Requirements

Treeing Walker Coonhounds have easy grooming needs:

  • Brushing: Weekly brushing with a hound glove
  • Bathing: As needed - they have a typical hound odor
  • Ear Cleaning: Weekly - critical for their long ears
  • Nail Trimming: Every 2-3 weeks
  • Dental Care: Daily brushing recommended

Is a Treeing Walker Coonhound Right for You?

Treeing Walker Coonhounds Are Great For:

  • Active families with large, fenced yards
  • Hunters wanting a versatile treeing dog
  • Runners and outdoor enthusiasts
  • Homes with other dogs
  • Those who appreciate the classic hound personality

Treeing Walker Coonhounds May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Apartment dwellers
  • Those with close neighbors (they are very vocal)
  • Sedentary owners
  • Homes with small pets
  • People wanting reliable off-leash obedience
