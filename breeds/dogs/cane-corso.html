A mastiff that makes decisions

Cane corsos are Italian mastiffs, commonly 90 to 120 pounds, with a life often in the 9 to 12 year range when the heart and the hips allow it. They are loyal to their people and suspicious of everyone else. That is the breed, not a failure of one puppy class. Socialization means the dog can look at a stranger without launching, not that the dog must greet every stranger.

Housing is a secure fence, a leash that you can actually hold, and insurance that allows the breed. Children in the home can do well. Children running through the yard as guests are a different picture. Training starts before the dog outweighs you, which happens early.

Hips, eyes, and a stomach that can twist

Hip dysplasia, cherry eye, entropion, and idiopathic epilepsy show up. A heart check for cardiomyopathy belongs on the adult calendar. Bloat is the deep-chest emergency: retching with no vomit and a tight abdomen. VCA's GDV page is worth reading once. Any sudden abdominal swelling is a drive, and the checker should not be the thing that delays the car.

Cane corso questions

Is a cane corso a first dog?

No. They are powerful, territorial, and quick to decide a stranger does not belong. A first-time owner who wants a loyal guard usually gets a liability.

Do they tolerate other pets?

Some live with dogs they were raised with. Many do not tolerate strange dogs, and same-sex conflict is serious. The introduction is a management plan, not a dog-park hope.

How much exercise is enough?

A working walk, training, and a job. A bored corso in a suburban yard will guard the fence line and rehearse barking.
