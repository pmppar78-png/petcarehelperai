A mastiff that makes decisions

The Cane Corso is an Italian guardian, about 85 to 110 pounds and 23.5 to 27.5 inches, with a short coat and a 9 to 12 year lifespan. Loyalty to the household is the trait people buy. Wariness of everyone else is the trait they underestimate. A puppy that is not walked through ordinary life, calmly, for months, becomes an adult that cannot be walked. Training on the cost table is listed at $500 to $2,000 in the first year because a group class for golden retrievers is the wrong room.

Same-sex dogs are a frequent fight. Children in the home can be fine when the dog was raised with them and is never left as the babysitter. Off-leash in public is a no. The fence should be tall, dig-proof, and closed. Exercise is 1 to 2 hours of leash work, training, and play, with joints spared until the dog is done growing.

Joints, eyes, stomach

Hip and elbow dysplasia and cruciate tears are the orthopedic bill. Entropion, ectropion, and cherry eye are common enough that a squint or a red lump at the eye is a clinic visit, not a wait. Dilated cardiomyopathy belongs on a screening list. Bloat, a swelling abdomen with unproductive retching, is an emergency drive. Some puppies get demodectic mange; a bald, itchy face needs a skin scrape. Idiopathic epilepsy shows up in some families. A first seizure is not "the storm."

Food often runs $800 to $1,400. A full routine year is about $2,500 to $5,700, and many puppies sell for several thousand dollars before that starts. AKC's breed list will not tell you whether this household can handle the dog. Retching or collapse uses the checker. A limp that lasts uses the limping page and then radiographs.

Cane Corso questions

Is a Cane Corso a good first dog?

No. They are powerful, wary of strangers, and quick to decide another dog is a problem. The right owner has already finished training a large dog and has a fenced yard that actually holds.

Can you take a Cane Corso to the dog park?

That is a common way to have a fight. Same-sex aggression is frequent. Socialize with known, calm dogs. Do not outsource manners to a crowd of strangers.

What health tests matter?

Hips, elbows, a cardiac exam for cardiomyopathy, and a look at the eyelids. Bloat is an emergency risk. Epilepsy and demodex show up in some lines. Ask who trained the parents, not only what color they are.
