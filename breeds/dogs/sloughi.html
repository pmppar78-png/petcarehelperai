Sloughi Overview

The Sloughi (pronounced "SLOO-ghee"), also known as the Arabian Greyhound or Berber Greyhound, is an ancient North African sighthound bred by the Berber and Bedouin peoples of Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, and Libya. For centuries, they have been prized for hunting hare, gazelle, and wild boar across the desert landscapes.

Elegant and athletic with a short, smooth coat in shades of sand, fawn, red, and brindle, the Sloughi combines the speed of a coursing hound with remarkable endurance. Often confused with the Saluki, the Sloughi is a distinct breed with a shorter coat and more angular build.

The Sloughi is a breed that commands attention not just for its physical appearance but for the depth of personality and capability it brings to a household. With a lifespan averaging 12-15 years, the decision to welcome a Sloughi into your family is one that will shape your daily routine, activity levels, and emotional life for well over a decade. This breed's noble, reserved, graceful temperament is the product of generations of selective breeding for specific traits—understanding this heritage provides valuable insight into why your Sloughi behaves the way it does and what it needs from you as an owner to truly thrive.

The difference between a good Sloughi: Complete Breed Guide owner and a great one comes down to understanding what this particular animal actually needs, rather than projecting assumptions based on appearance or general expectations. Every Sloughi: Complete Breed Guide has traits rooted in its background that influence behavior, health, and daily care requirements. Working with those traits — instead of against them — is the foundation of a successful experience.

Welcoming a Sloughi: Complete Breed Guide into your home is less about adding a pet and more about adopting a new set of daily responsibilities. Their needs will influence how you organize your mornings, what you prioritize on weekends, and how you plan time away from home. The transition is smoother for owners who go in with realistic expectations about what this commitment actually looks like day to day.

Temperament & Personality

Sloughis have a refined, sensitive temperament:

  • Noble & Dignified: They carry themselves with a quiet, aristocratic grace and expect respectful treatment.
  • Deeply Loyal: They form intense bonds with their family, often becoming one-person dogs.
  • Reserved with Strangers: They are naturally cautious around unfamiliar people and need time to warm up.
  • Gentle & Sensitive: They are emotionally attuned to their owners and dislike conflict or harsh treatment.
  • Calm Indoors: Despite their athletic ability, they are quiet and peaceful in the home.
  • High Prey Drive: Their hunting heritage means they will chase small animals - never off-leash in unfenced areas.

The noble, reserved, graceful nature of the Sloughi is not a simple personality label—it is a complex behavioral profile shaped by breed history, individual genetics, early socialization experiences, and ongoing environmental factors. What this means in practice is that two Sloughi from different lines, raised in different environments, can display meaningfully different behavioral tendencies while still sharing core breed characteristics. Understanding this distinction helps owners set realistic expectations and develop training strategies tailored to their individual dog rather than relying solely on breed generalizations.

Social behavior in Sloughi develops through distinct life stages, and each stage presents opportunities and challenges for owners. The critical socialization window (roughly 3-16 weeks) is when exposure to varied people, animals, environments, and experiences has the greatest positive impact on long-term behavioral stability. However, socialization is not a one-time event—it is an ongoing process that requires continued positive exposure throughout the dog's life. Sloughi that are well-socialized as puppies but then isolated can experience social regression, while dogs with less-than-ideal early socialization can improve significantly with patient, positive exposure later in life. The key is consistency and quality of experiences rather than sheer quantity.

Managing the Sloughi's energy and drive within a household context requires strategic thinking rather than just exercise. While physical activity is important, mental stimulation is equally essential for this breed's behavioral balance. Sloughi that receive adequate physical exercise but insufficient mental engagement often develop nuisance behaviors such as excessive barking, destructive chewing, or repetitive behaviors. Effective mental stimulation for Sloughi includes structured training sessions, puzzle toys, scent work, novel environment exploration, and activities that engage their breed-specific instincts in appropriate ways. Many experienced Sloughi owners report that 15 minutes of focused mental exercise produces more behavioral satisfaction than an hour of repetitive physical activity.

Common Health Issues

Sloughis are generally healthy with few breed-specific problems:

Potential Health Concerns

  • Progressive Retinal Atrophy (PRA): A genetic eye condition that can lead to blindness. DNA testing is available.
  • Anesthesia Sensitivity: Like all sighthounds, they require special anesthesia protocols due to low body fat.
  • Autoimmune Disorders: Some Sloughis may develop autoimmune conditions.
  • Hemophilia: Occasionally reported in the breed; genetic testing available.

Physical Considerations

  • Cold Sensitivity: Their short coat and lean build make them susceptible to cold weather.
  • Thin Skin: Prone to cuts and scrapes, especially during high-speed activities.
  • Bloat Risk: Deep-chested breeds are at risk for gastric torsion.

Health Screening Recommendation

Request PRA testing and cardiac evaluations from breeders. Ensure your veterinarian understands sighthound physiology. Consider Embark DNA testing for comprehensive genetic screening.

Exercise & Activity Requirements

Sloughis need regular opportunities to run and exercise:

  • Daily Exercise: 1-2 hours of activity including long walks and running opportunities
  • Secure Fencing: Minimum 6 feet - they are fast and athletic jumpers
  • Lure Coursing: Excellent outlet for their natural hunting instincts
  • Running: They love to sprint and need safe areas to do so
  • Couch Potato Mode: Despite their energy needs, they love lounging at home
  • Warm Weather: They thrive in warm climates and enjoy basking in the sun

Training Tips for Sloughis

Sloughis are intelligent but require patient, relationship-based training:

  • Build Trust First: Training is most effective when a strong bond exists
  • Positive Reinforcement: Harsh methods will damage trust and set back progress
  • Respect Their Sensitivity: They are emotionally intelligent and respond to gentle guidance
  • Early Socialization: Critical for preventing excessive shyness with strangers
  • Accept Independence: They may understand but choose when to comply
  • Never Off-Leash: Their prey drive makes reliable recall nearly impossible

Nutrition & Feeding

Sloughis need quality nutrition to maintain their lean physique:

  • High-Quality Protein: Named meat sources should be the primary ingredient
  • Maintain Lean Build: Visible ribs and muscle definition are normal and healthy
  • Don't Overfeed: Extra weight is unhealthy for their joints and heart
  • Two Meals Daily: Split feedings help prevent bloat
  • Quiet Feeding: A calm environment helps prevent gulping and bloat risk

Grooming Requirements

Sloughis are low-maintenance in the grooming department:

  • Brushing: Weekly brushing with a soft brush or hound glove
  • Bathing: Only as needed - they are naturally clean dogs
  • Nail Trimming: Every 2-3 weeks
  • Ear Cleaning: Weekly checks and cleaning as needed
  • Dental Care: Daily brushing recommended
  • Skin Care: Monitor for cuts and scrapes; protect from sunburn

Is a Sloughi Right for You?

Sloughis Are Great For:

  • Experienced sighthound enthusiasts
  • Those wanting a loyal, devoted companion
  • Warm climate residents with secure yards
  • Quiet households that appreciate a calm indoor dog
  • Lure coursing and dog sport participants

Sloughis May Not Be Ideal For:

  • First-time dog owners
  • Families with young children or small pets
  • Cold climate residents without adequate indoor accommodation
  • Those wanting an immediately friendly, outgoing dog
  • People without secure, high fencing
  • Owners wanting off-leash reliability

A Sloughi is not for everyone, and that is perfectly fine. What matters is making the choice based on realistic expectations rather than idealized breed descriptions. Spend time around actual Sloughi dogs before committing. Visit rescues, attend meet-ups, or ask a friend who owns one if you can dog-sit for a weekend. That firsthand experience is worth more than a hundred online guides.

Experienced Sloughi owners will tell you the same thing: the first year is the hardest, and every year after that gets easier and more enjoyable. By the time your Sloughi hits its stride as an adult, you will wonder how you ever lived without one.
