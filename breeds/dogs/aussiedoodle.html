Aussiedoodle Overview

The Aussiedoodle (also known as Aussiepoo) is a striking designer breed created by crossing an Australian Shepherd with a Poodle. This combination produces an incredibly intelligent, athletic, and often stunningly beautiful dog that excels in active households.

Aussiedoodles combine the Australian Shepherd's herding instincts, work ethic, and stunning coat patterns (including merle) with the Poodle's intelligence and low-shedding qualities. They're known for being some of the smartest designer dogs, making them excellent for dog sports and advanced training.

Parent Breed Characteristics

  • Australian Shepherd: A highly intelligent herding breed known for their stunning coat colors, intense loyalty, and tireless work ethic. They're athletic, versatile, and bond closely with their families.
  • Poodle: Highly intelligent, athletic, and hypoallergenic. Available in Standard, Miniature, and Toy sizes, determining the Aussiedoodle's size.

The Aussiedoodle is a breed that commands attention not just for its physical appearance but for the depth of personality and capability it brings to a household. With a lifespan averaging 10-14 years, the decision to welcome an Aussiedoodle into your family is one that will shape your daily routine, activity levels, and emotional life for well over a decade. This breed's intelligent, energetic, loyal temperament is the product of generations of selective breeding for specific traits—understanding this heritage provides valuable insight into why your Aussiedoodle behaves the way it does and what it needs from you as an owner to truly thrive.

The difference between a good Aussiedoodle owner and a great one comes down to understanding what this particular animal actually needs, rather than projecting assumptions based on appearance or general expectations. Every Aussiedoodle has traits rooted in its background that influence behavior, health, and daily care requirements. Working with those traits — instead of against them — is the foundation of a successful experience.

Welcoming an Aussiedoodle into your home is less about adding a pet and more about adopting a new set of daily responsibilities. Their needs will influence how you organize your mornings, what you prioritize on weekends, and how you plan time away from home. The transition is smoother for owners who go in with realistic expectations about what this commitment actually looks like day to day.

Temperament & Personality

Aussiedoodles are known for their exceptional intelligence and energy:

  • Extremely Intelligent: Both parent breeds rank among the smartest, making Aussiedoodles exceptional learners.
  • High Energy: Need significant daily exercise and mental stimulation.
  • Loyal & Devoted: Form very strong bonds with their families.
  • Playful: Maintain puppy-like enthusiasm throughout their lives.
  • May Herd: Can inherit herding instincts, sometimes herding children or other pets.
  • Velcro Dog: Often want to be with their owners at all times.

The intelligent, energetic, loyal nature of the Aussiedoodle is not a simple personality label—it is a complex behavioral profile shaped by breed history, individual genetics, early socialization experiences, and ongoing environmental factors. What this means in practice is that two Aussiedoodle from different lines, raised in different environments, can display meaningfully different behavioral tendencies while still sharing core breed characteristics. Understanding this distinction helps owners set realistic expectations and develop training strategies tailored to their individual dog rather than relying solely on breed generalizations.

Common Health Issues

Aussiedoodles can inherit health conditions from either parent breed:

Eye Conditions

  • Progressive Retinal Atrophy (PRA): Causes gradual vision loss; DNA testing available.
  • Collie Eye Anomaly: Inherited from the Australian Shepherd side.
  • Cataracts: Can develop at various ages.

Drug Sensitivities

  • MDR1 Gene Mutation: Very important - Australian Shepherds often carry this gene causing sensitivity to certain medications (ivermectin, etc.). DNA testing essential.

Other Concerns

  • Hip Dysplasia: Common in both parent breeds.
  • Elbow Dysplasia: Can occur in larger Aussiedoodles.
  • Epilepsy: Can be inherited from either parent breed.
  • Thyroid Issues: Hypothyroidism can occur.
  • Allergies: Skin and food allergies are possible.

Critical: MDR1 Testing

Australian Shepherds commonly carry the MDR1 gene mutation causing severe reactions to certain medications. Always test your Aussiedoodle for this gene. Request MDR1 testing from breeders or use Embark DNA testing for comprehensive genetic screening including MDR1.

Preventive care for an Aussiedoodle is not just about annual exams — it is a mindset. Watching for changes in appetite, mobility, coat texture, and energy at home provides early clues that something may be developing beneath the surface. When you bring those observations to your vet consistently, you create a health timeline that makes pattern recognition possible. That partnership between attentive ownership and professional guidance is what keeps most Aussiedoodles in good shape throughout their lives.

Understanding your Aussiedoodle's genetic makeup can guide decisions about everything from exercise intensity to supplement choices. Breed-relevant DNA panels identify carrier status for conditions that may not show up for years, giving owners and veterinarians time to plan rather than scramble. It is one of the more practical tools available for anyone committed to keeping their Aussiedoodle in the best possible shape.

Planning for your Aussiedoodle's senior phase begins well before the grey appears. Around the midpoint of their expected lifespan, it makes sense to discuss enhanced screening options with your vet and consider whether their current diet and exercise regimen still fits their changing body. Aussiedoodles that receive thoughtful, consistent care through this transition tend to maintain vitality and comfort far longer than those whose care remains static.

Exercise & Activity Requirements

Aussiedoodles have HIGH exercise needs:

  • Daily Exercise: 1-2 hours of vigorous activity
  • Mental Stimulation: Equally important as physical exercise
  • Dog Sports: Excel at agility, flyball, dock diving, and frisbee
  • Hiking & Running: Make excellent adventure companions
  • Puzzle Toys: Need mental challenges to prevent boredom
  • Training: Enjoy and benefit from ongoing training throughout their lives

Training Tips for Aussiedoodles

Aussiedoodles are highly trainable but need consistent mental engagement:

  • Start Early: Begin training and socialization immediately
  • Challenge Their Minds: They get bored with repetitive, simple training
  • Positive Methods: Respond excellently to reward-based training
  • Manage Herding: Redirect herding instincts appropriately
  • Advanced Training: Consider dog sports or advanced obedience
  • Prevent Destructive Behavior: Boredom leads to destructive behaviors

Nutrition & Feeding

Active Aussiedoodles need proper nutrition:

  • High-Quality Protein: Active dogs need sufficient protein
  • Appropriate Calories: Match food intake to activity level
  • Joint Support: Consider supplements for active dogs
  • Omega Fatty Acids: Support coat and brain health
  • Portion Control: Despite activity, monitor weight

Top Food Choices for Aussiedoodles

The Farmer's Dog - Fresh, high-protein meals | Ollie - Custom fresh food for active dogs | Hill's Science Diet - Performance formulas

Good nutrition is the foundation of Aussiedoodle health, but that does not mean you need the most expensive food on the shelf. What matters is choosing a diet with quality protein sources, appropriate fat and fiber levels, and no unnecessary fillers. Your Aussiedoodle's response — steady weight, good energy, healthy coat, firm stools — is the best indicator that you have found the right food.

Grooming Requirements

Aussiedoodle coats need regular maintenance:

  • Coat Types: Wavy, curly, or straight; can inherit merle patterns
  • Brushing: Every 1-2 days to prevent matting
  • Professional Grooming: Every 6-8 weeks
  • Bathing: As needed, especially after outdoor activities
  • Ear Cleaning: Weekly, important for floppy ears
  • Nail Trimming: Every 2-4 weeks

Is an Aussiedoodle Right for You?

Aussiedoodles Are Great For:

  • Very active individuals or families
  • Those interested in dog sports or advanced training
  • People who can provide extensive mental stimulation
  • Homes with yards or access to outdoor activities
  • Experienced dog owners
  • Those wanting a loyal, devoted companion

Aussiedoodles May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Sedentary lifestyles or those with limited time for exercise
  • First-time dog owners (high maintenance)
  • Apartment dwellers without commitment to intense exercise
  • Those away from home frequently
  • Families with very small children (herding instincts)

Bringing any dog into your home is a long-term commitment, and the Aussiedoodle is no exception. Before signing papers or putting down a deposit, make sure the people you live with are equally on board. A Aussiedoodle thrives in a household where everyone participates in care, not just the person who wanted one. Shared responsibility makes the experience better for the dog and the family alike.
