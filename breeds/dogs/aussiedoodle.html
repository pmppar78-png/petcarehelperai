Aussiedoodle Overview

The Aussiedoodle (also known as Aussiepoo) is a striking designer breed created by crossing an Australian Shepherd with a Poodle. This combination produces an incredibly intelligent, athletic, and often stunningly beautiful dog that excels in active households.

They're known for being some of the smartest designer dogs, making them excellent for dog sports and advanced training.

  • Extremely Intelligent: Both parent breeds rank among the smartest, making Aussiedoodles exceptional learners.
  • High Energy: Need significant daily exercise and mental stimulation.
  • Loyal & Devoted: Form very strong bonds with their families.
  • Playful: Maintain puppy-like enthusiasm throughout their lives.
  • May Herd: Can inherit herding instincts, sometimes herding children or other pets.
  • Velcro Dog: Often want to be with their owners at all times.

At 10-23 inches (varies by size), a Intelligent, Energetic, Loyal Aussiedoodle still has MDR1 Gene Mutation on the health list and on the week. Other dogs are rated Good.

Common Health Issues

Aussiedoodles can inherit health conditions from either parent breed.

Eye Conditions

  • Progressive Retinal Atrophy (PRA): Causes gradual vision loss; DNA testing available.
  • Collie Eye Anomaly: Inherited from the Australian Shepherd side.

Drug Sensitivities

  • MDR1 Gene Mutation: Very important - Australian Shepherds often carry this gene causing sensitivity to certain medications (ivermectin, etc.). DNA testing essential.

Other Concerns

  • Hip Dysplasia: Common in both parent breeds.
  • Elbow Dysplasia: Can occur in larger Aussiedoodles.
  • Epilepsy: Can be inherited from either parent breed.
  • Allergies: Skin and food allergies are possible.

Critical: MDR1 Testing

Australian Shepherds commonly carry the MDR1 gene mutation causing severe reactions to certain medications. Always test your Aussiedoodle for this gene. Request MDR1 testing from breeders or use Embark DNA testing for comprehensive genetic screening including MDR1.

Preventive care for an Aussiedoodle is not just about annual exams — it is a mindset. That partnership between attentive ownership and professional guidance is what keeps most Aussiedoodles in good shape throughout their lives.

Understanding your Aussiedoodle's genetic makeup can guide decisions about everything from exercise intensity to supplement choices.

Planning for your Aussiedoodle's senior phase begins well before the grey appears. Aussiedoodles that receive thoughtful, consistent care through this transition tend to maintain vitality and comfort far longer than those whose care remains static.

Exercise & Activity Requirements

Aussiedoodles have HIGH exercise needs.

  • Mental Stimulation: Equally important as physical exercise
  • Dog Sports: Excel at agility, flyball, dock diving, and frisbee
  • Hiking & Running: Make excellent adventure companions
  • Puzzle Toys: Need mental challenges to prevent boredom
  • Training: Enjoy and benefit from ongoing training throughout their lives

Training Tips for Aussiedoodles

Aussiedoodles are highly trainable but need consistent mental engagement.

  • Start Early: Begin training and socialization immediately
  • Challenge Their Minds: They get bored with repetitive, simple training
  • Positive Methods: Respond excellently to reward-based training
  • Manage Herding: Redirect herding instincts appropriately
  • Advanced Training: Consider dog sports or advanced obedience
  • Prevent Destructive Behavior: Boredom leads to destructive behaviors

Nutrition & Feeding

  • High-Quality Protein: Active dogs need sufficient protein
  • Appropriate Calories: Match food intake to activity level
  • Joint Support: Consider supplements for active dogs
  • Omega Fatty Acids: Support coat and brain health
  • Portion Control: Despite activity, monitor weight

Grooming Requirements

Aussiedoodle coats need regular maintenance.

  • Coat Types: Wavy, curly, or straight; can inherit merle patterns

Aussiedoodles Are Great For:

  • Those interested in dog sports or advanced training
  • People who can provide extensive mental stimulation
  • Homes with yards or access to outdoor activities
  • Experienced dog owners

Aussiedoodles May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Sedentary lifestyles or those with limited time for exercise
  • First-time dog owners (high maintenance)
  • Apartment dwellers without commitment to intense exercise
  • Those away from home frequently
  • Families with very small children (herding instincts)
