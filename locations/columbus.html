Seasonal Pet Care in Columbus

Heartworm prevention is recommended at minimum April through November, though many vets now recommend year-round protection. Lyme disease is increasing in the upper Midwest.

Year-Round Safety in Columbus, Ohio

  • Summer: Hot and humid summers with heat waves common in July-August. Thunderstorms can be severe. Firework season in summer causes pet anxiety and escapes.
  • Winter: Harsh winters with extended sub-zero temperatures. Limit outdoor time during wind chills below 0°F. Road salt irritates and cracks paw pads — use pet-safe booties or wax.
  • Spring: Rapid flooding from snowmelt creates hazards. Tornado season begins in spring. Tick populations are expanding northward across the Midwest.
  • Fall: Hunting season increases risks for outdoor pets — use bright vests. Antifreeze toxicity risk rises as temperatures drop.
  • Severe Weather: Tornadoes are a significant risk spring through early fall. Include pets in your tornado safety plan and identify pet-safe shelter areas in your home.

How to Choose a Veterinarian in Columbus

Selecting the right veterinarian is one of the most important decisions you'll make for your pet. The American Animal Hospital Association (AAHA) accreditation indicates a practice voluntarily meets higher-than-average care standards — only about 12-15% of veterinary practices in North America achieve this certification.

Key Factors When Choosing a Columbus Vet

  • Accreditation: AAHA-accredited practices undergo rigorous evaluations of their medical protocols, facilities, and continuing education
  • Emergency protocols: Understand how the practice handles after-hours emergencies — do they have a referral relationship with a 24-hour hospital?
  • Communication style: A good vet explains diagnoses and treatment options clearly, welcomes questions, and respects your role in healthcare decisions
  • Specialties: If you have an exotic pet (bird, reptile, small mammal), verify the practice has experience with your species
  • Fear-free practices: Fear Free certification indicates staff trained to minimize stress, anxiety, and fear during veterinary visits
  • Reviews and reputation: Check Google reviews, Yelp, and ask for recommendations from local Columbus pet owners and breed-specific groups
