Emergency Veterinarians in

Houston has multiple 24-hour emergency veterinary hospitals spread throughout the metro area. Given Houston's size, know the closest emergency facility to your home before an emergency happens.

Tips for Pet

  • Gulf Coast Veterinary Specialists is a major emergency and specialty hospital
  • Multiple VCA and BluePearl locations serve the metro area
  • Traffic can be severe - know alternate routes to your emergency vet
  • Summer heat emergencies are common - watch for heatstroke signs

Pet Stores in

Houston has numerous pet stores from major retailers to locally-owned specialty shops. Many focus on specific pet types like aquariums or reptiles.

  • Local stores often carry products suited to Texas climate
  • Many stores offer delivery for heavy items like food and litter
  • Specialty aquarium stores cater to Houston's fishkeeping community

Regular Veterinarians in

Houston has veterinary practices throughout the metro area, from neighborhood clinics to large specialty hospitals. Finding one near your home or work makes regular care more convenient.

Tips for Finding

  • Many Houston vets offer extended hours and Saturday appointments
  • Ask about their heat-related emergency protocols
  • House-call vets are available throughout the Houston area
  • Texas A&M veterinary school provides referral services

Dog Parks in

Houston has numerous dog parks throughout the city and suburbs, many with shaded areas essential for Texas summers. Several parks have water features to help dogs cool off.

Tips for Dog

  • Visit early morning or evening to avoid extreme heat
  • Always bring water for your dog - not all parks have fountains
  • Watch for signs of overheating: excessive panting, drooling
  • Hot pavement can burn paws - test with your hand first

Pet Groomers in

Houston has groomers for every budget and need, from basic cuts to luxury spa treatments. Mobile grooming is especially popular given Houston's heat and sprawling layout.

  • Mobile grooming keeps pets in air conditioning during hot months
  • Book holiday grooming appointments well in advance
  • Summer cuts help dogs stay cool but don't shave double-coated breeds
  • Ask about flea and tick treatments given Houston's climate

Exotic Pet Veterinarians

Houston has several exotic animal specialists who treat birds, reptiles, and small mammals. Texas's warm climate means reptile ownership is popular, and exotic vets are in demand.

Tips for Exotic

    Houston licensing, rabies, and the city shelter

    Veterinarians who practice in Houston are licensed by the Texas Board of Veterinary Medical Examiners. The board publishes its own site at veterinary.texas.gov.

    Texas Health and Safety Code section 826.021 says the owner of a dog or cat must have the animal vaccinated against rabies by four months of age, and again on the schedule the health department sets. A veterinarian who gives the vaccine issues a certificate. The same section bars a county or city from registering or licensing an animal that has not been vaccinated as this section requires.

    The municipal shelter is BARC Animal Shelter and Adoptions. The City of Houston lists it at houstontx.gov/barc.

    The Companion Animal Parasite Council's heartworm guidelines place the largest numbers of canine cases in the southeastern United States and the Mississippi River Valley, and they name Texas among the regions where feline heartworm cases cluster. Forecasts and tropical-cyclone briefings for this coast come from the National Weather Service office in Houston/Galveston (weather.gov/hgx), which also points to the Harris County flood-warning system.
