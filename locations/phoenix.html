Find Vets & Pet Services in Phoenix

The Phoenix metro area, known as the Valley of the Sun, requires pet owners to be especially mindful of heat-related issues. From Scottsdale to Tempe, from Gilbert to Glendale, this guide helps you find quality pet services throughout the Phoenix area.

Emergency Veterinarians in Phoenix

Phoenix has several 24-hour emergency veterinary hospitals throughout the Valley. Heat emergencies are common in summer, so know your nearest emergency vet and recognize heatstroke signs.

Tips for Phoenix Pet Emergencies

  • Heat-related emergencies are the most common in Phoenix summers
  • Signs of heatstroke: excessive panting, drooling, lethargy, vomiting
  • Keep emergency vet addresses saved in your phone
  • Never leave pets in cars - even with windows cracked
  • Emergency visits typically start at $125-250 before treatment

Tip: Save the nearest emergency vet's number in your phone now — during a crisis, you won't have time to search.

Tip: Ask your regular vet which emergency clinic they refer to — this ensures smooth record transfers.

Tip: Emergency clinics in surrounding suburbs may have shorter wait times during peak hours.

Seasonal Pet Care in Phoenix

Valley fever is a major concern for dogs in Arizona, Nevada, and New Mexico. Heartworm prevention is recommended year-round despite arid conditions.

Year-Round Safety in Phoenix, Arizona

  • Summer: Extreme heat is the primary danger, with temperatures exceeding 110°F in summer. Pavement can reach 170°F. Walk pets only before 7 AM or after 8 PM. Carry water on every walk.
  • Winter: Mild winters in low-elevation areas. Higher elevations can see snow and freezing temperatures. Desert temperature swings between day and night can be dramatic.
  • Spring: Rattlesnake season begins in March as snakes emerge from brumation. Consider rattlesnake avoidance training for dogs. Valley fever (coccidioidomycosis) is endemic and affects dogs.
  • Fall: Scorpion activity peaks in fall. Coyote encounters increase as urban expansion meets wildlife habitat. Monsoon season (July-September) can cause flash flooding.
  • Severe Weather: Monsoon thunderstorms cause flash flooding July-September. Haboobs (dust storms) reduce visibility and can cause respiratory issues for pets.
