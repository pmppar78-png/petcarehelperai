Seasonal Pet Care in Phoenix

Valley fever is a major concern for dogs in Arizona, Nevada, and New Mexico. Heartworm prevention is recommended year-round despite arid conditions.

Year-Round Safety in Phoenix, Arizona

  • Summer: Extreme heat is the primary danger, with temperatures exceeding 110°F in summer. Pavement can reach 170°F. Walk pets only before 7 AM or after 8 PM. Carry water on every walk.
  • Winter: Mild winters in low-elevation areas. Higher elevations can see snow and freezing temperatures. Desert temperature swings between day and night can be dramatic.
  • Spring: Rattlesnake season begins in March as snakes emerge from brumation. Consider rattlesnake avoidance training for dogs. Valley fever (coccidioidomycosis) is endemic and affects dogs.
  • Fall: Scorpion activity peaks in fall. Coyote encounters increase as urban expansion meets wildlife habitat. Monsoon season (July-September) can cause flash flooding.
  • Severe Weather: Monsoon thunderstorms cause flash flooding July-September. Haboobs (dust storms) reduce visibility and can cause respiratory issues for pets.
