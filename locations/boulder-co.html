Year-Round Safety in Boulder, Colorado

  • Summer: Intense UV radiation at high altitude increases sunburn risk for light-colored and thin-coated pets. Afternoon thunderstorms are common June-August with lightning danger.
  • Winter: Severe cold and heavy snowfall in mountain areas. Altitude sickness can affect pets traveling from lower elevations. Road salt and magnesium chloride require paw washing after walks.
  • Spring: Rapid snowmelt creates dangerous stream crossings. Spring avalanche risk in mountain areas. Tick populations are expanding to higher elevations due to warmer temperatures.
  • Fall: Early snow can arrive by September at elevation. Hunting season requires high-visibility gear for pets. Wildlife encounters increase as animals prepare for winter.
  • Severe Weather: Mountain weather changes rapidly. Be prepared for sudden temperature drops and storms when hiking with pets. Carry emergency supplies on any backcountry outing.
