Cat Vomiting: Colors, Causes, and When to Worry

Cats vomit more often than dogs, but frequent or unusual vomiting needs attention.

Why Is My Cat Vomiting Brown Liquid?

Brown vomit is concerning and can indicate several conditions:

  • Digested blood - Looks like coffee grounds; indicates bleeding in stomach or upper GI tract
  • Eaten brown food - Less concerning if cat otherwise acts normal
  • Fecal vomiting - Foul-smelling brown vomit can indicate intestinal blockage - EMERGENCY
  • Bile mixed with food - Dark yellow to brown color from liver bile

When to worry: Brown vomit that looks like coffee grounds, smells fecal, occurs repeatedly, or accompanies lethargy and appetite loss requires immediate veterinary attention.

Cat Throwing Up After Eating Dry Food

Many cats vomit shortly after eating kibble due to:

  • Eating too fast - Kibble doesn't get chewed, then expands in stomach
  • Food sensitivity - Allergies to grains, chicken, or other ingredients
  • Hairballs - Dry food doesn't lubricate hairball passage like wet food
  • Inflammatory bowel disease - Chronic condition requiring veterinary management

Solutions: Use a slow-feeder or puzzle bowl, try grain-free options, switch to wet food partially or completely, feed smaller meals more frequently, and consider elevated feeding stations for better digestion.

Why Does My Cat Vomit Clear Liquid?

Clear or white foamy vomit typically indicates an empty stomach. The clear liquid is gastric juice and saliva. Common causes:

  • Empty stomach too long - Feeding schedule issue
  • Hairball attempt - Trying to bring up hair without success
  • Nausea - From various causes including motion sickness
  • Gastritis - Stomach inflammation

Occasional clear vomit followed by normal behavior isn't alarming. Repeated clear vomiting, especially with lethargy or food refusal, warrants a vet visit.

Cat Not Eating: Causes and What To Do

Why Is My Cat Not Eating But Drinking Water?

A cat who drinks but won't eat is showing signs of nausea or discomfort. Unlike food refusal with no water intake (more severe), maintaining hydration while avoiding food suggests:

  • Dental pain - Eating hurts but drinking doesn't
  • Nausea - Stomach upset, kidney disease, or medication side effects
  • Respiratory infection - Congestion reduces smell, making food unappealing
  • Stress - New pet, move, household changes
  • Early kidney disease - Causes nausea; cats drink more to compensate

Critical warning: Cats who don't eat for 48+ hours can develop hepatic lipidosis (fatty liver disease), a life-threatening condition. Never wait more than 2 days before seeking veterinary care for a cat not eating.

How Long Can a Sick Cat Go Without Eating?

Unlike dogs who can survive longer fasts, cats have unique metabolisms that make food refusal dangerous:

  • 24 hours: Concerning; try high-value foods (warmed wet food, meat baby food without onion/garlic, tuna water)
  • 48 hours: Veterinary intervention needed; fatty liver disease can begin developing
  • 72+ hours: Emergency; liver damage may already be occurring

Overweight cats are at even higher risk of hepatic lipidosis. A cat not eating is never "just picky" for multiple days - always seek veterinary care.

Cat Hiding and Not Eating or Drinking

Hiding combined with refusing both food and water is one of the most serious behavioral changes in cats. This indicates your cat feels too sick or vulnerable to engage normally. Possible causes:

  • Severe illness - Infections, organ failure, cancer
  • Pain - Cats hide when hurting
  • Toxin ingestion - Plants, chemicals, medications
  • Urinary blockage - Especially in male cats
  • Trauma - Falls, fights, injuries

This requires same-day veterinary care. A cat hiding and refusing water can become critically dehydrated within 24 hours.

Cat Litter Box Problems: Complete Guide

Why Is My Cat Pooping Outside the Litter Box Suddenly?

Sudden inappropriate defecation has different causes than urinating outside the box:

  • Constipation pain - Cat associates litter box with painful defecation
  • Arthritis - Climbing into box or posturing causes pain; try low-sided boxes
  • Inflammatory bowel disease - Urgency means they can't reach box in time
  • Dirty litter box - Some cats refuse to defecate in soiled litter
  • Stress marking - Response to household changes
  • Box location - Too far, too exposed, or near something scary

Solutions: Add more boxes (one per cat plus one), try different litter types, scoop twice daily, use low-entry boxes for seniors, and rule out medical causes with a vet visit.

Male Cat Straining to Pee But Nothing Comes Out

THIS IS A LIFE-THREATENING EMERGENCY.

Male cats can develop complete urinary blockages from crystals, mucus plugs, or stones. Signs include:

  • Repeated trips to litter box with little or no urine
  • Crying or vocalizing when trying to urinate
  • Licking genital area excessively
  • Vomiting (toxins building up)
  • Lethargy or hiding
  • Distended, painful abdomen

A blocked male cat can die within 24-48 hours from kidney failure and toxic buildup. Do not wait for a morning appointment - go to an emergency vet immediately, even at 2 AM.

Cat Peeing Small Amounts Frequently

Frequent urination of small amounts indicates lower urinary tract issues:

  • Urinary tract infection - More common in females
  • Feline idiopathic cystitis (FIC) - Stress-related bladder inflammation
  • Bladder stones - Cause irritation and frequent urge
  • Early blockage - In males, can progress to complete blockage
  • Diabetes - Also causes increased thirst

Schedule a vet appointment within 24 hours. Monitor male cats closely for signs of complete blockage.

People Also Ask: Urgent Cat Health Questions

Why Is My Cat Breathing Fast While Resting?

Normal resting respiratory rate for cats is 15-30 breaths per minute. Count breaths for 60 seconds while your cat is completely relaxed or sleeping. Rates consistently over 40 breaths per minute at rest can indicate:

  • Heart disease - Causes fluid buildup in lungs
  • Pleural effusion - Fluid around lungs
  • Asthma - Inflammatory airway disease
  • Pain - Causes rapid, shallow breathing
  • Fever or infection - Body fighting illness

Emergency signs: Open-mouth breathing in cats is ALWAYS abnormal and often indicates severe respiratory distress or heart failure. Blue-tinged gums or tongue means oxygen deprivation - seek emergency care immediately.

Why Does My Cat Meow Loudly at Night for No Reason?

Excessive nighttime vocalization, particularly in senior cats, often has medical causes:

  • Hyperthyroidism - Very common in cats over 10; causes restlessness, vocalization, weight loss despite good appetite
  • High blood pressure - Often secondary to kidney disease or hyperthyroidism
  • Cognitive dysfunction - Feline dementia causing disorientation, especially at night
  • Vision/hearing loss - Disorientation in darkness
  • Pain - Arthritis often worsens at night
  • Hunger - Especially if on weight management diet

Senior cats who start yowling at night should have bloodwork to check thyroid levels and kidney function. Treatment for hyperthyroidism often dramatically improves nighttime behavior.

Why Is My Cat Drooling Suddenly?

Unlike dogs, cats don't normally drool. Sudden drooling indicates a problem:

  • Dental disease - Painful teeth, gum disease, oral tumors
  • Nausea - Pre-vomiting, car sickness, illness
  • Toxin ingestion - Plants (lilies!), chemicals, medications
  • Foreign body - String, object stuck in mouth or throat
  • Oral ulcers - From kidney disease or viral infections
  • Bitter taste - Licking flea medication or bitter substances

Check your cat's mouth carefully (if they allow). Look for redness, swelling, broken teeth, or strings wrapped around the tongue. If cause isn't obvious and drooling continues, see a vet same day.

How Can I Tell If My Cat Is in Pain?

Cats hide pain well. Signs to watch for:

  • Behavior changes: Hiding, decreased activity, aggression when touched
  • Facial expressions: Squinted eyes, ears flattened, tense muzzle
  • Posture: Hunched position, reluctance to jump
  • Grooming changes: Over-grooming one area or neglecting grooming entirely
  • Appetite decrease: Painful cats often eat less
  • Vocalization: Unusual meowing, growling when approached
  • Litter box changes: Difficulty posturing, accidents outside box

Can Cats Eat Dog Food in an Emergency?

A few bites won't harm a cat, but dog food should never be regular diet. Cats need:

  • Taurine - Essential amino acid; deficiency causes blindness and heart failure
  • More protein - Cats are obligate carnivores
  • Vitamin A - Cats can't convert beta-carotene like dogs
  • Arachidonic acid - Essential fatty acid cats can't synthesize

In a true emergency (disaster, no cat food available), a healthy adult cat can eat dog food for 1-2 days. Never feed dog food to kittens, pregnant cats, or cats with health conditions.

Cat Emergency Warning Signs

These symptoms require immediate veterinary attention:

  • Male cat straining to urinate - Life-threatening blockage possible
  • Open-mouth breathing - Always abnormal in cats, indicates severe distress
  • Blue or pale gums - Oxygen deprivation
  • Collapse or inability to stand - EMERGENCY
  • Not eating for 48+ hours - Risk of fatty liver disease
  • Hiding and refusing water - Severe illness
  • Known toxin ingestion - Lilies, antifreeze, medications
  • Seizure lasting more than 2 minutes - EMERGENCY
  • Difficulty giving birth for more than 1 hour - EMERGENCY
