Hidden Costs Most Red-Eyed Tree Frog Owners Overlook

Red-eyed tree frogs need a tall humid enclosure, live plants or broad leaves, and a misting routine that dries back a little between sprays. The repeating costs are gut-loaded crickets, calcium and a multivitamin dust, treated water, and the electricity for a thermostat and a light on a timer.

Temperatures that sit in the mid-70s to low 80s Fahrenheit during the day, with a drop at night, match the way they are usually kept. A new frog stays in a separate enclosure until it is eating. Standing water deep enough to drown a tree frog, and a diet of dust-free crickets, are the two expensive mistakes.
