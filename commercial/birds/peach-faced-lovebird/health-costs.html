Peach-Faced Lovebird › Health Costs

Health Costs Vet

Quick Answer

A peach-faced lovebird is a small parrot with a disproportionate bite and a loud voice for its size. They need a flight cage, pellets and vegetables, and either a compatible mate or a person who is actually home. A mirror is not a friend. They can become egg-bound if they lay repeatedly. Nesting behavior, a bird on the cage floor, or a swollen abdomen needs an avian vet.

Best for Preventive

For adult Peach Faced Lovebirds, baseline bloodwork every two to three years is reasonable; for seniors, annual or biannual bloodwork becomes the standard of care.

Preventive Care Investment

Routines that match the Peach Faced Lovebird's actual energy and social preferences produce a more cooperative animal than routines adapted from other breeds.

Senior Nutrition Needs

Senior Peach Faced Lovebirds — typically age seven and up — benefit from a distinct approach to preventive care.

The conditions most likely to drive veterinary spend in the Peach Faced Lovebird's senior years — dental disease, orthopedic change, renal or hepatic drift — are detectable early with routine bloodwork and physical exam.

Establish baseline health metrics during your Peach-Faced Lovebird's first comprehensive examination: weight, body condition score, bloodwork panels, and any species-appropriate screening tests for this species.
