African Grey Parrot Nutritional Profile

Across a lifespan of 40-60 years (some live 80+ years), dietary consistency directly influences vitality and longevity. Larger birds like African Grey Parrot need controlled calorie intake to support their frame without excess weight that stresses joints. Omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids are particularly beneficial for African Grey Parrot to maintain plumage health and joint function.

Life-Stage Feeding Guide for African Grey Parrot

African greys eat a pellet and vegetable diet for decades, and the pellet is the base while seed stays a treat. Chicks on formula wean onto a softened pellet and soft vegetables, then onto a measured adult pellet. Adults do best with a formulated pellet as the base, dark leafy and orange vegetables daily, and a few nuts as training pay. Calcium matters in this species, especially for laying hens, and it comes from the diet and a cuttlebone, with blood calcium decided by an avian vet. Older greys often need the same foods in easier shapes if the beak is worn, plus a weight check, because they hide fat under feathers and also waste away quietly.
