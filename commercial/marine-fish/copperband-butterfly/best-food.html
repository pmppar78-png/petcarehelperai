What to feed

Copperband butterflyfish, Chelmon rostratus, are specialized pickers. The long snout takes food out of crevices. Wild copperbands often refuse pellets, refuse mysis, and pick at rockwork until the body thins behind the head. A tank that can hold one is large, with mature rock, and the fish still needs a diet you can see it eat. Some individuals take Aiptasia, clams, and mysis. Some never do. Watch the fish eat before you buy it, and plan on several small offerings a day of whatever it has accepted.

A copperband that is learning may take live blackworms or a clam on the half shell and ignore everything else. Use that food while you introduce mysis and a small pellet. A fish that gets thinner every week is not in a training program. It is starving. The snout and the eyespot do not change. The body does. Judge the line behind the head.

They are usually reef safe with corals and dangerous to fan worms and small shrimp, which they see as food. Pull uneaten clams and mysis the same day. A copperband that eats a varied meaty menu and holds a full profile is a success with this species. Many never get there. Older copperbands that do eat still need frequent small meals. They do not become easy flake fish with time.
