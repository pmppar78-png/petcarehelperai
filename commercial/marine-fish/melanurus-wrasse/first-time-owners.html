Setting up the tank a melanurus wrasse can sleep in

A melanurus wrasse stays near 5 inches and comes from the western Pacific. Shops sell it as a beginner reef fish because it hunts flatworms, pyramidellid snails, bristleworms, and small snails. That job is real. The husbandry around the job is a cycled 50 gallon, a lid, and a sand bed the fish can dive into when the lights go off. Bare glass and crushed coral give it nowhere to sleep. It ricochets and damages the mouth.

Build the system before you choose the fish. A cycled 50 gallon and live rock commonly land in the $50 to $300 band on this page. Frozen mysis and brine shrimp are the $60 to $120 band. Sand deep enough to enter, plus caves, is the $30 to $150 band. Salt, a test kit, and a lid that closes the gaps around pipes sit on the same first receipt. Together those lines still total $290 to $980 on this checklist. A hole that looks too small is still a hole. Melanurus leave when the lights go out.

Feed meaty food from the first evening: mysis, brine shrimp, and a pellet the fish will take off the rock. The pest-hunting is a bonus on top of that meal. A wrasse that only finds the occasional flatworm loses weight while the glass stays clean. Offer food where you can see it eaten. An uneaten cube left in the sand becomes a nitrate problem in a tank you are also trying to keep at reef ranges.

If corals share the tank, the mineral background on this page is alkalinity of 8 to 12 dKH, calcium of 380 to 450 ppm, and magnesium of 1250 to 1350 ppm. Test those before the wrasse arrives, and keep ammonia and nitrite at 0 ppm. A new tank that has not finished cycling will kill this fish on the same schedule it kills any other reef introduction.

Reef-safe on a label is the optimistic half. A melanurus may eat small shrimp, a mandarin's copepods, or a tiny ornamental crab. Stock the shrimp after you have watched the wrasse for a month, or leave them out. Semi-aggressive behavior shows up toward smaller wrasses. One melanurus in a 50 gallon is the usual plan. Two is a fight unless the tank is much larger and the rock is broken into territories.

Quarantine the new fish and watch the appetite. Flashing on the rock, a refusal to eat, or a dusting of white spots is marine ich or flukes until a marine veterinarian says otherwise. A beginner label does not make the fish immune. The call is the same week, while the fish is still in the hospital tank, with a sand tray so it can sleep. Copper and other treatments stay with that clinic's protocol for the species in front of them. This page does not print a dose.

The first month goes wrong in a predictable order: no lid, a dusting of sand in one corner, a meal plan that is only "it will eat the pests," and a shrimp tank that loses its cleanup crew. Spend the $290 to $980 on the cycled tank, the lid, the sand, and the freezer food. Then buy the wrasse that is already eating at the shop.

Walk the rim with a flashlight after the lid is on. Overflow teeth, filter cutouts, and the gap behind a powerhead are the exits. Plug them before the fish learns the route at lights-out. A melanurus on the floor is a lost fish and a lost $290 to $980 setup's whole point.
