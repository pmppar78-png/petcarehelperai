Alkalinity of 8 to 12 dKH, calcium of 380 to 450 ppm, and magnesium of 1250 to 1350 ppm are the reef side of a tank you hope will keep its corals while this fish works the rocks for pests.

The melanurus wrasse bill starts with a cycled 50 gallon and live rock, commonly $50 to $300 before the fish is even chosen, then frozen mysis and brine shrimp at $60 to $120 and a sand bed plus caves at $30 to $150. Salt, a test kit, and a lid that a wrasse cannot jump through go on that same first receipt. The supply total used on this species' checklist runs $290 to $980.

Quarantine the new wrasse and watch the appetite. A marine veterinarian is the right call if it stops eating or flashes on the rock, because a beginner-labeled wrasse still dies of the same ich and flukes as the rest of a new reef.
