What to feed

Coral beauty angelfish, Centropyge bispinosa, are hardy dwarf angels by angel standards. They reach a few inches, act semi-aggressive, and need a mature reef with grazing room, about 70 gallons being a fair target. Feed spirulina, mysis, and foods made for angelfish, twice a day. A mature reef with algae on the back surfaces suits that habit better than a sterile new tank full of dry rock. They will nip coral polyps when the prepared food is thin.

A coral beauty that arrives fat and then thins is telling you the tank is too clean or the other fish are taking the mysis. Add a clip of seaweed and a pellet it can learn. These angels usually accept prepared food more readily than a copperband does. A fish that fades, hides, and shows a hollow belly after a fight needs a look at territory as well as calories.

Two small meals and the natural film are the plan. Pull leftovers. A coral beauty with a deep purple-blue body and an intact belly is eating. White spots and flashing are ich. Treat the disease and keep the food available. Older coral beauties eat the same spirulina, mysis, and angel formula. They stay small. They do not grow into a large Pomacanthus diet.
