What to feed this Flame Angelfish

Flame angelfish, Centropyge loricula, are dwarf angels from the Pacific. A mature tank of about 70 gallons gives one enough grazing surface and swimming room. The diet is omnivore: spirulina, mysis, an angelfish formula, and algae, offered twice a day. They pick at film on the rocks between meals. A sterile new tank with only a pinch of mysis will not hold the body condition. Some individuals nip coral, and a hungry one nips more.

A flame angel that thins behind the head needs more frequent small meals, including a prepared sponge or angel food if it refuses pellets. Many learn pellets. A fish that never accepts them still has to eat, so keep the mysis and spirulina coming while you work the pellet in. Ich and a sudden refusal to eat travel together in new angels. Check the water and the skin. Keep feeding a fish that will still pick.

They are territorial. One dwarf angel is easier to feed than two that fight over the same clip. Pull uneaten mysis. A flame angel with a round flank, a steady red-orange color, and interest in both algae and mysis is on a workable week. Older flames eat that same mix. Shift the proportion toward algae if the fish gets a swollen belly on a mysis-only run.

Centropyge loricula picks through Pacific reef rock for tiny invertebrates and algae film, and a home specimen stays under about 4 inches. Give it a morning serving and a late serving, and leave a little film on the back glass so the angel can graze between them. A fish that has learned a pellet still needs the frozen mysis. Newly arrived flame angels sometimes spend several days on mysis before they accept anything dry, so keep those portions small enough to finish. A pellet made for a large emperor angel is the wrong size here. If this angel starts picking at LPS corals, add a third small feeding of angelfish formula and watch whether the picking slows. The red bars look sharp on a fish that is eating. An angel that fades and stays in a corner has either lost a territory fight or stopped finding the food, and the belly tells you which.
