What to feed

Blue damsels, Chrysiptera cyanea, are hardy omnivores and often the first fish in a new saltwater tank. One or a small group needs about 30 gallons, with the warning that a group may fight as they mature. They take flakes, pellets, and frozen food eagerly, and they will crowd a shy fish off the same pinch. Twice a day is enough. They will graze some film and still need the prepared food. They leave corals alone and they crowd other fish off the food.

A blue damsel that is thin is rare. The usual problem is the damsel eating everyone else's mysis. Feed shy species first, in another part of the tank. A damsel that darkens, stops eating, and scratches has ich or a water problem, which even hardy fish get in a new tank. Keep offering a small pellet to one that will eat. Do not pour a cup of flake into a quarantine tank.

Juveniles are bright blue with a yellow tail on many individuals, and the yellow fades with age or with sex. Judge nutrition by the belly. The yellow tail can fade with age or sex on a fish that is eating well. A hollow belly would be. Pull uneaten flake. Damsels are easy to overfeed because they always act hungry. Older blue damsels eat the same flakes, pellets, and frozen foods. They get more territorial with age. The diet stays flakes, pellets, and frozen foods.
