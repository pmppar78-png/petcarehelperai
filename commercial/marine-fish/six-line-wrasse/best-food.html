Feeding Guidelines for

Six-line wrasses, Pseudocheilinus hexataenia, hunt small crustaceans. Feed mysis or finely chopped seafood once or twice a day so they have a meal beyond the pests they wipe out. They are aggressive in a small tank, and a hungry one is worse. Give rock to hunt through and a lid. Flake sprinkled for the clowns will not keep this wrasse fed.

What to feed

Six-line wrasses, Pseudocheilinus hexataenia, are small, active carnivores. One fish needs about 30 gallons. The diet is mysis, brine shrimp, pellets, and a habit of hunting flatworms and other small pests. Two meals a day of prepared food matter, because a tank can run out of pests and the wrasse will then get thin or start harassing other fish. Feed the wrasse. Do not hire it as an unpaid cleaner and forget the freezer.

They are fast and will take food meant for shy gobies and slow cardinals. Feed the shy fish first or separately. A six-line with a hollow belly is unusual and means the fish is sick or newly added and not yet taking pellets. Offer mysis until the belly rounds, then keep the pellet in the rotation. They sleep in a mucus cocoon in the rock. A wrasse that is out at night and looks faded may be unwell.

They can pick on peaceful tankmates. A well-fed six-line is still territorial. Food reduces the hunting. It does not change the species. Pull uneaten mysis. A fish that eats, shows six intact lines, and spends the day picking through the rock is on the right menu. Older six-lines eat the same meaty foods. They stay small and they stay fast.
