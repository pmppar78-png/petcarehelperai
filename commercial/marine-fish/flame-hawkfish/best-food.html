Best for Transitioning Flame Hawkfish's Diet

What to feed

Flame hawkfish, Neocirrhites armatus, perch on rock and lunge at meaty foods. One fish needs about 30 gallons. Feed mysis and pellets to the perch, twice a day. They eat small crustaceans in the wild and will eat ornamental shrimp in a reef. Plan on that. A flame hawk that is not given mysis will hunt the shrimp you wanted to keep. They take food in the current at the perch. A flake at the far end of the tank is easy to miss.

A flame hawk with a hollow belly is usually new and not yet trained, or it is being outcompeted. They are bold once settled and then they are the ones outcompeting the gobies. Feed shy fish away from the perch. These hawkfish have no swim bladder, so they sit on the rock. A fish on its side on the rock may be resting. A fish that stops eating and shows white spots needs an ich check. Keep offering mysis to one that still strikes.

Two meals a day, a few pieces, are enough. They are easy to overfeed once they learn the tongs, and leftover mysis rots in the rock. A flame hawk that strikes, swallows, and returns to the same coral head is on the right menu. The red color holds on a normal marine diet. The red color holds when the diet is mysis and a marine pellet. Older flame hawks eat the same mysis and pellets from the same style of perch. They stay small and they stay predatory.

Neocirrhites armatus lives on Pocillopora heads in the Pacific and rarely swims far from that perch. Drop mysis and a small pellet just up-current of the coral or rock it has chosen, morning and evening. Four or five pieces are a meal. A flame hawk that learns the feeding stick will lunge and then sit again. That lunge is the whole feeding style. Shrimp you hoped to keep are on the same menu in the fish's mind, so feed the hawk before you add ornamental shrimp, and assume some risk anyway. The fish has no swim bladder, so a hawk resting on the rock is doing what the species does. Worry when it ignores the mysis. A sunken belly behind the red head means the food is landing somewhere else, usually in a wrasse's mouth. Move the feeding closer to the perch.
