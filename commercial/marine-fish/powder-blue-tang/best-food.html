Feeding Guidelines for

Powder blue tangs are herbivores. Keep dried seaweed available daily, add a spirulina preparation, and use mysis only as a side. Put the greens in the tank every day. A weekend-only clip leaves the fish hungry. A meat-heavy diet and a bare tank are how Acanthurus leucosternon thins and then catches ich. Clip the seaweed so it cannot sink under the rock and rot.

What to feed

Powder blue tangs, Acanthurus leucosternon, are striking and demanding herbivores. An adult needs on the order of 125 gallons, excellent water, and a daily algae diet: nori, spirulina, and algae pellets, with only a modest mysis side. They are famous for arriving thin and for catching ich. A powder blue that is not eating nori on the first days needs the clip placed where the fish already hides, several times a day, until the belly fills.

White spots, scratching, and a refusal to eat are the emergency pattern. Have a disease plan before the fish comes home. Food will not cure ich, and a powder blue that stops eating declines in days. Keep offering seaweed to any fish still willing to pick. A meat-heavy diet is the wrong rescue. These tangs graze. Give them algae.

They can be aggressive toward other tangs. One powder blue per tank is the usual limit, with the food planned for that one fish. Pull old nori. A fish with a deep blue body, a yellow scalpel area, and a rounded belly is in good condition. A pinched profile means the clip is losing. Older powder blues eat the same nori and spirulina. Stability of the diet matters more than variety for its own sake.
