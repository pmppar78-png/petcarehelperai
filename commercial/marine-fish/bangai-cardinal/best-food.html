What to feed

Banggai cardinalfish, Pterapogon kauderni, are slow, heavy-bodied cardinals from a small range in Indonesia. A pair or a small group needs about 20 gallons with branching cover. They eat small crustaceans. Mysis, brine shrimp, and a marine pellet they can chew, twice a day, are the foods. Damsels and wrasses often clear that food before a Banggai leaves the branches. Feed the Banggai on purpose, with a baster if you have to.

They are mouthbrooders. A male holding eggs eats less or not at all until he releases the fry. That fast is normal. The fry then need tiny foods, and the adults return to mysis. A Banggai that is not holding eggs and still has a hollow belly is missing meals. The silver bars and black spots stay visible even on a thin fish. Judge the depth of the chest.

They are poor hunters of fast prey and good at a stationary pellet. Choose a pellet that hovers long enough for a slow Banggai to bite it. Pull what they miss. A Banggai that eats, holds a calm hover, and keeps a deep chest is fed. Ich and a sudden stop in appetite need a water and disease check. Older Banggais eat the same mysis and pellets. They do not speed up with age, so the feeding method stays targeted.
