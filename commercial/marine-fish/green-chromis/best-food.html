What to feed

Green chromis, Chromis viridis, are schooling planktivores. A real school needs about 30 gallons or more and open water above the rock. Feed small flakes, small pellets, and frozen mysis or brine shrimp, twice a day, scattered through the water column. They eat zooplankton in the ocean. A clip of nori under them is for a tang. A green chromis that only gets leftover algae film will starve in a tank that looks well stocked.

The school should rush the food together. Individuals that peel off, darken, and show a pinched belly are the ones to watch. Green chromis are often added in groups and then lost to ich, which looks like white spots and frantic scratching. Separate the healthy feeders' needs from the treatment plan, and do not stop feeding fish that are still eating. Choose a pellet small enough that they swallow it. Spit pellets become waste.

Six fish is a starting school, and the bioload has to match the filter. Pull uneaten mysis and brine. A green chromis with a silver-green side, a steady place in the school, and a filled belly is on the right diet. Older green chromis keep eating the same small pellets and mysis. They do not move to the sand to become scavengers, and they should not be fed like one.

Green chromis are the same planktivore, Chromis viridis, and they need the food in the water column. Twice a day, scatter fine flakes or small pellets and a portion of frozen mysis or brine shrimp across the length of a 30-gallon or larger tank. The school should rush it together. A fish that drops out, darkens, and shows a pinched belly is missing meals or is sick. Check the water either way. They spit oversized pellets, which then rot on the sand, so match the pellet to the mouth and remove what they reject. A nori clip in the same tank belongs to a tang if you have one. Chromis in that tank still need the pellet and the mysis. Keep the group large enough to be a school and small enough for the filter. A green chromis with a silver-green side and a steady place in the group is on this diet.
