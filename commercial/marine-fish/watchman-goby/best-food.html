What to feed

Yellow watchman gobies, Cryptocentrus cinctus, live in a burrow, often with a pistol shrimp, and eat small meaty foods. A pair fits in about 20 gallons with a sand bed they can dig. Feed frozen mysis, brine shrimp, and a small pellet at the burrow mouth, twice a day. They will not cross a long tank to find a flake, and they will not sift sand the way a diamond goby does. Food has to land where they perch.

A watchman with a hollow belly is usually losing the mysis to a faster fish. Use a baster at the hole after the lights dim. The shrimp roommate does not feed the goby. A goby that never comes out may be guarding a molt of the shrimp or may be starving in the back of the burrow. Look for it at feeding time over several days before you add another fish.

They are hardy eaters once the food arrives. Pull leftover mysis off the sand so the burrow does not fill with rot. A watchman that pops out, takes a mysis, and retreats is the normal pattern. White spots and scratching are ich. Keep feeding a fish that still comes to the door. Older watchmen eat the same mysis and pellets at the same hole. They do not change address or menu just because they have lived in the tank for a year.
