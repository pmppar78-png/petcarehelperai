Feeding Guidelines for Longnose Hawkfish

Feed a longnose hawkfish, Oxycirrhites typus, at its perch twice a day: mysis and a bit of chopped shrimp or a small marine pellet. Take away what falls into the coral. It has no swim bladder and will not chase food across the sand the way a wrasse does. A diet of only dried seaweed leaves this fish thin. Watch it swallow the mysis before you add more.

What to feed

Longnose hawkfish, Oxycirrhites typus, perch in sea fans and other structure and eat small crustaceans. One fish needs about 30 gallons or more if a sea fan or a tall rock gives it a perch. Feed mysis, small pieces of seafood, and a pellet, twice a day, at the perch. The long snout picks food out of the current. Mysis and a small pellet match it. Nori is the wrong staple. A longnose that never leaves the fan and gets thin is not seeing the food. Use tongs or a baster at the perch. They are less ravenous than a flame hawk and easier to underfeed in a busy reef. A hollow belly behind a long snout is the picture of a missed week. Once they learn the feeding stick they become reliable. Keep the pieces small enough for the mouth.

They sit still for hours. That rest is normal when the fish still strikes at feeding time. A hawk that ignores mysis and breathes hard needs the water checked. Pull uneaten food out of the coral. Older longnose hawks eat the same small meaty meals. They do not switch to a grazer diet, and they do not need a larger and larger pellet as if they were groupers. The snout stays the feeding tool.
