Strengths and Tradeoffs

Decision Guidance for Old English Sheepdog vs Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever

A coated drover beside the smallest retriever

The Old English Sheepdog card is a herding dog of 60 to 100 pounds and 21 inches and up, living about 10 to 12 years. Exercise is moderate to high, on the order of one to two hours, and the shed line says the coat requires extensive grooming. The profile prices professional grooming at $1,200 to $2,400 a year. Food is $600 to $1,000, routine veterinary care $300 to $600, insurance $500 to $900. That grooming number is the comparison. It is larger than the toller's entire published food band.

The Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever card is the smallest of the retrievers: 35 to 50 pounds, 17 to 21 inches, 12 to 14 years. Exercise is very high. The profile asks for 90 minutes or more of vigorous work and names dock diving, agility, flyball, and obedience. Food is $400 to $900, often described as two to three cups of a sport formula because the metabolism is high when the dog is actually working. Routine veterinary care is $300 to $600. Insurance shows up as $350 to $700 a year and, in the insurance note, as about $40 to $65 a month. Grooming is $100 to $400. A yearly total on that page runs about $1,800 to $4,000. Housing language asks for an active home, outdoor access, and a fenced yard, with water nearby treated as a bonus.

Coat is the sheepdog's job and the toller's weekend. An Old English Sheepdog disappears under hair that has to be brushed to the skin or it mats into a pelt. The profile warns against shaving a neglected coat down to damaged skin. Plan a weekly session on the floor with a pin brush and a comb, and plan the $1,200 to $2,400 salon line if you will not keep that session. The toller is brushed two or three times a week, daily during the heavy seasonal shed, and bathed about monthly. Ears and nails are ordinary retriever care. The shed is real. It is still a different bill from a corded, rolling sheepdog coat.

Health folders diverge in the conditions you say out loud at the breeder's kitchen table. For the sheepdog, ask for OFA hip and elbow scores, an eye certification, and thyroid testing. The page lists hip dysplasia, elbow dysplasia, progressive retinal atrophy, primary ciliary dyskinesia, deafness in some dogs, autoimmune disease including immune-mediated hemolytic anemia, and bloat. It cites an OFA hip figure of about 15.8 percent in evaluated Old English Sheepdogs at a typical 60 to 100 pounds. For the toller, the page lists progressive retinal atrophy, Addison's disease, other immune-mediated disease, collie eye anomaly, and hypothyroidism. Warning signs it prints for Addison's are lethargy, vomiting, and weight loss. It wants an annual eye exam because of PRA, and it says to monitor thyroid function.

Those lists change the first year. A sheepdog puppy is growing into 60 to 100 pounds on hips that the OFA number says are worth filming, with elbows on the same form, and with a deep chest that puts bloat on the list. Two meals, a rest after eating, and a clinic that can operate are the habits to start before the dog is heavy. A toller puppy is growing into 35 to 50 pounds and into a 90-minute engine. The early vet conversation is eyes, the Addison's signs, and the thyroid, plus hips because the toller page also names hip dysplasia. A limp in the sheepdog puppy is a large-dog growth problem. A toller who is dull, vomiting, or losing weight needs the Addison's conversation the same day, which is what that warning box is for.

Exercise is the daily split people underestimate because both dogs are "active." One to two hours for a sheepdog can be a long walk, training, and a chance to move a heavy body without pounding immature joints. Ninety minutes of vigorous work for a toller is a retriever day: retrieves, water if you have it, a class. A fenced yard matters for both, and the toller profile is the one that treats the fence as part of the housing sentence. The sheepdog will also patrol and will also bark. The coat makes a hot dog in summer. Work the sheepdog in the cool part of the day and keep the cords free of debris, because mats hold heat and hide skin.

Deafness is on the sheepdog list, so a puppy who does not wake, or who startles only when you enter the line of sight, needs a hearing check. Primary ciliary dyskinesia is named as something that can affect eyes and breathing. Those are veterinarian conversations, and they are reasons to meet the parents awake and moving. The toller's collie eye anomaly and PRA are reasons to read an eye certificate. A phone light is a poor stand-in for that paper. Hypothyroidism on the toller list matches the thyroid testing the sheepdog page also requests. Same gland, two breeds, two folders.

Money over the lifespan favors a clear head. The sheepdog's 10 to 12 years include a grooming line that can exceed $2,000 in a year when you pay a professional, plus food for a dog who may reach 100 pounds. The toller's 12 to 14 years include a smaller food band, a smaller grooming band, and a larger time bill. Insurance at $500 to $900 for the sheepdog and $350 to $700 for the toller is the planning range. Read whether hip disease, bloat, hereditary eye disease, and Addison's are covered before you bind anything. The $40 to $65 monthly toller note is the same conversation in smaller units. A gastropexy or an Addisonian crisis leaves either range.

Children are rated excellent on both cards when the household is actually set up. A sheepdog at 80 pounds under a mop of hair can knock a child down while being gentle. A toller at 45 pounds will do it at speed, because the profile's sports list is full of games that reward explosion. Teach a settle to both before the adult weight arrives. The sheepdog's adaptable, gentle, smart description is still a drover from the early nineteenth century who moved stock to market. The toller's intelligent, alert, outgoing description is still a duck dog from Nova Scotia who was bred to frolic at the shoreline and lure birds into range. The games look like play. The stamina is the job.

Grooming skill is the honest filter. If you like a dog you can dry and brush to the skin every week for a decade, the sheepdog's 21-inch-and-up frame and 60 to 100 pound band are the body under that coat. If you like a dog you can run, swim, and compete, and you will brush a retriever coat through two heavy sheds a year, the toller's 17 to 21 inches and 35 to 50 pounds fit a smaller crate and a louder exercise calendar. Beginner-friendly is scored 3 out of 5 on the toller page. The sheepdog page does not pretend the coat is optional. Pick the calendar you will keep when the puppy coat is gone.

At the sheepdog litter, part the parents' coats to the skin, watch them trot, and read hips, elbows, eyes, and thyroid. Ask who is deaf in the pedigree. At the toller litter, watch a dog work or at least watch the energy the profile promises, read eyes, and ask what the breeder does when a dog is lethargic or vomits. Weigh the puppy plan against the adult number you are actually buying: up to 100 pounds of coated herder, or up to 50 pounds of smallest retriever. The fence, the food scoop, and the afternoon are different objects.

Senior care follows the shorter sheepdog clock. Ten to twelve years of hip risk at 15.8 percent in the evaluated population means a lean old sheepdog is a welfare choice, and a matted old sheepdog is a skin emergency under the hair you stopped brushing. Twelve to fourteen years for a toller means the eye exam and the thyroid check continue after the agility weekends slow down. Addison's does not retire when the dog turns seven. Keep the warning signs on the fridge for the life of the dog: lethargy, vomiting, weight loss. Keep the sheepdog's bloat signs there too: restlessness, a swelling abdomen, unproductive retching. Different emergencies, both already written on these profiles, both worth more than another paragraph of temperament adjectives.

A practical sheepdog month also includes the floor. Hair mats in the feathering of the legs and in the pants, and it hides grass seeds. Part those areas every week during the growing season, and budget a groomer visit when the coat rolls into cords you can no longer get a comb through. The toller's month is shorter at the grooming table and longer at the park. Two or three brushes, a blow week when the undercoat dumps, and the 90 minutes on the days you would rather have stayed inside. If those minutes disappear, the toller page's sports list becomes a list of things the dog will improvise in the house.

Gear prices sit inside the bands you already have. The sheepdog's $1,200 to $2,400 grooming line can include a standing appointment every six to eight weeks plus the brushes you still use between visits. The toller's $100 to $400 grooming line is brushes, a rake for the blow, nail care, and the occasional bath. Crates, bowls, and a yard vacuum for the sheepdog shed are supplies on top of food. The toller's retrieves, a life jacket if you boat, and class fees sit on top of the $400 to $900 food band. Add them once, honestly, before you decide the smaller dog is the cheap dog. The smaller dog is the busy dog. The larger dog is the coated dog.

Bring both adult pictures to the clinic when you book puppy visits. Tell them you are choosing between a 60 to 100 pound sheepdog with a 15.8 percent hip figure in evaluated dogs and a 35 to 50 pound toller whose page flags Addison's, PRA, and thyroid. Ask which hospital can handle a bloat surgery tonight, and which one will run an Addison's test on a dull vomiting dog without sending you across the county. The answer is part of the breed choice. A perfect litter in a county with no overnight surgery is a weak plan for the sheepdog, and a perfect toller litter with no one who has treated Addison's is a weak plan for the retriever.
