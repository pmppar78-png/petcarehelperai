Temperament and Personality Differences

Bordoodle brings an intelligent, friendly, active energy to the household, compared to Border Terrier's affectionate, happy, plucky disposition.

Best for Families with Children

Bordoodle's intelligent nature and Border Terrier's affectionate temperament each present different dynamics with younger family members.

Which Is Right for Your Family?

Feeding and Nutrition Comparison

Bordoodle's Medium (30-60 lbs) frame and high energy demands require specific caloric targeting, while Border Terrier's Small (11-16 lbs) build and moderate activity level call for different nutritional proportions. Bordoodle's health profile (hip and joint concerns along with other health conditions common in this breed) may necessitate prescription or limited-ingredient diets, while Border Terrier's predispositions (joint-related conditions and other breed-specific health issues) have their own dietary implications.

Living Space and Habitat Requirements

Bordoodle at Medium (30-60 lbs) needs a crate appropriately scaled to their dimensions and high activity pattern, while Border Terrier at Small (11-16 lbs) requires crate sizing matched to their own build and moderate energy level. Bordoodle's intelligent, friendly, active temperament influences how they interact with their living space, while Border Terrier's affectionate, happy, plucky nature creates different environmental needs.

Long-Term Commitment Assessment

Bordoodle's intelligent, friendly, active temperament and high activity needs must remain compatible with your lifestyle through potential moves, career changes, and family growth. Border Terrier's affectionate, happy, plucky character and moderate demands create a different long-term compatibility profile. Care complexity evolves with age: Bordoodle's health predispositions (orthopedic problems) and Border Terrier's risks (orthopedic problems) may require increasing management in later years.
