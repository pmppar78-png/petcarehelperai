Multi-Pet Household Setup for Mudi

If introducing Mudi into a home with existing dogs or other animals, careful space planning prevents territorial conflicts and stress. Each animal should have their own crate, feeding station, and resting area. For Mudi with their versatile, intelligent, alert, courageous temperament, introduction should be gradual over days to weeks, starting with scent exchange before visual or physical contact. Shared common areas should have multiple exit points so no animal feels trapped. Resource guarding is common during transitions; provide duplicate resources (food bowls, water sources, enrichment items) in separate locations. Monitor interactions closely during the first several weeks, and be prepared to separate dogs if signs of aggression or excessive stress appear.

Safety-Proofing Your Home for Mudi

Making your home safe for Mudi requires addressing hazards specific to this breed. Secure or remove toxic plants common in households, including lilies, philodendrons, and poinsettias. Store cleaning chemicals, medications, and small ingestible objects out of reach. Cover or redirect electrical cords that a curious Mudi might investigate. Install appropriate barriers to prevent access to dangerous areas like balconies, pools, or garages. For Mudi at Medium (18-29 lbs) size, check for gaps or spaces where they could become trapped or escape. Secure window screens and ensure any fans or heating elements are protected. Regular safety audits of your Mudi's environment every few months catch new hazards as household items and arrangements change over time.
