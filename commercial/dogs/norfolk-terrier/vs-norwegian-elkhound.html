Best for Families

Norfolk Terrier's fearless nature and Norwegian Elkhound's bold temperament each present different dynamics with younger family members.

Health and Lifespan

Norfolk Terrier is predisposed to Heart Conditions, Orthopedic Issues, Other Concerns, with associated veterinary costs for monitoring and treatment. Ask the veterinarian about Mitral Valve Disease and Patent Ductus Arteriosus for the first animal and about Ear Infections and Bloat for the second before you decide, and keep both lifespan figures in the health section above beside that list.

Best for Low

Neither breed is truly "low maintenance" health-wise, but Norwegian Elkhound's longer lifespan and different condition profile may mean fewer intensive interventions in middle age compared to Norfolk Terrier.

Exercise and Activity

Norfolk Terrier requires moderate (30-45 minutes daily) levels of exercise and engagement, while Norwegian Elkhound needs high activity.

Grooming and Maintenance

Norfolk Terrier has low (wire coat) grooming needs, while Norwegian Elkhound requires high maintenance.

Cost of Ownership

Key cost differentials include: food costs scale with size (Small (11-12 lbs) vs Medium (48-55 lbs)), grooming costs reflect maintenance requirements (low (wire coat) vs high), and veterinary costs correlate with breed-specific health risks. Over a complete lifespan, Norfolk Terrier's 12-16 years expected life and Norwegian Elkhound's 12-15 years expected life mean different total cost horizons—the longer-lived dog accumulates more total costs but potentially offers more years of companionship.

Consider your daily schedule (Norfolk Terrier: moderate (30-45 minutes daily) engagement vs Norwegian Elkhound: high), grooming tolerance (low (wire coat) vs high), and personality preference (fearless vs bold).

Feeding and Nutrition

Norfolk Terrier (Small (11-12 lbs), moderate (30-45 minutes daily) activity) has different caloric and macronutrient needs than Norwegian Elkhound (Medium (48-55 lbs), high activity). Health-condition-specific dietary needs also differ—Norfolk Terrier's associations with Heart Conditions may warrant targeted nutrition, while Norwegian Elkhound's tendency toward hip dysplasia and other orthopedic problems calls for different dietary strategies.

Living Space and

Norfolk Terrier requires crate space suited to a Small (11-12 lbs) dog with moderate (30-45 minutes daily) exercise demands and a fearless, lovable, alert disposition. Norwegian Elkhound needs space accommodating their Medium (48-55 lbs) build, high activity needs, and bold, loyal, playful behavioral style.

Insurance and Health

Norfolk Terrier is predisposed to Heart Conditions and Orthopedic Issues, making coverage for hereditary conditions essential. Norwegian Elkhound's risk factors (joint and skeletal conditions and dental disease, skin conditions, and breed-related eye problems) require different policy features. Wellness coverage value also differs: Norfolk Terrier's moderate (30-45 minutes daily) activity level versus Norwegian Elkhound's high demands mean different injury risk profiles.

Long-Term Commitment Assessment

Norfolk Terrier (Small (11-12 lbs), good (eager but independent) care demands) and Norwegian Elkhound (Medium (48-55 lbs), moderate care demands) each require sustained dedication but in different ways. Norfolk Terrier's moderate (30-45 minutes daily) exercise requirements must be met consistently, just as Norwegian Elkhound's high activity needs cannot be neglected.
