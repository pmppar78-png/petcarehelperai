Detailed breed guide
Ownership and health

Temperament and Personality

Berger Picard is characterized by a loyal, observant, good-natured personality, while Bernedoodle tends toward loyal, goofy, affectionate traits. The household has to live with loyal, observant, good-natured on one side and loyal, goofy, affectionate on the other.

Best for Families

Berger Picard's loyal nature and Bernedoodle's loyal temperament each present different dynamics with younger family members.

Health and Lifespan

Berger Picard is predisposed to Eye Conditions, hip and joint concerns along with other health conditions common in this breed, with associated veterinary costs for monitoring and treatment. Ask the veterinarian about Progressive Retinal Atrophy (PRA) and Cataracts for the first animal and about Hip Dysplasia and Elbow Dysplasia for the second before you decide, and keep both lifespan figures in the health section above beside that list.

Best for Low

Neither breed is truly "low maintenance" health-wise, but Bernedoodle's longer lifespan and different condition profile may mean fewer intensive interventions in middle age compared to Berger Picard.

Grooming and Maintenance

Cost of Ownership

The size difference between Berger Picard (Medium to Large (50-70 lbs)) and Bernedoodle (Standard (70-90 lbs), Mini (25-49 lbs), Tiny (10-24 lbs)) significantly impacts costs across food, supplies, and veterinary care. Key cost differentials include: food costs scale with size (Medium to Large (50-70 lbs) vs Standard (70-90 lbs), Mini (25-49 lbs), Tiny (10-24 lbs)), grooming costs reflect maintenance requirements (low to moderate vs low to moderate), and veterinary costs correlate with breed-specific health risks. Over a complete lifespan, Berger Picard's 12-13 years expected life and Bernedoodle's 12-18 years expected life mean different total cost horizons—the longer-lived dog accumulates more total costs but potentially offers more years of companionship.

Consider your daily schedule (Berger Picard: high (1-2 hours daily) engagement vs Bernedoodle: moderate (45-60 min daily)), grooming tolerance (low to moderate vs low to moderate), and personality preference (loyal vs loyal).

Best for First

Feeding and Nutrition

Berger Picard at Medium to Large (50-70 lbs) needs caloric intake calibrated to their high (1-2 hours daily) activity level, while Bernedoodle at Standard (70-90 lbs), Mini (25-49 lbs), Tiny (10-24 lbs) requires nutrition matched to their moderate (45-60 min daily) energy output. Berger Picard's predisposition to Eye Conditions may require specialized dietary formulations, while Bernedoodle may benefit from diets supporting joint health and mobility.

Living Space and

Berger Picard (Medium to Large (50-70 lbs), loyal, observant, good-natured) occupies space differently than Bernedoodle (Standard (70-90 lbs), Mini (25-49 lbs), Tiny (10-24 lbs), loyal, goofy, affectionate). Daily activity patterns influence space usage—Berger Picard's high (1-2 hours daily) energy creates one footprint, while Bernedoodle's moderate (45-60 min daily) activity level creates another.

Long-Term Commitment Assessment

Berger Picard's loyal, observant, good-natured temperament and high (1-2 hours daily) activity needs must remain compatible with your lifestyle through potential moves, career changes, and family growth. Bernedoodle's loyal, goofy, affectionate character and moderate (45-60 min daily) demands create a different long-term compatibility profile. Care complexity evolves with age: Berger Picard's health predispositions (Eye Conditions) and Bernedoodle's risks (joint and skeletal conditions) may require increasing management in later years.
