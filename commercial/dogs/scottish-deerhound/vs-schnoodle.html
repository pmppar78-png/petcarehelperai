Best for Families

Scottish Deerhound's gentle nature and Schnoodle's alert temperament each present different dynamics with younger family members.

Health and Lifespan

Scottish Deerhound is predisposed to Major Health Concerns, thyroid conditions, allergies, and other hereditary predispositions, with associated veterinary costs for monitoring and treatment. Ask the veterinarian about Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM) and Osteosarcoma for the first animal and about Progressive Retinal Atrophy and Cataracts for the second before you decide, and keep both lifespan figures in the health section above beside that list.

Best for Low

Scottish Deerhound's predispositions typically require specific screening tests, while Schnoodle has its own set of conditions to monitor.

Exercise and Activity

Scottish Deerhound requires moderate to high (1-2 hours daily) levels of exercise and engagement, while Schnoodle needs moderate (30-60 min daily) activity.

Grooming and Maintenance

Scottish Deerhound has moderate grooming needs, while Schnoodle requires very low maintenance.

Cost of Ownership

The size difference between Scottish Deerhound (Giant (75-110 lbs)) and Schnoodle (Giant (60-80 lbs), Standard (30-50 lbs), Mini (13-20 lbs), Toy (6-12 lbs)) significantly impacts costs across food, supplies, and veterinary care. Key cost differentials include: food costs scale with size (Giant (75-110 lbs) vs Giant (60-80 lbs), Standard (30-50 lbs), Mini (13-20 lbs), Toy (6-12 lbs)), grooming costs reflect maintenance requirements (moderate vs very low), and veterinary costs correlate with breed-specific health risks. Over a complete lifespan, Scottish Deerhound's 8-11 years expected life and Schnoodle's 12-16 years expected life mean different total cost horizons—the longer-lived dog accumulates more total costs but potentially offers more years of companionship.

Consider your daily schedule (Scottish Deerhound: moderate to high (1-2 hours daily) engagement vs Schnoodle: moderate (30-60 min daily)), grooming tolerance (moderate vs very low), and personality preference (gentle vs alert).

Feeding and Nutrition

Scottish Deerhound's Giant (75-110 lbs) frame and moderate to high (1-2 hours daily) energy demands require specific caloric targeting, while Schnoodle's Giant (60-80 lbs), Standard (30-50 lbs), Mini (13-20 lbs), Toy (6-12 lbs) build and moderate (30-60 min daily) activity level call for different nutritional proportions. Scottish Deerhound's health profile (Major Health Concerns, breed-related eye, dental, and skin conditions that benefit from early detection) may necessitate prescription or limited-ingredient diets, while Schnoodle's predispositions (Eye Conditions, Other Concerns) have their own dietary implications.

Living Space and

Scottish Deerhound at Giant (75-110 lbs) needs a crate appropriately scaled to their dimensions and moderate to high (1-2 hours daily) activity pattern, while Schnoodle at Giant (60-80 lbs), Standard (30-50 lbs), Mini (13-20 lbs), Toy (6-12 lbs) requires crate sizing matched to their own build and moderate (30-60 min daily) energy level. Scottish Deerhound's gentle, dignified, polite temperament influences how they interact with their living space, while Schnoodle's alert, intelligent, loyal nature creates different environmental needs.

Insurance and Health

Scottish Deerhound is predisposed to Major Health Concerns and genetic predispositions to conditions like allergies, autoimmune disorders, and organ-specific diseases, making coverage for hereditary conditions essential. Schnoodle's risk factors (Eye Conditions and Other Concerns) require different policy features.

Long-Term Commitment Assessment

Scottish Deerhound's 8-11 years expected life will include a vibrant youth, stable adulthood, and eventual senior phase with increasing health needs related to Major Health Concerns. Schnoodle's 12-16 years trajectory follows a similar arc but with different condition profiles (Eye Conditions) and different care demands (very good versus moderate (willing but independent)).
