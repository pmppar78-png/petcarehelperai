Maltese › vs Lhasa Apso

Comparison

Best for Families

Maltese's gentle nature and Lhasa Apso's confident temperament each present different dynamics with younger family members.

Health and Lifespan

Maltese is predisposed to Dental Issues, Joint Issues, Other Conditions, with associated veterinary costs for monitoring and treatment. Ask the veterinarian about Portosystemic shunts, patellar luxation, and tracheal collapse are the three to know by name for the first animal and about Hip Dysplasia and Eye Conditions for the second before you decide, and keep both lifespan figures in the health section above beside that list.

Best for Low

Neither breed is truly "low maintenance" health-wise, but Lhasa Apso's longer lifespan and different condition profile may mean fewer intensive interventions in middle age compared to Maltese.

Exercise and Activity

Maltese requires low-moderate (30 minutes daily) levels of exercise and engagement, while Lhasa Apso needs moderate activity.

Grooming and Maintenance

Maltese has very low (hypoallergenic) grooming needs, while Lhasa Apso requires high maintenance.

Cost of Ownership

Key cost differentials include: food costs scale with size (Toy (4-7 lbs) vs Small (12-18 lbs)), grooming costs reflect maintenance requirements (very low (hypoallergenic) vs high), and veterinary costs correlate with breed-specific health risks. Over a complete lifespan, Maltese's 12-15 years expected life and Lhasa Apso's 12-15 years expected life mean different total cost horizons—the longer-lived dog accumulates more total costs but potentially offers more years of companionship.

The exercise gap is significant: Maltese demands low-moderate (30 minutes daily) activity versus Lhasa Apso's moderate needs—this alone dictates different daily routines. Maltese's gentle personality will define your household's dynamic differently than Lhasa Apso's confident character.

Feeding and Nutrition

Maltese's Toy (4-7 lbs) frame and low-moderate (30 minutes daily) energy demands require specific caloric targeting, while Lhasa Apso's Small (12-18 lbs) build and moderate activity level call for different nutritional proportions. Maltese's health profile (Dental Issues, Joint Issues) may necessitate prescription or limited-ingredient diets, while Lhasa Apso's predispositions (orthopedic problems such as ligament injuries and other genetic predispositions) have their own dietary implications.

Living Space and

Maltese at Toy (4-7 lbs) needs a crate appropriately scaled to their dimensions and low-moderate (30 minutes daily) activity pattern, while Lhasa Apso at Small (12-18 lbs) requires crate sizing matched to their own build and moderate energy level. Maltese's gentle, playful, charming temperament influences how they interact with their living space, while Lhasa Apso's confident, smart, comical nature creates different environmental needs.

Insurance and Health

Maltese is predisposed to Dental Issues and Joint Issues, making coverage for hereditary conditions essential. Lhasa Apso's risk factors (joint and skeletal conditions and hereditary conditions including potential eye, dental, and metabolic issues) require different policy features. Wellness coverage value also differs: Maltese's low-moderate (30 minutes daily) activity level versus Lhasa Apso's moderate demands mean different injury risk profiles.

Long-Term Commitment Assessment

Maltese (Toy (4-7 lbs), good (can be stubborn) care demands) and Lhasa Apso (Small (12-18 lbs), moderate care demands) each require sustained dedication but in different ways. Maltese's low-moderate (30 minutes daily) exercise requirements must be met consistently, just as Lhasa Apso's moderate activity needs cannot be neglected.
