Feeding Guidelines for Borzoi

Your veterinarian knows your Borzoi best — always verify dietary choices with them, especially if your dog has existing health conditions.

What to Look For

  • Whole protein source: The first listed ingredient should be an identifiable animal protein — real chicken, salmon, or lamb, not a vague by-product.
  • Clean ingredient list: Fewer ingredients often means fewer potential allergens. Avoid unnecessary fillers like corn syrup and artificial coloring.
  • AAFCO compliance: Make sure the label states the food meets AAFCO standards for your Borzoi's life stage.
  • Appropriate fat content: Fat fuels energy but excess leads to weight gain. Match the fat percentage to how active your Borzoi actually is.
  • Your Borzoi's response: Ultimately, the best food is one your dog eats willingly, digests well, and thrives on — not the one with the fanciest packaging.

Borzoi Nutritional Profile

Start any diet conversation about a Borzoi from the physical baseline (Large (60-105 lbs)) and behavioral baseline (gentle); nutrition choices flow from there. Over a 9-14 years lifespan, the right nutrition foundation prevents many common health issues. Larger dogs like Borzoi need controlled calorie intake to support their frame without excess weight that stresses joints. Slow-growth formulas help prevent developmental skeletal issues. A diet rich in animal-based proteins at 28-35% of total calories fuels Borzoi's active lifestyle, with fat content elevated slightly to sustain energy through longer activity sessions. Omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids are particularly beneficial for Borzoi to maintain coat health and joint function.

Growth-Phase Diet

Borzoi puppies typically double their birth weight within the first few weeks. Support this intense growth period with a puppy-specific formula that provides 25-30% protein from quality animal sources. Transition to three meals per day around four months, then to two meals as they approach maturity. Watch body condition closely — a slightly lean puppy grows into a healthier adult than an overfed one.

Prime-of-Life Nutrition

Maintenance formulas for Borzoi should reflect their moderate to high (1-2 hours daily) activity level with complete and balanced nutrition meeting AAFCO standards for adult dogs.

Adjusting Diet With Age

As your Borzoi enters their senior years, metabolism slows and nutritional needs shift. Reduce calorie density by 15-20% while maintaining protein levels to preserve muscle mass. Consider adding glucosamine and chondroitin for joint support, and look for formulas with easily digestible proteins. Senior dogs also benefit from increased fiber to support digestive regularity and antioxidant-rich ingredients for immune health.

Common Dietary Sensitivities in Borzoi

Watch for signs that your Borzoi's food is not agreeing with them: frequent scratching, red or waxy ears, inconsistent stool quality, or a dull coat. These can all point to dietary sensitivities. Rather than guessing by switching brands randomly, work with your vet on a structured elimination diet. It takes patience — typically two to three months — but it gives you a definitive answer about what your Borzoi can and cannot tolerate.

Ideal Portion Control for Borzoi

Portion control is straightforward but requires consistency. Start with the recommended serving size for your Borzoi's weight and age, then fine-tune. A Borzoi at a healthy weight has a discernible waist and ribs you can feel under a thin layer of padding. If your Borzoi is gaining, reduce portions by about 10%. If they seem thin or low-energy, increase slightly. Two meals a day works for most adult Borzois.

Best for Weight Management

If your Borzoi needs to lose or maintain weight, choose a formula with L-carnitine and higher fiber content. These help Borzoi feel satisfied while consuming fewer calories, which is especially important given their moderate to high (1-2 hours daily) activity level.

Expert Feeding Tips for Borzoi Owners

A few practical feeding tips from longtime Borzoi owners: establish a mealtime routine and stick to it. Avoid exercising your Borzoi immediately after eating. Rotate protein sources periodically (chicken, beef, fish) to reduce the risk of developing sensitivities to any single protein. Store food properly — an airtight container keeps kibble fresh and prevents fat from going rancid. If your Borzoi suddenly loses interest in a food they have been eating happily, check the batch number — formula changes happen without notice.

Understanding the heritage of Borzoi provides valuable context for dietary planning. This breed's Large (60-105 lbs) build reflects generations of development that created specific metabolic demands. With a natural gentle disposition and moderate to high (1-2 hours daily) activity pattern, Borzoi converts calories to energy in characteristic ways that differ from other dogs. Their 9-14 years lifespan means nutritional planning should account for extended periods in each life stage and the gradual metabolic shifts that occur with aging. Owners who research Borzoi's background gain insights that translate directly into better feeding decisions throughout every stage of their dog's life.
