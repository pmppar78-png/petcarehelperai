Havanese vs Harrier: Complete Comparison (2026)

Choosing between Havanese and Harrier works best when the decision starts with daily constraints: time, space, handling comfort, likely vet costs, and how much structure the household can maintain.

The goal is not to crown one choice. It is to identify which set of needs fits the life the owner can actually provide.

Temperament and Personality Differences

The temperament contrast between Havanese and Harrier is one of the most significant factors in choosing between these dogs. Havanese is characterized by an intelligent, outgoing, funny personality, while Harrier tends toward friendly, outgoing, active traits. In daily life, this means Havanese owners typically experience a dog that leans toward intelligent behavior, while Harrier owners find their dog more inclined toward friendly tendencies. Neither personality is objectively better — the right fit depends on your own temperament and how you live.

Best for Families with Children

Evaluate each breed's interaction style with children. Havanese's intelligent nature and Harrier's friendly temperament each present different dynamics with younger family members.

Health and Lifespan Comparison

Havanese has a typical lifespan of 14-16 years, while Harrier lives approximately 12-15 years. Health profiles differ significantly between these dogs. Havanese is predisposed to orthopedic problems, Eye Conditions, Other Concerns, with associated veterinary costs for monitoring and treatment. Harrier faces its own health challenges including Potential Health Concerns, Less Common Issues. Havanese has 3 documented predispositions compared to 2 for Harrier, though condition count alone doesn't determine overall health burden—severity and treatability matter more. Insurance considerations differ between the two dogs based on these risk profiles. Prospective owners should discuss breed-specific health screening with a veterinarian before making their decision.

Best for Low-Maintenance Health

Neither breed is truly "low maintenance" health-wise, but Harrier's longer lifespan and different condition profile may mean fewer intensive interventions in middle age compared to Havanese. That said, consistent preventive care is non-negotiable for both — the real question is which breed's health demands better fit your schedule and budget.

Exercise and Activity Level Differences

Activity requirements differ minimally between Havanese and Harrier. Havanese requires moderate (30-60 min daily) levels of exercise and engagement, while Harrier needs high (1-2 hours daily) activity. With activity levels comparable, the time burden is similar, so the decision comes down to other factors. Havanese owners should plan for 60-90 minutes of daily activity, compared to 60-90 minutes for Harrier. Under-exercised dogs of either breed develop behavioral issues, but the consequences and management strategies differ.

Grooming and Maintenance Comparison

Daily and periodic maintenance requirements differ between Havanese and Harrier. Havanese has low (hypoallergenic) grooming needs, while Harrier requires moderate maintenance. Professional grooming costs reflect these differences: Havanese owners typically spend $0-$200 annually on grooming, compared to $200-$400 for Harrier. Home maintenance — brushing, bathing, nails, dental care — matters as much as any professional grooming appointment. The time commitment for daily grooming and general home environment management is an important lifestyle consideration. Factor grooming costs and time into your total ownership commitment when deciding between these dogs.

Best for Low-Maintenance Owners

Between the two, the gentler-grooming, moderate-exercise choice fits constrained schedules; the more demanding option suits households with real daily bandwidth. Compare their grooming frequency, exercise minimums, and training requirements side by side — the breed that fits more easily into your existing routine is the practical choice.

Cost of Ownership Comparison

Total ownership costs for Havanese versus Harrier differ across several categories. The size difference between Havanese (Small (7-13 lbs)) and Harrier (Medium (45-60 lbs)) significantly impacts costs across food, supplies, and veterinary care. Larger dogs generally cost 30-60% more in recurring expenses due to higher food consumption, larger equipment needs, and higher medication dosages. Key cost differentials include: food costs scale with size (Small (7-13 lbs) vs Medium (45-60 lbs)), grooming costs reflect maintenance requirements (low (hypoallergenic) vs moderate), and veterinary costs correlate with breed-specific health risks. Insurance premiums also differ based on each breed's risk profile. Over a complete lifespan, Havanese's 14-16 years expected life and Harrier's 12-15 years expected life mean different total cost horizons—the longer-lived dog accumulates more total costs but potentially offers more years of companionship.

Which Is Right for Your Family?

The decision between Havanese and Harrier ultimately depends on matching dog characteristics with your family's specific situation. Choose Havanese if your lifestyle accommodates their moderate (30-60 min daily) activity needs, low (hypoallergenic) grooming requirements, and you're prepared for their intelligent temperament. Choose Harrier if you prefer their high (1-2 hours daily) energy level, can manage moderate maintenance, and appreciate their friendly personality. Consult with a veterinarian about any family-specific concerns such as allergies, living arrangements, or compatibility with existing dogs. Both Havanese and Harrier make wonderful companions for the right owner; the key is honest self-assessment about which breed's needs you can best fulfill throughout their entire lifespan.

Best for First-Time Owners

If this is a first dog, favour the less demanding breed — the learning curve is real, and margin for error matters. Havanese and Harrier each have their challenges, but the one with a calmer baseline temperament and more predictable behavior patterns will be easier to learn with. Consider enrolling in a training class regardless of which you choose — professional guidance during the first year prevents most common ownership mistakes.

Feeding and Nutrition Comparison

Nutrition planning for Havanese versus Harrier involves different considerations. Havanese (Small (7-13 lbs), moderate (30-60 min daily) activity) has different caloric and macronutrient needs than Harrier (Medium (45-60 lbs), high (1-2 hours daily) activity). Monthly food budgets reflect these differences: expect to spend more on the larger dog due to volume requirements. Health-condition-specific dietary needs also differ—Havanese's associations with orthopedic problems may warrant targeted nutrition, while Harrier's predisposition to Potential Health Concerns calls for different dietary strategies. Prospective owners should factor these recurring nutritional costs and complexity into their comparison of the two dogs.

Living Space and Habitat Requirements

Habitat compatibility is a practical differentiator between Havanese and Harrier. Havanese requires crate space suited to a Small (7-13 lbs) dog with moderate (30-60 min daily) exercise demands and an intelligent, outgoing, funny disposition. Harrier needs space accommodating their Medium (45-60 lbs) build, high (1-2 hours daily) activity needs, and friendly, outgoing, active behavioral style. Beyond the primary crate, consider exercise space: Havanese needs substantial active space, while Harrier demands significant room for exercise. Noise levels, destructive potential, and territorial behavior patterns also differ between these two breeds and should factor into your housing assessment.

Insurance and Health Coverage Comparison

Comparing insurance value between Havanese and Harrier requires analyzing each breed's lifetime health cost trajectory. Havanese faces health risks from orthopedic problems and Eye Conditions that generate specific claim patterns, while Harrier's Potential Health Concerns and Less Common Issues drives different insurance utilization. Over Havanese's 14-16 years lifespan, expected veterinary costs may differ significantly from Harrier's 12-15 years cost horizon. Size-driven cost differences (Small (7-13 lbs) versus Medium (45-60 lbs)) affect medication dosing, surgical complexity, and equipment costs—all factors that influence insurance claim amounts. The insurance decision should factor into your overall dog choice: a breed with higher insurance costs may still be the better financial choice if other ownership costs are lower.

Long-Term Commitment Assessment

Choosing between Havanese and Harrier is a commitment spanning 14-16 years or 12-15 years respectively. Beyond the daily care differences already outlined, consider how each dog fits your life trajectory. Havanese's intelligent, outgoing, funny temperament and moderate (30-60 min daily) activity needs must remain compatible with your lifestyle through potential moves, career changes, and family growth. Harrier's friendly, outgoing, active character and high (1-2 hours daily) demands create a different long-term compatibility profile. Care complexity evolves with age: Havanese's health predispositions (orthopedic problems) and Harrier's risks (Potential Health Concerns) may require increasing management in later years. The dog whose senior-care requirements you can most realistically commit to should weigh heavily in your decision. Both Havanese and Harrier deserve owners who can provide consistent care from adoption through their final days.

Best for Making the Final Decision

Name your non-negotiables honestly up front — exercise time, grooming willingness, budget tolerance — and the shortlist shrinks quickly. The right dog is the one whose worst-case demands you can still handle comfortably, not just whose best traits appeal to you most.

  • That internet-sample stories generalise — breed averages and individual ranges are different things
