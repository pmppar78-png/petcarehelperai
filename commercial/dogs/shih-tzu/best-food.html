Feeding Guidelines for

Use a small kibble or a wet food a Shih Tzu can pick up with a flat face, in two measured meals. Sticky sugary treats cling to crowded teeth, and dental disease is the breed's repeating bill. Feel for ribs under the coat once a week. Grapes, xylitol, and cooked bones stay off the floor. A wide, shallow bowl helps the nose clear the rim.

What to Look

  • Right formula for the life stage: Growing, adult, and senior Shih Tzus have different caloric and nutrient requirements.

Best Food by

Shih Tzu Nutritional Profile

Feeding a Shih Tzu well means accounting for their Small (9-16 lbs) frame and energy requirements. Smaller breeds need calorie-dense foods in manageable portions to fuel their faster metabolism.

Life Stage Feeding

Young Shih Tzus need nutrient-dense food with higher protein and fat to support growth — typically 20-40% more calories per pound than adults.

Growth-Phase Diet

Prime-of-Life Nutrition

Maintenance formulas for Shih Tzu should reflect their low-moderate (30-60 min daily) activity level with complete and balanced nutrition meeting AAFCO standards for adult dogs.

Adjusting Diet With

Common Dietary Sensitivities

Watch for signs that your Shih Tzu's food is not agreeing with them: frequent scratching, red or waxy ears, inconsistent stool quality, or a dull coat.

Ideal Portion Control

Use the manufacturer's guidelines as a starting point, then adjust based on your Shih Tzu's body condition — you should be able to feel the ribs without seeing them, and there should be a visible waist from above. Split daily food into two meals for adults, three to four for growing Shih Tzus, and keep treats under 10% of total daily calories.
