Temperament and Personality Differences

Shih Tzu brings an affectionate, playful, outgoing energy to the household, compared to Shikoku's brave, enthusiastic, alert disposition. In daily life, this means Shih Tzu owners typically experience a dog that leans toward affectionate behavior, while Shikoku owners find their dog more inclined toward brave tendencies.

Best for Families with Children

Shih Tzu's affectionate nature and Shikoku's brave temperament each present different dynamics with younger family members.

Health and Lifespan Comparison

Ask the veterinarian about Brachycephalic airway trouble, dry eye, corneal ulcers, and ear infections under the hair are the weekly problems for the first animal and about Hip Dysplasia and Elbow Dysplasia for the second before you decide, and keep both lifespan figures in the health section above beside that list.

Best for Low-Maintenance Health

Neither breed is truly "low maintenance" health-wise, but Shikoku's longer lifespan and different condition profile may mean fewer intensive interventions in middle age compared to Shih Tzu.

Exercise and Activity Level Differences

Shih Tzu requires low-moderate (30-60 min daily) levels of exercise and engagement, while Shikoku needs high activity.

Grooming and Maintenance Comparison

Shih Tzu has low (hair, not fur) grooming needs, while Shikoku requires moderate maintenance.

Best for Low-Maintenance Owners

Cost of Ownership Comparison

Key cost differentials include: food costs scale with size (Small (9-16 lbs) vs Medium (35-55 lbs)), grooming costs reflect maintenance requirements (low (hair, not fur) vs moderate), and veterinary costs correlate with breed-specific health risks. Over a complete lifespan, Shih Tzu's 10-18 years expected life and Shikoku's 10-12 years expected life mean different total cost horizons—the longer-lived dog accumulates more total costs but potentially offers more years of companionship.

Which Is Right for Your Family?

Choose Shih Tzu if your lifestyle accommodates their low-moderate (30-60 min daily) activity needs, low (hair, not fur) grooming requirements, and you're prepared for their affectionate temperament. Choose Shikoku if you prefer their high energy level, can manage moderate maintenance, and appreciate their brave personality.

Best for First-Time Owners

Feeding and Nutrition Comparison

Shih Tzu at Small (9-16 lbs) needs caloric intake calibrated to their low-moderate (30-60 min daily) activity level, while Shikoku at Medium (35-55 lbs) requires nutrition matched to their high energy output. Shih Tzu's predisposition to Brachycephalic Concerns may require specialized dietary formulations, while Shikoku may benefit from diets supporting joint health and mobility.

Living Space and Habitat Requirements

Shih Tzu requires crate space suited to a Small (9-16 lbs) dog with low-moderate (30-60 min daily) exercise demands and an affectionate, playful, outgoing disposition. Shikoku needs space accommodating their Medium (35-55 lbs) build, high activity needs, and brave, enthusiastic, alert behavioral style.

Insurance and Health Coverage Comparison

Shih Tzu is predisposed to Brachycephalic Concerns and Eye Conditions, making coverage for hereditary conditions essential. Shikoku's risk factors (orthopedic problems and additional hereditary conditions including allergies and age-related changes) require different policy features.

Long-Term Commitment Assessment

Shih Tzu's affectionate, playful, outgoing temperament and low-moderate (30-60 min daily) activity needs must remain compatible with your lifestyle through potential moves, career changes, and family growth. Shikoku's brave, enthusiastic, alert character and high demands create a different long-term compatibility profile. Care complexity evolves with age: Shih Tzu's health predispositions (Brachycephalic Concerns) and Shikoku's risks (orthopedic problems) may require increasing management in later years.

Best for Making the Final Decision
