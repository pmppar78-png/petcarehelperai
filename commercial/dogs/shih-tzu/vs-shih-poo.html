Choose Shih Tzu If...

  • Your weekly schedule reliably absorbs the Shih Tzu's exercise, training, and enrichment minimums — not just on good weeks.
  • The Shih Tzu's social and behavioural baseline lines up with the people, kids, or other pets already in the home.
  • You can plan around the Shih Tzu's known health predispositions without that planning crowding out other priorities.

Choose Shih-Poo If...

  • You already enjoy the kind of human-dog interaction style the Shih-Poo is known for — the Shih Tzu's style would feel like a stretch.
  • The Shih-Poo's long-term health outlook is one you can support with consistent preventive care and appropriate insurance.

Temperament and Personality

Shih Tzu's affectionate, playful, outgoing character creates a fundamentally different ownership experience than Shih-Poo's affectionate, playful, friendly nature.

Best for Families

Shih Tzu's affectionate nature and Shih-Poo's affectionate temperament each present different dynamics with younger family members.

Health and Lifespan

Ask the veterinarian about Brachycephalic airway trouble, dry eye, corneal ulcers, and ear infections under the hair are the weekly problems for the first animal and about Patellar Luxation and Hip Dysplasia for the second before you decide, and keep both lifespan figures in the health section above beside that list.

Best for Low

Exercise and Activity

Shih Tzu requires low-moderate (30-60 min daily) levels of exercise and engagement, while Shih-Poo needs low to moderate (20-30 minutes daily) activity.

Grooming and Maintenance

Shih Tzu has low (hair, not fur) grooming needs, while Shih-Poo requires low (often hypoallergenic) maintenance.

Cost of Ownership

Key cost differentials include: food costs scale with size (Small (9-16 lbs) vs Small (8-18 lbs)), grooming costs reflect maintenance requirements (low (hair, not fur) vs low (often hypoallergenic)), and veterinary costs correlate with breed-specific health risks. Over a complete lifespan, Shih Tzu's 10-18 years expected life and Shih-Poo's 10-18 years expected life mean different total cost horizons—the longer-lived dog accumulates more total costs but potentially offers more years of companionship.

Consider your daily schedule (Shih Tzu: low-moderate (30-60 min daily) engagement vs Shih-Poo: low to moderate (20-30 minutes daily)), grooming tolerance (low (hair, not fur) vs low (often hypoallergenic)), and personality preference (affectionate vs affectionate).

Best for First

Feeding and Nutrition

Shih Tzu at Small (9-16 lbs) needs caloric intake calibrated to their low-moderate (30-60 min daily) activity level, while Shih-Poo at Small (8-18 lbs) requires nutrition matched to their low to moderate (20-30 minutes daily) energy output. Shih Tzu's predisposition to Brachycephalic Concerns may require specialized dietary formulations, while Shih-Poo may benefit from diets supporting joint health and mobility.

Living Space and

Shih Tzu (Small (9-16 lbs), affectionate, playful, outgoing) occupies space differently than Shih-Poo (Small (8-18 lbs), affectionate, playful, friendly). Daily activity patterns influence space usage—Shih Tzu's low-moderate (30-60 min daily) energy creates one footprint, while Shih-Poo's low to moderate (20-30 minutes daily) activity level creates another.

Long-Term Commitment Assessment

Shih Tzu (Small (9-16 lbs), moderate (can be stubborn) care demands) and Shih-Poo (Small (8-18 lbs), moderate (can be stubborn) care demands) each require sustained dedication but in different ways. Shih Tzu's low-moderate (30-60 min daily) exercise requirements must be met consistently, just as Shih-Poo's low to moderate (20-30 minutes daily) activity needs cannot be neglected.
