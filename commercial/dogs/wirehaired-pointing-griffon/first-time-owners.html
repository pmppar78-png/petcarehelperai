The first-supply total on this checklist is $290 to $980, and most of it is the crate and the food. The collar is the cheap line.

Schedule the veterinarian before the hunting season starts. Hip and elbow films, plus a progressive retinal atrophy test, are the three results a griffon puppy's file should already hold. A wire coat that is clipped flat changes texture, so ask the clinic who in town still hand-strips this breed.

A griffon's first hunting season at home

A wirehaired pointing griffon is a hunting dog built to point and retrieve in rough country. Adults on this page run about 50 to 70 pounds. The coat should be harsh and stripped on a schedule. Clipping as a habit changes the texture and the weather protection the wire is for. Ears need drying after water. Hips, elbows, and a progressive retinal atrophy test are the three results a puppy's file should already hold, or should be scheduled before the season starts.

The startup kit is a sporting-dog kit. A crate that fits 50 to 70 pounds is the $50 to $300 line. A month of food is $60 to $120. A bed is $30 to $150. A stripping comb is $20 to $80. Counted together, those four lines still come to $290 to $980. A collar is the cheap item. The expensive item you cannot see yet is an undiagnosed hip and a dog that is asked to hunt on it.

Ask the breeder for OFA hip and elbow numbers and for an eye certification or a PRA DNA result. Embark and similar panels are a tool. They do not replace the orthopedic films. Take whatever papers you have to the first veterinary visit and ask which tests are still missing. A relatively healthy reputation in the breed still leaves the films to be taken on this dog.

Exercise in month one is relationship and mileage the puppy's joints can stand. A full day in the field waits. A weekend walk does not spend a griffon. A griffon with no job invents one, usually involving the trash, the bird feeder, or the neighbor's cat. Short retrieves, a place command, and a recall that pays are the work while the growth plates are still open. Save the all-day hunt for a body that has been cleared.

The coat and the ears are weekly work. A once-a-season bath leaves both of them wrong. Strip a little, often, or book a groomer who strips wire coats. Dry the ears after every swim and every bath. A wet canal under a furnishings coat is an infection you will pay for in the same year you paid for the crate. Tell the clinic if the dog shakes its head. Bring a calendar of swims if you have one.

Food is measured for a 50 to 70 pound athlete or for a 50 to 70 pound house dog, and those are different cups. A griffon in hard condition can use the top of the $60 to $120 month. A griffon that only works on Saturdays gets fat on that cup, and fat is a hip problem you added. Weigh monthly. Two meals beat one kettle of food before a ride in the truck.

Before opening day, the folder should show hip films or a plan to take them, elbow films, a PRA result, a current vaccine record, and a veterinarian who has seen the dog move. The $290 to $980 startup buys the crate, the food, the bed, and the comb. The season goes well when the dog is empty of undiagnosed joint pain and the coat is still wire. A clipped pelt is a different dog.

After a water retrieve, dry the ears before the dog goes back in the crate for the ride home. A wet canal in a closed car is the ear infection you will treat next week. Pack a towel in the same bag as the stripping comb.
