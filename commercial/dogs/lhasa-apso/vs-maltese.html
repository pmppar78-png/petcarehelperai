Lhasa Apso vs Maltese: Complete Comparison (2026)

Choosing between Lhasa Apso and Maltese works best when the decision starts with daily constraints: time, space, handling comfort, likely vet costs, and how much structure the household can maintain.

The goal is not to crown one choice. It is to identify which set of needs fits the life the owner can actually provide.

Temperament and Personality Differences

Understanding how Lhasa Apso and Maltese differ in temperament is essential for making the right choice. Lhasa Apso's confident, smart, comical character creates a fundamentally different ownership experience than Maltese's gentle, playful, charming nature. In daily life, this means Lhasa Apso owners typically experience a dog that leans toward confident behavior, while Maltese owners find their dog more inclined toward gentle tendencies. Both are viable — choose the one that maps onto your actual home and routine.

Best for Families with Children

Evaluate each breed's interaction style with children. Lhasa Apso's confident nature and Maltese's gentle temperament each present different dynamics with younger family members.

Health and Lifespan Comparison

Lhasa Apso has a typical lifespan of 12-15 years, while Maltese lives approximately 12-15 years. Health profiles differ significantly between these dogs. Lhasa Apso is predisposed to joint-related conditions and other breed-specific health issues, with associated veterinary costs for monitoring and treatment. Maltese faces its own health challenges including Dental Issues, Joint Issues, Other Conditions. Lhasa Apso has 2 documented predispositions compared to 3 for Maltese, though condition count alone doesn't determine overall health burden—severity and treatability matter more. Insurance considerations differ between the two dogs based on these risk profiles. Prospective owners should discuss breed-specific health screening with a veterinarian before making their decision.

Best for Low-Maintenance Health

Neither breed is truly "low maintenance" health-wise, but Maltese's longer lifespan and different condition profile may mean fewer intensive interventions in middle age compared to Lhasa Apso. That said, consistent preventive care is non-negotiable for both — the real question is which breed's health demands better fit your schedule and budget.

Exercise and Activity Level Differences

Activity requirements differ notably between Lhasa Apso and Maltese. Lhasa Apso requires moderate levels of exercise and engagement, while Maltese needs low-moderate (30 minutes daily) activity. This difference has major practical implications for daily routines. Lhasa Apso owners should plan for 30-60 minutes of daily activity, compared to 15-30 minutes for Maltese. Under-exercised dogs of either breed develop behavioral issues, but the consequences and management strategies differ.

Grooming and Maintenance Comparison

Daily and periodic maintenance requirements differ between Lhasa Apso and Maltese. Lhasa Apso has high grooming needs, while Maltese requires very low (hypoallergenic) maintenance. Professional grooming costs reflect these differences: Lhasa Apso owners typically spend $400-$800 annually on grooming, compared to $0-$200 for Maltese. At-home grooming covers brushing, bathing, nail trims, and dental hygiene between professional visits. The time commitment for daily grooming and general home environment management is an important lifestyle consideration. Factor grooming costs and time into your total ownership commitment when deciding between these dogs.

Best for Low-Maintenance Owners

Time-constrained households usually land on the lower-grooming, moderate-exercise option; households with more daily hours can carry the other. Compare their grooming frequency, exercise minimums, and training requirements side by side — the breed that fits more easily into your existing routine is the practical choice.

Cost of Ownership Comparison

Total ownership costs for Lhasa Apso versus Maltese differ across several categories. Both Lhasa Apso and Maltese are similarly sized at Small (12-18 lbs), so recurring costs for food and supplies are comparable between the two breeds. The primary cost differentials come from health profiles and grooming requirements. Key cost differentials include: food costs scale with size (Small (12-18 lbs) vs Toy (4-7 lbs)), grooming costs reflect maintenance requirements (high vs very low (hypoallergenic)), and veterinary costs correlate with breed-specific health risks. Insurance premiums also differ based on each breed's risk profile. Over a complete lifespan, Lhasa Apso's 12-15 years expected life and Maltese's 12-15 years expected life mean different total cost horizons—the longer-lived dog accumulates more total costs but potentially offers more years of companionship.

Which Is Right for Your Family?

Choosing between Lhasa Apso and Maltese requires weighing daily lifestyle impact over emotional preference. The exercise gap is significant: Lhasa Apso demands moderate activity versus Maltese's low-moderate (30 minutes daily) needs—this alone dictates different daily routines. Lhasa Apso's confident personality will define your household's dynamic differently than Maltese's gentle character. Neither is objectively superior—the better dog is the one whose needs you can consistently meet. Consult with a veterinarian about any family-specific concerns such as allergies, living arrangements, or compatibility with existing dogs. Both Lhasa Apso and Maltese make wonderful companions for the right owner; the key is honest self-assessment about which breed's needs you can best fulfill throughout their entire lifespan.

Best for First-Time Owners

A first dog is best with the less demanding of the two options; the first months are steep even on the easier breed. Lhasa Apso and Maltese each have their challenges, but the one with a calmer baseline temperament and more predictable behavior patterns will be easier to learn with. Consider enrolling in a training class regardless of which you choose — professional guidance during the first year prevents most common ownership mistakes.

Feeding and Nutrition Comparison

Dietary requirements differ between Lhasa Apso and Maltese based on their distinct physical builds and metabolic profiles. Lhasa Apso at Small (12-18 lbs) needs caloric intake calibrated to their moderate activity level, while Maltese at Toy (4-7 lbs) requires nutrition matched to their low-moderate (30 minutes daily) energy output. Similar sizing means food costs are comparable, but ingredient requirements may differ based on each breed's health predispositions. Lhasa Apso's genetic predisposition to joint conditions may require specialized dietary formulations, while Maltese may benefit from diets supporting Dental Issues. Both dogs benefit from high-quality, species-appropriate nutrition, but the specific formula, portion size, and feeding schedule will differ.

Lhasa Apso (Small (12-18 lbs), confident, smart, comical) occupies space differently than Maltese (Toy (4-7 lbs), gentle, playful, charming). Long-Term Commitment Assessment

The long-term view reveals important differences between Lhasa Apso and Maltese. A 12-15 years commitment to Lhasa Apso versus 12-15 years with Maltese means different duration but also different intensity curves. Lhasa Apso (Small (12-18 lbs), moderate care demands) and Maltese (Toy (4-7 lbs), good (can be stubborn) care demands) each require sustained dedication but in different ways. The most successful dog owners are those who honestly assess their capacity to meet these demands not just today, but five, ten, and fifteen years from now.

Best for Making the Final Decision
