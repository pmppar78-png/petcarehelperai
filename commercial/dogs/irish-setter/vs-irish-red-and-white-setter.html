Decision Guidance for

A solid red gun dog beside a lighter red-and-white hunter

The Irish Setter card on this site is a large sporting dog at 60 to 70 pounds and 25 to 27 inches, with a lifespan of 12 to 15 years. The Irish Red and White Setter card is also called large, and the numbers are lower: 35 to 60 pounds and 22 to 26 inches, living about 11 to 15 years. Anyone comparing them as "the same setter in two colors" is reading the names and skipping the tables. The solid red is the heavier, taller dog on these pages. The red-and-white dog's band starts twenty-five pounds lighter and an inch or two shorter. Food, crates, and fences follow that split.

The solid red was developed in Ireland as a bird dog, and the profile describes a mahogany or chestnut coat, a friendly rollicking household style, and a trainability note that says good and slow to mature. Excellent with children is the family line on that card. Grooming is listed at $200 to $500 a year, food at $600 to $1,000, routine veterinary care at $300 to $600, and insurance at $400 to $700. The coat is feathered and needs brushing, and it does not carry the higher grooming band printed for the red-and-white dog.

The red-and-white profile is explicit that this is a separate hunting dog, with spaniel ears that infect when they stay wet, and with a home that skips exercise ending up with a noisy underworked setter. Courageous, spirited, and friendly are the temperament words, and children are rated good when they are respectful. Exercise and grooming are both marked high. Food runs $600 to $1,200, grooming $300 to $600, insurance $400 to $800, and a first-year training line of $200 to $500. The ear canal is the weekly job the solid red's grooming paragraph does not emphasize the same way. Dry those ears after every wet field and every bath.

Health screening is where the two folders should look similar and then diverge. The Irish Setter page asks breeders for Orthopedic Foundation for Animals hip evaluations, PRA-rcd1 DNA, and CLAD DNA, and it says to discuss bloat prevention with a veterinarian. It lists hip dysplasia, osteochondrosis in growing puppies, gastric dilatation-volvulus, canine leukocyte adhesion deficiency, progressive retinal atrophy, epilepsy, and megaesophagus. The same page cites an OFA hip dysplasia figure of about 12.3 percent in evaluated Irish Setters at a typical 60 to 70 pounds. The red-and-white page tells buyers to ask for CLAD and progressive retinal atrophy results by name. Bring both lists to a litter visit. A breeder who only says "they are healthy" has not answered either page.

Bloat is the solid red's emergency that the deep chest makes plausible. Two meals, a rest after eating, and a clinic that can operate at night are the plan to build while the puppy is still small. Osteochondrosis is a growth-cartilage problem, so skip endless pounding on pavement during the 60 to 70 pound dog's first year. Megaesophagus, if it appears, is a swallowing problem for a veterinarian, and the page names it so owners do not file every gag as a hairball. Epilepsy is on the setter list as a seizure disorder some dogs develop. Write down what you saw, how long it lasted, and call. CLAD is an immune disorder with a DNA test. PRA-rcd1 is the retinal test named for this breed, and a clear result on the parents is a paper you can hold.

The red-and-white dog shares the CLAD and PRA questions and adds the ear. A hunting dog who swims or works wet grass will push debris and moisture into a heavy leather. Head shaking and a sour smell are the appointment, the same week, before the canal swells shut. That visit is ordinary for this breed and less central in the solid red's written health list. Budget it inside the $300 to $600 routine veterinary line, and expect some years to break out of that band when an infection needs a culture.

Daily work differs even though both cards say the dogs are active. The solid red's comparison page describes high engagement on the order of one to two hours. The red-and-white card says high and the profile warns that a low-exercise house gets noise. Slow maturity on the solid red means a two-year-old who still trains like a juvenile. The red-and-white dog's spirited label means the same recall problem shows up as a hunter who keeps working the bird and delays the delivery until the work is paid and repeated. Both need a fenced place to run. The solid red at 25 to 27 inches needs a crate and a vehicle that fit a 70 pound feathered dog. The red-and-white dog may be a 40 pound youngster or a 60 pound adult, so buy the crate for the top of that dog's own band. A generic setter measurement picks the wrong box.

Money over a lifespan is the two clocks on the cards: 12 to 15 years beside 11 to 15 years. They overlap. The solid red's extra pounds show up in the food bag, and the red-and-white dog's higher grooming line, $300 to $600 against $200 to $500, shows up at the salon or in Saturday mornings with a comb. Insurance bands overlap at $400 and the red-and-white ceiling is $800 against $700. Neither band is a quote. They are the profile's planning ranges. A bloat surgery or a chronic ear will leave either range behind, which is why the DNA papers and the ear-drying habit are the cheap part.

Children do well with the solid red on a card that says excellent, and with the red-and-white dog on a card that says good with respectful kids. A setter of either color knocks over a toddler when the doorbell rings. Teach a place command before you need it in the kitchen. The solid red's sweet-natured reputation is still a bird dog. The red-and-white dog's friendly reputation is still a dog who was bred to quarter a field. Apartment life with one short walk fails both of them, and it fails the red-and-white dog loudly.

At a breeder visit for the solid red, read the hip numbers, the PRA-rcd1 result, and the CLAD result, and watch the parents move. Ask how the kennel feeds, because a deep-chested breed's meal routine is part of bloat prevention and the page says to have that talk with a veterinarian. At a visit for the red-and-white dog, read CLAD and PRA, look in the ears of the adults, and ask what the dogs do on a week with no hunt scheduled. A bench of red-and-white dogs who only come alive in a field will be miserable in a house that hikes on Sundays. A solid red who has never been off a lead except in a yard will not magically point, and the household still owes that dog the one to two hours.

Growth is the year you can still change the adult. Keep the solid red lean while the OFA-described hip risk and the osteochondrosis risk are both in play. Keep the red-and-white dog lean because a 35 to 60 pound athlete who is soft still blows cruciate tissue and still has to carry feathering that hides the ribs. Weigh both monthly. Part the coat. The color you bought, solid mahogany or red with white, tells you which breed standard you are feeding. It does not tell you the body condition. The scale does.

Choose the solid red when you want the taller 60 to 70 pound family bird dog, you can live with slow maturity, and you will collect hip, PRA-rcd1, and CLAD papers and learn the bloat plan. Choose the red-and-white when you want the parti-color hunter, you will dry ears, you can use a dog whose card starts at 35 pounds, and you will not treat the white markings as a discount version of the mahogany dog. They can live in the same kind of active house. This site's own numbers keep them distinct.

Gear follows the taller dog if you pick the solid red. A crate has to let a 25 to 27 inch setter stand and turn at 60 to 70 pounds, and a car barrier has to survive a bird dog who loads himself like a projectile. The red-and-white adult may stop at 40 pounds or reach 60, so measure that dog at a year and again at two before you call the setup finished. Beds that are only decorative get destroyed by both. A washable bed you can actually launder after a wet Saturday is the one that matches the fieldwork.

Osteochondrosis on the solid red list is a reason to treat a persistent puppy limp as a growth injury. Rest it and call. Do not add miles to "work it out" while cartilage is still developing. The red-and-white page does not center the same growth-cartilage warning, which means you still watch a parti-color puppy's gait and you spend relatively more attention on the ears and the PRA and CLAD papers. Epilepsy, if a solid red ever seizes, gets a written log: time of day, length, and what happened just before. Megaesophagus shows up as trouble swallowing. That is a clinic visit with the food bowl story attached, including whether meals are elevated or whether the dog eats and then coughs.

A normal week for the solid red is a long outing, a training rep that ends while the dog is still listening, and a brush through the feathering so mats never sit in the armpits or behind the ears. Baths happen when the mahogany coat is actually dirty, then the dog is dry to the skin. A normal week for the red-and-white dog adds the ear check after every wet walk. The $200 to $500 grooming band and the $300 to $600 band are the yearly versions of those two weeks, multiplied. If you hate the comb, the red-and-white bill is the one that will hurt more, and the solid red will still mat if you skip a month.

Senior years use the lifespan overlap. A solid red at twelve is inside a 12 to 15 year card and may already be slowing on the hips the OFA figure warned about. Keep the weight down then, when a 70 pound dog with sore hips feels every extra pound. A red-and-white dog at twelve is inside an 11 to 15 year card and may be the one whose eye exam starts to matter more than his speed. Book the eye recheck for both if PRA was the test you bought the puppy on. The DNA result on the parents was the start. The annual look is the follow-through.

Insurance is easier to buy before a bloat scare or a chronic ear exists. Use the profile ranges only as a planning band, $400 to $700 and $400 to $800, and read whether hereditary eye disease and gastric emergencies are covered. The cheap years are the ones when the papers were clean and the ears stayed dry. The expensive years are the ones those habits were meant to prevent, on a dog you will still be walking when the puppy photos are a decade old.
