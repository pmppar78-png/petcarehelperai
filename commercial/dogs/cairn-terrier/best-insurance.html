What a Cairn policy is actually for

The monthly insurance line on the cost page runs about $15 to $60. The reason to read the exclusions before you pick a number is the Cairn Terrier Club of America list that AKC publishes: patellas, heart, kidney ultrasound, ocular melanosis, globoid cell leukodystrophy DNA, and liver screening for a portosystemic shunt or microvascular dysplasia. A policy that pays accidents and then writes hereditary liver disease out of the sample contract does not match this breed's paperwork. Ask the insurer, in writing, how they treat congenital shunts, DNA-proven storage disease, and kneecap surgery.

Timing beats brand. Buy while the puppy has a blank record. VCA's handout includes those liver conditions, globoid cell leukodystrophy, Legg-Calve-Perthes disease, craniomandibular osteopathy, luxating patellas, and later cataracts, glaucoma, and mitral valve dysplasia. Once any of those phrases sits in a chart, the next policy will call it pre-existing. The premium you were comparing is then irrelevant. The deductible and the annual cap matter more on a shunt workup than on a torn nail, because the expensive Cairn claims cluster early and then again at the eyes and heart in the second decade. AKC's 13 to 15 year expectancy means a policy you drop at age seven abandons the cataract years.

Read the bilateral clause on orthopedic claims. A repaired left patella sometimes locks the right stifle out of future coverage. Cairns are small enough that owners postpone imaging, and the second knee becomes the bill the policy was supposed to catch. Kidney ultrasound is a screening visit, and an abnormal kidney is the illness. Confirm whether a normal screening invoice is your cost, which it should be, and whether an abnormal finding starts a waiting-period clock. Ocular melanosis exams go on that same list. An eye certification visit is maintenance. Pigment disease that needs a veterinary ophthalmologist is the claim.

Dental, atopy, and skin are where Cairn owners feel nickel-and-dimed. VCA lists atopic dermatitis beside the coat-care notes. Some policies pay the drugs and exclude the food. Some pay neither unless the skin is infected. If your dog is likely to need a prescription diet trial, price that exclusion on purpose. Wellness riders that reimburse vaccines are optional math. They do not replace the hereditary coverage above. A cheap accident-only plan is a poor fit for a breed whose club led with liver, eyes, knees, kidneys, heart, and a DNA test.

Bring the parent results to the first vet visit anyway. Insurance pays treatment. It does not tell you whether the sire was scanned. The club list is still the buying checklist. The policy is how you pay if the checklist missed something, which it sometimes does, because a clear parent panel is a probability, and one puppy can still throw a luxating patella.
