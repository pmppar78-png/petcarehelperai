Feeding a 14-pound earthdog

AKC's weight line is 14 pounds for a male Cairn and 13 for a female. That is a small-bite dog. A large-breed formula marketed for a shiny coat is the wrong bag. The food decision is calories versus the wire coat's skin, plus the liver diseases VCA names. A shunt or microvascular dysplasia changes what the veterinarian wants in the bowl. Until those are ruled out, feed a complete small-breed or all-life-stages diet you can actually keep in stock, and keep treats inside the same calorie count. The $30 to $100 monthly food band on the cost page covers that ordinary plan, including a bit of training food.

Prey drive is the practical feeding hazard. VCA notes that Cairns chase small animals. A dog who catches a vole on the walk will skip dinner and still look pleased. That skipped dinner is fine once. A dog who hunts every evening and then refuses the measured meal is how you lose track of body condition. Score the ribs with your hands each week. You should feel them under the harsh coat without a thick pad of fat, because UKC's standard wants a working terrier, agile, with good bone that is still moderate. The coat hides a gaining waist. Trust the hands, then the scale at the clinic, against those 13 and 14 pound marks.

Atopy, also on the VCA list, is the reason some Cairns end up on a hydrolyzed or selected-protein diet. That switch is a trial your veterinarian runs, with a rule that nothing else passes the lips, including the dental chew and the pill pocket. Starting that trial because a neighbor's Westie needed it wastes the month. Starting it because this dog's feet are rust-stained from licking is the actual indication. Globoid cell leukodystrophy and craniomandibular osteopathy do not yield to a diet change. They are reasons a puppy who will not chew, or who cries when the jaw moves, needs a veterinarian this week, while you keep offering a soft complete diet so the puppy does not drop weight during the workup.

Hand-stripping does not change the nutrient spec. It does change how much dead undercoat is in the house, and owners sometimes "feed for the coat" with extra oil until the dog is soft and heavy. UKC already specifies the undercoat as short, soft, and close. Extra fat in the bowl will not turn a clipped coat back into wire. If the jacket feels cottony, the fix is the stripping knife and a groomer who still uses one, while the bowl stays at the calorie level that holds 13 to 14 pounds.

Water and teeth ride along with the food plan. A Cairn who empties the bowl in a minute is a candidate for a slow feeder, because a fast meal in a small chest is how you get a dog who asks for more an hour later. Brush the teeth on the same evening you brush the coat. VCA lists dental care next to the grooming paragraph for a reason: a 14-pound jaw with crowded teeth becomes a dental bill that dwarfs a year of kibble.

Diet sources: AKC weights, UKC coat and build, VCA.

Puppies and seniors do not share one scoop. A Cairn puppy heading toward the AKC adult weights of 13 and 14 pounds needs a growth diet with controlled calories so the kneecaps and the Legg-Calve-Perthes hips VCA lists are loaded by muscle, by a round belly. Split the day's food into three meals until six months, then two. Free feeding hides the moment the puppy overshoots. Weigh monthly on a kitchen scale and write it down. A smooth curve that lands near the registry figures is the goal. A jump of a pound in a month on a dog this size is a lot of percent.

Liver disease changes the recipe only when the veterinarian has bile acids or imaging in hand. Portosystemic shunts and microvascular dysplasia are on both the VCA handout and the parent-club screen. Some of those dogs do better on a protein plan the clinic designs, often with smaller, more frequent meals so the compromised liver handles one load at a time. Inventing a low-protein bowl because a forum said "Cairns have liver problems" underfeeds a normal dog. Wait for the test, then follow the clinic's gram scale, and keep the treats inside that same plan.

Treats are where the 14-pound frame gets away. A tablespoon of peanut butter is a serious fraction of a Cairn's day. Use the kibble itself for the find-the-glove game, and keep cheese for the recall on the long line. Dental chews that are longer than the muzzle encourage gulping. UKC wants a scissors bite and large teeth in a muzzle that is powerful and still moderate in length. Break the chew. Watch the dog finish it. A choked terrier is a feeding failure, and a dental chew is still a calorie.

Water stays available, including after a dig in dry soil. A dog who has been underground in a cardboard tunnel comes out thirsty and then inhales the bowl. That is fine. What you watch is vomiting the water back up, or a sudden thirst with weight loss, which in any older Cairn deserves a senior panel even though the breed's famous problems are eyes, knees, and liver. Ordinary kidney disease of old age still happens alongside the aplasia the club ultrasound is meant to catch in youngsters.
