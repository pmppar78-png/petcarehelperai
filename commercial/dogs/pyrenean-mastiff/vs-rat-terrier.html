Choose Pyrenean Mastiff If...

  • Your weekly schedule reliably absorbs the Pyrenean Mastiff's exercise, training, and enrichment minimums — not just on good weeks.
  • The Pyrenean Mastiff's social and behavioural baseline lines up with the people, kids, or other pets already in the home.

Detailed breed guide

Temperament and Personality

Pyrenean Mastiff's gentle, noble, calm character creates a fundamentally different ownership experience than Rat Terrier's friendly, curious, lovable nature.

Best for Families

Pyrenean Mastiff's gentle nature and Rat Terrier's friendly temperament each present different dynamics with younger family members.

Health and Lifespan

Ask the veterinarian about Hip Dysplasia and Elbow Dysplasia for the first animal and about Patellar Luxation and Hip Dysplasia for the second before you decide, and keep both lifespan figures in the health section above beside that list.

Best for Low

Neither breed is truly "low maintenance" health-wise, but Rat Terrier's longer lifespan and different condition profile may mean fewer intensive interventions in middle age compared to Pyrenean Mastiff.

Exercise and Activity

Pyrenean Mastiff requires moderate (1-1.5 hours daily) levels of exercise and engagement, while Rat Terrier needs moderate to high (40-60 min daily) activity.

Grooming and Maintenance

Pyrenean Mastiff has high (long double coat) grooming needs, while Rat Terrier requires moderate (seasonal) maintenance.

Cost of Ownership

The size difference between Pyrenean Mastiff (Giant (120-220+ lbs)) and Rat Terrier (Small-Medium (10-25 lbs)) significantly impacts costs across food, supplies, and veterinary care. Key cost differentials include: food costs scale with size (Giant (120-220+ lbs) vs Small-Medium (10-25 lbs)), grooming costs reflect maintenance requirements (high (long double coat) vs moderate (seasonal)), and veterinary costs correlate with breed-specific health risks. Over a complete lifespan, Pyrenean Mastiff's 10-13 years expected life and Rat Terrier's 12-18 years expected life mean different total cost horizons—the longer-lived dog accumulates more total costs but potentially offers more years of companionship.

Choose Pyrenean Mastiff if your lifestyle accommodates their moderate (1-1.5 hours daily) activity needs, high (long double coat) grooming requirements, and you're prepared for their gentle temperament. Choose Rat Terrier if you prefer their moderate to high (40-60 min daily) energy level, can manage moderate (seasonal) maintenance, and appreciate their friendly personality.

Best for First

Feeding and Nutrition

Pyrenean Mastiff (Giant (120-220+ lbs), moderate (1-1.5 hours daily) activity) has different caloric and macronutrient needs than Rat Terrier (Small-Medium (10-25 lbs), moderate to high (40-60 min daily) activity). Health-condition-specific dietary needs also differ—Pyrenean Mastiff's associations with joint and skeletal conditions may warrant targeted nutrition, while Rat Terrier's genetic predisposition to joint conditions calls for different dietary strategies.

Living Space and

Pyrenean Mastiff requires crate space suited to a Giant (120-220+ lbs) dog with moderate (1-1.5 hours daily) exercise demands and a gentle, noble, calm disposition. Rat Terrier needs space accommodating their Small-Medium (10-25 lbs) build, moderate to high (40-60 min daily) activity needs, and friendly, curious, lovable behavioral style.

Long-Term Commitment Assessment

Pyrenean Mastiff (Giant (120-220+ lbs), good (willing to please) care demands) and Rat Terrier (Small-Medium (10-25 lbs), excellent care demands) each require sustained dedication but in different ways. Pyrenean Mastiff's moderate (1-1.5 hours daily) exercise requirements must be met consistently, just as Rat Terrier's moderate to high (40-60 min daily) activity needs cannot be neglected.
