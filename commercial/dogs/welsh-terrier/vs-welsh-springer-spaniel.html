Cost of Ownership

Both Welsh Terrier and Welsh Springer Spaniel are similarly sized at Medium (20 lbs), so recurring costs for food and supplies are comparable between the two breeds. Key cost differentials include: food costs scale with size (Medium (20 lbs) vs Medium (35-55 lbs)), grooming costs reflect maintenance requirements (low (wire coat) vs moderate), and veterinary costs correlate with breed-specific health risks. Over a complete lifespan, Welsh Terrier's 12-15 years expected life and Welsh Springer Spaniel's 12-15 years expected life mean different total cost horizons—the longer-lived dog accumulates more total costs but potentially offers more years of companionship.

Consider your daily schedule (Welsh Terrier: moderate to high (45-60 minutes daily) engagement vs Welsh Springer Spaniel: high (1-2 hours daily)), grooming tolerance (low (wire coat) vs moderate), and personality preference (friendly vs happy).

Welsh Terrier at Medium (20 lbs) needs a crate appropriately scaled to their dimensions and moderate to high (45-60 minutes daily) activity pattern, while Welsh Springer Spaniel at Medium (35-55 lbs) requires crate sizing matched to their own build and high (1-2 hours daily) energy level.
