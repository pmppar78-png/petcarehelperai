Black Russian Terrier Nutritional Profile

Life-Stage Feeding Guide for Black Russian Terrier

Black Russian Terriers are large, coated, and slow to mature. Puppies need a large-breed growth diet for more than a year, with ribs you can find under the furnishing. Adults eat a complete food in two meals. The coat hides gain until the dog is already heavy and the elbows are paying for it. Part the hair monthly and score the body. Seniors often need a calorie drop and a groomer who can see the skin. A matted jacket plus extra food is a skin infection waiting. Split meals for the deep chest. Training treats come out of the daily cup. This breed does not need a parade of novel proteins unless a veterinarian is running a real elimination trial.
