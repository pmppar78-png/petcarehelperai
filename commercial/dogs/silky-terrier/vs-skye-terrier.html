Temperament and Personality

Silky Terrier's friendly, quick, keenly alert character creates a fundamentally different ownership experience than Skye Terrier's loyal, courageous, dignified nature.

Best for Families

Silky Terrier's friendly nature and Skye Terrier's loyal temperament each present different dynamics with younger family members.

Health and Lifespan

Skye Terrier faces its own health challenges including Orthopedic Concerns, Other Conditions. Ask the veterinarian about Patellar Luxation and Legg-Calve-Perthes Disease for the first animal and about Premature Closure of Growth Plates and Intervertebral Disc Disease for the second before you decide, and keep both lifespan figures in the health section above beside that list.

Best for Low

Neither breed is truly "low maintenance" health-wise, but Skye Terrier's longer lifespan and different condition profile may mean fewer intensive interventions in middle age compared to Silky Terrier.

Cost of Ownership

The size difference between Silky Terrier (Toy (10 lbs)) and Skye Terrier (Medium (35-45 lbs)) significantly impacts costs across food, supplies, and veterinary care. Key cost differentials include: food costs scale with size (Toy (10 lbs) vs Medium (35-45 lbs)), grooming costs reflect maintenance requirements (low vs moderate), and veterinary costs correlate with breed-specific health risks. Over a complete lifespan, Silky Terrier's 13-15 years expected life and Skye Terrier's 12-14 years expected life mean different total cost horizons—the longer-lived dog accumulates more total costs but potentially offers more years of companionship.

Consider your daily schedule (Silky Terrier: moderate (30-45 minutes daily) engagement vs Skye Terrier: moderate (30-60 minutes daily)), grooming tolerance (low vs moderate), and personality preference (friendly vs loyal).

Feeding and Nutrition

Silky Terrier at Toy (10 lbs) needs caloric intake calibrated to their moderate (30-45 minutes daily) activity level, while Skye Terrier at Medium (35-45 lbs) requires nutrition matched to their moderate (30-60 minutes daily) energy output. Silky Terrier's predisposition to Orthopedic Issues may require specialized dietary formulations, while Skye Terrier may benefit from diets supporting Orthopedic Concerns.

Living Space and

Silky Terrier (Toy (10 lbs), friendly, quick, keenly alert) occupies space differently than Skye Terrier (Medium (35-45 lbs), loyal, courageous, dignified). Daily activity patterns influence space usage—Silky Terrier's moderate (30-45 minutes daily) energy creates one footprint, while Skye Terrier's moderate (30-60 minutes daily) activity level creates another.

Insurance and Health

Silky Terrier faces health risks from Orthopedic Issues and Other Conditions that generate specific claim patterns, while Skye Terrier's Orthopedic Concerns and Other Conditions drives different insurance utilization. Over Silky Terrier's 13-15 years lifespan, expected veterinary costs may differ significantly from Skye Terrier's 12-14 years cost horizon. Size-driven cost differences (Toy (10 lbs) versus Medium (35-45 lbs)) affect medication dosing, surgical complexity, and equipment costs—all factors that influence insurance claim amounts.

Long-Term Commitment Assessment

Silky Terrier's friendly, quick, keenly alert temperament and moderate (30-45 minutes daily) activity needs must remain compatible with your lifestyle through potential moves, career changes, and family growth. Skye Terrier's loyal, courageous, dignified character and moderate (30-60 minutes daily) demands create a different long-term compatibility profile. Care complexity evolves with age: Silky Terrier's health predispositions (Orthopedic Issues) and Skye Terrier's risks (Orthopedic Concerns) may require increasing management in later years.
