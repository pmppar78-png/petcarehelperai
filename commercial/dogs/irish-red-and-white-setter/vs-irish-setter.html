Strengths and Tradeoffs

Ears, white markings, and a setter who is lighter on the card

Start with the Irish Red and White Setter's own table. This site lists 22 to 26 inches, 35 to 60 pounds, a lifespan around 11 to 15 years, high exercise, and high grooming. Food is $600 to $1,200 a year, grooming $300 to $600, routine veterinary care $300 to $600, insurance $400 to $800, and first-year training $200 to $500. The profile's useful sentence is the one that separates this dog from the solid Irish Setter and then tells you to ask for CLAD and progressive retinal atrophy DNA, because the ears are spaniel ears and they infect when they stay wet.

The solid Irish Setter, on the facing card, is 25 to 27 inches and 60 to 70 pounds, living 12 to 15 years. That is a heavier band with no 35 pound floor. Grooming money is lower on that profile, $200 to $500, food $600 to $1,000, insurance $400 to $700, veterinary care still $300 to $600. Trainability is described as good and slow to mature. The coat people recognize is mahogany or chestnut, a flowing red, and the family rating is excellent with children against the red-and-white card's good. If you came here to pick a color, the cards are telling you the pounds and the grooming bill changed with the white.

Fieldwork is the red-and-white dog's week. Wet grass, a retrieve, a bath, and then the ear leather. Dry the canal. Smell it the next morning. Head shaking is the appointment. Treat it as an ear, and book the visit. The solid red can work the same wet field and the health list you will be handed is more about hips, growth cartilage, bloat, and eyes than about a standing ear-cleaning demo. Both are bird dogs. The maintenance that shows up in the bathroom is louder on the parti-color.

Put the DNA papers in two columns only after you have looked at the pounds. For the red-and-white litter, CLAD and progressive retinal atrophy are the tests the profile names. For the solid red litter, the breed page asks for OFA hips, PRA-rcd1, and CLAD, and it publishes an OFA hip dysplasia rate of about 12.3 percent among evaluated dogs at the 60 to 70 pound size. Osteochondrosis is listed for growing solid-red puppies. Gastric dilatation-volvulus is listed because the chest is deep. Megaesophagus and epilepsy are on that page too. A red-and-white buyer who only copies the solid red's hip speech has skipped the ear, and a solid red buyer who only copies the ear speech has skipped a 12.3 percent hip figure and a bloat plan.

Exercise is high on the red-and-white card, and the profile predicts noise when the dog is underworked. Plan a real outing most days, plus training that ends while the dog still wants the toy. The solid red's one-to-two-hour engagement note is the neighboring demand, with the extra warning that maturity comes late, so a flashy six-month-old still has training left at two. Neither card supports a studio apartment and a lunchtime lap. The red-and-white dog at the bottom of 35 pounds is still a hunting dog. The solid red at 70 pounds is a lap only in the sense that he will try.

Grooming cost is the cleanest dollar difference on the two profiles: $300 to $600 beside $200 to $500. Feathering on the red-and-white dog mats behind the ears and in the trousers if it is ignored between the wet days that also cause the infections. A standing salon appointment or a Sunday comb, all the way to the skin, is the size of that bill. The solid red's feathering is real and the published band is smaller. Brushing still happens. It is a different line item.

Food bands meet in the middle and the red-and-white ceiling is higher, $1,200 against $1,000, even though many red-and-white adults weigh less than a solid red. Activity spends the calories. The breed name on the bag does not. A hunted dog at 50 pounds can out-eat a couch-kept dog at 65. Measure the cup. Keep a waist you can find under the feathering. Two meals matter more for the deep-chested solid red because of the bloat warning, and they are still a good habit for the red-and-white dog who comes home ravenous and will gulp a single bowl.

Insurance planning ranges overlap, $400 to $800 against $400 to $700. Read the exclusion list for hereditary eye disease and for immune disease if CLAD is the worry you came in with. Ears may be treated as routine infections and paid, or they may be capped. Ask before you need the third culture of the year. The lifespan lines, 11 to 15 and 12 to 15, mean you are buying a decade of that policy plus the years after a senior dog's hearing and sight change. PRA is on both lists. An annual eye look is cheaper than discovering the miss at the top of the stairs.

Children and guests get a spirited greeting from the red-and-white dog. Teach a settle before the dog is tall enough to clear a coffee table on the way to the door, which on a 22 to 26 inch hunter happens early. The solid red's excellent-with-kids line still assumes the same manners, on a taller dog. Respectful handling is the phrase the red-and-white card uses. Use it. A setter who is allowed to muzzle-punch faces as a puppy does it harder at 50 pounds.

When you visit red-and-white adults, look in the ears, watch a dog work or at least watch one hold a point or a retrieve, and read the CLAD and PRA papers out loud with the breeder. Ask what the dogs do on dark weeks in January, because a hunting calendar with empty months is how the noise problem in the profile shows up. When you visit solid reds, watch the parents trot, read hips and PRA-rcd1 and CLAD, and ask how meals are spaced. The white on a puppy is the breed. It is also a place mud shows, which is a washing problem, and it is the reason strangers on the internet will tell you that you bought a mismarked Irish Setter. The profile disagrees. Feed the dog in front of you, dry the ears you can see, and keep the papers that match the tests each page actually names.

White markings change the housework. Mud on a red-and-white chest shows before mud on a solid mahogany chest, so the bath happens sooner and the ear-drying step happens with it. A towel in the field bag is equipment. So is a dryer or a patient hour in a warm room, because a damp canal under feathering is the infection the profile is warning you about. The solid red still needs to be dried after rain. The parti-color dog makes the job obvious, which is useful if you use it and expensive if you only notice the smell on day four.

The first-year training line of $200 to $500 on the red-and-white profile buys a class or a handful of private sessions. A finished hunting dog takes more months than that line item. Use it for a recall that pays, a place command, and a retrieve that ends in your hand. The solid red's slow-maturity note means that same money buys manners, and the bird work continues into the second year. Either way, a puppy class that is only socialization will not spend a setter. They already like people. They need a job with a start and a stop.

January is the month the red-and-white profile is quietly describing. Hunting season pauses. The dog is still courageous and spirited in the kitchen. If the only exercise left is a lap of the block, the noise arrives. Build a wet-weather plan before winter: indoor retrieves, a flirt pole used with rules, a training walk in the rain followed by the ear routine. The solid red in the same January still needs the one to two hours, and the deep-chest bloat rules still apply when the dog is bored and gulps dinner. Two meals, a pause after eating, and no rolling games on a full stomach are the household version of the bloat talk the setter page tells you to have with a veterinarian.

Read the hips on the solid red even if you think you are only shopping color. About 12.3 percent of evaluated Irish Setters in the OFA figure on that breed page had hip dysplasia, at a typical 60 to 70 pounds. Osteochondrosis is the puppy cartilage problem on the same list. If a solid red youngster persists in a limp, rest and call. Adding roadwork because the other setter in the house is fine is how a growth injury gets a longer story. The red-and-white dog's card does not lead with that percentage. You still do not run a parti-color puppy into the ground, and you do spend the vet time on ears, eyes, and the CLAD result.

Over 11 to 15 years the red-and-white grooming band of $300 to $600 repeats every year, and the food band of $600 to $1,200 flexes with how often the dog actually hunts. Over 12 to 15 years the solid red's food band of $600 to $1,000 sits on a heavier body, and the grooming band of $200 to $500 assumes someone still owns a comb. Insurance at $400 to $800 or $400 to $700 is the planning range, and a third ear culture or a gastric emergency leaves it. Ask the insurer about hereditary retinal disease before you bind the policy. PRA is named on both breed pages. An eye certificate you never scheduled is a paper that cannot help a twelve-year-old on a dim staircase.

Pick the red-and-white dog if you will dry ears, brush feathering, give a hunting dog something to do in the off season, and you want the 35 to 60 pound card with the white intact as its own breed. Pick the solid red if you want the 60 to 70 pound mahogany dog, you will collect hip, PRA-rcd1, and CLAD papers, and you will learn a bloat habit while the chest is still growing. A friendlier family blurb arrives only after the exercise is real. The cards already printed the pounds. Use them.
