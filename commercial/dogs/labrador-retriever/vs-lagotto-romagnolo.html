Ownership and health

Temperament and Personality

Labrador Retriever is characterized by a friendly, active, outgoing personality, while Lagotto Romagnolo tends toward affectionate, keen, undemanding traits.

Best for Families

Labrador Retriever's friendly nature and Lagotto Romagnolo's affectionate temperament each present different dynamics with younger family members.

Health and Lifespan

Lagotto Romagnolo faces its own health challenges including Neurological Conditions, hip and joint issues, Other Concerns. Ask the veterinarian about Exercise-induced collapse is a DNM1 mutation: a fit dog can wobble and go down after hard play, then recover for the first animal and about Benign Familial Juvenile Epilepsy (BFJE) and Lagotto Storage Disease (LSD) for the second before you decide, and keep both lifespan figures in the health section above beside that list.

Best for Low

Labrador Retriever's predispositions typically require specific screening tests, while Lagotto Romagnolo has its own set of conditions to monitor.

Exercise and Activity

Labrador Retriever requires high (1-2 hours daily) levels of exercise and engagement, while Lagotto Romagnolo needs moderate to high (60+ minutes daily) activity.

Grooming and Maintenance

Labrador Retriever has high (double coat) grooming needs, while Lagotto Romagnolo requires low (hypoallergenic coat) maintenance.

Cost of Ownership

Key cost differentials include: food costs scale with size (Large (55-80 lbs) vs Medium (24-35 lbs)), grooming costs reflect maintenance requirements (high (double coat) vs low (hypoallergenic coat)), and veterinary costs correlate with breed-specific health risks. Over a complete lifespan, Labrador Retriever's 10-12 years expected life and Lagotto Romagnolo's 15-17 years expected life mean different total cost horizons—the longer-lived dog accumulates more total costs but potentially offers more years of companionship.

Elbow dysplasia is the Labrador Retriever comparison point, and progressive retinal atrophy is the other health item in the same matchup. Choose Lagotto Romagnolo if you prefer their moderate to high (60+ minutes daily) energy level, can manage low (hypoallergenic coat) maintenance, and appreciate their affectionate personality.

Best for First

Feeding and Nutrition

Labrador Retriever at Large (55-80 lbs) needs caloric intake calibrated to their high (1-2 hours daily) activity level, while Lagotto Romagnolo at Medium (24-35 lbs) requires nutrition matched to their moderate to high (60+ minutes daily) energy output. Labrador Retriever's tendency toward hip dysplasia and other orthopedic problems may require specialized dietary formulations, while Lagotto Romagnolo may benefit from diets supporting Neurological Conditions.

Living Space and

Labrador Retriever requires crate space suited to a Large (55-80 lbs) dog with high (1-2 hours daily) exercise demands and a friendly, active, outgoing disposition. Lagotto Romagnolo needs space accommodating their Medium (24-35 lbs) build, moderate to high (60+ minutes daily) activity needs, and affectionate, keen, undemanding, trainable behavioral style.

Long-Term Commitment Assessment

Labrador Retriever's 10-12 years expected life will include a vibrant youth, stable adulthood, and eventual senior phase with increasing health needs related to hip and joint issues. Lagotto Romagnolo's 15-17 years trajectory follows a similar arc but with different condition profiles (Neurological Conditions) and different care demands (excellent versus excellent).

Best for Making

Reading about a breed only goes so far; real interaction reveals whether Labrador Retriever's personality or Lagotto Romagnolo's energy aligns with your daily life.

Two water dogs, one of them now a truffle dog

UKC's Labrador is a gun dog whose ancestor, the Saint Johns Retriever, came off fishing boats and was kept for retrieving from water. The modern standard still judges the dog as a worker: square or slightly long, short dense double coat, otter tail no longer than the hock, soft mouth, good nose. AKC's Lagotto Romagnolo page starts in the same element and leaves it. The breed goes back at least to Renaissance Italy as a waterfowl retriever in the marshlands of Ravenna. Lago means lake. AKC also calls the dog the Romagna Water Dog, then describes the modern job as truffle hunting, and states that the Lagotto is the only purebred dog recognized as a specialized truffle searcher. Both dogs have a nose and a history in water. One is still scored as a retriever. The other is scored as a curly dog who hunts a smell underground.

The bodies make the budget obvious. AKC puts Labrador males at 22.5 to 24.5 inches and 65 to 80 pounds, females at 21.5 to 23.5 inches and 55 to 70 pounds, with a life expectancy of 11 to 13 years. The same club puts Lagotto males at 17 to 19 inches and 28.5 to 35 pounds, females at 16 to 18 inches and 24 to 31 pounds, with a life expectancy of 15 to 17 years. A Lagotto male at the top of the standard weighs about half of a Labrador male at the bottom. Food follows the ribs. This page prints $120 to $280 a month for the Labrador and $100 to $320 for the Lagotto. The overlap is real, and the reason is coat and care, because the smaller dog can spend more at the groomer than the larger dog spends at the feed store. AKC's Lagotto life line is also four years longer at the top, 17 against the Labrador's 13, so a monthly cost that looks similar becomes a larger lifetime pile if you simply multiply. Seventeen years at $200 a month is a different promise from thirteen years at the same number.

Coat care is the daily difference you will feel. The Labrador jacket, in the UKC standard, lies close, sheds, and wants the weekly brush and the post-swim bath VCA describes. Seasonal shedding is heavy. The house dust is Labrador-colored for a few weeks twice a year, and the rest of the time a brush and a washable cover cope. The Lagotto coat, on the AKC care page, is rough-looking and waterproof, thick tight curls over the whole body except the head, with a beard, eyebrows, and whiskers. AKC says the curls behave more like hair than fur, shedding is minimal, little tufts can still appear, and the coat needs regular trimming because some dogs mat more than others. Ears get a weekly check. A Labrador owner who skips a month of grooming has a dusty house. A Lagotto owner who skips a month can have a felted jacket that hurts. The grooming line is a subscription for the Lagotto and a brush in a drawer for the Labrador, which is how the smaller dog's monthly band can reach $320.

Exercise looks similar on a breed-selector form and splits in the yard. VCA wants the Labrador to swim, retrieve, and train, and warns that a bored dog chews, carries, and sometimes swallows objects. The Minnesota lab's DNM1 work puts a ceiling on frantic intensity: dogs with two copies of the collapse variant, a few percent up to about 13 percent of tested Labradors depending on the group, can go down 5 to 20 minutes into hard work, in Merck's timing, and then look fine an hour later. A Lagotto, in AKC's exercise section, needs an active life with both mental work and physical work, does well at scenting and dock diving, and is described as needing the family nearby. The truffle job is nose work with a walk attached. The Labrador job is a marked retrieve with a swim attached. A household that hikes and hides food will suit the Lagotto. A household that throws a bumper into a pond will suit the Labrador. Swapping them produces a curly dog who is asked to be a retriever, or a Labrador who is asked to be calm after one lap of the block.

Health packets are different documents. For the Labrador, take hips, elbows, eyes, and a DNM1 result, then read VCA's list: hip and elbow dysplasia, osteochondritis dissecans, retinal problems, tricuspid valve dysplasia, epilepsy, atopy, bloat, mast cell tumors, and later hemangiosarcoma and cataracts. OFA's 1974-to-2009 hip file holds 208,931 Labrador evaluations and a 12.0 percent dysplastic rate, with 17.6 percent excellent. For the Lagotto, AKC's parent-club list is patella evaluation, hip evaluation, an ophthalmologist exam, a DNA test for benign familial juvenile epilepsy, and a DNA test for Lagotto storage disease. The same AKC health note says a responsible breeder also screens for elbow dysplasia, and that the breed is generally a healthy one with those conditions on the radar. Juvenile epilepsy and a storage disease are the Lagotto-specific DNA questions. They are the wrong questions to skip because you already asked a Labrador breeder about exercise-induced collapse. Bring the form that matches the puppy. A Lagotto puppy's clear EIC test does not answer BFJE, and a Labrador puppy's clear BFJE test is a test for a disease the Labrador form was not built around.

Training tone follows the job. UKC says the Labrador is eager to please and easy to teach complex tasks, with shyness in a mature dog treated as a serious fault. AKC calls the Lagotto alert, intelligent, and lively, good at agility and obedience, and strong in scent work. Both can live in a family. The Labrador's failure mode is enthusiasm with a 70-pound chest and a mouth that collects household objects. The Lagotto's failure mode is a brilliant nose and a coat that seized into mats while everyone was busy admiring the beard. Socialize both. Provide a crate. Check ears on both, because the Labrador's drop ear holds swim water and the Lagotto's ear hides under curls. Then pick the mess you prefer: hair on the sofa for a decade, or a grooming appointment on the calendar for the longer Lagotto lifespan.

The monthly comparison on this page is a fair start and a poor ending. Use it after you have weighed the dog you will actually feed, 28 to 35 pounds against 65 to 80 for the males at the standard tops and bottoms, and after you have priced a trim schedule against a weekly brush. Add the years. AKC gives the Lagotto 15 to 17 and the Labrador 11 to 13, so the curly dog may be in the budget after the retriever's chapter has closed. Add the emergency you are insuring. Bloat and elbow surgery sit on the Labrador list. Storage disease and juvenile epilepsy sit on the Lagotto list. A family that wants a pond dog who carries a bumper, sheds, and lives in the low teens of years is describing the UKC Labrador. A family that wants a smaller curly dog, a truffle-style nose, minimal shed, and a longer life is describing the AKC Lagotto. The breeds share a waterfowl past. The dogs in the yard diverged when one kept the otter tail and the other grew the beard.

Pairing sources: AKC Lagotto, AKC Lab, UKC, VCA, OFA.
