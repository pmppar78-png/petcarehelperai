What a Labrador policy is actually asked to cover

The premiums already listed on this page run about $10 to $25 a month for accident only, $30 to $80 for accident and illness, and another $10 to $25 if you add wellness. Those are the predictable numbers. The claims follow VCA's age split. In the first year the handout names hip dysplasia, elbow dysplasia, osteochondritis dissecans, retinal dysplasia, and tricuspid valve dysplasia. From one year to eight it names exercise-induced collapse, epilepsy, atopic dermatitis, progressive retinal dysplasia, gastric dilatation-volvulus, and mast cell tumors. After eight it names hemangiosarcoma and cataracts, and it tells owners to have new lumps, appetite changes, and low energy checked because cancer screening matters as Labs age.

Ask for the parents' own hip and elbow reports before you compare policies. A policy bought while the puppy is still unexamined is the one that can be asked to pay for the imaging and, if the dog is clinical, the surgery. A policy bought the week after a veterinarian writes "hip dysplasia" on a limp will treat that diagnosis as already known. The same timing applies to elbows and to a heart murmur that turns out to be tricuspid valve dysplasia. Accident-only coverage, the cheaper row, leaves illness off the page. Hip dysplasia, epilepsy, atopy, and the cancers on VCA's list are illness. Bloat, gastric dilatation-volvulus, is an emergency that can also be an illness with a surgical bill, and it is on the adult list. Match the row to that list before you pick the $10 plan because the monthly number looks kind.

Exercise-induced collapse sits in an awkward spot for insurance. Merck's 2026 review says there is no treatment and that affected dogs avoid intense activity. The University of Minnesota test tells you whether the dog is clear, a carrier, or has two copies of the DNM1 variant. A DNA test is a small line next to an orthopedic surgery. The collapse itself may produce an emergency visit the first time it happens, because a dog who goes down at the park looks like heat stroke, a heart event, or a seizure until someone who knows the breed asks about the five-to-twenty-minute window Merck describes. After the diagnosis, the cost shifts from treatment, which the review does not offer, to the life you build so the episode does not repeat. Read the policy for whether a genetic disease known before enrollment is excluded. Buying the accident-and-illness plan in the puppy months, and running the DNA test in those same months, keeps the result and the coverage in the right order.

Wellness add-ons are for the ordinary year: the exam, the vaccines, the ear cytology after a summer of swimming. They are a poor substitute for the illness plan if your real fear is the VCA adult list. Atopy can be the quiet multi-year claim, a dermatology visit and a medication every season, smaller than a bloat surgery and larger than the grooming line. Cataracts and hemangiosarcoma land late, which is why a policy you cancel at age seven abandons the senior chapter AKC's 11-to-13-year expectancy still includes. Ask what happens to the premium as the dog ages, using the ranges on this page as today's quote only. The $2,000 to $10,000 figure already printed below the tables is the scale of a single bad year, and it sits in the same conversation as GDV, a fracture, or a cancer workup, each of which can clear that range on its own.

Insurance sources: VCA, Merck, Minnesota, AKC.
