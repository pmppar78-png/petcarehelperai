Ownership and health

Temperament and Personality

Saint Bernard's playful, charming, inquisitive character creates a fundamentally different ownership experience than Saluki's gentle, dignified, independent nature. The household has to live with playful, charming, inquisitive on one side and gentle, dignified, independent on the other.

Best for Families

Saint Bernard's playful nature and Saluki's gentle temperament each present different dynamics with younger family members.

Health and Lifespan

Saluki faces its own health challenges including Cardiac Conditions, genetic predispositions to conditions like allergies, autoimmune disorders, and organ-specific diseases. Ask the veterinarian about Dilated cardiomyopathy, hip dysplasia, osteosarcoma, and entropion for the first animal and about Heart Murmurs & Arrhythmias and Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM) for the second before you decide, and keep both lifespan figures in the health section above beside that list.

Best for Low

Saint Bernard's predispositions typically require specific screening tests, while Saluki has its own set of conditions to monitor.

Grooming and Maintenance

Saint Bernard has high grooming needs, while Saluki requires low to moderate maintenance.

Cost of Ownership

Key cost differentials include: food costs scale with size (Large (120-180 lbs) vs Large (40-65 lbs)), grooming costs reflect maintenance requirements (high vs low to moderate), and veterinary costs correlate with breed-specific health risks. Over a complete lifespan, Saint Bernard's 8-10 years expected life and Saluki's 10-17 years expected life mean different total cost horizons—the longer-lived dog accumulates more total costs but potentially offers more years of companionship.

Consider your daily schedule (Saint Bernard: moderate engagement vs Saluki: high (1-2 hours daily)), grooming tolerance (high vs low to moderate), and personality preference (playful vs gentle).

Best for First

Feeding and Nutrition

Saint Bernard at Large (120-180 lbs) needs caloric intake calibrated to their moderate activity level, while Saluki at Large (40-65 lbs) requires nutrition matched to their high (1-2 hours daily) energy output. Saint Bernard's genetic predisposition to joint conditions may require specialized dietary formulations, while Saluki may benefit from diets supporting Cardiac Conditions.

Living Space and

Saint Bernard (Large (120-180 lbs), playful, charming, inquisitive) occupies space differently than Saluki (Large (40-65 lbs), gentle, dignified, independent). Daily activity patterns influence space usage—Saint Bernard's moderate energy creates one footprint, while Saluki's high (1-2 hours daily) activity level creates another.

Insurance and Health

Saint Bernard is predisposed to skeletal and joint concerns and eye conditions, skin allergies, and age-related joint deterioration, making coverage for hereditary conditions essential. Saluki's risk factors (Cardiac Conditions and specific genetic predispositions that regular veterinary screening can catch early) require different policy features. Wellness coverage value also differs: Saint Bernard's moderate activity level versus Saluki's high (1-2 hours daily) demands mean different injury risk profiles.

Long-Term Commitment Assessment

Saint Bernard's 8-10 years expected life will include a vibrant youth, stable adulthood, and eventual senior phase with increasing health needs related to skeletal and joint concerns. Saluki's 10-17 years trajectory follows a similar arc but with different condition profiles (Cardiac Conditions) and different care demands (moderate (independent thinker) versus moderate).

Best for Making

Reading about a breed only goes so far; real interaction reveals whether Saint Bernard's personality or Saluki's energy aligns with your daily life.
