Best for Transitioning Bristlenose Pleco's Diet

What to feed

Bristlenose plecos, Ancistrus, are small armored catfish that rasp biofilm, soft algae, and wood. Adults stay around 4 to 5 inches, and 20 gallons is a fair minimum for one, with more room if other fish are present. Real driftwood has to be in the tank. Blanched zucchini, a sinking algae wafer, and the wood itself are the staple once the glass is clean. Algae is only part of what they eat, and a bare tank with no wood leaves them thin.

Feed the wafer at night. Bristlenose are more active then, and the other fish are less likely to steal it. A sunken belly means the wafer is being eaten by someone else or the wood has not had time to grow a film. Males guard a cave and a clutch, and they still need the same foods. Fry graze the slime on the cave and then move to crushed wafer and wood.

Pull the zucchini before it turns to mush. A bristlenose with a round waist and worn-looking bristles on a male is usually eating well. White patches on the body are more often a water or injury issue than a sign to change the brand of wafer. Keep the menu as vegetables, wood, and a spirulina or algae wafer.

Ancistrus bristlenose plecos stay near 4 to 5 inches and need real driftwood plus a sinking algae wafer and blanched zucchini in a tank of about 20 gallons. Feed at night, under the wood, so the other fish do not take the wafer. A sunken belly means the food was stolen or the wood is too new to carry a film. Males guard a cave and a clutch and still need the same foods left at the entrance. Fry graze the slime inside the cave and then take a crushed wafer. Pull the zucchini in the morning before it dissolves. A bristlenose with a round waist is eating. White patches on the skin are a reason to check the water, and the wafer brand is rarely the cause. Keep the menu as wood, vegetables, and an algae or spirulina wafer.
