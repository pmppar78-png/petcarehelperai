What to feed

Otocinclus are small armored catfish, about 2 inches long, that graze biofilm and soft algae in planted South American streams. A group belongs in a planted tank of at least 10 gallons that has already grown a film on the glass, wood, and leaves. Algae wafers, blanched zucchini, and a gel food such as a repashy-style mix are the foods to add once that film is gone. They starve in a new, spotless tank even when flakes are raining down for the tetras above them.

Feed the wafers after the lights dim so the faster fish do not take every piece. A pinched belly on an Otocinclus is a fish that is not getting food. A pinecone raise of the scales is dropsy, a different problem, and it needs a water check the same day. Keep a school. Single otos stop grazing and fade. They do not need a meaty pellet, and a carnivore stick meant for a big catfish is too large and too rich.

Blanch the zucchini until a fork dents it, clip it where the otos already rest, and remove it the next morning. Replace the slice before it dissolves into the substrate. A fish with a round belly and a steady rasping motion on the glass is eating. One that hangs at the surface or loses the belly curve needs more aufwuchs on the glass and the wood.
