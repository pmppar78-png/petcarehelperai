An otocinclus startup is the planted tank, paid for before the fish. A 10 gallon is the minimum on the species card, and the useful version already has algae on the glass. Budget the tank, light, and sponge filter at $50 to $300, algae wafers and blanched greens at $60 to $120, and plants plus a hide at $30 to $150. A first-aid and water-test kit is about $30 to $50. The whole first shop, counted the way this page's supply list counts it, falls between $290 and $980.

Thin bellies on arrival are a feeding emergency. Call a veterinarian who treats freshwater fish if the group arrives pinched, and keep them in a quarantine bucket with algae on a rock until they are eating in front of you.

A month-by-month start for otocinclus

Otocinclus are suckermouth catfish about 1 to 2 inches long, with a lifespan around 3 to 5 years when the tank is already mature. Ten gallons is the smallest volume on the species card, and only if algae and biofilm are already growing. The fish are peaceful. The intermediate care rating is about starvation. A new aquarium wiped clean and stocked with one oto the week it is filled is a common way to lose the group inside a fortnight.

Set the tank, the light, and the sponge filter before you look at fish. That equipment is the $50 to $300 line on this page. Plants and a piece of wood with a film on it are the $30 to $150 line. Algae wafers and blanched greens are the $60 to $120 line. A water-test kit is about $30 to $50. Counted on this page's own bands, that opening shop still lands between $290 and $980. Most of that money is the tank that has to finish cycling while you still have an empty stand.

Water on the card is 72 to 79°F and pH 6.0 to 7.5. Ammonia stays at 0 ppm and nitrite stays at 0 ppm. Nitrate climbing toward 20 ppm is already high for a group this sensitive. Test before the bags come home, and again the morning after a water change. A cooler, older planted tank beats a brand-new high-tech scape that was scraped the night before.

They graze soft green film and the aufwuchs on aged driftwood. They will not police a dirty tank into balance, and they are a different fish from the Chinese algae eater, which grows larger and behaves differently. If the film is gone, feed blanched zucchini, algae wafers, and a gel food directly, and keep doing it every day. A thin oto with a sucked-in belly is already in trouble. Buy round bellies only. Leave the pinched ones at the shop.

Buy a group. One oto in a show tank hides, stops feeding where you can see it, and fails. Quarantine the group in a mature holding tank that already has biofilm, on a rock or a leaf with algae, until they are eating in front of you. A long trip plus a bare dealer tank is why so many die in the first month. Add them to a display only after the holding tank has shown round waists for several days.

Week one at home is dim light, no new tankmates, and a vegetable clipped near the wood. Watch the glass in the evening. Otos that patrol the film are the ones to keep. Otos that hover at the surface or lie on the sand with hollow bellies need a freshwater veterinarian who will see fish, and they need that call while they are still in the quarantine bucket. Home remedies and a hotter heater do not replace food that is already growing.

Common first-month mistakes are a cycling tank, a lone fish, a menu of flakes, and a scrape of every leaf the day before purchase. The startup list on this page is the antidote: pay for the planted tank first, confirm 72 to 79°F and pH 6.0 to 7.5, and put the veterinarian's number on the stand before the bags are opened. The $290 to $980 shop is cheaper than replacing a group that starved in a spotless aquarium.

Transport them home in a dark box and float the bags only long enough to match temperature. A long drip of display water into a bag of stressed wild-caught fish adds ammonia from the bag to the stress. Move them into the quarantine tank that already has algae, keep the light low, and skip a big water change on arrival day. The next morning, test ammonia and nitrite, then offer zucchini where you can see who is eating. A group that is still round on day three can join the display. A group that hollowed out overnight needs the fish veterinarian the same day, with the water numbers written down.
