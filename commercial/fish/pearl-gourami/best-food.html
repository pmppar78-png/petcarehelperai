What to feed this Pearl Gourami

Pearl gouramis, Trichopodus leerii, are larger labyrinth fish, around 4 to 5 inches, and need about 30 gallons for a small group. They eat insects, insect larvae, and some plant matter. A pellet or flake, frozen brine shrimp or bloodworms, and a vegetable flake or blanched greens cover the week. Two meals a day suit adults. They feed slowly at the surface and midwater and lose food to barbs.

The pearl spots and the orange throat on males look their best when the fish is eating and the water is clean. A pearl gourami that fades, folds its fins, and shows a hollow chest is underfed or unwell. Judge a pearl gourami by whether it eats the pellet and by the shape of the belly. They can live a fairly long time. Keep the same omnivore menu as they age, and reduce the amount only if the belly starts to sag into a real bulge.

Males build bubble nests and may skip a meal. Return to the normal pellet afterward. Fry need infusoria and then brine shrimp. Soak pellets if a fish gulps and lists. The trip to the surface for labyrinth air is normal. A fish that hangs there and breathes hard for hours is in trouble with the water or the gills. Check ammonia before you offer a richer food.
