What to feed

Frontosa, Cyphotilapia frontosa, are deep-water predators from Lake Tanganyika. Adults pass 12 inches and need 75 gallons at a minimum, often much more for a group, with open sand and rock. In the lake they hunt at dusk. In the tank they do well on a large cichlid pellet, krill, prawn, and earthworms, offered once a day or even every other day for a grown fish. Three heavy meals will foul the tank and can bloat a fish built for a large, infrequent prey item.

Feed at lights-out, when frontosa actually hunt. A pellet thrown in at noon may be ignored and then rot. Juveniles eat more often, smaller pellets, while they grow into the hump. The hump is normal in males. A hard swollen belly on a fish that will not eat is the separate problem. A swollen, hard belly with a fish that will not eat is a different problem. Skip feeder fish. They bring parasites, and a frontosa does not need them to thrive.

These cichlids are slow and can be outcompeted if you house them with fast mbuna. Feed the frontosa at their end of the tank. A fish with a thick shoulder and a calm hover over the sand is eating. One that stays pale in a cave and ignores krill needs a water check and a look at whether another fish is the real diner.
