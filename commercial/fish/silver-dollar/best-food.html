Feeding Guidelines for Silver Dollar Fish

Silver dollars are herbivores and they will strip a planted tank. Feed spirulina flakes, a vegetable pellet, and blanched greens such as spinach or zucchini, twice a day. Meat is a side at most. Give enough plant matter that they leave each other alone. A carnivore pellet as the main food is the wrong menu for a fish built to graze.

What to feed this Silver Dollar

Silver dollars, Metynnis and related genera, are schooling characins that eat plants. A group of five or more needs 75 gallons or larger. Blanched lettuce, spinach, zucchini, and herbivore pellets should be the normal meal, once or twice a day. They will strip a planted tank. If you want plants, feed the greens first and still expect damage. A carnivore pellet is the wrong staple for a fish built to crush seeds and leaves.

They are nervous and need the school. A single silver dollar hides and eats poorly. A fish with a hollow flank in a school that looks round is being shut out or is sick. Spread the vegetables so more than one fish can graze. Remove the leaves before they turn to slime. These fish produce a lot of waste on the diet they actually need.

Juveniles eat the same greens in smaller pieces, plus a fine herbivore crumb. Adults can live a long time. Older silver dollars do not switch to a meaty food. A little frozen brine shrimp is only a side. The belly should stay deep without looking stuffed. A pinecone swell is dropsy and needs a water check the same day. Color, the silver scale, looks dull when the water is dirty, which happens if the lettuce is left to rot.
