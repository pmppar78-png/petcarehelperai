What to feed

The chromis on this page are green chromis, Chromis viridis, sold as blue-green or green. They are planktivores that hover in a school above the rock. Give them 30 gallons or more and feed a small marine pellet plus frozen mysis or brine shrimp twice a day, scattered so the whole group gets some. They will ignore a clip of nori that a tang is raiding underneath them.

A green chromis school eats in the open. If your fish only dart out and then dive into the rock, something is hunting them or they are unwell. A pinched belly on a chromis is easy to see against the light. That fish needs a targeted cloud of mysis. Do not assume the pellet you poured in at the front reached the back of the school.

They are reef safe and they are not algae control. A diet of leftover film will starve them. Pull uneaten frozen food the same day. Ich shows as white spots and rapid scratching and spreads in a stressed school. Food is supportive. The white spots need a disease plan of their own. A chromis that eats, holds a tight school, and keeps a silver-green flank is on the right menu.

Chromis viridis feeds on plankton in the open water above a reef, and a school in a 30-gallon tank needs small pellets and frozen mysis or brine shrimp scattered through the water column twice a day. They will spit a pellet that is too big, so choose a fine marine pellet and watch that it is swallowed. A chromis that peels away from the school, turns brown, and shows a pinched belly is the one missing food or the one getting sick. Keep at least six if the filter can handle them. A pair hides and eats poorly. Nori clipped for a tang does not feed these fish. Pull uneaten mysis the same day. A green or blue-green chromis that holds its place in the school and keeps a filled flank is on the right diet for this page.
