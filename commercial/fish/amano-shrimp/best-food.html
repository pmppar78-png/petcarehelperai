What to feed

Amano shrimp, Caridina multidentata, come from streams in Japan and Taiwan and spend the day scraping biofilm and algae. A planted tank of at least 10 gallons, already running long enough to grow that film, is the setup they work in. Add sinking shrimp pellets and a clip of blanched spinach so the colony still eats after the glass looks clean. Their larvae need saltwater to develop, so a freshwater tank will not produce the next generation, but the adults still need a daily meal.

A failed molt is the health sign to watch. The shrimp looks half out of an old shell, or it dies with a white ring behind the head, when minerals or a sudden parameter swing interrupt the shed. Offer the pellet in the evening and take out what is left the next day so it does not rot under the moss. Amanos will eat leftover fish food, and they will also eat a soft leaf, but biofilm and algae are the wild diet.

A hungry Amano climbs every leaf and then sits still once the spinach is in the water. Overfeeding shows up as a cloudy film and rising nitrate. Amano shrimp do not get usefully fatter from the extra pellet. Keep the portions small and steady. Older Amanos eat the same pellets and greens in smaller pieces. They do not switch to a different formula with age.

Caridina multidentata grazes all day, and a colony in a 10-gallon planted tank can clear soft algae and still need the sinking pellet and the spinach clip. Drop the pellet where the shrimp already walk, often on moss or a leaf, in the evening when fish are less interested. Blanched spinach can stay on a clip overnight. Take it off in the morning if it is ragged, and sooner if it is starting to slime. Females carrying eggs still graze. The eggs hatch into larvae that need saltwater, so do not expect babies in a freshwater tank, and do not change the adult diet because of that. A shrimp that fails a molt, stuck between the old shell and the new one, is a water and mineral problem. Keep feeding the shrimp that are still grazing, and fix the water before the next shed.

Known for their translucent bodies adorned with distinctive dots and dashes, Amano Shrimp are hardy, long-lived, and peaceful additions to community tanks.

They will not erase a tank that is already choked with black beard algae in a weekend, and they will starve in a spotless tank where nobody feeds them.
