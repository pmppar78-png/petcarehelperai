Betta Fish Nutritional Profile

Every Betta Fish has nutritional demands driven by its 5 gallons (19 liters) build, aggressive (males toward males) energy, and expected 3-5 years lifespan. Betta fish with moderate exercise demands need a caloric intake carefully calibrated to prevent both underweight and overweight conditions.

What to feed

Bettas, Betta splendens, are surface feeders from shallow, warm water. A single male needs about 5 gallons with a heater and a lid. The useful foods are a betta pellet and frozen or freeze-dried items such as brine shrimp and bloodworms, a few bites once or twice a day. A betta's stomach is about the size of the eye. A pinch that floats for an hour is too much. Insects are the wild diet. A varied pellet plus brine is the practical version.

Dry food that is gulped at the surface can bring on constipation and a fish that lists. Soak the pellet, and use daphnia as a softer meal when a betta looks bloated and stops eating. Peas are a poor fit for a carnivore this small. Fast a swollen fish for a day or two and then offer a soaked pellet or a brine shrimp. Fin rot and ich are water and temperature problems that show up as frayed edges or white spots. Food will not cure them, and a fish that will not eat needs the water checked first.

Females and short-finned fish eat the same foods. Betta fry need infusoria first, and newly hatched brine shrimp once they can take them. The adult pellet is too large for them. Older bettas eat the same pellet and brine, sometimes less eagerly. They do not switch to a senior formula. A betta that flares and then eats, with a gently rounded belly and intact fins, is on a workable plan.

A betta's stomach is small, about the size of its eye, so a few soaked pellets or a couple of brine shrimp once or twice a day are a full meal in a 5-gallon heated tank. Bloodworms are a treat a few times a week. The everyday meal is the soaked pellet or a brine shrimp, because a betta stuffed on bloodworms lists and stops eating. Soak dry pellets so the fish does not gulp air at the surface. If the belly is already swollen, skip a day and then offer one brine shrimp or a soaked pellet. Fin rot and white spots are water and temperature problems. Test the tank and warm it into the high 70s Fahrenheit before you look for a new food. Females and short-finned bettas eat the same way. A betta that flares, eats, and keeps a slight belly curve is fed. Older bettas often eat more slowly and still use the same pellet and brine.
