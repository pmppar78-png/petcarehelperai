What to feed

Cherry shrimp, Neocaridina davidi, graze biofilm, algae film, and leftover food in a planted tank. Five gallons can hold a small colony if the tank is mature. Ten gallons is an easier size for a group that is breeding. Sinking shrimp pellets and a bit of blanched spinach or nettle cover the days when the glass has been wiped. They breed in freshwater, and the tiny shrimplets eat the same film the adults do, so a bare tank with only a big pellet starves the young.

Watch molts. A cherry that dies stuck in a split shell, or shows a stark white ring around the body, missed the mineral balance or was stressed by a sudden water change. Powder the pellet so shrimplets can pick at the crumbs. Pull large uneaten chunks the same day. Cherries also eat a dead leaf and a soft algae patch. A large cherry shrimp colony still needs biofilm and a sinking pellet, because leftover fish flakes run out.

Color fades when the diet is only leftover flake and the water is neglected. A varied biofilm, a weekly spinach clip, and stable parameters keep the red. Feed lightly. A colony that leaves a full pellet overnight is already getting enough from the tank. Berried females still graze. They do not need a separate breeding chow.
