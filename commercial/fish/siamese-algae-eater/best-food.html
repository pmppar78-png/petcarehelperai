What to feed this Siamese Algae Eater

Siamese algae eaters, Crossocheilus oblongus, are stream fish that graze algae, including some of the red and black brush algae other fish ignore, and they also eat animal foods. Adults reach about 6 inches and need 20 gallons or more, with more room for a group. A sinking wafer, blanched zucchini, and frozen bloodworms or brine shrimp round out the grazing. A fish kept in a spotless tank with no extra food gets thin and then aggressive.

They are often confused with Chinese algae eaters, which become large and rough on tankmates. The Siamese fish has a clear black stripe that continues into the tail. Feed the group where they can all reach the wafer. One piece hidden in a cave feeds only the boldest fish. They do best in a small group, and a lone adult may pick at other fish once the algae is gone.

Offer vegetable matter every day and a meaty frozen food a few times a week. Pull leftovers. A Siamese algae eater with a flat belly and frayed fins is usually underfed or living in poor water, and the two problems arrive together because a hungry fish is often in a tank that was over-cleaned and under-maintained.
