What to feed

Green terrors, Andinoacara rivulatus, are large South American cichlids that reach 10 inches or more and need 75 gallons or more. They eat insects, crustaceans, and small fish in the wild. A cichlid pellet, earthworms, and krill or prawn are the tank menu. Two meals a day suit a juvenile. A grown terror can do well on one substantial meal. Feeder fish are unnecessary and risky.

They are strong feeders and will take food meant for every other fish in the tank. Feed them last, at their end, or house them alone. A green terror with a hollow belly is rare unless it is sick or being bullied, which is also rare. The common error is the opposite: a stuffed fish and a fouled tank. Remove uneaten worms.

Color, the iridescent scales and the spots, holds on a varied pellet and krill diet. A fish that goes dark and stops eating, with a swollen vent, needs the feeding stopped and the water checked. They will sample plants and rip them up. Do not count the plants as the vegetable portion. A little blanched pea is optional. The staple is animal-based pellets and worms.
