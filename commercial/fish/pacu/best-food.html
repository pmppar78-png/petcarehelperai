What to feed

Pacu, Colossoma and Piaractus, are the vegetarian cousins of piranhas, and they become huge. An adult needs 300 gallons or more. The wild diet is fruit, nuts, and plant matter that falls into the river, plus some animal food. Feed greens, pellets made for herbivorous giants, blanched vegetables, and the occasional prepared omnivore food. A carnivore pellet as the only food is the wrong shape for this fish.

They will eat aquarium plants, fingers of soft fruit, and anything that hits the surface. Grapes and similar soft fruits can be an occasional treat, cut up. The daily meal stays the herbivore pellet and the greens. Seeds and hard pits are a choking risk. Two meals a day for a juvenile, one or two for a huge adult, with the understanding that one adult can eat a surprising volume and then produce the matching waste.

A pacu that shreds every plant and still looks hollow needs more vegetable pellet and more greens. These fish are not efficient predators. A sunken belly and listlessness in a fish this large is a crisis of water quality as often as hunger, because the bioload hides the problem. Feed a measured ration and change the water. Older pacu eat the same greens and pellets. Growth slows. The appetite often does not.
