What to feed this Ghost Shrimp

Ghost shrimp in the aquarium trade are usually Palaemonetes, small scavengers that eat leftover flakes, soft algae, and a sinking pellet. A tank of 5 gallons or more with hiding spots suits a few of them. They are hardy omnivores, and they will take a frozen bloodworm or a bit of blanched vegetable along with the film on the glass.

They also eat each other when one is soft from a molt, so offer a pellet on those nights and give the soft shrimp a plant thicket. A stalled molt or a shrimp dead in a half-off shell points back to water quality and minerals. Pull uneaten meaty food the same day. Ghost shrimp are often sold as feeders, and a thin, pale one from that system needs several small meals before it looks clear and active.

They will not keep a planted tank free of algae by themselves. Biofilm and a pinch of pellet are the real diet. A ghost shrimp that sits in the open and fans food with its feet is eating. One that stays jammed in a corner and turns opaque may be about to shed, or it may be failing the shed.
