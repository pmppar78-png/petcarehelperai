Feeding Guidelines for

German blue rams, Mikrogeophagus ramirezi, take small foods: a micro pellet, crushed flake, and frozen baby brine, twice a day. A big cichlid stick is the wrong size. They fade when the water is cool or dirty, so feed lightly and test ammonia if they ignore a meal they ate yesterday. Warm, clean water is part of getting them to eat.

Best for Transitioning German Blue Ram's Diet

What to feed

German blue rams, Mikrogeophagus ramirezi, are small, warm-water dwarf cichlids. A pair needs about 20 gallons, soft water, and temperatures often held in the low 80s Fahrenheit. They eat small invertebrates. A pellet they can chew, frozen daphnia, cyclops, and brine shrimp put more condition on a ram than flakes alone. Two or three tiny meals a day suit a fish with a fast metabolism and a short life, often only two or three years in the trade lines.

The belly tells the truth. A ram that looks head-heavy, with a pinched gut and faded neon colors, is starving or sick. Many arrive that way from wholesale. Start with live or frozen baby brine and daphnia before you insist on a pellet. Extra frozen cubes, wafers, and peas dumped into the tank are what usually add the weight on the wrong animals and foul the water. Feed the ram, then stop.

These fish crash when the water is dirty or cool. A ram that gasps and refuses daphnia needs a water test and a temperature check the same day. The diet will not fix ammonia. Once the fish is eating, rotate daphnia, cyclops, and brine shrimp through the week. Parents guarding fry still need a small feeding at the cave. Fry eat infusoria and then newly hatched brine.

A German blue ram that holds color, swims out for cyclops, and keeps a gently rounded belly is on a workable plan. Keep portions small. This species is often overfed in the hour after it finally starts eating, and the leftover food is what knocks the tank back.

Mikrogeophagus ramirezi comes from warm, slow tributaries of the Orinoco basin, and the electric-blue strain in shops is a farmed line with the same small mouth. Hold the tank in the low 80s Fahrenheit and feed three tiny meals on the days the fish is still thin from shipping. Newly hatched brine shrimp and cyclops reach that mouth more reliably than a community flake. A pair guarding a slate or a flat stone will eat if you place the food at the entrance. Fry are on a different schedule: infusoria first, then the same brine shrimp the adults are already getting, in a separate pinch so the parents do not take all of it. A ram that colors up, flares at the glass during feeding, and keeps a soft rounded belly is through the hard part. Keep the water changes steady, because this species loses its appetite as soon as the tank goes stale.
