Feeding Guidelines for

Neon tetras need tiny food: crushed flake, a micro pellet, and baby brine shrimp, once or twice a day in a pinch the school finishes at once. A community pellet sized for a gourami sinks past them. They are fragile in a brand-new tank, so pair the small meals with a test for ammonia. Overfeeding a neon school is how a cycled tank slips backward.

What to feed

Neon tetras, Paracheirodon innesi, are small schoolers from South American blackwater. A group belongs in about 10 gallons or more, with more fish and more volume if you want a real school. They eat tiny invertebrates and insect matter. Crushed flake, micro pellets, frozen cyclops, and baby brine shrimp match a 1.5-inch mouth. Two small meals a day cover a neon school. A whole community wafer is irrelevant to them.

Neons are often lost to a combination of bad water, a too-small school, and a disease that shows as a pale patch or a wasting body. A neon that separates, loses the blue line, and gets a hollow belly needs the water checked. More flake will not fix ammonia. Feed the school a pinch it finishes. Leftover powder rots in a small tank.

They do best in a group of six or more. A single neon rarely eats confidently. Color is genetic and looks stronger in clean, tannin-stained water than it does from a color pellet. Keep the menu small and varied. Older neons eat the same micro foods. They are short-lived compared with cichlids, often under five years, so a steady tiny ration matters more than a life-stage formula.
