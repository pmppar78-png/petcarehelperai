Feeding Guidelines for Electric Yellow Cichlid (Labidochromis)

Electric yellows, Labidochromis caeruleus, are mbuna and they graze. Feed a spirulina flake or a vegetable pellet in small meals two or three times a day, plus blanched zucchini. A high-protein, fatty diet is associated with Malawi bloat. Keep mammal meat and a beef-heart habit out of this tank. Offer a little food to pick through the day. One huge dumping is how the water fouls.

What to feed

Electric yellow labs, Labidochromis caeruleus, are mbuna from Lake Malawi. They reach about 4 to 5 inches and belong in a group in 55 gallons or more, with rock piles. In the lake they graze aufwuchs, which is algae plus the tiny animals in it. A spirulina flake or pellet, a veggie-based cichlid food, and only an occasional meaty item match that. Bloodworms and beefheart, fed as the staple, are the usual story behind a lab that bloats. Bloat in these fish is a swollen belly, lifting scales, and a fish that stops eating. It is an emergency, and the usual history is too much protein and too little vegetable matter.

Feed once or twice a day, only as much as the group cleans up quickly, and do not leave a pile in the rocks. Mbuna keep eating if the food is there. A lab with a gently rounded belly and bright yellow color is in the right range. A fish that hides, goes dark, and swells needs the feeding stopped and the water checked the same day.

They will graze the rocks between meals if any film exists. A sterile pile of boiled rock will not replace the pellet. Mix males and females the way a Malawi tank usually does, and feed the group together so one dominant fish does not take every spirulina wafer. Older labs eat the same vegetable-based foods. They do not graduate to a meaty senior formula.

A meatier food does nothing extra for a lab that has simply grown.
