What to feed

Discus, Symphysodon, are round cichlids from warm Amazon waters. A group needs about 55 gallons, bare or planted, held near the mid-80s Fahrenheit. Wild fish eat insects, insect larvae, and aufwuchs. In the tank, a quality discus granule, frozen bloodworms, brine shrimp, and the occasional finely chopped prawn are the useful foods. Beefheart was a hatchery habit. It is messy, and a prepared granule with a varied frozen side is a cleaner staple.

Discus feed best when the group feeds together, three to five small meals a day for growing juveniles and two or three for adults. A fish that hangs off to the side and refuses the granule while the others eat is the one to watch. Dark color, clamped fins, and a hollow belly travel together in this species, often with a parasite or with water that has drifted. Pull uneaten food promptly. Discus live in warm water with a heavy bioload, and leftovers turn into ammonia fast.

Parents produce a slime coat the fry pick at. The adults still need their own meals once the fry are taking brine shrimp. Offer greens only as a minor side. A discus needs warm water and a discus granule. A herbivore pellet made for silver dollars is the wrong staple. Soak dry granules if a fish gulps air and lists afterward.
