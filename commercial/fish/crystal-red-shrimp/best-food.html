Feeding Guidelines for Crystal Red Shrimp (CRS)

Crystal red shrimp eat biofilm, a shrimp pellet, and blanched spinach or Indian almond leaf. Offer a small amount daily and remove what they ignore. Copper and most fish medicines kill them. A dirty pile of uneaten wafer spikes ammonia in a small tank and takes the colony with it. Feed lightly and let them graze the leaves.

What to feed

Crystal red shrimp are a Caridina cantonensis line that needs soft, acidic water and a mature tank of about 10 gallons or more. They eat biofilm and fine algae, plus a small share of sinking shrimp food and a leaf such as blanched spinach or Indian almond. Bee shrimp starve when a new setup is wiped clean and the only food is a big community pellet the fish finish first.

This line is sensitive around molts. A failed shed after a large water change is the usual loss, so keep parameters steady and offer a mineral source the way the colony already has it, such as the buffering substrate the tank was built on. Feed a pinch the colony can finish. Uneaten food wrecks the very water these shrimp need. Young crystals eat the same film. Crush the pellet.

A crystal that stays active on the moss and completes a clean molt is on a workable diet. One that hides, stops grazing, and then dies mid-shed needs a water check before the ration is increased. Do not switch them onto a high-protein carnivore stick. Their wild feeding is scraping, with only a little animal matter.
