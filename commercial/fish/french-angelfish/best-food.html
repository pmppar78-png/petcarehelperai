Feeding Guidelines for

French angelfish, Pomacanthus paru, need marine algae and meaty foods both. Clip dried seaweed in the tank daily, and offer mysis, chopped clam, or a marine angel preparation once or twice a day. A meat-only diet starves the grazing habit. They are large marine fish. Pull leftovers off the rock so shrimp does not rot against the corals.

Best for Transitioning French Angelfish's Diet

What to feed

French angelfish, Pomacanthus paru, are large marine angelfish. Adults need a very large tank, well beyond a standard 75, because they reach a foot or more. Juveniles are dark with yellow bands and pick at algae and sponges. Adults need sponge-based angel foods, frozen mysis, and algae or nori, offered often. Two or three small meals a day match a fish that grazes. A single handful of mysis is a poor imitation of that.

They will nip corals and clam mantles, especially if the prepared food is missing. Feed the angel a full algae and sponge menu so it has a meal of its own. A French angel that thins behind the head and ignores nori may still be learning prepared food. Offer sponge angel formula and mysis together, several times a day, until the belly fills. A fish that stops eating entirely and breathes hard needs the water checked. Large angels produce serious waste once they do eat.

Juveniles accept foods more easily than adults that were wild-caught and are still looking for sponge. Ask how the fish is feeding before it comes home. Pull uneaten mysis and nori. Older French angels eat the same sponge, mysis, and algae menu. The bands fade into the adult pattern as the fish grows. The pattern change comes with growth.

Pomacanthus paru juveniles wear yellow bands and pick constantly, and the adult loses those bands as it passes 6 inches on the way to a foot or more. Both stages need several small offerings a day of sponge-based angelfish food, mysis, and nori. A juvenile in a tank that is already too small for the adult still has to eat, and it will outgrow a 75-gallon tank while the diet stays the same. Wild adults sometimes refuse pellets for a week. Keep the sponge formula and the mysis on a short schedule until the face fills in. These angels nip corals and clam mantles when the clip is empty. Refresh the nori before you blame the fish. A French angel with a deep body and a steady interest in the clip is eating. One that breathes hard and ignores every food needs a water test the same day.
