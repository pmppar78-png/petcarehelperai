Best for Budget-Conscious French Angelfish Owners

A French angelfish is a marine patient. White spots after a stressful move are ich until proven otherwise, and the first tools are a test kit and a quarantine tank. Structure the food spend around dried marine seaweed plus mysis or chopped clam for a French angelfish, offered daily, with meat-only feeding avoided. Once salinity, ammonia, and temperature check out and the fish is still fading, call an aquatic veterinarian who sees marine fish. No ordinary pet policy covers Pomacanthus. Keep an emergency fund for copper-free treatment decisions and the exam.

The habits that matter are daily salinity checks, a seaweed clip that is actually refilled, and a quarantine tank that is already mixed and heated before the angel is purchased.

Hidden Costs Most French Angelfish Owners Overlook

Pomacanthus paru grows toward 15 inches in salt water. The repeating costs are synthetic salt, a refractometer, test kits for salinity, alkalinity, ammonia, nitrite, and nitrate, and the media or collection cup in a protein skimmer sized for a tank of about 180 to 250 gallons. Juveniles graze algae and also need mysis and a sponge-based food.

Dried seaweed on a clip is a regular purchase. A quarantine tank with salt water and a heater is where a new angel stays, because marine ich shows up after the fish is already home. Plan on the electricity for that volume and on replacement powerheads. A freshwater gravel vacuum and a bottle of dechlorinator do not run this system.
