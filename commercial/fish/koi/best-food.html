The Koi's evolutionary background directly influences modern dietary needs. What to feed this Koi

Koi are pond carp, Cyprinus rubrofuscus lines, that grow past 2 feet in a proper pond. A 20-gallon tank cannot hold a koi. The diet changes with temperature. In warm summer water they eat a staple growth pellet made for koi, plus vegetables and the insects they find. In cool water, a wheat-germ pellet is easier to digest. When the pond is very cold, around 50 Fahrenheit and below, digestion nearly stops and feeding should stop with it. Feeding a summer pellet into cold water is how koi sicken.

Two or three small meals on warm days beat one dump that sinks into the muck. Koi beg at the surface. That surface rush is trained. Measure the pellet for the season and the body, then stop. A fish with a sunken belly in summer is underfed or sick. A fish with a swollen belly and red sores needs the water checked and a fish veterinarian. Hold the pellets until you know what you are treating. Color foods with carotenoids can support reds. They do not replace the staple pellet.

Remove uneaten food. Pond filters are not magic. Fruit and silkworms are occasional. Bread is a poor meal. Older koi eat the same seasonal pellets, and a large old fish can still foul a small pond if the ration is a tourist's handful every hour. Measure the food. A koi that eats, flashes its color, and returns to cruising is fed. One that isolates and clamps needs the water tested the same day.
