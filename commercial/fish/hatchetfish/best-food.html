What to feed

Hatchetfish, Carnegiella and Gasteropelecus, are surface fish with a deep chest and a straight back. A school needs 20 gallons or more, a tight lid, and calm surface water. They eat insects that fall onto the water. Floating flake, small floating pellets, and fruit flies or frozen brine shrimp offered at the surface are the foods that match. A sinking pellet is a meal they often miss.

They jump. An open tank loses the fish you just fed. They also starve in a busy community if faster fish take the surface flake. Feed the hatchets and watch the surface. A hatchet with a hollow chest and a faded silver side is missing meals. The body shape already looks thin, so compare fish to each other.

Keep at least six. A single hatchet hides at the glass and eats little. They do not need greens as a staple and they do not feed on the gravel. Pull floating food that is still there after a few minutes so it does not soak and sink into a rotten pile. Older hatchets eat the same surface foods. A fish that lists or gasps needs the water and the temperature checked. The deep chest is the normal shape of a hatchetfish. That deep keel is normal.
