Feeding Guidelines for Celestial Pearl Danio (Galaxy Rasbora)

Celestial pearl danios, Danio margaritatus, need micro food: powdered fry food, crushed flake, and baby brine shrimp. Offer a pinch twice a day. They ignore a community pellet sized for a danio three times their length. Keep the light low and the portions tiny so the shy fish at the bottom actually eat.

What to feed

Celestial pearl danios, Danio margaritatus, are tiny fish from small pools in Myanmar. A school belongs in 10 gallons or more with plants and low competition. Crushed flake, a micro pellet, and frozen baby brine shrimp or cyclops are the foods that fit. Two small meals a day cover a celestial pearl danio school. A community wafer meant for a pleco is irrelevant to them, and a fast zebra danio in the same tank will take the pinch before a CPD leaves the plants.

They feed shyly. Watch the plants, where this school actually feeds. A CPD with a hollow belly and faded pearly spots is starving in a tank that looks well fed from the front. Move the food into their cover or remove the faster fish. They appreciate live or frozen foods more than a dry flake, especially when they are settling in.

Fry are tiny and need infusoria and then newly hatched brine. Adults spawn in plants and do not guard a meal for the fry. Remove uneaten cyclops. These danios are poor at competing and excellent at disappearing. Feed the fish you can see in the leaves, and keep the ration matched to that small mouth.

This is particularly true for Celestial Pearl Danio (Galaxy Rasbora), where the animals themselves, along with any tank mates, plants, and established biological filtration, represent a significant investment of both money and time that is worth protecting with reliable equipment.

A tank of 10 gallons or more, densely planted, is the minimum that lets a school behave like itself.

In a bare tank with fast roommates they simply vanish into a corner and stop showing the spots you bought them for.
