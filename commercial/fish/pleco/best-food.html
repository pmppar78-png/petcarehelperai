What to feed

This page is about the common pleco, the large Hypostomus-type fish that reaches well over a foot. It is a different animal from a bristlenose. Adults need 75 to 100 gallons and produce a lot of waste. Feed sinking algae wafers, blanched zucchini or cucumber, and a meaty food such as bloodworms or a bit of prawn through the week. A shop juvenile nibbling algae is not showing you the adult diet.

Put the food in after dark, near the cave the pleco uses. Daytime feeding mostly feeds the other fish. Check the belly by looking from above: a flat or concave underside means the wafers never arrived. Remove uneaten vegetable in the morning. A rotting slice under a log spikes nitrate.

Common plecos rasp wood and will eat soft plants when they are hungry. Give the wood and the wafers so the pleco can leave the plants alone. Growth from a 3-inch juvenile to a tank-busting adult is the normal outcome on a good diet. Stunting the fish in a small box does not keep it small in a healthy way.

A common pleco sold at 3 inches is the same Hypostomus that later needs a tank of 75 to 100 gallons and a filter to match the waste. Feed a sinking algae wafer and a slice of blanched zucchini at night, and add a meaty food such as bloodworms or a bit of prawn twice a week once the fish is longer than your hand. Put the food under the driftwood the pleco already uses. A daytime feeding in the open is eaten by other fish, and the pleco gets thin while the tank looks well fed. Check the underside. A concave belly means the night feeding missed. Remove the zucchini in the morning. A common pleco will also rasp wood, so leave a real piece in the tank, and still provide the wafer. The adult appetite is the one to plan for.
