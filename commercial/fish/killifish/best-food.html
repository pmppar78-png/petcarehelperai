What to feed

Killifish are a wide group. Many of the ones in shops, such as Fundulopanchax and Aphyosemion, are small insect eaters with big appetites for live and frozen foods. A pair or a trio often lives in 5 to 10 gallons, with a lid. Baby brine shrimp, daphnia, mosquito larvae, and similar frozen or live foods keep the belly rounded in a way flake rarely does. A quality flake can be part of the week once the fish accepts it. Two or three small meals a day suit species that live only a year or two. Annual killies have a short natural life. Feeding them well does not make them permanent.

A killifish that refuses flake and gets thin needs the frozen or live food kept on the menu. Offer the flake alongside brine until it takes both. Males in full color that stay rounded are eating. A fish that fades and shows a knife-edge belly is not. Pull uneaten brine shrimp, baby brine, daphnia, and flake the same day. Small killie tanks foul quickly.

Eggs of annual species may sit in a dry medium for months. Those eggs are waiting out a dry season. The fry, once they hatch, need newly hatched brine shrimp immediately. Adults of bottom-spawning species eat the same insect foods. Do not house tiny killies with fast danios if you want the killies to get the pinch.
