What to feed this Rabbit Snail

Rabbit snails, Tylomelania, come from Sulawesi lakes and need warmer, mineral-rich water. Adults are long and slow. A tank of 20 gallons or more with sand they can burrow in suits them. They eat biofilm, soft algae, leftover food, and vegetables such as blanched zucchini and spinach. A wafer helps, and it has to sit on the sand long enough for a snail that loses every race to a fish.

Feed at night and shield the piece with a small cave or a leaf if fish steal it. A rabbit snail that never fully extends, or that stays buried and loses weight along the shell, is missing meals. They also need calcium. A ridged shell that turns chalky and thin is a warning. They reproduce slowly, often with a single tiny snail, so the adults you bought are the ones you have to feed.

Pull uneaten zucchini before it sours in the sand. These snails produce noticeable waste. A varied film plus two or three vegetable feedings a week matches their pace better than a daily pile. They will not mow a tank of hair algae by themselves.
