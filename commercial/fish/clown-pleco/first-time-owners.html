A clown pleco stays 3 to 4 inches and needs real wood in the tank, so the startup money is a 20 gallon, a piece of driftwood, and the filter, together about $50 to $300, plus vegetables and a wafer the fish will mostly ignore at $60 to $120. Caves and the wood itself can land in the $30 to $150 band. The first-supply total on this page's checklist is $290 to $980. Replacing the wood later is part of feeding the fish.

Have a freshwater veterinarian's number before the bag is opened. A pleco that never touches the wood, or that arrives with a sunken belly, needs that call in the first week while it is still in quarantine.

Wood, water, and the first month for a clown pleco

A clown pleco is a small striped catfish that rasps driftwood. Twenty gallons is a home for one fish. A common pleco grows into a different problem and needs a different tank. The card's water is 73 to 82°F, pH 6.8 to 7.6, ammonia 0 ppm, nitrite 0 ppm, and nitrate under 20 ppm. An uncycled tank will kill this fish on the same schedule it kills any other new catfish.

Buy the wood before the fish. Real driftwood, boiled or aged until it stops dumping a heavy tan into the water, is the meal and the furniture. The fish rasps the wood and digests biofilm and the wood itself. A tank of resin ornaments and a stack of algae wafers is a tank where a clown pleco slowly starves while the glass stays dirty, because algae was never the main job. Blanched zucchini can be a side dish. It does not replace the wood. If the goal is an algae cleaner, an otocinclus or a bristlenose is the different conversation. This fish is a wood chewer that happens to be striped.

The startup money follows the same bands this page already uses for a small freshwater setup. A 20-gallon tank, a filter, and a heater sit in the $50 to $300 line. Wood, plants, and a cave sit in the $30 to $150 line. Wafers and vegetables sit in the $60 to $120 line beside the wood. They stay a side dish. A test kit is about $30 to $50. The first shop, counted the way this checklist counts it, falls between $290 and $980. Most of the welfare is in the wood and the finished cycle. The stripe is just how you find the fish.

Quarantine a new pleco in a bucket or a bare hospital tank that still contains a piece of the same wood. Watch the belly for a week. A sunken belly on arrival is a feeding emergency. Waiting it out as a settling-in phase is how the fish is lost. Call a freshwater veterinarian if the fish never touches the wood. Keep the fish out of the display until it is rasping in front of you. A pleco that only appears at night can still be checked with a dim light an hour after lights-out.

One clown pleco in 20 gallons is the stocking. Adding a second because the stripe looks good crowds a fish that already owns the wood. Tankmates should be peaceful and too large to fit in the pleco's mouth. Leave the driftwood to the pleco. A school of boisterous barbs that pin the pleco in a corner will stop it feeding.

Week one is stable heat, no big water changes that crash the cycle, and the wood left where the fish found it. Moving the decor every day to "see the pleco" is how a shy catfish stops eating. Test ammonia after any new addition. Zero is the only acceptable number on the card.

The first-month mistakes are a cycling tank, a decorative skull in place of wood, a common-pleco feeding chart, and a sunken belly ignored because the fish is "supposed to hide." Have the veterinarian's number before the bag is opened. Put the wood in first. Then buy the pleco that is already rasping at the shop.

New driftwood will stain the water tea-brown. That tannin is ordinary and often welcome. Rinse the wood and keep it. A resin log is the swap that starves the fish. Rinse filter floss if the flow slows, and leave the biological media alone during week one. The cycle is the part you spent the waiting time to grow.
