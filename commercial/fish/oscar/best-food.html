What to feed

Astronotus ocellatus is a South American cichlid that hunts insects, crustaceans, and small fish along flooded river margins. Adults commonly reach 12 to 14 inches, so one Oscar belongs in 75 gallons and a pair needs 125 gallons or more, with a lid that stops a jump. The useful menu is a large cichlid pellet plus earthworms and krill. Feeder goldfish are a bad staple: they carry parasites and the enzyme thiaminase. A grown Oscar does well on one or two meals a day. A juvenile still putting on length can take three smaller meals of the same foods, chopped so the pieces match the mouth.

Lift out anything an Oscar leaves. Prawn shells and pellet crumbs foul a tank this size faster than the filter can clear them. Soak dry pellets if the fish gulps air at the surface and then lists. Color comes from the varied menu, and a fish kept on one feeder stays dull and thin through the shoulders. Hole-in-the-head shows up as small pits on the head and along the lateral line. Dirty water and a narrow diet make those pits worse, so the fix is water changes and a rotation of pellets, worms, and krill.

An Oscar that rushes the glass at every passing shadow is hungry for attention as much as for food. Keep the ration steady anyway. Extra meals pile waste into a tank that already runs warm and heavy. Offer greens only as a side. This species is a carnivore that will sample a pea and then ignore it. Evening is a good second feeding because Oscars stay active after the room lights dim.
