Best for Budget-Conscious Fiddler Crab Owners

Fiddler crabs need brackish water and a dry shore. A crab in fully fresh or fully marine water is a husbandry failure before it is a patient. Check salinity and ammonia, and look at the shell, before you seek a vet. Structure the food spend around sinking pellets, a pinch of blanched vegetable, and the occasional bloodworm for a brackish fiddler that also needs a dry shore. An aquatic or invertebrate veterinarian is the person to call if the water is right and the crab is still down. Pet insurance does not exist for a fiddler. The emergency fund is the salinity test and the consult.

The habits that matter are checking specific gravity, keeping sand damp on the land side, and confirming the crab can get fully out of the water whenever it wants.

Hidden Costs Most Fiddler Crab Owners Overlook

A fiddler crab's repeating costs are a marine salt mix brought to a specific gravity around 1.005 to 1.015, a hydrometer or refractometer, and a test kit for ammonia and salinity. The enclosure needs sand deep enough to burrow, a land area the crab can stay on, shallow brackish water, and a lid, because they climb out.

Sinking pellets, a little vegetable, and calcium for the molt are the food bill. A spare container with damp sand and the same brackish water is where a crab molts without being dug up. A heater belongs on the list if the room falls below the mid-70s Fahrenheit. The crab has to leave the water completely and still keep the gill chamber damp.
