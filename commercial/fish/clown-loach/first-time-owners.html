Growing a clown loach past the shop size

Clown loaches are sold at about two inches and kept for a fish that passes a foot and can live 15 to 25 years. The tank on this page is 75 gallons, longer if the stand allows, with caves and driftwood the group can stack in. One striped fish in a 20-gallon planted tank is the wrong purchase. If the shop animal is a striped bottom fish in a two-inch body, ask which species it is before it goes home. A clown loach and a smaller loach do not share a future tank.

The first receipt matches the bands already on this page. The 75-gallon tank, filter, and heater are the $50 to $300 line. Sinking foods and frozen worms are the $60 to $120 line. Caves that stay larger than the growing fish are the $30 to $150 line. Count the first shop at $290 to $980, and expect to spend a large part of it again when the group outgrows the first aquarium. A fish that lives 15 to 25 years will see more than one tank.

They are a group. Three is a bare minimum. Five or more in a long tank is when they pile up and come out in the evening. A single loach hides, eats poorly, and lives a smaller life than the species is built for. Aggression inside the group shows up when the group is too small or when one fish cannot get into the pile. Give more caves than fish.

They click. The sound is a pop from the mouth when they squabble or when they are startled. A clicking loach is communicating. Choking looks like gasping at the surface, which is a different emergency. Learn the sound in week one so a later click stays ordinary, and so a fish that is gasping at the surface gets a different response.

They are ich-prone. A temperature drop during a water change, a long trip, or a tank that runs cool will bring white spots out on a clown loach faster than on the tetras beside them. Match the new water to the tank. Quarantine the group. Watch the waist behind the head for a week before they join a display. Skinny disease leaves a sunken belly on a fish that eats and still thins. A juvenile that arrives thin often never fills out. Leave the cheap thin one at the shop. No home dosing chart fixes a fish that has been wasting for a month.

The diet is meaty and ongoing: sinking pellets, bloodworms, and the occasional snail. People buy them as snail control. A group will reduce a snail boom and then still need pellets every day for 15 to 25 years. Feed after the lights dim, when the pile comes out. Food that only lands in the current at the top never reaches a loach under the wood.

Phone a fish veterinarian the same day if the whole group arrives with red skin, or if a loach stays on its side after the light comes up and will not right itself. Have that number before the bags are opened. The $290 to $980 startup is the tank they can grow in. A two-inch body is only the shop size.
