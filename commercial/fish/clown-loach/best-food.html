What to feed

Clown loaches, Chromobotia macracanthus, are social bottom fish that grow to around a foot and need 75 gallons or more for a group. They eat snails, insect larvae, and worms in the wild. Sinking pellets, bloodworms, and the occasional snail are what keep a large loach fed. A group of clown loaches needs a sinking pellet and worms for as long as you keep them. Snail hunting is only a side effect. A group of juveniles in a small tank will outgrow both the food plan and the box.

Feed in the evening, under the decor, because clown loaches hide and then feed in a pile. A single wafer in the open feeds the other fish. They are prone to ich when stressed by a move or a dirty tank, which shows as white spots and flashing. That is a water and temperature problem as well as a stress problem. A sunken belly on a loach that is flashing needs the ich and the water addressed. Bring the usual worm ration back once the fish is eating.

They live a long time when the tank is big enough. Feed two modest sinking meals a day to juveniles and a steady evening ration to adults. Pull leftovers. A clown loach will also eat small shrimp. Count that as predation. The intended menu is the pellet and the worm.
