What to feed

Dwarf crayfish such as the Mexican dwarf, Cambarellus patzcuarensis, stay small enough for a 5-gallon tank with a lid and plenty of cover. They are omnivores. Sinking pellets, blanched zucchini, and a frozen bloodworm or two are a sound week. They graze biofilm as well. A tank with only floating flakes feeds the crayfish last, if at all.

They molt to grow. A crayfish hidden for a day, then back with a brighter shell, has shed. Leave the old shell so it can eat the minerals. A failed molt leaves the animal stuck, often with the shell split at the joint between tail and body. That is a water-quality and mineral problem, and extra food will not peel the shell off. They may catch a slow shrimp, so plan the tank around that.

Feed a small portion at dusk and remove what is left in the morning. A dwarf crayfish with a full tail and active foraging is eating. One that stays in the cave and gets thin needs the food placed at the entrance. They live only a few years. Older dwarf crayfish take the same pellets and vegetables, more slowly, and still need the shed to succeed.
