Feeding Guidelines for

Gouramis are surface omnivores with a labyrinth organ. Feed a flake or small pellet at the surface twice a day, and add frozen bloodworms or a little blanched vegetable a few times a week. They gulp air, so leave the surface clear of a thick oil film. A constantly running feeder overfills a fish that will eat whenever food appears.

What to feed

Gouramis are labyrinth fish, and the name covers dwarf, honey, pearl, and larger species such as the blue gourami. The tank runs from about 10 gallons for the small species to 55 for the big ones. All of them eat insects and will take a pellet or flake at the surface, with frozen brine shrimp or bloodworms as the richer meal. Two meals a day fit the small species. Larger gouramis eat more, still as two measured meals a day.

They breathe air at the surface. Leave a gap under the lid. A gourami that lists after dry food needs the pellet soaked. A gourami that hangs at the surface all day and refuses food needs a water test. Fry of the bubble-nesting species need infusoria. The adults go back to the same pellet and frozen foods.
