What to feed

Peacock bass, Cichla, are large predatory cichlids. A single adult needs a tank measured in the hundreds of gallons, on the order of 180 gallons and up, with serious filtration. In the wild they eat fish. In a tank they can learn pellets made for big carnivores, plus prawn, krill, and fish fillet. Feeder goldfish are a disease risk and a poor nutrient package. Do not build the diet on them.

Feed a grown peacock bass every other day or once a day, a meal it finishes. Lift out anything it spits. Juveniles eat more often while they grow. A fish that ignores pellets and only lunges at movement needs the pellet paired with a prawn until it accepts the pellet. This conversion matters, because live fish as a forever diet is how keepers import parasites.

A peacock bass with a sunken belly and a refusal to eat is sick or is being kept too cold and too dim to hunt. Check temperature and water before you add more food. The waste from one large meal is enormous. Gravel-vac after feeding if anything remains. These fish jump. A lid is part of keeping the animal you just fed.
