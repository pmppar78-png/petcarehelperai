Feeding Guidelines for

Chili rasboras, Boraras brigittae, are tiny. Powdered food and baby brine shrimp, several small offerings a day, match the mouth. A standard flake is too big, and they lose the race to any faster tankmate. Feed after the lights come up, when they leave the plants. An overfeed you cannot see still produces ammonia in a small tank.

What to feed

Chili rasboras, Boraras brigittae, are tiny red fish from peat swamps. A school needs 5 gallons or more of planted, gentle water, and 10 gallons is easier. Powder food, a crushed flake, and tiny frozen foods such as baby brine shrimp and cyclops fit the mouth. Two very small meals a day are enough. A standard flake is a plate they cannot use. Shake the dust off it or buy a food made for nano fish.

They vanish into plants and lose the feeding to any larger tankmate. A chili that stays pale brown and shows a hollow belly is starving in a tank you thought was fed. Feed into the plants and watch for the red school to flicker out and eat. They are sensitive to a dirty tank. Leftover powder will hurt them faster than it hurts a danio.

Fry are nearly invisible and need infusoria. If you are not raising fry, keep the adult pinch tiny. Color is the signal: a deep red school that moves together is eating. A washed-out school is either stressed by water or missing food. Check both before you increase the ration.
