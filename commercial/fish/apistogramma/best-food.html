Feeding Guidelines for

Apistogramma have small mouths. Feed crushed flake, a micro pellet, and frozen baby brine or cyclops, in two or three tiny meals. A large cichlid stick sinks uneaten. They live in soft, warm water, and a heavy feeding that the filter cannot clear will wipe out a pair faster than a skipped sprinkle.

What to feed

Apistogramma are dwarf cichlids, mostly 2 to 3 inches, from soft South American streams. A pair can live in a 10- to 20-gallon tank with cover and a cave. The mouth is small. Baby brine shrimp, frozen daphnia, and a pellet they can chew are the foods that fit. Flakes sized for big community fish sail past them. Two small meals a day keep a pair in condition. Fry need microworms or newly hatched brine shrimp, and any danio in the tank will eat those fry foods first.

Females guarding a cave still eat, but they may only take food placed at the entrance. A female that gets thin while the male looks round is losing that race. Extra frozen cubes piled in the open water are what usually add the weight on the wrong fish. Target the cave. These dwarfs are sensitive to stale water. A fish that fades and hides, with a pinched gut, needs a water check and a return to small live or frozen foods.

They will sample a crushed flake. Use the crushed flake as a bridge onto the pellet and the frozen foods. A varied week of daphnia, brine, and a micro pellet keeps color in the males. Remove uneaten food the same hour. A 15-gallon tank fouls quickly. Apistogramma are small dwarf cichlids from soft, acidic South American streams, and they pick live or frozen daphnia, brine shrimp, and bloodworms off the bottom.
