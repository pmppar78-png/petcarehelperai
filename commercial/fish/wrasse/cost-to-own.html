Best for Budget-Conscious Wrasse Owners

A wrasse that arrives thin, or that never eats, is a starvation case. Confirm it is swallowing food, and test the marine water, before you medicate the display. Structure the food spend around frozen mysis and finely chopped seafood for a wrasse, once or twice a day, plus a sand bed if the species buries at night. An aquatic veterinarian who sees marine fish is the call when the salinity is right and the fish is still losing muscle. No household policy covers a wrasse. The emergency fund pays for quarantine and the exam.

The habits that keep a wrasse affordable are a tight lid, a daily check that it ate, and quarantine in a separate saltwater tank before it ever sees the display.
