What to feed this Wrasse

Wrasses are a family of carnivores, and the fish on this page need meaty food every day: mysis, brine shrimp, and marine pellets. Algae sheets are a tang's meal. A wrasse that is expected to live on pests alone gets thin once the pests are gone, and then it may pester corals or tankmates. Feed the wrasse twice a day as its own animal. Tank size depends on the species, from small flasher wrasses to fish that need a much longer sand bed to sleep in.

Match the food to the species you bought. A fairy wrasse wants a cloud of mysis in the water column. A Halichoeres wants mysis and a sand bed. A cleaner wrasse is a separate problem and often starves on prepared food. Do not use one pellet plan for all of them. A hollow belly behind the head is the shared warning that the plan is failing.

Wrasses are fast. They will take the food you meant for a firefish. Feed the slow fish first. Pull uneaten mysis the same day. Many wrasses dive into the sand at night. A bare-bottom tank removes that rest, and a stressed wrasse eats poorly. Give the species a place to sleep and a meal that does not depend on pests. Older wrasses eat the same meaty foods. Identify the adult size before you decide the pinch is enough.
