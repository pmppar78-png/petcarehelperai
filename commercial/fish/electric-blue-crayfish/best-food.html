What to feed

The electric blue crayfish, Procambarus alleni, is a burrowing omnivore that reaches several inches and needs about 20 gallons, a lid, and sand or fine gravel it can dig. Feed sinking crayfish or shrimp pellets, blanched greens, and a meaty offering such as bloodworms or a bit of fish a few times a week. Algae film is only a snack.

Molting is the dangerous point. The crayfish may vanish under a cave for a day or two. Leave it alone and leave the cast shell. A stuck shed, with the old skin clinging at the claws or the tail, follows a bad parameter swing or a tank with no minerals. Protein matters for this species more than it does for a nerite, but a diet of only meat fouls the water and is unnecessary.

They will eat plants, snails, and slow tankmates. Feed a regular pellet and a meaty offering through the week so a meal is already in the tank. Pull leftovers the same day. A bright blue crayfish that explores at dusk and finishes a pellet is on track. Faded color and a soft, bent claw can mean a recent molt or a chronically poor diet. Watch whether the next shed comes off clean.
