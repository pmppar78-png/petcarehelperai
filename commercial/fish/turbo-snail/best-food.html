Feeding Guidelines for Turbo Snail

Turbo snails graze film algae and dried seaweed. A new tank with clean glass starves them. Clip nori and leave rock that already has a film, and offer that every day. They are marine snails. Copper and many flatworm treatments kill them. A pellet meant for a clownfish is a weak substitute for the algae they are constantly combing.

What to feed this Turbo Snail

Turbo snails in the trade are marine turban snails kept for algae, often around 2 inches across. They rasp film off rock and glass. A reef that is wiped bare and stocked with a new, dry rock will not feed one. Offer a clip of dried seaweed or an algae wafer pressed against the rock if the surfaces stay clean, and watch that the snail actually visits it. Many starve in tanks that look beautiful and empty.

They can knock over loose frags, and they will wander into a powerhead. None of that replaces the algae. A turbo that sits still for days, smells, and falls out of the shell has died. A living one holds on and leaves a clean swath. Calcium and stable salinity matter for the shell as much as the food does. A pitted shell on a snail that is still roaming needs a look at alkalinity.

One large turbo is a lot of grazing pressure on a small reef. Add a second only when the film keeps up. Remove uneaten seaweed the next day so it does not decay in a corner. These snails do not eat hair algae as reliably as keepers hope, and they do not eat nuisance bubble algae as a job.
