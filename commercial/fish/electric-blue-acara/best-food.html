Feeding Guidelines for Electric Blue Acara

Electric blue acaras are omnivores. A cichlid pellet once or twice a day, plus frozen mysis or bloodworms a few times a week, and a blanched vegetable, is a sound rotation. Skip feeder fish. Skip beef heart as the daily food. Feed what they finish before it sinks into the gravel and rots.

  • Digestive tolerance: Feed a cichlid pellet and frozen or fresh meaty foods.

What to feed this Electric Blue Acara

Electric blue acaras are a color line of Andinoacara, medium cichlids that reach about 6 inches and need 30 gallons or more. They are omnivores with a taste for insects and worms. A cichlid pellet, earthworms, and frozen bloodworms or brine shrimp cover the week. Two meals a day suit an adult. They are calmer than many Central American cichlids and still produce a heavy bioload if you feed them like a larger Oscar.

Offer earthworms as one meal in the week, then go back to the pellet. A fish that gulps three worms and then ignores the pellet is easy to overfeed. The belly should stay rounded. Stringy feces and a hard gut mean the last meal was too large. Pull leftovers. These acaras will also sample a blanched vegetable. Keep that as a side. The staple is the pellet and the worm.

Juveniles need smaller pellets and more frequent meals while they color up. A pale, thin juvenile that hides is usually underfed or in a tank with faster fish. Feed it where it sits. Color in this line is genetic. Food will not paint a dull fish electric, but a poor diet will make a blue fish look grey and hollow. Hole-in-the-head is uncommon if the water stays clean and the menu varies. Watch the head anyway if the tank is neglected.
