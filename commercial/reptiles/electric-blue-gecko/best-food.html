Life-Stage Feeding Guide for Electric Blue Day Gecko (Lygodactylus williamsi)

Electric blue day geckos are tiny nectar-and-insect specialists. Hatchlings need fruit flies and pinhead crickets dusted with calcium, plus a small lick of commercial day-gecko diet. Adults eat small insects several times a week and a prepared nectar or fruit puree replaced daily so it does not ferment. Citrus and baby-food jars are a poor substitute. They are too small for a pinky mouse at any age. A female laying eggs needs reliable calcium and UVB. An older gecko may eat less and dry out faster. Mist, watch the tail for fat stores, and keep the food dishes off hot surfaces. Handle almost never. Stress is how these geckos stop eating.
