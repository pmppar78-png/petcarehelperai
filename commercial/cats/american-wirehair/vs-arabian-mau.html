American Wirehair vs Arabian Mau: Complete Comparison (2026)

The comparison below works through each of those in turn — costs, exercise, grooming, training, health, and lifestyle fit — so the decision rests on lived constraints rather than first impressions.

Both the American Wirehair and the Arabian Mau are well-documented breeds with clear ownership profiles, but the differences that matter for a real household are rarely the ones highlighted in breed marketing. The aim here is to surface the operationally meaningful gaps between the two so the right choice is obvious by the end.

Temperament and Personality Differences

Personality is where American Wirehair and Arabian Mau diverge most clearly. American Wirehair brings an easygoing, affectionate, playful energy to the household, compared to Arabian Mau's affectionate, independent, active disposition. These differences shape every daily interaction. In daily life, this means American Wirehair owners typically experience a cat that leans toward easygoing behavior, while Arabian Mau owners find their cat more inclined toward affectionate tendencies. Neither is an objectively better temperament; the right pick is the one that suits your lifestyle.

Best for Families with Children

Evaluate each breed's interaction style with children. American Wirehair's easygoing nature and Arabian Mau's affectionate temperament each present different dynamics with younger family members.

Health and Lifespan Comparison

American Wirehair has a typical lifespan of 14-18 years, while Arabian Mau lives approximately 12-14 years. Health profiles differ significantly between these cats. American Wirehair is predisposed to Coat-Related Concerns, General Health Concerns, with associated veterinary costs for monitoring and treatment. Arabian Mau faces its own health challenges including breed-specific conditions. American Wirehair has 2 documented predispositions compared to 1 for Arabian Mau, though condition count alone doesn't determine overall health burden—severity and treatability matter more. Insurance considerations differ between the two cats based on these risk profiles. Prospective owners should discuss breed-specific health screening with a veterinarian before making their decision.

Best for Low-Maintenance Health

Neither breed is truly "low maintenance" health-wise, but Arabian Mau's longer lifespan and different condition profile may mean fewer intensive interventions in middle age compared to American Wirehair. That said, consistent preventive care is non-negotiable for both — the real question is which breed's health demands better fit your schedule and budget.

Exercise and Activity Level Differences

Activity requirements differ notably between American Wirehair and Arabian Mau. American Wirehair requires moderate levels of exercise and engagement, while Arabian Mau needs high activity. This difference has major practical implications for daily routines. American Wirehair owners should plan for 30-60 minutes of daily activity, compared to 60-90 minutes for Arabian Mau. Under-exercised cats of either breed develop behavioral issues, but the consequences and management strategies differ.

Grooming and Maintenance Comparison

Daily and periodic maintenance requirements differ between American Wirehair and Arabian Mau. American Wirehair has low (special care) grooming needs, while Arabian Mau requires very low maintenance. Professional grooming costs reflect these differences: American Wirehair owners typically spend $0-$200 annually on grooming, compared to $0-$200 for Arabian Mau. Day-to-day grooming at home includes brushing, bathing, nail trims, and dental care. The time commitment for daily grooming and general habitat maintenance is an important lifestyle consideration. Factor grooming costs and time into your total ownership commitment when deciding between these cats.

Best for Low-Maintenance Owners

If available time is tight, favour the lower-grooming, more-moderate-exercise option; if the household has more capacity, the other rewards that investment. Compare their grooming frequency, exercise minimums, and training requirements side by side — the breed that fits more easily into your existing routine is the practical choice.

Cost of Ownership Comparison

Total ownership costs for American Wirehair versus Arabian Mau differ across several categories. The size difference between American Wirehair (Medium (8-12 lbs)) and Arabian Mau (Medium to Large (8-16 lbs)) significantly impacts costs across food, supplies, and veterinary care. Larger cats generally cost 30-60% more in recurring expenses due to higher food consumption, larger equipment needs, and higher medication dosages. Key cost differentials include: food costs scale with size (Medium (8-12 lbs) vs Medium to Large (8-16 lbs)), grooming costs reflect maintenance requirements (low (special care) vs very low), and veterinary costs correlate with breed-specific health risks. Insurance premiums also differ based on each breed's risk profile. Over a complete lifespan, American Wirehair's 14-18 years expected life and Arabian Mau's 12-14 years expected life mean different total cost horizons—the longer-lived cat accumulates more total costs but potentially offers more years of companionship.

Which Is Right for Your Family?

The decision between American Wirehair and Arabian Mau ultimately depends on matching cat characteristics with your family's specific situation. Choose American Wirehair if your lifestyle accommodates their moderate activity needs, low (special care) grooming requirements, and you're prepared for their easygoing temperament. Choose Arabian Mau if you prefer their high energy level, can manage very low maintenance, and appreciate their affectionate personality. Consult with a veterinarian about any family-specific concerns such as allergies, living arrangements, or compatibility with existing cats. Both American Wirehair and Arabian Mau make wonderful companions for the right owner; the key is honest self-assessment about which breed's needs you can best fulfill throughout their entire lifespan.
