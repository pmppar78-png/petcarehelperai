American Rabbit Nutritional Profile

Every American Rabbit has nutritional demands driven by its Large (9-12 lbs) build, friendly energy, and expected 8-12 years lifespan. Larger small animals like American Rabbit need controlled calorie intake to support their frame without excess weight that stresses joints.

Life Stage Feeding

American rabbits eat hay first, at every age that has teeth. Kits wean onto alfalfa hay and a measured alfalfa-based pellet, then transition. Adults need unlimited grass hay, a small pellet portion, and a limited green salad. Treats and seed mixes are how they get fat and then stop eating when the gut slows. Seniors often need dental radiographs more than a new senior kibble. A rabbit that is losing weight may have molar spurs and cannot chew the hay. Offer the same hay, and see an exotics vet. Change foods slowly. A sudden salad binge causes soft cecotropes and sometimes full stasis. Water is free choice. Carrots are a treat, like candy.

Common Dietary Sensitivities

Some American Rabbits develop food sensitivities that show up as persistent itching, ear infections, loose stools, or vomiting after meals.

Signs Your Is

Look for these signs that your American Rabbit's diet is working: steady weight maintenance without effort, well-formed stools with no persistent gas or loose bowel movements, a coat that stays shiny between grooming sessions, calm and consistent energy levels, and enthusiasm at mealtimes without obsessive food-seeking behavior.

Understanding American Rabbit's Dietary Heritage

An American Rabbit's dietary needs are not arbitrary — they are rooted in what the breed was developed to do. Understanding that context helps you choose food that genuinely matches your American Rabbit's biology rather than defaulting to whatever is popular or heavily advertised.
