Abyssinian Guinea Pig Nutritional Profile

The Abyssinian Guinea Pig has specific dietary requirements shaped by its Medium (1.5-2.5 lbs / 0.7-1.2 kg) build and friendly temperament. Abyssinian Guinea Pig small animals with high - very energetic exercise demands need a caloric intake carefully calibrated to prevent both underweight and overweight conditions. A diet rich in animal-based proteins at 28-35% of total calories fuels Abyssinian Guinea Pig's active lifestyle, with fat content elevated slightly to sustain energy through longer activity sessions.

Life Stage Feeding

Abyssinian guinea pigs grow rosettes, and the diet is still hay. Babies and growing pups do well with alfalfa hay plus a measured vitamin C pellet and greens. Adults should move to unlimited grass hay, a limited pellet that contains vitamin C, and a small daily pile of bell pepper, cilantro, or romaine. The rosettes do not create a special kibble. They do hide skin problems if the coat is dirty, so feeding and grooming both matter. Seniors often need the same hay and a dental check, because overgrown molars stop a pig from eating overnight. Vitamin C stays on the menu at every age. Rabbit food lacks enough of it. Skip yogurt drops and seed sticks.
