What to Look For in Pet Insurance

  • Scope of what is insured: look for plans that name hereditary, congenital, behavioural, and dental illness explicitly in the covered list.
  • Reimbursement percentage: commonly 70%, 80%, or 90%. Higher percentages cost more up front but cushion big years.
  • Per-year payout ceiling: plans range from $5,000 per year to truly unlimited. For a breed prone to surgery, unlimited is usually worth the premium.
  • Deductible mechanics: annual deductibles reset each policy year; per-incident deductibles apply separately to every new condition.
  • Waiting periods and retroactive clauses: most plans exclude anything diagnosed or treated in the 14 days after signup and the 6 months for orthopaedic issues.

The Three Coverage Tiers

  • Accident-only plans: Cover injuries from accidents like broken bones, lacerations, and ingestion of foreign objects.
  • Comprehensive plans: Cover both accidents and illnesses including cancer, infections, and chronic conditions.
  • Wellness plans: Add-on coverage for routine care like routine screenings, dental cleanings, and annual checkups.

When to Upgrade or Switch Californian Rabbit Insurance

Insurance needs for Californian Rabbit evolve across their 5-10 years lifespan, and periodic policy reviews ensure coverage keeps pace. Review your Californian Rabbit's policy annually during renewal, comparing current premiums, deductibles, and coverage limits against competing options. Key triggers for policy changes include: diagnosis of a new chronic condition (verify the current policy covers ongoing treatment), significant premium increases exceeding 15-20% year-over-year, changes in your financial situation affecting deductible tolerance, or your exotic veterinarian recommending specialist care not covered by your current plan. When switching insurers, be aware that conditions diagnosed under the previous policy may be classified as pre-existing by the new provider. For Californian Rabbit with established health histories involving respiratory issues, maintaining continuous coverage with a single insurer often provides the strongest protection against coverage gaps.
