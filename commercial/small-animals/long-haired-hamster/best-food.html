Life-Stage Feeding Guide for Long-Haired (Teddy Bear) Hamster

Long-Haired (Teddy Bear) Hamster nutritional needs shift meaningfully across life stages. Young Long-Haired (Teddy Bear) Hamsters need nutrient-dense food with higher protein and fat to support growth — typically 20-40% more calories per pound than adults. As your Long-Haired (Teddy Bear) Hamster enters the senior phase (roughly the last third of their 2-3 years lifespan), a lower-calorie formula with added joint support becomes appropriate.

Common Dietary Sensitivities in Long-Haired (Teddy Bear) Hamster

Watch for signs that your Long-Haired (Teddy Bear) Hamster's food is not agreeing with them: frequent scratching, red or waxy ears, inconsistent stool quality, or a dull coat. Ideal Portion Control for Long-Haired (Teddy Bear) Hamster

Measured meals beat free-feeding for virtually every Long-Haired (Teddy Bear) Hamster. Use the manufacturer's guidelines as a starting point, then adjust based on your Long-Haired (Teddy Bear) Hamster's body condition — you should be able to feel the ribs without seeing them, and there should be a visible waist from above. Weigh your Long-Haired (Teddy Bear) Hamster monthly and nudge portions up or down by 10-15% if weight trends in the wrong direction. Split daily food into two meals for adults, three to four for growing Long-Haired (Teddy Bear) Hamsters, and keep treats under 10% of total daily calories.

Best for Weight Management

Long Haired Hamsters on a weight programme benefit from a modest, consistent increase in daily activity rather than dramatic exercise bursts.

Signs Your Long-Haired (Teddy Bear) Hamster Is Thriving on Their Diet

Healthy digestion, consistent weight, an alert demeanor, and a coat that looks good without supplements — these are the signs your Long-Haired (Teddy Bear) Hamster is getting what they need from their food.
