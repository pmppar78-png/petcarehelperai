Vet help

This page is educational reference material for preparing questions. A licensed veterinarian examines the animal, makes the diagnosis, and sets the treatment.

These pages help you decide how fast to reach a clinic. They do not diagnose. A veterinarian who can see, hear, and touch the animal outranks any article, including this one.

If breathing is hard, the animal collapsed, or a male cat cannot urinate

Go to an open veterinary emergency hospital. If a toxin is involved and the pet is still stable enough for a phone call, the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center is (888) 426-4435. A consultation fee may apply.

How this section is meant to be used

Start with the checker if you are staring at a sick pet and need a speed: urgent, soon, or monitor. Open a symptom guide when you want the reasoning behind that speed. Breed and species profiles link here from their red-flag sections so the same advice is not copied onto every animal page.

Urgent means an emergency hospital now. Soon means a clinic today or the next open appointment, and an emergency hospital if your regular clinic is closed and the pet is worsening. Monitor means a bright animal with a mild, brief change, plus a promise to call if the picture shifts.

What we will not do

We will not name a dose of a human painkiller. We will not tell you a swelling belly can wait until morning. We will not pretend a telemedicine chat replaces oxygen, surgery, or a urine sample. The AI pet helper on this site is educational in the same way. If the pet is in danger, close the tab and drive.
