Quiet or fluffed bird

A bird who is suddenly quiet is not being polite. Prey species hide weakness. By the time a parakeet, cockatiel, or parrot sits fluffed on the cage floor, the illness is often advanced. Same-day care with an avian veterinarian is the default, not the dramatic option.

Signs that should move you now

Watch the breathing before you watch the personality. A tail that bobs with each breath, an open beak, a click or whistle on the inhale, or a bird who sleeps on the floor of the cage instead of a perch needs an emergency or avian clinic today. Droppings that are fewer, or that change from the bird's normal mix of dark feces and white urates, are an early clue people miss. Take a paper towel sample or a photo.

One closed eye, a tilted head, vomiting (head flicking seed onto the cage), blood anywhere, or a female who is straining to pass an egg are also same-day problems. Egg binding can kill a small hen within hours. Do not press on the belly to "help the egg."

While you travel

Keep the carrier warm, dark, and quiet. A hospital cage can be a small carrier over a heating pad set low, with a towel so the bird cannot touch the pad directly. Offer the usual food and water if the bird can perch safely. Do not force millet into a gasping bird, and do not give water by syringe to a bird who is too weak to swallow. Aspiration is easy.

Skip oils, vinegar, and over-the-counter bird tonics. Bring the food bag, any nonstick pan that overheated recently (fumes from PTFE-coated pans kill birds), and a note about new toys, candles, or cleaners. Airborne toxins are a classic cause of a bird who was fine at noon and is on the floor at dinner.

Finding the right clinic

Not every dog-and-cat clinic stocks avian drugs or knows how to handle a budgie. The Association of Avian Veterinarians keeps a public locator at aav.org. If the only open door is a general emergency hospital, go there and say clearly that the patient is a bird. Waiting for the perfect specialist until morning is the wrong trade when the bird is tail-bobbing tonight.

Quiet bird questions

Why do birds hide illness?

A sick bird who looks obviously weak is an easy target in the wild. Pet birds often look almost normal until they are very ill.

What is a tail bob?

The tail pumps with each breath because breathing takes extra work. That pattern should be seen by an avian veterinarian the same day.

Can I offer a heat lamp and wait?

Gentle warmth can support a bird on the way to the clinic. It is not a substitute for an exam if the bird is fluffed, on the cage floor, or not eating.
