Dog vomiting

This page is educational reference material for preparing questions. A licensed veterinarian examines the animal, makes the diagnosis, and sets the treatment.

A dog who vomits once and then trots off to beg for dinner is a different case from a dog who cannot stop retching. The first job is to separate those two pictures before you reach for rice, ginger, or a leftover pill.

What the episode looks like

Vomiting is an active heave: abdominal effort, then food, fluid, or bile. Regurgitation is quieter and usually brings up undigested food in a tube shape, often right after eating. That distinction matters because regurgitation points more toward esophagus problems, while repeated vomiting points toward the stomach, intestines, pancreas, toxins, or a blockage.

Yellow foam usually means bile from an empty stomach. One bile episode in an otherwise bright dog can follow a long gap between meals. Green material is also bile. Blood that looks like coffee grounds, or fresh red blood, needs a same-day exam. So does vomit that smells like stool, which can happen with a serious intestinal obstruction.

Go to an emergency clinic now

  • Unproductive retching with a tight, swelling belly, especially in a deep-chested dog. That pattern is gastric dilatation-volvulus until a veterinarian says otherwise. Do not wait it out at home.
  • Collapse, pale gums, a racing heart, or vomiting with labored breathing.
  • Known or possible toxin: chocolate, xylitol gum, grapes, raisins, rodent bait, human painkillers, or a chewed bottle.
  • A puppy who vomits, especially with diarrhea, listlessness, or blood. Parvovirus still kills unvaccinated puppies.
  • Vomit that continues for more than a few hours, or more than two or three episodes in a day, in any dog.

The ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center is (888) 426-4435. A fee may apply. If the dog is collapsing, skip the phone call and drive.

When a clinic visit can be the same day

Call your regular veterinarian the same day if an adult dog vomits several times but still stands, walks, and greets you. Bring a photo of the vomit, a list of foods and chews from the last day, and any missing toy or sock you cannot find. Foreign bodies are common and do not always show up as a dramatic belly.

Pancreatitis often shows up as vomiting plus a praying posture, where the chest is down and the hind end stays up. Garbage, fatty table scraps, and some medications raise that risk. Kidney disease, liver disease, and Addison's disease can also announce themselves as a suddenly vomiting dog who used to be fine. Those are not home-kitchen problems.

A single vomit in a bright adult

If the dog vomited once, is playful, and has normal gums, many clinics will let you pick up water after a short pause and skip the next meal. Offer a small portion of the usual food at the following meal, not a new recipe. A sudden diet change is a common reason the vomiting continues. Puppies, dogs under about ten pounds, and diabetic dogs should not be fasted while you watch the clock. Call first.

Do not give ibuprofen, naproxen, acetaminophen, or pepto-style products unless a veterinarian names the product and the dose. Several of those drugs injure the stomach, kidneys, or liver.

What to tell the clinic

Count the episodes. Note the color. Say whether the dog is still drinking, whether urine and stool are normal, and whether the belly seems bigger than this morning. Mention vaccines if the patient is a puppy, and mention heat cycle, trash raids, and new chews. That history shortens the visit more than a long internet search does.

Dog vomiting questions

When is dog vomiting an emergency?

Go now for unproductive retching, a swelling belly, collapse, blood, repeated vomiting, or a puppy who is dull or dehydrated.

Can I give a dog human anti-nausea medicine?

No. Several human drugs are dangerous for dogs. Ask a clinic before giving anything from your cabinet.

Should I withhold food after one vomit?

A bright adult dog who vomits once can often skip the next meal and keep water available. Do not fast puppies, toy dogs, or diabetics without calling a clinic.
